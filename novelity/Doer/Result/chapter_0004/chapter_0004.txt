Old Zheng bit into the chicken foot in his hand and spat out a bone.
"Too salty."
He wiped his mouth and put the food down.
"Now then, let's talk about why we are here."
He stopped and looked at the crowd surrounding him. Everyone was staring, waiting for him to speak.
"What are you all looking at? Done training? You think listening to me talk will make you stronger? Move!"
He picked up a thin wooden stick from behind his chair and whipped it through the air. The harsh snaps forced the men to scatter in pain, leaving only Wei He standing in the yard.
"Right. Where were we?" Old Zheng scratched his messy hair.
"You were about to explain what makes us different from regular people," Wei He said quickly.
"Ah, yes. That."
The old man moved so slowly it made people want to hit him. He took a long breath in, then let it out.
"Why do we train? Simple. To beat other people in a fight, right?"
"Right," Wei He agreed. He was curious about the martial arts of this world.
In his past life on Earth, traditional martial arts became weak because people stopped testing them in real fights. Modern fighters easily beat them. Wei He did not believe traditional fighting was useless from the start. Throughout history, strong fighters existed. The real problem was that people stopped fighting for real, hiding behind old titles. He wanted to know if this world was any different.
Old Zheng finally stopped wasting time.
"We train so we can win fights. That is the whole point. Martial arts are just methods to beat people up or kill them."
He rubbed his beard. "I don't care what others say. That is the basis of my Mountain-Returning Fist. And winning comes down to basic things: who is stronger, who is faster, who hits clearer, and who can take a beating."
"Well said, Master," Wei He said right away.
Old Zheng smiled. "The theory is easy. But how do you get stronger and faster? You train. That is our goal. But..." His tone shifted. "No one can do everything."
He sighed. "A person only has so much energy and talent. If you can master just one thing, you are doing well."
He looked at Wei He's arms. "My style focuses on your hands and fists. Rich people buy thick gloves to protect their hands and hit harder. But you are poor."
Old Zheng leaned back lazily. "So train your hands. Make your knuckles hard and your muscles strong. That is how you hurt people."
He stood up and pointed at a tall, quiet man standing nearby. "Zhao Hong, take your new junior brother. Teach him how to build strength first."
"Okay," the tall man replied in a flat, honest voice.
Wei He looked at Zhao Hong. The man was built like a bear, much larger than the others. He looked like the kind of honest guy who ended up doing all the hard work in the yard.
"Junior Brother, I am Zhao Hong, the First Senior Brother," the man said. "I will teach you how to build raw power. But to gain strength, you need to eat meat. You have to find a way to get better food."
"I understand," Wei He said. "Brother Zhao, have you ever heard of internal force masters?"
"Internal force? What is that? Never heard of it," Zhao Hong said, shaking his head.
"You just joined today, so follow me and lift stones."
"Lift stones?" Wei He felt a bit cheated. He paid a huge fee just to lift rocks?
"Yes, but there is a trick to it," Zhao Hong said as he led Wei He to a corner filled with heavy rocks. "You have to lift slowly. And you must remember our movement rhyme."
Zhao Hong recited a simple line: "Stand like a bent tree in the wind, keep your feet rooted deep in the ground."
Wei He stayed quiet. It sounded like a cheap street chant.
"Watch me first," Zhao Hong said. He picked up a stone as wide as his chest and held it against his stomach. He moved it up and down with soft, easy movements. The rock moved smoothly, but his feet stayed completely still on the ground, like they were glued to the dirt.
Wei He began to get it. "This is how we build power?"
"Yes. Pick a stone that fits you," Zhao Hong explained. "First you build strength. Then you learn practical moves, toughen your skin, and learn footwork. Last comes real fighting. Once your blood grows thick and your fists can break wooden posts without hurting, you move to the next step."
"How do I know when my blood is thick enough?" Wei He asked.
"Our style has three stages for skin training: Cow Hide, Stone Hide, and Iron Hide. It describes how hard your hands get. Once you reach Cow Hide, your strength and blood are ready."
"Got it. When do we start?"
"Right now," Zhao Hong said cleanly.
Wei He picked a stone with Zhao Hong's help and started training. By the end of the afternoon, his whole body hurt, and his arms felt useless.
Then something surprising happened.
Old Zheng provided a special medicine soup called Small Ten-Thousand Soup. Every paying student got one bowl a day. Zhao Hong brought him a bowl. Wei He drank it, and when he woke up the next morning, the deep soreness in his muscles was completely gone.
Wei He realized this world had its own hidden secrets. Old Zheng charged a huge fee, equivalent to a full year of living costs for a normal family, yet people still came. The old man knew what he was doing.
From that day on, Wei He trained hard with Zhao Hong every day. He took one break every four days. On his rest days, he continued doing homework for rich kids to earn extra money and learn how to read. He managed to make enough money to cover his costs and even save a little.
His sister, Wei Ying, moved into a small, broken room owned by Old Zheng near the yard. Old Zheng let her stay for free since she helped with chores. It was run-down, but it was safe from Chen Biao and his two thugs.
With his life set up, Wei He put all his focus into training. He was not strong enough to kill Chen Biao yet, but he was patient. Time was on his side.
Days turned into weeks.
Wei He woke up before sunrise every day to train his body. Even though the school offered food, he bought extra meat cakes to eat after work out. He needed the extra food to grow his body and feed the mysterious Break-Through Bead inside his chest.
As he spent money on food, his body grew wider and stronger. The bead on his chest slowly turned darker, though it was not pure black yet.
Seeing that Wei He was settled at the school, Chen Biao's group backed off slightly. But whenever Wei He walked home, he could still feel Chen Biao watching him like a snake in the dark.
Three months passed in a flash.
It was early morning, long before dawn.
Wei He wrapped his arms around a heavy black stone, moving it up and down as his muscles worked hard. Other men were around him doing the same thing.
After teaching him the basics, Zhao Hong left him to train on his own and went to teach a new student.
The work was boring, but feeling his body grow stronger every single day gave Wei He a sense of peace.
"Junior Brother Wei, how is it going today? Ready for the heavier stones in the second row?"
A young man named Cheng Shaojiu walked over, pulled off his outer robe, and picked up a stone to warm up.
Cheng Shaojiu was the Third Senior Brother. Over the last few months, he had grown close to Wei He. At first, he only talked to Wei He because he had eyes on Wei He's sister. But after spending time together, he realized Wei He was a quiet, tough guy who kept his promises and spoke with good sense. Cheng Shaojiu started to truly like him as a friend.
"Not yet," Wei He said, putting the rock down to rest his joints. "I haven't been training long enough."
"No rush," Cheng Shaojiu smiled. "There is a music gathering in the south part of town this afternoon. A famous lute player is performing. I am going to check it out. Want to come?"
Cheng Shaojiu liked to act like a cultured gentleman, so he liked hanging out with Wei He, who knew how to read. He asked Wei He out every time there was an event like this. Wei He never went, but Cheng Shaojiu kept asking, mostly to show off that he had the money to enjoy music in hard times like these.
"I'll pass," Wei He said, lifting the rock again. "Third Brother, did you find any news about the matter I asked you about?"
