Daylight just broke.
Wei He flipped over and sat up from his bed.
His face looked exhausted. Sweat drenched his forehead, and the veins at his temples throbbed heavily.
"I haven't dreamed in a long time... I didn't expect to dream about the past again."
He recalled many things from his past life. Now, it always felt like looking through a sheet of blurry glass—like flowers in the mist or the moon reflected in water, impossible to see clearly.
In contrast, every single scene of his current life was as sharp as a videotape. He could recall any moment whenever he wanted.
He did not know if this was because his blood and vitality were strong enough to retain more details, or if it was due to something else.
He pulled back the quilt and got out of bed. He quickly scooped cold water from the water barrel and scrubbed his body down with it. Then he changed into a fresh set of clothes.
Wei Ying was already in the kitchen making breakfast.
Now, under Wei He's strong demand, the two of them had set a routine of eating breakfast every day.
You had to understand that ordinary families could not afford breakfast at all. In this current world, most people were lucky if they could just get enough to eat.
Eating only one or two meals a day was normal, let alone three.
Luckily, Wei He brought back game from his hunts from time to time. The mountain forest was a strange place. Even though there was a constant severe drought, the woods remained half-dead and stubborn. The yellow-green grass and trees refused to fall.
Animals and game frequently hid inside it.
Wei He did not know why, but because of this, he could occasionally catch some game, so he did not bother thinking too much about it.
"I heard Daoist Ming Xian is giving a sermon today. A lot of people plan to check it out. Xiao He, I want to go too. Can I?" Wei Ying looked at Wei He with a bit of hope, holding a coarse grain bun she had made.
"Is it like the last sermon? The one with a huge crowd?" Wei He asked. He looked down, grabbed a strip of dried meat, and stuffed it into his mouth.
"Yeah. But... there were a lot of people last time. Now, there might not be many left," Wei Ying replied with a sigh.
"Can you even understand it? I couldn't understand a single thing that Daoist was talking about," Wei He said.
"I don't understand it, but they give out free food!" Wei Ying answered, opening her eyes wide.
"...Then don't join the crowd for such a small thing. We aren't short of that little bit of food at home," Wei He rejected her.
"Alright... fair enough."
After the two finished breakfast, Wei Ying mentioned something else while cleaning up the dishes.
"Oh right, Xiao He, I heard something from Master Zheng yesterday. It seems someone did a strange thing near Red Stone Town recently."
"A strange thing?"
"Yeah. Word is, that person put a massive piece of iron ore on top of a pile of dried meat. He said as long as anyone could lift the stone, they could take the meat for themselves," Wei Ying said in a casual tone, as if she found it interesting.
Wei He froze for a moment when he heard this.
"And then?"
"And then, a lot of people went up and tried, but no one could move it.
In the end, a man named Xu Ran stepped up. He lifted it with both hands and raised that iron stone effortlessly—the same stone no one else could budge. Then he took the meat away."
Wei Ying sighed in praise, then added, "The guy who set down the meat invited Xu Ran to his house as a guest and treated him to a huge feast. The next day, he started putting down meat under a stone again. This time, the stone was even heavier than before. Anyone who could lift it could take the meat. Xiao He, do you want to go give it a try?"
In Wei Ying's mind, her younger brother had trained in martial arts step by step to build himself up. He was a very, very powerful person now. Lifting a stone should be no problem for him, right?
Wei He felt differently.
He was not like regular people. His experience and knowledge were on a different level. As soon as he heard this, he understood what was going on.
Someone was building up a reputation to make a name for himself!
What was the goal of making a name for oneself? Nothing more than attracting attention.
Whose attention?
The Seven Families Alliance, the Hong Family Fort, and the crowds of common people.
Only with fame came the chance to stand high and call out to others, gathering a crowd of followers. Only then would a person have the chance to show their talent and earn an invitation or respect from those in power.
This method was called self-recommendation.
Of course, there was another possibility. It could be a rough method used by a big faction to screen for talent among the common folk.
However, if a big faction acted, it likely would not be this crude.
Wei He suddenly felt an urge. Every day, he did nothing but train in martial arts, go home, hunt, and kill people. He had almost no other entertainment.
Now that he heard his second sister mention this, he suddenly wanted to see how she usually passed the time.
Even though the nearby Mountain-Returning Fist Academy helped keep an eye on things, it was still better to carefully check for any potential hidden dangers.
At this thought, he stood up and quickly went back to his room. He carefully put on all his gear, then asked his second sister Wei Ying about her usual route in detail.
Finally, he opened the door and strode away.
Leaving the house, he did not head toward the Mountain-Returning Fist Academy. Instead, he walked in the opposite direction toward the town market.
The market in Stone Bridge Town was only open for two hours in the morning. If you did not go early, everything would be snatched up very quickly.
Wei He walked along the street, rushing toward the market.
Along the way, he saw that the closer he got to the market, the more men and women he saw in different kinds of clothes. Carrying all sorts of containers, they rushed toward the market in a hurry.
Some of these people wore ragged clothes, while others looked somewhat clean and neat. But they all had one thing in common: none of them were dressed richly.
The best among them were merely neat with a few patches.
Wei He's clean and tight-fitting outfit actually stood out in the crowd.
He did not care, though. Along the way, he had never realized that so many living people could come out of these quiet, run-down houses by the roadside.
He had almost thought the outer city was completely empty.
But now, seeing these weak, malnourished people walking out one by one made him feel the cold misery unique to this era.
Soon, after crossing a small area of gray single-story houses, several stone tablets stood ahead. Written on the tablets in black ink were the two large words: Vegetable Market.
The stone tablets were lined up one by one, blocking the large open ground behind them.
There was only a very small entrance left for people to enter and leave.
At the entrance, a group of strong men dressed in black stood guard. They held short knives in their hands, looking very intimidating.
"Form a line! Enter one by one, no pushing!" Shouts kept echoing out.
Wei He walked closer and stood outside the stone tablets. He watched the crowd slowly enter and then begin trading in front of the vegetable and meat stalls on the open ground.
"Who are these men in black?" He grabbed a thin young man and asked.
The man looked at Wei He. He wanted to blow his top, but seeing Wei He's build and intimidating aura, he had to swallow his anger. He said in a low voice, "They are the tough guys from the City-Connecting Gang. Going in doesn't cost money, but when you come out, no matter what you bought or traded for, you have to give them one-tenth of it. That is the rule of this market."
"One-tenth?" Wei He understood.
He let go of the man. Instead of going inside, he turned around and rushed toward the next spot.
Every day, Wei Ying had to buy vegetables here first. Then she would go to the oil shop to get oil, occasionally visit the cloth shop, and finally go home to filter water, cook, wash clothes, and clean up.
Wei He followed the street, left the vegetable market, and soon reached the oil shop.
There were only two oil shops. A long line of people waited at the entrance. The shop opened only a small door less than a meter wide, allowing only one person to pass at a time.
The clothes and spirit of the people buying oil here were much better than those at the vegetable market.
There were far fewer patches on their clothes.
Wei He took a walk around. Wherever he went, people lowered their heads, not daring to look at him.
His height was six feet tall, and his body was packed with muscle. Added to the fact that he had killed many men and wild beasts, he clearly looked dangerous.
Because of this, others did not even dare to look at him more than once.
After walking around, Wei He headed toward the cloth shop.
Leaving the short street where the oil shop was located, the houses on both sides of the road gradually became cleaner.
Clearly, people swept them often.
By the roadside, one could occasionally see burnt incense sticks stuck into the dirt.
Some doors were pasted with fresh pictures of Door Gods and Lucky Generals. Some people even pasted couplets they wrote themselves.
The street between the oil shop and the cloth shop was not long, but there were quite a few shops.
Right now was the peak time for the market. Every shop opened its doors one after another.
This was the first time Wei He had ever seen such a lively scene. At this time of day, he was usually practicing hard to toughen his skin at the Mountain-Returning Fist Academy. How could he ever see such a lively sight?
The last time he saw a scene this busy was before he started practicing martial arts.
Feeling curious, Wei He looked left and right, checking out the shops that were open.
Although he did not count carefully, only a dozen or so shops were actually open. They only looked plentiful because they were spread out.
However, a huge crowd was pouring in.
When there were many people, it naturally looked lively. From jewelry to bamboo crafts, from chairs to medicinal herbs, to farm tools, everything was available.
Yet, after walking around, Wei He noticed that the shops here were all selling second-hand junk.
It seemed to be stuff bought at low prices from various households.
Traces of everyday use could be seen on many of the items.
His heart stirred, and he started browsing the surrounding area as well.
Beside the shops, people set up street stalls, seemingly selling things taken from their own homes.
Large straw mats were spread over the stalls. On top lay pots, bowls, plates, chairs, stools, pillows, blankets, combs, hairpins, and all sorts of items.
Wei He walked up to a stall, stopped, squatted down, and reached out to gently pick up a small wine cup.
The wine cup was made of white porcelain and looked very clean. A branch of red plum blossoms was painted on the outside.
"A set of twelve wine cups. Take them all for one pound of coarse grain flour," the stall owner said weakly.
Wei He put down the wine cup and scanned the stall.
Soon, he focused his attention on a messy pile of books and papers.
The books looked chewed up by rats or something, in terrible condition.
He picked one up and flipped through it. It was full of rules and study materials for taking the civil and military exams.
"These books were my late father's treasure. A pile of fifteen books. Even though some pages are missing, I'll let you have them for ten pounds of coarse grain flour. What do you think?" Seeing Wei He touch the books, the stall owner suddenly felt a ray of hope.
These books were the most valuable things on his stall. If he had not run out of options, he would never have put them up for sale.
But now, his wife and child were at home waiting for grain to fill the pot. If he kept hiding them away, by the time his family starved to death, these things would belong to someone else anyway.
Wei He flipped through them. Although the book did not teach any martial arts techniques or moves, it mentioned a fair amount of related knowledge and stories.
He bargained with the owner and traded four small strips of dried meat he carried on him for the bag of books.
The four small strips of dried meat were his daily rations for boosting his nutrition, not a main meal. They weighed less than half a pound in total.
After all, times were hard, so he had to save food where he could. Even so, this trade satisfied the stall owner completely.
Taking these hard, dried strips of meat back, he could cut off a tiny piece with a knife each time and mix it into porridge, which could sustain them for a long time.
Eating coarse grains alone could not stave off hunger at all.
