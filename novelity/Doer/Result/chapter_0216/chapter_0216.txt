Wei He looked at the boy. The last time he saw him, he was just a kid playing with ants. Now he was taller, looking like a young teenager.
"Master, Mr. Wei," Yuan Bao said nervously, bowing to both of them.
"My formula has halted the plague for now," Jiuying said. "It won't spread, but a full cure will take time. I asked you here so you could see Yuan Bao, and also..."
He pulled out a stack of papers from his robe and handed them over.
"This is the prescription. Keep it safe. If you get the chance, spread it to as many people as you can."
Wei He took it. "Why don't you publish it yourself? You could gain great fame."
"With you, the grand Sect Master Wei, around, why should I worry about that?" Jiuying smiled slightly.
"Fair enough. I'll make sure your name is on it. Anything else?" Wei He asked.
"Nothing else. My only wish now is to protect Yuan Bao and my old friend's two children. The rest can wait." Jiuying sighed.
Wei He looked at him, feeling a bad premonition.
Jiuying didn't look like a martial artist at all right now. Like the sick people outside, his face was pale, his energy drained.
"Why look at me like that?" Jiuying laughed softly. "I left my notes and research materials at the lab for you. Use them as you see fit. I won't be going back anytime soon. If Yuan Bao has questions, answer them if you feel like it. If not, don't worry about it."
"Alright," Wei He agreed.
"Good. We've been through a lot together, but there's not much left to say. I've lived for decades. I've had highs and lows. I enjoyed what I could and experienced enough. No regrets." Jiuying grinned, showing a row of yellow teeth.
"Master..." Yuan Bao sensed something was wrong and grabbed Jiuying's hand.
"Also... about the research topics you asked me before," Jiuying added. "You solved the Silk Force yourself. As for the Whale Breath state, I found a clue for you."
Wei He's eyes lit up. "What clue?"
"I found an old record. A famous artist once painted a picture called the Moon and Whale. It holds two artistic concepts: the Whale Breath and the Moon Breath. If you find that painting, you should find the state you're looking for."
Jiuying spoke a lot in one breath. His cheeks flushed red, and he leaned down to cough quietly.
"The Moon and Whale painting... I'll look for it," Wei He said. "Rest now. This clue is important to me. If it's real, I owe you a favor."
"Then I wish you success," Jiuying smiled.
"Good."
Wei He didn't say more and turned to leave.
He planned to use his sect's network and ask the Wang family to help search for the painting.
Besides that, after discussing Wang Shaojun's offer with his senior members, Wei He decided to join the Taian Army.
They never wanted to be hunted down by the Chijing Army again. Joining the Taian Army meant allying with the Wang family and the Governor's faction.

Thunder rumbled in the distance. Heavy rain poured down, drowning Xuanjing City in a cold grey mist.
Swallows flew low through the storm. Martial artists occasionally leaped across the wet rooftops.
In Yuanqi District, a middle-aged man with a yellowish complexion walked down the street. He wore black robes with wide sleeves, a sword at his waist, and had an unreadable face. He held a black oil-paper umbrella with white willow leaves painted on it.
He stopped in front of a shop with a sign that read 'Autumn Wind and Fine Rain'.
The shop had a half-open door. It was quiet inside, with faint sounds of birds and soft chatter.
In Xuanjing City, private clubs like this were common. But this place was special because it offered tea and allowed guests to read basic books and travel logs. Books were expensive luxuries, so it drew a small crowd.
The man stepped under the roof, closed his umbrella, and looked down. His shoes were covered in thick mud.
He sighed, put the umbrella into a bamboo basket, wiped his feet, and walked inside.
Inside, screens separated the tables. It was half full.
The man walked to the corner and sat down across from an old woman in green. She wore a black veil, a raincoat, and rested her hand on a dragon-head cane.
"Long time no see," the old woman said coldly.
"It has been a long time," the man replied, his face expressionless.
"Thirty years?"
"Thirty-two."
"I thought you would never come back. Yet here you are," she mocked.
"Just a coincidence," he sighed. "I forgot the past. I only came back for my duties."
"Who could force you to obey?" she asked, surprised.
"I cannot say. But now, the Xunri and Liuye organizations control Taizhou from the shadows and the light. This province is firmly in our hands."
"You changed," the old woman said darkly. "You were never this cruel."
"That was the past. And I am not cruel," he said softly. "I am just following orders. If you came to beg for mercy, you are wasting your time."
"The Chijing Army, the Tieyi Army... you manipulated both? You used the martial arts world to destroy our biggest threats with empty promises. No wonder the Governor trusts you so much. But now you want to strike your own family? Do you even remember your own name?" she demanded.
"To gain something, you must sacrifice something. The past cannot hold me back," he said calmly. "Besides, I came because you are my family."
"Is this how you treat family?"
"If people see that I am strict even with my own family, no one can complain when my policies hit them."
"So you plan to wipe out every sect in Taizhou?!" The old woman stood up abruptly, pointing a finger at him.
"Reforms always require blood," the man said, looking up. "Sister, do you know what I saw out there? If we don't change, do you know what awaits us?"
"I don't care! You are bloodthirsty and lost!" She turned away in anger. "From now on, you are no longer my brother, nor a member of the Xie family!"
She turned and left.
The man remained seated, his back straight. He didn't watch her leave. He simply picked up his teacup and drank it clean.

"By order of the Governor: All small and medium sects in the province must be cleared within one year. No martial arts sects, noble families, or dojos are allowed to form factions."
"All fertile land and resources will be reclaimed. Those who commit evil will be executed. Those who obey will be recruited. Resistance means death!"
A messenger in black read the orders aloud from a official cloth scroll stamped with a red seal.
The Wang family, led by Wang Zhihe, bowed as they listened.
Wang Shaojun and Wei He stood near the back. In front of them were the core members of the Wang family, including Wang Shaojun's father, Wang Yehe.
"General Wang, take the order," the messenger said, handing it over.
Wang Zhihe closed his eyes, sighed, and accepted it with both hands. "Wang Zhihe accepts the order."
"The Commander expects great things from you. Don't disappoint him," the messenger smiled.
"I understand," Wang Zhihe said solemnly.
In truth, he felt helpless. Martial arts were deeply rooted in Taizhou. Finishing this task in one year was nearly impossible.
"Don't worry," the messenger added. "The Xunri and Liuye organizations will handle the main work. You only need to handle the minor details. After all, these martial artists are just violent threats. Removing them is what everyone wants."
Wang Zhihe remained silent.
Behind him, Wang Shaojun and Wei He exchanged a glance.
Soon, the messenger left in his carriage.
The Wang family called an emergency meeting, while Wang Shaojun and Wei He returned to a private courtyard.
After the servant girls served tea and left, the two men sat across from each other.
"Now we know," Wang Shaojun smiled bitterly. "Why the Chijing Army attacked the Tianyin Sect, why the Lishan Sect fell into trouble, and why the Danyang Sect had issues. It was all planned from the top."
Wei He stayed quiet. He felt lucky. If he weren't close friends with Wang Shaojun, his Wanqing Sect would have been targeted too.
Even though his sect was safe for now, he still needed to help to prove his loyalty.
"Old Wei, I need your help with this," Wang Shaojun said seriously.
"How so?" Wei He asked.
"This order is also a test to see how capable local leaders are. If the Wang family completes this well, our position will rise."
Wei He fell silent.
