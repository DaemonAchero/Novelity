The next morning.
Before dawn, Wei He was already on the road inside a wooden carriage. The wooden wheels bumped over the uneven dirt, shaking the cabin up and down.
Inside, Wei He carefully checked his gear. He went through his weapons, tools, poisons, and special antidotes.
His White Jade Skill could block basic poisons, but it was useless against the really lethal stuff. At its core, the technique worked by building up tolerance. You took mild doses of dangerous herbs over time, forcing your body to adapt. But if a poison was too strong or completely unfamiliar, the trick failed. On top of that, making your body immune to toxins also meant normal healing medicine no longer worked on you.
Wei He sat quietly in the dark cabin. Outside, the horses snorted against the cold air. Listening to the steady rhythm, his mind slowly settled.
The trip from Xuanjing City to Zhongling Mountain Manor was long. You had to pass the Tianyin Sect along the way, making the full journey even further than coming straight from the sect itself.
After passing the main gates of the Tianyin Sect, the road grew crowded. More horses and carriages filled the path, all moving in the same direction toward the manor.
At midday, Wei He and his driver pulled over at a roadside tea stall to rest.
People in Taizhou were tough and ruthless. They built tea stalls right on the open highway without fear. The owner was a blind old man with a wooden crutch, served by a few quiet young workers. One look was enough to tell he was an old fighter who had retired from the violent world of the martial arts.
The stall was crude—just a few long wooden planks topped with dried leaves for shade, shelter for about ten rough tables and chairs.
The weather was heavy and humid. Wei He picked a clean spot and sat down.
One table over, three large men sat with their shirts off. They had cropped short hair, thick muscles, and heavy calluses on their knuckles—classic fist fighters. They were eating snacks, gulping down hot green tea, and talking loudly about the manor.
"...So about this marriage tournament," one said. "You said anyone can enter as long as they win, right? If I win, do I get to marry Wan Qingqing?"
"Keep dreaming," his friend mocked. "A big event like this isn't for weak trash. With your pathetic skills, you'd get thrown off the stage in one second!"
"Bullshit! I've trained hard for thirteen years! Why should I fear some young woman?"
"Then go try it! If you get lucky and win, you become the husband of the Senior Sister of the Wanqing Branch. You'd walk around Xuanjing City like a king!"
"Yeah, maybe ten guys will tire her out first. Then you step up, hit her when her legs go weak, and take the prize!"
The two teased their friend, leaving the man red-faced and arguing back. He knew he wasn't good enough, but he refused to lose the verbal fight.
Wei He looked away and signaled the worker to take some tea and food to his driver.
Old Li, his driver, was a veteran worker from the sect. He knew basic martial arts, but because of his age, he had no hope of breakthrough. He relied on crude leg techniques and body hardening. His blood force was barely at the Second Blood level, but he knew how to survive a real fight.
Wei He had invited him to sit, but the old man stubbornly refused, insisting on watching the carriage so no one would steal their belongings. Wei He didn't force him.
After sitting for a short while, the horses finished drinking water and eating their grass. Wei He stood up to leave.
Suddenly, the sound of fast hooves echoed down the dirt road.
These were no ordinary horses. They were lean, strong, with glossy coats. Most strangely, their hooves ended in thick, cat-like paws with soft pads beneath them. They ran almost silently, moving like wild beasts hunting prey.
The riders were just as strange. Men and women alike had perfect posture, lean power, and cold, sharp eyes. They rode four beasts in total, moving in total unison, barely kicking up any dust as they sped past.
They didn't stop. They flew past the tea stall and vanished down the road.
"The Chijing Army... truly different from normal men," someone muttered after a long silence.
"I heard the rebel army in Chenzhou is getting bigger," another man said softly. "They broke the Imperial army again. Their leader, Zhang Song, calls himself King of the Mountain now. He's building his own government..."
"The city hall posted a notice yesterday," another sighed. "Drafting men for the war isn't far off."
"No way... We have a hundred thousand Chijing soldiers here. Aren't those rebels just poor farmers?"
"Exactly. Commander Yuchi Zhong is a veteran general. He won't let rebels cross into our land. Besides, Taizhou is full of fighters. If those rebels come, they'll die here!"
The conversation spread across the tables. Everyone joined in, arguing loudly.
No one was talking about the marriage tournament anymore. Now they spoke of the army, wild beasts, bandit attacks, and local gossip.
Wei He held his warm tea cup, feeling a strange detachment.
To martial artists like him, the fights, grudges, and schemes between sects felt like the entire world. But outside that little bubble, the real world was falling apart. Rebel armies grew, warlords claimed land, the Emperor lost control, and chaos spread everywhere.
Normal people only cared about staying alive today. The struggles of martial artists were just tiny drops of water in a massive storm.
"Are you... Senior Brother Wei He from Yubei District?"
A soft voice pulled Wei He back to reality.
He turned and saw two young women wearing the outer robes of the Tianyin Sect standing nearby. They looked nervous, worried they had guessed wrong.
They were not from his branch. One had a plain, gentle face; the other looked sharp and confident. Their bodies were tight and fit from years of training, though they tried to hide their heavy arm muscle beneath custom clothes.
"And you are?" Wei He asked calmly. He didn't know them, but this was what he had been waiting for—travel companions.
"You don't know us, but we know your name!" the confident one said with a wide smile. She pointed to her shy friend. "She is Meng Qing, and I am Zhang Lingxu. We saw you from a distance a few times back at Yuxing Market. People say you reached the Force stage in just a few years and became a district leader. We didn't expect to run into you here."
"Zhang Lingxu, Meng Qing. Good names," Wei He smiled. "Which branch are you from?"
"We are just outer members, not like you," Zhang Lingxu said, blushing slightly. "But Qing's talent is good. She'll enter the inner branch soon. If she joins the Wanqing Branch, please look after her, Senior Brother!"
"Sure," Wei He agreed. "I'm not anyone grand in the inner court, but I can protect a junior sister."
They sat together and talked. As expected, the two were also going to Zhongling Mountain Manor to watch the event. Wan Qingqing was a top-tier fighter at the peak of the Force stage. Opportunities to see such a master fight in public were rare.
Since they were heading the same way, they agreed to travel together.
Wei He noticed the two women weren't alone. A group of ten strong guards followed them, including two men who clearly possessed Second Blood power. Their families were obviously rich.
More people meant safer travel. Wei He was pleased with the setup.
Zhongling Mountain Manor.
Early morning. Near the arena built for the tournament stood a small building against the mountain face.
Inside the main hall, Wan Ling, the master of the Wanqing Branch, sat straight in the high seat, wearing a fine silk dress.
To her right sat a calm middle-aged man with the air of a scholar. He wore wide black robes, but the loose fabric failed to hide his thick, powerful muscles. A neat black beard hung to his chest. The mix of brute strength and scholarly air made him impossible to ignore.
This was Zhou Shun, one of the Vice Masters of the Tianyin Sect.
Next to Wan Ling sat Xiao Qingyu, leader of the Zhenglin Branch and one of the Nine Blades of Tianyin. Since both were female leaders, they were close, and Wan Ling had invited her for support.
An old man named Liu, the chief manager of the manor, sat nearby. He looked like an ordinary old farmer, but several Third Blood fighters stood behind him as bodyguards, showing his true status.
Below them sat various young fighters, including Wan Qingqing and Zhou Yugui.
A large chandelier hung from the ceiling, holding over a hundred burning candles that drove every shadow from the room.
Wan Ling stood up and bowed slightly to the two high-ranking masters. "Thank you, Vice Master Zhou and Leader Xiao, for coming to oversee my daughter's event."
"You are too polite, Master Wan," Zhou Shun replied with a gentle smile.
"Why be so distant with me?" Xiao Qingyu said warmly. "I watched Qingqing grow up. Of course I would come."
Wan Ling looked at her friend with genuine gratitude. She had few allies in the sect, but Xiao Qingyu had always stood by her. It was thanks to Xiao Qingyu's backing that Qingqing had been granted the freedom to choose her own husband.
Xiao Qingyu was over fifty, but her skin remained smooth and unwrinkled. She was a plain-looking woman, but her steady support meant everything to Wan Ling.
Pulling her thoughts back, Wan Ling clapped her hands.
"Now that everyone is here, let us settle the rules. Many men have come today, but I have only one daughter. To keep weak fighters from wasting her strength before the real matches begin, we will use three examiners to filter out the weak."
