“What’s your plan?” Wei He narrowed his eyes.
“Keep things as they are,” Mo Shiyan said softly.
Wei He’s mind raced.
“We’ll help Sun Zhu keep running,” she continued. “That lets you stay next to Zhu Chen and keep your power. As long as Sun Zhu stays alive, we buy more time. All we ask from you is a bit of inside info.”
“You want me as a mole?” Wei He asked straight out.
“Call it a partnership. Being a mole is up to you.” She smiled, waiting for his move.
Wei He needed time too. The longer this dragged on, the more he could use the Breakthrough Bead. With the Silkworm Silk Power speeding things up, he was close to finishing his core foundation. After that, he could train his organs. Time was everything right now.
If someone else took the heat off Sun Zhu, this might actually work.
“Good idea,” Wei He said. “But we don't trust each other.”
“What builds trust for you, Sect Leader Wei?”
“How do I know you’re not a spy from Sunri Liuye? Prove who you are.”
“Easy.” She clapped once.
A figure in blue dropped down lightly beside them and tossed something onto the ground.
Wei He looked down. It was a bloody, severed head!
It belonged to Zhan Heng—the Frost Punch master!
“Is this enough proof?” Mo Shiyan smiled.
She clapped again, and the blue-clad person vanished into the reeds.
“Just hold out until July 1st,” she said with cold certainty. “When our Alliance Master arrives, Zhu Chen dies.”
“July 1st...” Wei He took a deep breath, staring at the head on the grass.
Mo Shiyan clearly had a powerful faction behind her, just like Zhu Chen did. Even though Zhan Heng was already hurt, taking his head so easily showed terrifying strength.
They wanted him to stay near Zhu Chen and leak intelligence. This ruined his original plan. He had planned to go into hiding, using the threat of random strikes to force Zhu Chen to back off and keep the Ten Thousand Green Sect safe. But staying hidden seemed like a bad move now.
“One last question,” Wei He said.
“Ask.”
“How strong is the Engraved Sense realm, really?”
“Engraved Sense changes your perceptions,” Mo Shiyan explained. “Your senses mutate depending on what you perceive when you break through.”
“And?”
“And that mutation alters your force.”
“Meaning?”
“Simple,” she said. “Two things happen. First, your force mutates and sharpens your senses way beyond normal humans. Second, that force becomes alive. It completely crushes anyone at the First Realm.”
Wei He fell silent. She knew too much about this.
“You guessed it,” she said, her smile fading. “My father was at that realm... until he failed.”
She looked at him. “You probably had your own plan before I showed up. I've said my piece. It’s your call.”
She flicked her wrist, sending a small white object flying toward him.
Wei He caught it cleanly. A small white pouch.
“What’s this?”
“Burn it if you're in. Soak it in water if you're out.”
Mo Shiyan turned, grabbing the severed head with a wave of invisible force.
“Farewell.”
She leaped into the air and vanished into the thick reeds.
Wei He stood alone, thinking fast. He opened the pouch. Inside was a tiny wooden cube carved with strange, natural patterns. It gave off a faint scent that quickly faded away.
Checking to make sure it was safe, he locked it inside a sealed box, turned around, and vanished into the night.
He had one goal now: keep training, stack the Breakthrough Bead, break through all nine martial arts, finish his cloud patterns, and rebuild his bones.
Two months passed in a flash.
By May, the rain had only gotten worse. The Jing River overflowed, swallowing whole streets along the banks. People had to use boats just to get around.
The farmlands outside the city were ruined. Floods destroyed crops, leaving farmers ruined and weeping in the mud. Food prices in the market shot through the roof.
The non-stop rain brought endless trouble, giving the ruling Wang family a massive headache.
Luckily, Xuantian City’s grain stores held up. The recent purges and mass executions had filled the empty vaults to the brim. And with the rebel army pulling back, the military stopped draining their supplies, allowing resources to build up again.
Inside a restaurant in the Central District, a group of young scholars sat around a table, drinking and chatting.
“The real food drain was the martial artists and gangs,” one said. “Fewer fighters means more food for everyone else. Prices are up, but people can still live.”
“General Wang’s move to let Zhu Chen clean out the gangs was a great call. The thugs who preyed on commoners are almost gone. The city is finally peaceful.”
“A toast to the General!”
“Cheers!”
While the scholars celebrated, a group sat quietly behind a screen in the corner.
Liang Zhen looked at his two business partners across the table, listening to the noise outside.
“Looks like the market here is stable,” Liang Zhen said. “Good time for us to move in.”
He patted the heavy-set youth next to him.
“Besides business, I need a favor from you two.”
The man across from him was fat, smiling, and dressed for wealth—Boss Zhou, one of the new big rice traders in town. The woman next to him, Boss Qin, was draped in gold and pearls, her eyes sharp and cold.
“We’ve done business for years, Old Liang,” Zhou laughed, waving his chubby hand. “Just say the word.”
“Same here,” Boss Qin said slowly. “Your shops helped us out before. I can handle a small favor.”
Liang Zhen smiled warmly, hiding his true thoughts.
“My nephew here has been obsessed with martial arts since he was a kid. He’s got talent, but our small town couldn't offer much. Now that we’re in Xuanjing, I want to find him a real master. Can you point us to the right door?”
The boy sitting next to him, Liang Shen, was about fifteen or sixteen. He had a thick build and pure, innocent eyes.
“I’ve heard about young Liang Shen,” Boss Qin said with a sigh. “So many martial schools have been shut down. If he wants to learn now, it has to be private instruction. Besides, he’s getting a bit old to start.”
“Is it true there’s a list of the Top Ten Masters in Xuanjing?” Liang Zhen asked.
“Just rumors,” Boss Zhou dismissed with a wave. “No one can agree on who’s strongest. But with the current rules, martial arts schools are banned. You’ll have to knock on doors and ask around.”
Liang Zhen nodded.
He had no idea his nephew’s mind was somewhere else entirely.
Liang Shen hadn't come to Xuanjing just to learn martial arts. He was born with a strange, hidden quirk that made him obsessed with women. In his small hometown, there weren't many attractive women to look at. But Xuanjing was massive.
He was practically drooling. On the outside, he acted like an obedient kid, but his mind was chasing every pretty girl and young woman who passed by.
After dinner, Liang Zhen brought him back to their new house and told him to rest.
Liang Shen nodded obediently, but as soon as his uncle closed the door, he changed his clothes and snuck out the back way.
The moment he stepped into the street, a group of riders galloped past. Leading them on a small white horse was a stunning girl with a fiery body and long braided hair.
Liang Shen froze against the wall, staring right at her. His heart pounded in his chest.
“Big cities really hit different...” he muttered.
He didn't dare do anything stupid—he just enjoyed the view.
“Pretty hot, isn't she?” a voice whispered. A hand dropped onto his shoulder.
Liang Shen jumped, spinning around fast.
A pale, handsome young man stood behind him, smirking right at his face.
