Third floor. Silence fell like a sudden chill, then snapped into loud disbelief. Nobody bought Yao Hansheng’s words.
"Senior Brother Yao," a man with a light mustache stood up, his voice cold and his rat-like eyes flashing with malice. "You just broke through to the Force Stage. But aren't you going too far, using yourself as a joke just to hype up military generals?"
"Junior Brother Wang," Yao Hansheng glanced at him with a deadpan expression. "Ten days ago, five top masters from the Sword-Zhe Temple and the Danyang Sect escorted two prominent scholars from Jiangzhou. On their way, they were ambushed by Jiangzhou pursuers. Five masters. One Bone Forging expert, four at Great Force Stage. Four dead, one wounded. And the troops that ambushed them? Only five hundred men."
"Nonsense!" The mustache man slammed his hand on the table, his chair scraping back hard. "Even if a Bone Forging expert doesn't focus on raw defense, killing an enemy commander in an army of thousands is as easy as taking something from a pocket! How could just a few hundred soldiers slaughter them?!"
"That was in the past," Yao Hansheng said, his voice dropping an octave. "Word is, the army has a new weapon. A firearm. Its power is lethal. When used in rotating ranks, the fire is continuous. No gaps. No flaws."
"Impossible!" another disciple interjected, frowning. "How can ordinary soldiers react faster than martial artists? No matter how strong a crossbow is, can a peasant even aim fast enough to track us?"
"They don't need to be as strong as us," Yao Hansheng replied flatly. "They only need to reach First or Second Blood level—just enough to boost their reaction time so they can track our movement. At a distance, formed up in battle ranks... the outcome is obvious."
Firearms?!
Wei He felt a cold spike in his chest.
He never expected firearms to exist in this world, in this era.
"And don't forget," Yao Hansheng continued, looking at the silent crowd. "The military has massive supplies of meat-fields to feed their men. Combined with strict discipline and brutal daily training, do you really think military martial artists are rare? When I visited my older brother at the Taizhou Army Base, I couldn't enter due to military rules, but I saw enough from the outside to gauge their strength."
He paused, letting the heavy silence linger.
"In Taizhou, every single officer—from Lieutenants up—is a Blood-refinement martial artist. First Blood or Second Blood. Captains? All Third Blood. Commanders? Every single one is at the Force Stage. The sheer number of masters in the army is terrifying."
"I've heard about this too," Wan Qingqing suddenly stood up, offering a calm, reassuring smile. "But there's no need to panic. Military masters carry countless hidden injuries, and their prime years are extremely short. Besides, they rarely cross paths with us. Their martial arts are designed for war, not one-on-one duels. Their top powerhouses can't match ours either. At best, both sides have their pros and cons."
Meanwhile, a servant led Wei He to an empty seat. Wei He cupped his fists toward Zhou Xingsu, the host, as a polite greeting.
Zhou Xingsu cupped his hands back, though his mind was clearly elsewhere, hooked by the intense debate.
"Even so, it's horrifying," Yao Hansheng added heavily. "I heard that over in Jinzhou, rebel forces turned on the local sects. Overnight, they wiped out two ancient sects with hundreds of years of history... The Golden Feather Blade Sect and the Qi Fengming Light Fist Sect... erased."
The room went dead silent. Everyone knew about the disaster in Jinzhou.
Two massive sects, boasting hundreds of members and guarded by Bone Forging grandmasters, wiped out overnight.
"Alright, everyone! Today is a celebration. Let's not talk about such gloomy things!" Zhou Xingsu stood up smoothly, raising his wine cup with a broad smile. "Our Heavenly Ink Sect has the Nine Masters presiding over us, not to mention Master Shangguan Ji, a true hero. How can small fry like the Golden Feather Blade Sect compare to us? Come, drink!"
The heavy mood finally lifted a bit.
He was right. Small sects couldn't compare to the Heavenly Ink Sect. Any one of their Nine Masters was at the Bone Forging stage. Above them sat the sect master himself, Shangguan Ji, the Fire-Eyed Golden Ape.
As Zhou Xingsu walked around toasting people, the atmosphere warmed up. Wan Qingqing helped by shifting the conversation to lighter topics.
The fifteen inner disciples were split across three tables, breaking off into smaller groups.
At Wei He's table, the man to his left was completely silent. He just kept drinking and eating, ignoring everyone. The "wine" served was actually just a sweet, low-alcohol drink, so nobody was getting drunk. The man seemed just like Wei He—someone who preferred to listen rather than talk.
To Wei He's right sat a young woman with soft features. She wasn't as naturally striking as Wan Qingqing, but she wore skillful makeup and carried a smooth, experienced charm that made her far more attractive and social than average.
Within minutes of sitting down, Wei He already learned the names of everyone at the table, purely because of her smooth small talk.
Her name was Zhao Wanzhu. Her family was a wealthy merchant clan in Xuanjing City. She was currently at the second layer of the Cloud-Gathering Art and had been in the Wanqing Hall for over six years.
The silent man on his left was Qian Rui, a newcomer who had joined less than a year ago.
Out of the five at the table, Qian Rui and Wei He were silent blocks of ice. The remaining two were female disciples whispering private gossip to each other, their eyes constantly drifting toward Zhou Xingsu at the main table.
In fact, almost every female disciple in the room—except for Wan Qingqing and Chen Lin—seemed interested in Zhou Xingsu.
He was filthy rich, handsome, polite, and possessed impressive talent. His future was bright and obvious. It was natural for women to be drawn to him.
Most female disciples in other branches were thick and overly muscular. But because of the unique nature of the Cloud-Gathering Art, the women of Wanqing Hall didn't bulk up. They had smooth, pale skin and lean, fit bodies. With good energy and neat features, almost all of them looked like beauties.
Wei He sat quietly, stuffing his face with food, completely uninterested in talking.
He hated gatherings like this. He only showed up when he absolutely had to. The main reason? He was broke.
If someone invited you, you had to return the favor later. Returning the favor meant spending money. Wei He didn't even have enough money for his own training, let alone hosting fancy dinners.
At the main table, Zhou Xingsu raised his glass again, giving a polished speech thanking everyone for coming. He added that his family had deep connections in Xuanjing City, promising that if anyone ever ran into trouble, he wouldn't hesitate to lend a hand.
It was smooth, heroic, and grand.
Many of the younger female disciples, who spent all their time training and lacked real-world experience, were instantly charmed by his grand words.
In reality, almost anyone who could enter the Inner Hall came from money anyway. Without money, who could afford seventy taels of gold every month just for martial arts resources? Even those without noble families had their own steady businesses.
Some inner disciples were nearly forty years old. The average age was between twenty and thirty-five. Because pregnancy severely delayed martial arts progress, few got married during their prime years under forty. But after staying in the Inner Hall for so long, everyone had accumulated their own contacts and safety nets.
"Our new junior brother really knows how to talk, doesn't he?" Zhao Wanzhu suddenly turned to Wei He, whispering with a light laugh. "You've known Yang Guo for so long, didn't she teach you any of these social tricks?"
She gave him a friendly smile. "Yang Guo and I are close friends. She mentioned you before. You used to be an outer disciple of the Wushi Sect, right?"
"No," Wei He said mildly. "I never made it into their outer hall."
"Fair enough, let's skip that," Zhao Wanzhu chuckled, looking over at Zhou Xingsu. "A man needs to be like Junior Brother Zhou to be popular with women. Yang Guo told me you spend all day shut inside your house, training like crazy, never talking to anyone. That's no good."
"Just personal preference," Wei He replied politely.
"You're in the Inner Hall now, a Third Blood master," Zhao Wanzhu said warmly. "From here on, it's just slow, painstaking work. If you can reach the third layer of the core technique and step into the Force Stage before age forty, you'll be considered a genius.
"Of course, whether you reach the third layer or not, the Inner Hall rule says you can only stay for nine years before you must leave. If I remember right, you're only twenty-one this year? You only have an older sister, so you don't have many family burdens.
"The journey from Third Blood to the Force Stage takes a long time. Nine whole years, from age twenty-one to thirty. You've already made a career by reaching Third Blood and joining the Inner Hall. Now, all you need is to settle down and start a family."
Wei He was genuinely surprised.
Since entering the Inner Hall, he had been dead silent and extremely low-key. At this banquet, he was practically invisible. Nobody paid attention to him, nobody cared.
Yet Zhao Wanzhu accurately called out his name, his age, and his exact family situation.
She could have heard his name from Yang Guo, but knowing the rest proved she had actively looked into him.
If an invisible nobody attends a high-class banquet, ignored by everyone, and suddenly a popular person walks up, calls out his details, and genuinely discusses his future... most people would instantly feel valued and warm up to her.
Wei He felt it too.
However, his face remained completely deadpan, showing zero emotion. Given his deeply paranoid nature, he didn't lower his guard for a second.
"Nine years is indeed a long time," Wei He nodded calmly.
He had to admit she made sense.
