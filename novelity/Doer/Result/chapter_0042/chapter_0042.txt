For five straight days, Ding Hai showed Wei He around the wilderness near Feiye City. He pointed out terrain, faction territories, and dangerous beast zones. Ding Hai kept plenty of secrets, but Wei He felt his grain was well spent.
He even shared a hidden cave for emergency shelter.
They caught almost nothing in those five days—just a single grey rabbit. Still, they gathered plenty of dead vines to weave together and hide the cave entrance.
Once Wei He got the hang of things, he started going out alone.
Ding Hai had dropped a massive lead about his parents' disappearance: the Shaoyang Sect liked to hunt for victims around sunset.
To dig deeper into the sect, Wei He went straight to Cheng Shaojiu, a guy who always knew what was happening in town.
"Shaoyang Sect? One of the Three Gangs and Two Sects. Why bring them up?" Shaojiu sat in a tavern, the dark circles under his eyes looking a bit lighter than usual.
"You know I've been looking into my parents. I think Shaoyang Sect is behind it," Wei He said plainly.
There was no point hiding it. Everyone knew his parents and eldest sister were gone. With how hard he was digging around, anyone could piece it together.
"Shaoyang Sect keeps to themselves," Shaojiu said, taking a sip of wine. "They have very few members. When they do come out, it's quick, and they never leave the Shaoyang Mountain area. No other faction dares to poke that nest. Your parents were dragged off to Mindes Temple for stonework along with a whole crew. You aren't the only one looking for answers. Why not join forces with the other families?"
"I tried," Wei He said flatly. "Eleven stonemasons went missing. None of their families want to dig anymore."
"That sect is small, but every guy they send out is a real deal," Shaojiu warned. "Don't mess with them lightly, Xiao He. If you're serious, talk to Master Zheng. He knows way more than we do."
"Got it."
"Wait, there's another way," Shaojiu slapped his hand on the table. "Aren't you a guest fighter for the Qingdu Sect now? You collect a monthly payment from them. Go ask them. Both sects operate outside the city walls. They've clashed plenty, so Qingdu's information network is far better than ours."
"Qingdu Sect... Thanks for the tip, Brother Cheng." Wei He nodded.
He recalled Manager Bai, the man who had recruited him. Bai Honghou was the second-in-command for Qingdu's operations inside Feiye City. He always carried a warm, gentle smile. But a man sitting in that chair with a second Blood-Refinement breakthrough was never just a nice guy.
Wei He left Shaojiu and went straight to the Qingdu Sect outpost in Jiujie Town.
By the time he arrived, the sun was sinking fast. Jiujie Town was neither too wild nor too dead. Along the unpaved streets, burnt incense sticks stuck out of the dry dirt in small patches.
The outpost sat in the busiest strip of the area, sandwiched between a general store and a quiet gambling house. It was a simple two-story building guarded by two men wearing green headbands.
Wei He tied his green cloth badge around his arm and walked up. The guards glanced at him, bowed slightly without a word, and let him pass.
He bowed back and pushed open the heavy wooden door.
Inside was a simple red-painted hall. A dozen broad-shouldered men and women were scattered around. Some chatted, others played chess, and a few were arm-wrestling over drinks.
Only a few looked up when Wei He walked in. The rest ignored him and kept doing their own thing. Wei He noticed everyone here wore the same green cloth on their arms. They were all guest fighters.
He walked over to a young guy leaning against a wooden pillar, staring blankly out the window.
"Excuse me, brother. Is Manager Bai Honghou around?" Wei He asked with a hand gesture.
"Manager Bai is upstairs," the man replied with a friendly smile. His wide mouth and pale skin gave him an unforgettable face. "You look new. Join this year?"
"Yeah, just recently," Wei He nodded.
"I'm Jin Xiufeng. Anyone who lands a spot with Qingdu Sect isn't nobody in Feiye City. We should stick together."
"Wei He. Good to meet you."
Wei He took the stairs to the second floor. Near a large window, he found Bai Honghou alone, practicing his calligraphy with a brush.
Bai Honghou looked up, recognizing the new recruit.
"Little Brother Wei, rare to see you here. Sit, sit." He set his brush down and walked over.
Every year, he hired a few fresh blood fighters like Wei He who had reached the second Blood-Refinement level. Every year, just as many guest fighters vanished or died for various reasons. To keep the numbers steady, he filled the spots as soon as they opened.
"What brings you here today?" Bai Honghou asked with a warm smile.
"Manager Bai, what can you tell me about the Shaoyang Sect?" Wei He asked in a low voice.
"Shaoyang Sect?" Bai Honghou's smile faded, his brow furrowing slightly. "Did you run into trouble with them?"
"No. I just need to know what they are about."
"Shaoyang Sect..." Bai Honghou sneered. "Well, we would have warned you about them soon anyway. They've been at our throats for years. Their main compound is hidden deep in Shaoyang Mountain. Nobody knows exact details. They don't have many people, but every member they send out is brutal, sneaky, and strong. If you bump into them, watch your back."
"How strong are we talking?" Wei He cut straight to the point.
"They move in two ways," Bai Honghou explained. "One is trading for supplies. The other is hunting. They don't hunt animals. They hunt people. If you run into a trading party alone, a guy with your strength can escape. But if you hit a hunting crew, hide your face and keep quiet about Qingdu Sect. Their hunting teams have at least two second-breakthrough fighters working together, sometimes three, plus a swarm of slave-thralls."
"Slave-thralls?"
"Shaoyang Sect uses toxic drugs to break people's minds and turn them into suicidal meat shields. That's a slave-thrall. They suffer constant pain and ruined bodies, so they are only slightly stronger than normal folk. Just watch out for cheap ambushes."
"How do I spot a Shaoyang Sect member?"
"Spot them?" Bai Honghou sneered with disgust. "Those freaks are impossible to miss. Go near Shaoyang Mountain. If you see a guy in white clothes who looks two or three times fatter than a normal human, that's them."
"They're that fat?" Wei He was surprised.
"Their path requires pure virginity. They cultivate raw Shaoyang Qi, and their secret manual expands their bodies into massive tanks. Don't fight them head-on. Their physical power is terrifying."
"Thank you, Manager Bai."
Wei He finally had a clear picture in his head.
He left the outpost and headed back to prepare. He didn't rush into Shaoyang Mountain right away. Instead, he got fresh poison powder from Shaojiu and packed his gear carefully.
On top of lime powder, poison, and hidden darts, he prepared a set of rusted iron spikes soaked in filth and human waste.
He bought these ruined iron spikes cheap from a blacksmith.
In a lawless era short on medicine, getting stabbed by these rusty, filthy spikes meant guaranteed infection, rotting flesh, and lockjaw.
In his past life, Wei He had been stepped on a rusty nail and learned how lockjaw actually worked. It needed two things: severe dirt and an oxygen-deprived deep wound. A deep puncture filled with rust, bacteria, and blood clots carried an eighty percent chance of deadly infection.
Without access to rare snake venom, these filthy spikes were the ultimate budget killer.
Once the infection set in, the target suffered violent muscle spasms, permanent paralysis, or a painful death from high fever. Cheap, dirty, and deadly.
After checking his gear, Wei He drew a crude map of the nearby territories, packed dried rations and water, and took a leave of absence from Master Zheng.
Then, he headed toward Shaoyang Mountain.
His official reason was hunting wild game to train his survival skills. Buying blood-nourishing meat was burning through his money too fast. If other hunters could pull it off in the wild, so could he.
More importantly, he needed to learn the wilderness inside out. If total war broke out in the city, he needed a clear path to run with his family.
