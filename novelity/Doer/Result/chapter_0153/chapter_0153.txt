After silencing the witness, the two of them parted ways.
Wang Shaojun kept his word.
Within days, rumors about the illegal weapons trade spread through the teahouses and taverns of Xuanjing City.
Zhou Shun was dragged into the mess and faced repeated interrogation by the Chijing Army.
If he were not so strong, and if those above him were not reluctant to move against him easily, anyone else in his position would have been locked up already.
Even so, his hands were tied. He faced trouble at every turn and dared not make any big moves.
Meanwhile, Wei He waited for the situation to boil over while steadily pushing forward his martial arts and study of pharmacology.
Ever since he turned down Wan Ling's marriage proposal, everything had returned to normal.
The branches of the Tianyin Sect began acting independently. What was once a single massive sect had now split into twelve separate factions, losing much of its former influence.
Some among the Tianyin Nine still hoped Shangguan Ji would step up to lead, but he remained unmoved.
In the blink of an eye, another month passed.
At their secluded hideout.
"What? You want to write a book?!" Wei He stared in shock at a tired Jiuying inside the cottage.
"Why not? Am I not allowed to write a book? How could everything I have learned be contained in a simple study of poisons?" Jiuying sneered.
"It's not that," Wei He asked back. "Mainly, once you write it, who is going to read it?"
"You don't need to worry about that," Jiuying waved his hand. "There are far too many people in this world who want to study poisons. I just don't want to carry a lifetime of knowledge into my coffin."
He recalled the countless piles of bones and the skeletal children he had seen while gathering samples, and the thought in his mind grew firmer.
"Don't worry," Wei He added. "I'll burn your body. Don't even dream of getting a coffin."
"Heh... You sure are thoughtful, kid," Jiuying muttered, ignoring him as he went back to his own research.
Wei He was left speechless.
Still, credit where it was due: while the old man was about equal to him in applying poison, Jiuying was far superior when it came to pharmacology and the theory behind recipes.
The old man held dozens of poison and medical formulas he had created himself. It made Wei He deeply envious.
Setting his thoughts aside, Wei He continued his own research.
His current focus was using the Iron Ridge Armor technique to strengthen his Cloud Rain Force.
His Iron Ridge Armor had reached the Three-Blood stage and could break through into Force at any moment.
Once he unlocked Force, he could complete the first upgrade to his Cloud Rain Force. This was a move he had planned long ago to make up for the weak defensive capabilities of Cloud Rain Force.
He planned to see how much power the upgrade would add, making this step crucial.
Wei He stepped out of the straw cottage into an open space, walking into a formation hung with heavy bags of iron sand.
The surface of each iron sand bag was covered in metal spikes. With a light push, they swung back and forth, crashing wildly through the air.
Without changing his expression, Wei He walked inside, focused his breathing, and let the spikes smash against his body.
Bang! Bang! Bang!
Heavy thuds rang out as the spikes hit him. Wei He suppressed the automatic defense of his Cloud Rain Force, instead letting the three-blood energy of the Iron Ridge Armor cover his entire body.
He had already mastered this step. Being hit by these spiked sandbags was a necessary part of training the Iron Ridge Armor.
With the Autumn Deer Technique simulated in his earlobe, he had already trained a trace of Iron Ridge Armor force there. Now, he was simply spreading it across his whole body.
Going from Three-Blood to Force was a matter of slow, steady accumulation. Wei He was in no rush. He had accumulated power for so long that once he reached completion, he could use the Breakthrough Bead to force his way through.
As for his main art, the Whale Tide Technique, it was currently stuck as he explored the Whale Breath state. He understood why; a martial art this powerful could not be mastered too easily.
If anyone could become an absolute master just by gathering materials, then everyone in ancient times would have been an unstoppable fighter.
There would always be barriers and bottlenecks to separate people into different ranks. The gap between the strong and the weak was never decided by the level of the martial art itself, but by differences in accumulation, talent, and natural physique.
With the Whale Tide Technique paused for now, Wei He focused on Cloud Rain Force, using the Flying Dragon Art and Iron Ridge Armor to boost his speed and defense respectively.
The fifth level of Cloud Rain Force also required long, slow work. It would take a while to break through, and Wei He was patient.
He was only twenty-two years old, and his current speed was already remarkably fast.
While Wei He focused on his training, Jiuying seemed set on finishing his book. Day after day, he buried himself in deep thought, writing non-stop.
What puzzled Wei He was that Jiuying looked more haggard by the day. His spirit and energy were draining rapidly, making him look more and more like a frail old man.
This went against the nature of a Force master. A master's body was guarded by inner force, locking their spirit and energy inside. He should not have drained this quickly in such a short time.
However, Wei He could not ask directly; they were only partners, after all.
Over time, the new reforms in Taizhou slowly began to roll out.
Although Xuanjing City was only a secondary city, it was among the first to respond.
They recruited soldiers, opened new schools, and established several new departments. The stagnant water of Xuanjing City began to stir once more.
Under constant investigation, Zhou Shun gradually kept a lower profile, pulling back his forces.
Clatter!
A fine piece of three-leaf lotus porcelain shattered against the floor.
Zhou Shun's chest heaved as his face flushed red. He paced back and forth in the main room, in a foul mood.
"Brother Zhou, you need to find a way out," Xie Yan said coldly from her chair, watching him throw a temper. "The secret is out. Old man Yuchi Zhong doesn't care about your Zhou family or the Wang family. If he decides to strike, it will be swift and ruthless. We can't just sit here and wait to die."
Xie Yan was sixty-two years old, but powerful martial artists retained part of their youth. Though she did not look as young or beautiful as Wan Ling of the Tianyin Nine, she appeared no older than her late thirties. Dressed in plain gray robes and pants, she looked like a middle-aged woman.
"What good is throwing a tantrum here?" Xie Yan was not like the others and had no intention of pampering Zhou Shun.
The two were equal in strength, neither willing to back down. At worst, they would simply part ways.
"I have already sent a letter to the Sect Master! A reply should arrive soon!" Zhou Shun suppressed his anger and spoke in a cold voice.
"Do you really believe that?" Xie Yan sneered. "Shangguan Ji is an old turtle who has endured for this long. Do you think he will ruin his patience just because you are in trouble?"
"With our strength combined with our current influence, even if Yuchi Zhong wants to move, he has to weigh the consequences. You don't need to..."
Before Zhou Shun could finish, Xie Yan raised a hand to cut him off.
"You said those exact words a month ago. Look at where we are now," Xie Yan said coldly. "Since you come from the Zhou family, why not ask them for help? Don't leave any stone unturned if you can borrow strength."
"...Fine!" Zhou Shun held back from saying that the current situation was already the result of his family stepping in.
However, Xie Yan was a snake who followed profit and betrayed loyalty. If things went sour, she would be the first to abandon him, so he could not reveal his weak hand to her.
"Think it over yourself. I'm leaving," Xie Yan stood up, snorted, and walked away.
"Show her out," Zhou Shun called out loudly.
A servant guided Xie Yan out, and she soon vanished into a carriage outside the courtyard gate.
Only when the carriage was completely gone did Zhou Shun exhale deeply.
"Someone come here."
"I am here, Master. What are your orders?" The housekeeper, Xue Dewang, walked in with deep respect.
"How are things with that old eunuch?" Zhou Shun asked impatiently.
"Master, he says he has found the key clue," Xue Dewang replied.
"And our belongings? Are they packed?" Zhou Shun asked again.
"All packed, Master. We can leave at any time."
"Good. Make preparations. We leave tonight," Zhou Shun said grimly.
As the situation grew worse, he decided to donate all his stored grain to the Chijing Army to buy time. Meanwhile, he would hide at a Zhou family estate outside the city.
Old man Yuchi Zhong only wanted to seize grain and goods. Since he could not beat him, he would let the old man have his way.
By hiding outside, he could stay clear of the mess, temporarily leaving the center of trouble to focus on finding out what happened to Zhou Yugui.
"Tell that old eunuch to give me a list. As long as they aren't too troublesome to handle, take them all secretly when we leave. Have the Night Eagle Pavilion assist," Zhou Shun suddenly added.
"Master, the list came in yesterday. Would you like to look at it?" Xue Dewang answered quickly.
"He's fast," Zhou Shun felt his temper cool slightly.
Rechecking old clues for anything suspicious was a task he had handed to the old eunuch, Meng Tinghuan. He had not expected him to finish so quickly.
Xue Dewang left for a moment and returned holding a piece of light yellow paper.
"This is the list of nine people in total. Please take a look."
Zhou Shun took the paper and swept his eyes across it.
Row after row of names were written neatly. The fifth name on the list was Wei He.
"All of these people have problems?" Zhou Shun asked.
"According to Meng Tinghuan, these nine people had major flaws and contradictions in their statements. They require strict investigation," Xue Dewang reported.
"Nine people..." Zhou Shun weighed his options. Taking nine people at once during such a sensitive time might cause trouble.
Yet Yuchi Zhong was pressing hard from the other side. If he waited any longer, more complications would arise.
After a brief thought, Zhou Shun made up his mind.
"We strike tonight. I will go personally to take all nine of them, and then we leave the city to lie low!"
"Yes!" Xue Dewang nodded firmly.

Inside the secluded straw cottage.
Wei He watched as Jiuying returned, once again exhausted.
It seemed that while writing his book, the old man was also working on something else.
Lately, his vitality was draining fast, as if he were burning through all his energy.
Wei He paused his training and walked out of the cottage.
In the distance, the sun sank slowly, casting beams of red light through the forest.
He leaped into the air, tapping lightly across the treetops as he moved rapidly toward the Western Mountain's Wanqing Yard.
Just as he reached the foot of the mountain, a white shadow flickered into sight, blocking his path.
"There's trouble," Wang Shaojun said with a grave expression. "I rushed over the moment I got the news."
"What is it? You rarely come to find me in person," Wei He narrowed his eyes.
"Zhou Shun is cornered and grabbing people secretly in the city. If I hadn't kept an eye on him, I wouldn't have noticed. He is targeting everyone linked to Zhou Yugui's disappearance. Don't tell me Zhou Yugui's matter has nothing to do with you," Wang Shaojun said in a heavy voice.
"..." Wei He shook his head.
He looked out toward the blood-red setting sun.
"The one you should notify right now isn't me, but the Chijing Army."
Then, a sudden thought struck him.
"No, there's no need. Given Commander Yuchi Zhong's style, now that he has an excuse to act, he won't miss news that even you know about."
"You mean...?" Wang Shaojun paused.
"If I were the Commander, facing a master like Zhou Shun, I would either stay still or strike to wipe him out completely," Wei He said seriously.
"Right now, do you really think he will just let the man walk away?" Wei He asked.
"..." Wang Shaojun was left without a word.
