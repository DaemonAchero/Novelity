Morning, light faint.
On the official road at the edge of Black House Mountain, a string of horse carts and ox carts moved slowly toward Yunzhou. They carried loads of goods, guarded by fifty people.
The lead carriage flew a big merchant flag. On it was the word Fang.
This was a merchant caravan going to Yunzhou.
Joining a caravan was the disguise Wei He and his group chose. When he came from Yunzhou before, his cultivation was weak—only Blood Refinement Level Three. He had to be extra careful back then.
Now, Wei He had mastered Bone Forging and was training his internal organs.
Wan Qingqing was also in Bone Forging. Two female disciples came along to care for her.
Both girls were heavily built, thick-waisted, and broad-shouldered. They were Martial Masters, picked because they were good with poison. There was no need for the old, cautious trouble anymore.
The Wan Poison Sect had taught poison courses for nearly two years now. A few good talents had emerged.
Wei He felt his art had successors. These two were useful for this trip.
If nothing else, they could use his unique drugs and Jiuying's healing remedies well.
Wei He and Wan Qingqing sat in the second-to-last carriage. The two female disciples rode horses beside them.
To the rest of the caravan, the four were strangers who paid to join halfway.
Wei He’s size and the bulk of the two female disciples looked unusual.
But the caravan leader was experienced. He checked that they only wanted to travel to Yunzhou and stopped asking questions.
A man built like Wei He had real strength. If trouble hit the road, he might help.
The leader of the Fang family group was a middle-aged man named Fang Zheng.
The best age for a caravan leader was thirty to forty-five.
At this age, a man had steady strength, money, and road experience.
Fang Zheng fits this. He knew martial arts. His thrown darts hit anything within thirty meters.
Though he was only Blood Refinement Level Two, he knew his limits. He hired three relatives from his clan, all Level Three fighters.
Fang Hong, Fang Sha, and Fang Zhi.
Two men and one woman. One used legs; two used weapons.
There were also twelve Level One guards.
These guards lacked talent and money for real training.
Under Fang Zheng, the twelve worked well together. They proved useful in tight spots.
The rest were ordinary hired hands to save money.
Carriage wheels turned slowly.
Wei He sat with Wan Qingqing for a while, then stepped outside. He walked forward to a small carriage in the middle.
Its curtain was up, showing a small window. Food, drinks, and everyday items like scissors, thread, and bug repellent sat by the frame.
It was a mobile shop created by the small caravan.
"Give me some Yellow Qin wine." Wei He passed over a few horn coins. Ten of these thick horn pieces equaled one gold bead. They were common currency.
"Sure. How many bottles?" The vendor was a sharp-eyed, quick girl.
She wore a simple ponytail. Her skin was rough from sun and wind, but her features were fine and neat. Men in the caravan often looked her way.
"Six bottles, for good luck," Wei He said with a smile.
He had no sect chores now. He only had to accompany his wife. Even Qingqing did not need him every moment, since she still trained her inner force.
She was pregnant, but this training helped the baby grow.
So Wei He had free time. He used his Silk Force to penetrate his inner organs, speeding up his training.
This was his second day with the caravan. On the first day, he bought Yellow Qin wine here to taste it.
The wine smelled too good to pass up.
After one bottle, Wei He liked the taste. It reminded him of the orange juice from his past life.
He did not like sour drinks usually, but this taste brought back memories.
"Too much drink is bad for you, Mr. Wei," the girl said kindly.
"It is fine. This little bit is nothing," Wei He said. The alcohol was weak, lower than beer. With his body, dozens of bottles would not make him drunk.
He drank it just for the taste.
"Alright, here is your wine." The girl handed the bamboo tubes to Wei He.
The wine came in yellow bamboo tubes, stopped with wooden plugs. Simple and natural.
Wei He walked beside the cart, drinking. He planned to finish before going back to Qingqing, so the smell would not bother the baby.
"Uncle Wei, are you going to Yunzhou to visit family?" the girl asked.
"Yes, visiting family. Why else would I bring my family with so few goods?" Wei He nodded.
"Now is a bad time. Yunzhou has a big famine. Times are hard," the girl said softly.
"I know. Otherwise, your caravan could not make money selling grain there," Wei He said.
Out here, he was no sect master. Just an ordinary traveler heading home.
"What is your name, little one? Where are you from?" he asked.
"Call me Fang Yongchun, or Little Chun. The leader picked me up and raised me. Where my real home is, who knows. But most of our group comes from Yunzhou."
"Where in Yunzhou?"
"Suchen."
"Suchen... that is near my hometown, Feiye City," Wei He said.
"Feiye City... I heard they fought hard against the Xiangqu rebels. The Hong Army took heavy losses. The city is still a mess," Fang Yongchun said, shaking her head.
"Is that so? Where will your caravan stop? Will you pass Feiye City?" The smile left Wei He's face.
"We will make a round. Final stop is Yunzhou Capital to trade grain for local goods, then head back," Fang Yongchun nodded. "Last time we went, the capital had a severe drought. Water was scarce. People went long without washing."
Little Chun kept talking while serving customers.
Listening to her, Wei He drank his fruit wine and drifted into old memories.
Memories of Feiye City.
...
Yunzhou, Feiye City.
Inside a pitch-black room.
Wei Chun woke up with a start. Sweat soaked her skin as she pushed herself up from the bed.
She lowered her head. Sweat dripped from her face onto her hand.
"Third time... this dream..."
Since returning to Feiye City, she had worked hard labor with her parents. They barely earned enough food for three.
Then the Xiangqu rebels caused trouble again. The Hong family failed to suppress them in time. Half the grain stores burned. Most of the inner city was destroyed.
Their half-full meals became half-empty.
Every day, the family went to work hungry and returned at dark.
Weak and tired, they could go nowhere after work.
Life had been like this for a long time.
In quiet moments, Wei Chun thought of her younger sister and brother. She wondered how they were doing.
The Black Water Gang was long gone. Old acquaintances were almost all dead.
Her current friends were fellow workers from the mines. They survived by helping each other.
"Sigh..." Wei Chun sighed, walked to the water jar, took her ladle, and drank.
At least the drought eased last year. Rain came more often now.
That was why people could still hold on.
Wei Chun fixed her clothes and pushed through the curtain.
Dawn was just breaking outside.
She lived in a rebuilt part of the new district. Two mud-brick huts. One for her, one for her parents next door.
Many fellow workers lived here too.
Wei Chun tightened her belt and walked down the alley.
The area was packed with mud huts, bordered by the high walls of rich estates.
Past the alleys lay one of Feiye City's three new squares.
"Da Chun, why so early?" A man carrying water buckets passed by and greeted her.
"Bad dream woke me up. Is your worksite opening this early?" Wei Chun asked.
"Yes. We work before dawn these days. But we get full pay, so no complaints. Having work is good enough," the man smiled. It was hard labor, but his smile was real.
"True."
Wei Chun smiled too.
She passed him and turned through the winding alleys until she reached a ruined yard filled with broken bricks and wood.
The walls were full of holes. Weeds covered the courtyard. Deep holes marked the roof from past fights between fighters.
The main support pillars had deep round indentations, like finger prints.
Every time Wei Chun passed, she liked to touch those marks and test her fingers inside.
It made her feel like a martial master for a moment. It felt good.
She walked inside to the inner rooms.
The inner area used to be the main hall, study, and bedrooms. It was messy, but cleaner than the yard outside.
On the only clear spot, a white-haired old man lay on a hand-woven chair, soaking in the early sun, half asleep.
