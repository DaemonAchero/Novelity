Wei He listened from the side. Bai Dou’s story was sad, but he had killed far too many people.
To avenge his family, he did not just slaughter his enemies. He killed all ten neighboring households to wipe out any witnesses. Old people, small kids—he spared no one.
It was way too much. The man was a total radical.
After lunch, the convoy kept moving.
Ever since Wei He showed his skill, the group had split into two sides: the merchants on one end, and Wei He on the other.
Fang Zheng’s guards did not dare get close. They respectfully offered gifts, then stayed up front to lead the way, silently hoping Wei He would help if trouble showed up.
By late afternoon, they were near Sword-Hide Gorge.
Grey birds started appearing in the sky. These fuzzy birds were over a meter tall with three-meter wingspans. They had three legs, reddish heads, and sharp beaks on human-like faces. They were called Jueri birds.
They were packed with lethal poison. The last time Wei He passed through, he had to be extremely careful. But now was different.
He wanted to catch a few to boost his toxic art, making his force even more poisonous.
He had already used up all the poisons he carried. The ones that were too strong had been diluted into his art. The tricky part of this art was finding the right poisons. Every new stage needed an upgraded version of the first nine poisons he ever used.
Now that he was at the force-entry stage, his supply was dry. Jueri birds were a natural, pure source of venom. He could extract the poison, then eat the meat. Perfect.
The convoy picked up speed.
With the three-horned leopard around, wild beasts stayed far away. Three-Poison Monk relied on this leopard he raised from a cub to roam these dangerous woods and sing freely. The beast was fast, sharp, and strong—a solid forest buddy.
Late in the afternoon, the convoy stopped at the gorge entrance.
Fang Zheng walked up to Wei He carefully and cupped his hands.
"Mr. Wei, it’s getting late. Going through the gorge now is risky. Maybe we should wait until tomorrow morning while the birds are asleep?"
They knew Wei He was strong, but if a flock of Jueri birds swarmed them, the cargo would be destroyed even if they survived. Passing quietly was best.
With beast-repelling incense and bait, they had a good chance. They just feared this mysterious powerhouse might cause trouble on a whim.
"Sure," Wei He said.
He actually wanted to find Bai Dou, the hidden weapon master. The Myriad Poison Sect really lacked a guy like that.
Poisoning hidden weapons was basic common sense. If he paired his venom with top-tier throwers, it would be a deadly weapon.
Fang Zheng and his men quickly found an open spot, lit a fire, parked the carts in a circle, and set up guards. They scattered bug repellent and lit the incense.
Wei He sat cross-legged by the fire. His force kept turning into silk-force, soaking into his organs.
The biggest hurdle from bone-tempering to organ-training was using silk-force to make fragile organs tough from the inside out.
This training boosted the entire body. It did not add much raw attack power, but it greatly raised stamina, breathing, lifespan, and immunity.
Organ-training was the key stage for a warrior to live longer.
It made force control smoother, force capacity bigger, and blood vitality stronger. It also brought defense force up to the strength of bone-force.
However, Wei He’s defense force was already way stronger than regular bone-force. Combining twelve defensive forces gave him a defense score of 60, six times higher than a normal organ-training master at 10. Going to the organ level would only raise his score to 65. Not a big deal.
What he really cared about was what League Master You Fu said: building a base for the mind.
This stage was meant to balance the five organs and lay a foundation for the mind, preparing for the future sense-fixing stage. After all, the mind rests on the organs.
"Qingqing, rest up. I’m stepping out," Wei He said, standing up. He glanced at Three-Poison Monk.
The monk took the cue, stood up, and followed.
The two melted into the dark night.
Wan Qingqing did not worry. She pulled out a book and read quietly by the fire, just like she always did when he was away.

"Eat up. Was work tough today? You look pale. Get some rest after this."
Wei Chun held her wooden bowl, staring at the food with no appetite.
The oil lamp flickered in the night wind near the window. Her parents looked at her with tired, caring eyes, pulling her thoughts back from her master.
"I’m fine. Just thinking about a problem," Wei Chun replied.
"A problem? Just endure it and it’ll pass. Peace is a blessing. Don't stick your neck out," Wei Tang sighed. He thought his daughter was facing some unfair trouble at work and feeling choked up.
"I know," she nodded.
Her mother, Li Cui, put down her chopsticks.
"Chun'er, I want your opinion on something. Think about it."
"What is it?" Wei Chun asked.
"Aunt Wang’s youngest son next door is an adult now. He’s a bit weak, so they want a strong, healthy girl to share a life with and look after him. You’re having a tough time alone, and their family is better off than ours. If you’re willing..."
Clang!
Wei Chun slammed her chopsticks onto the table.
"I’m not getting married!" she snapped.
She had seen that neighbor kid a few times. He was skinny as a stick and looked like a walk of bad luck. Nobody would take him, let alone her. His family just had a slightly better income, that was all.
Besides, she was dealing with a massive headache right now. She had no time for this.
Seeing her reaction, the couple dropped the topic with a sigh.
After a quiet moment, Wei Tang mentioned the news from town.
"Fine, if you don't want to. But don't take that road to the site tomorrow. Take a detour." He paused. "Someone died on that street today. Martial artists were killing each other. I saw the guards carrying out bodies."
"The road to the site?" Wei Chun stopped chewing.
"Yeah. But it should be fine now. The Enshrined Masters from the hall took over," Li Cui whispered. "Once they move, everything gets fixed."
Wei Chun nodded.
Ever since Hong Fortress took over Feiye City, they set up an Enshrined Hall.
It had two levels: Red-robe and White-robe. White-robe masters were on the lower end, but still far beyond normal people—each as strong as a top student from the old martial schools.
Red-robe masters were few, but all monstrously strong.
Besides the hall, Wei Chun also knew about the Hong List in Feiye City.
It listed top-tier warriors. Originally, it was just a wanted poster with bounties from the Hong family. But people ranked the criminals by their reward money, and as time passed, the rankings proved to be dead accurate.
It became a gold standard for measuring real power.
Since most experts on the list were hunted criminals, it stood against the Enshrined Hall.
People called it the Black List.
Wei Chun gripped her chopsticks hard.
She could never, ever let anyone know that the Chongstar Cup was with her. Otherwise, her parents would be dragged down.
'As long as I don't dig it up, no one will ever know,' she thought.
...
Night fell.
On a cliff at the edge of Sword-Hide Gorge sat an old stone house. It had a small vegetable plot enclosed by a fence.
An oil lamp flickered inside the house.
Bai Dou bent over, straightening vegetable sprouts and pinching off bug-eaten leaves into a wooden tub. This was his dinner.
He was forty-six years old. Muscular bulges rippled across his skin like little mice with every move. Even in a plain sleeveless shirt, his bulk showed.
Pinch. Pinch. He kept clearing the plot.
Moonlight stretched his shadow long against the stone wall.
He was used to being alone.
"Hey, Bai Dou!"
A rough voice called from outside the fence.
Bai Dou stopped and looked over.
Three-Poison Monk stood at the gate, looking solemn.
"Long time no see. I bought hidden weapons from you last time, remember?" The monk forced a smile.
"What do you want?" Bai Dou stood up, his voice cold.
"Not me. My boss wants to see you. He thinks you..."
Smack.
A large hand grabbed the monk’s bald head and pulled him aside.
A huge figure wrapped in a cloak stepped forward from behind.
With his back to the moon, his cold, hard face stayed buried in shadow.
"They say you're the strongest bandit around here," the man grinned.
"Want to try robbing me?"
