Dusk fell over Yishuang County.
Along the banks of the Penghai River, wooden ships lit with lanterns drifted slowly like fish in the dark water. The vessels swayed, spilling out the faint sounds of zithers, laughter, and guests drinking wine.
A crescent moon hung high in the sky.
Among the dozen ships scattering light across the ripples, one stood apart.
It stayed quiet. Wooden signs hung from its bow and stern, marked with symbols showing it was a private vessel. No tourists or random visitors were allowed aboard. Three stories tall and painted pure black, its only light came from glowing yellow lanterns rocking in the night wind.
Guards in black suits and young women in dark dresses stood along the decks. From time to time, small rowing boats pulled up to drop off guests before heading back to the riverbanks.
Down on the shore, Wei He stood wearing a black cloak and a wide bamboo hat. He tilted his head, staring up at the dark ship.
He had already settled the mess at the Thousand Poison Sect. Whether people called them the Wan Qing Sect or the Thousand Poison Sect did not matter to him anymore. Since everyone insisted on calling him evil, he would show them what real poison looked like.
Jiuying was clinging to life. In his attempt to spread a plague, the old man had almost killed himself. Wei He had pulled him back from death just in time, but even with full medical treatment, Jiuying’s body was broken. He had only a few years left to live.
Still, Jiuying was Wei He's only assistant for testing new poison recipes. Wei He had to keep him alive.
Now, the Thousand Poison Sect was forced to hide deep in the Black House Mountains. After checking his mother-in-law Wan Ling’s wounds and making sure she was out of danger, Wei He packed the loot he had taken. He brought gold bars, rare beasts, poison ingredients, sharp weapons, secret manuals, and rare treasures.
His sect had suffered heavy losses when their stronghold was attacked, but Wei He had slaughtered the Sun-Hunting Willow Night agents in revenge. Their storage rooms had been left behind completely untouched, and he took everything.
Financially, he was richer than before.
The only tragic part was the body count. Less than a hundred members of the Thousand Poison Sect remained alive, including those scattered across the region.
"I hope this trip is worth it," Wei He muttered.
He came here for two reasons: to meet Mo Shiyan’s Anti-Nest Alliance, and to trade for rare resources in the black market. Jiuying’s recovery required specific materials that could never be found in Xuanjing. He needed to step outside his old borders to find new chances.
He also wanted to know more about the Nest in the capital. What was it? What did it want? How many masters did it hide?
Wei He walked down to the water. Boatmen waited along the bank, offering rides to the floating ships. He picked a strong man and stepped onto his skiff.
"Take me to Ship Number One."
"Ship One? You got it, boss!" The boatman grinned. Ship Number One was special. They didn't take a cut from the boatmen; they paid them extra.
Still, Old Jiang knew that ship was strange. It only accepted specific guests—men who were tall, thick, and built like ruthless fighters.
Wei He stood in the middle of the small boat as it cut through the water. Bright red and yellow lights from neighboring party boats reflected on the waves. Drunken shouting, singing, and the thick scent of cheap perfume filled the night air.
As the black ship grew larger, Wei He felt the heavy presence of powerful fighters resting inside.
When a warrior reached a high level, their heartbeat and breathing grew massive. Their bodies became so dense and powerful that simply breathing felt like thunder to normal men, unless they deliberately hid their strength.
On this black ship, however, the masters inside were letting their heavy aura bleed out freely.
The skiff bumped against the hull, and a wooden plank was lowered. Wei He paid the driver and stepped up onto the deck.
Two other large men wearing cloth face masks climbed up beside him. As the three men stepped onto the wood, a cold, sharp-looking girl stepped forward.
"Please follow me," she said quietly.
Wei He glanced at the two men. They wore grey face wraps and yellow hats, hiding their features. Sensing his gaze, the pair instantly stiffened. Their muscles tightened, ready to explode into violence at a second's notice.
"Are you Sect Master Wei of Xuanjing?" one of them asked. His voice sounded old and steady.
"I am. And you two?" Wei He remained calm, surprised his name had reached this place.
"I am Han Shi of the Zhongyu Sect, and this is my head disciple," the old man said respectfully. "We heard you killed Zhu Chen of the Seven Star Knives in a one-on-one fight. Truly impressive."
Wei He had never heard of the Zhongyu Sect, so he merely nodded. "It was kill or be killed."
Understanding his mood, the old man stayed silent.
The girl led them inside. The first floor was a dark room filled with over a dozen martial artists resting on chairs.
Mo Shiyan sat in a white dress near a bald, one-eyed man. When she saw Wei He step through the door, her smile widened. She stood up immediately to greet him.
Half the room turned to look. Whispers broke out across the dark hall.
"That's Wei He?"
"He slaughtered all the Sun-Hunting Willow Night spies in Xuanjing. Even Zhu Chen disappeared after fighting him."
"Doesn't look like much to me. I saw Zhu Chen once from a distance. The man gave off a chill that made my spine freeze."
"Appearances lie. He probably hides his true breathing."
Wei He heard every single whisper clearly. His blood energy was too strong for his senses to miss anything.
"Sect Master Wei, you came," Mo Shiyan said, guiding him to a seat on the left.
The bald, one-eyed man at the front looked Wei He up and down. He smiled warmly. "I am You Fu, leader of the Anti-Nest Alliance. You did us all a favor, Sect Master. If you hadn't taken out Zhu Chen, Xuanjing would be a slaughterhouse by now."
"You flatter me. I was forced into a corner," Wei He replied smoothly, sizing the man up.
You Fu looked built like a mountain bandit, but his eyes were unnaturally soft. He gave off no oppressive aura at all.
As time passed, more fighters filled the remaining seats. Food and wine were served.
"I was planning to go to Xuanjing myself to handle Zhu Chen," You Fu sighed. "I didn't expect you to beat me to it. The young really are taking over."
"I got lucky," Wei He said plainly. "Zhu Chen was far stronger than me. In a direct fight, I would be dead."
"You used the Three-Headed Raccoon, didn't you?" You Fu said, seeing through the events. "To force a creature like that to fight Zhu Chen while you waited in the shadows... that takes real cunning."
"Thinking back on it still gives me cold sweats," Wei He admitted.
"It takes skill to pull that off," You Fu chuckled. "When I was fifty-nine and just broke into the Mind Perception realm, I explored the Black House Mountains and ran into that exact beast. We fought a terrible battle. It was a tragic creature... a failure who lost its mind trying to break through."
Wei He wanted to ask more about the dangerous secrets of the Mind Perception realm, as his manual lacked details.
He opened his mouth, but heavy, crushing footsteps echoed outside the corridor.
The door burst open. A woman built like a mountain of moving fat squeezed through the narrow frame, reshaping her body like jelly just to get inside.
Wei He stood up instantly, his eyes locked on her.
"Yu Qingyan?!"
