Wei He collected homework from the girl with pigtails, as well as three other kids who paid him to do theirs.
Back in his past life, he had a special trick. He could copy different styles of handwriting, and a child’s messy handwriting was the easiest to fake.
After gathering the paper rolls, Wei He rolled them up, hid them inside his jacket, stood up, and walked fast toward home.
Crossing the Stone Bridge Street near the river, he heard a low commotion from the tall weeds along the bank.
It was mixed with the crying screams of a woman and the loud roars of a man. There was also the dull thud of wooden clubs hitting flesh.
Wei He paused for a second, then moved past even faster.
The Lecture Hall was in the Inner City. That place was safe, with guards patrolling all the time.
His family lived in the slums, which were chaotic and wild. Stone Bridge Street was already part of the slums.
When he reached home, Wei He used a piece of charcoal he had prepared earlier to finish the homework one by one. Then he wiped off the charcoal dust, rolled the papers back up, and put them away.
His second sister, Wei Ying, made a single dish and a bowl of soup from their leftover vegetables. The two of them sat at the table, waiting for the rest of the family to come home.
The sky grew dark.
Their eldest sister, Wei Chun, still had not returned.
There was no news from their parents either.
Wei He’s parents were craftsmen who carved stone statues for a living. Lately, they had been working outside the city, carving Buddha statues at the Mingde Temple.
"Mom and Dad must be staying at the temple again tonight," Wei Ying said softly. "But why isn't Big Sis back yet?"
Wei Chun came home every single day.
Wei He said nothing. He looked out the window. The sun was almost fully set, no longer visible.
The wind blowing inside turned cold, carrying the faint smell of food from other houses.
"Big Sis always comes back by this time," Wei Ying mumbled, staring at her brother. She sounded as if she were speaking to him, or maybe just to herself.
"I'll go take a look. Stay inside and don't go anywhere," Wei He said, standing up. He felt that something was wrong, and his mind was uneasy.
"Come back quickly," Wei Ying warned.
"Yeah."
Wei He threw on an outer coat. It was covered in patches, but it kept him warm.
He pushed open the wooden door and immediately saw several tall, burly men standing at the entrance of the alley to his right.
There were three men in total. They wore short gray shirts that exposed their arms, showing dark skin and heavy muscle.
Wei He knew these three. The leader was Chen Biao, a well-known thug in the area, along with his two followers.
Chen Biao had no real job. He wandered around all day doing nothing, and no one knew where he got the money to survive or stay so heavily built.
Wei He had heard rumors that Chen Biao had ties to the Theft Gang. He often helped them kidnap people to sell off.
They took kids, single women, and even decent-looking men.
Normally, none of this mattered to Wei He. But Chen Biao had seen Wei Ying before, and ever since, he had been hanging around their house.
He only backed off a bit after getting into a fight with Wei Chun.
Wei Chun was part of the Black Water Gang. She was not a boss, but she was not someone a low-level thug could push around.
From then on, Chen Biao only hung around when Wei Chun was not home.
Wei He looked over from a distance and locked eyes with Chen Biao.
Chen Biao’s small eyes flashed with a strange light. He smiled at Wei He, then turned back to talk to his two men.
Wei He’s chest tightened.
He knew the guy was waiting for his eldest sister. One reason Wei Chun made sure to come home on time every day was to keep an eye on this creep.
Gang members lived dangerous lives filled with constant trouble. No one knew when something bad might happen to them.
Chen Biao definitely had evil ideas in his head.
'This is bad.' Wei He picked up his pace, left the alley, and walked toward the local branch of the Black Water Gang.
Wei Chun looked nothing like her siblings. She was naturally broad, heavily built, short-tempered, and very strong. Because of that, she had a bit of a reputation in the gang branch.
The branch was a small square courtyard. Inside, a few men were lifting heavy stone weights while a man with a small braid stood by, yawning.
"Wei Chun? She took a mission this morning. She said if she finished it, she’d make enough money to move her family to the Inner City and pay for her little brother’s martial arts training. It should be an important task. Did she not tell you?"
"No," Wei He said, his heart sinking.
"A lot of guys wanted that task, but the leader only picked a dozen good fighters. Your sister was one of them. No need to rush," the man replied with a laugh.
"Do you know when they'll be back?" Wei He asked.
"No idea. Important tasks usually take time, so don't worry. Anyway, we are training here. Go home and wait. As soon as she returns, I'll let her know you came." The man waved his hand, telling him to leave.
Wei He knew that was all he could get out of him. The man would not tell him anything else.
He turned and walked slowly out the gate.
Standing at the entrance, he looked back at the sign hanging above that read: Black Water Branch.
'The Black Water Gang is just a low-rate gang. There are dozens, maybe a hundred of them in the Outer City. Where could they find a job that pays so much money?' Wei He felt a heavy weight on his chest.
He had a bad feeling.
He was not like the other uneducated peasants. He knew something was wrong.
But she was already gone, and he was just a sixteen-year-old boy who knew nothing useful.
He could read a few words, but what good was that?
When he returned home, Chen Biao and his men were no longer at the mouth of the alley.
However, when he opened the door, his second sister, Wei Ying, was pale and looked terrified.
Seeing him come back, she let out a long sigh, as if a massive weight had been lifted off her shoulders.
"Xiao He... Big Sis, how is she?"
"Did Chen Biao come here just now?" Wei He asked in a deep voice.
"He did. I hid inside and didn't make a sound. He thought no one was home and left," Wei Ying said, her voice shaking from the scare.
Wei He noticed thin marks of yellow dirt on the door where it had been kicked. He knew there had been a big disturbance just a moment ago.
"Big Sis went on an important mission and won't be back for a while. Let's eat first," he said, trying to stay calm.
It was just the two of them now. If he panicked as the male of the house, his timid sister would be even more terrified.
"Okay," Wei Ying replied softly.
They closed the door, shut the window, and sat in silence under the faint light peeking through the cracks, eating their food slowly.
The meal was fried carrots with bean crumbs, along with cabbage and tofu soup. The main food was wheat bran rice mixed with a lot of corn.
There was no meat. Normal families could not afford it. Wei Chun was built so big mostly because of her broad frame, plus the gang provided extra food for her.
The Wei family lived on the exact same level as everyone around them. Having a little bit of oil in their food was already considered good.
After eating, Wei Ying went to wash the dishes, while Wei He spread out the homework on their only table to finish it for the kids.
Only then did he dare to use a flint to light the oil lamp and write under the light.
Wei Ying sat nearby, carefully mending clothes with a needle and thread. Neither of them said a word.
When night came, they went to sleep on their own beds.
The next day, Wei He returned the homework and collected the money. He had finally saved up enough money to learn martial arts.
Wei Chun still did not return.
Even their parents, Wei Tang and his wife, sent no word back.
Wei Ying was too scared to leave the house to take on work. She stayed inside all day, waiting for Wei He to come and go, bringing back news.
Wei He went back to the gang branch, but the man who spoke to him before was nowhere to be seen. Maybe he just did not want to waste his breath anymore.
The guards stopped Wei He right at the door.
Three more days passed.
There was no news from their parents or from Wei Chun.
Meanwhile, Chen Biao and his gang appeared at the alley entrance more and more often. They spent more time staring toward the Wei house.
Wei He’s heart sank to the absolute bottom.
He knew the worst possibility in his mind was likely happening.
He had to find a way out as fast as possible.
In this world, people went missing all the time. They met bandits, got attacked by wild beasts, or fell sick on the road and died without treatment.
There were too many reasons for a person to vanish.
On the morning of the fourth day, Wei He took his money.
Before dawn, he picked up a wooden stick as thick as an arm and slipped out of the house.
He tightened his grip on all the money he had saved to join a martial arts school, then turned to look at his second sister, Wei Ying.
"Hide inside the house. Don't go out, and don't make a sound."
"Okay," Wei Ying nodded hard. She could tell her brother was different today.
With their sister and parents gone, they only had each other to depend on. She was determined not to be a burden to him.
Under the dim morning light, Wei He took a deep breath and slowly blew it out to calm his nerves.
"I wanted to save a bit more for food before going, but I have no choice now..."
He could feel his heart pounding wildly against his chest. He was carrying two months' worth of savings. Success or failure depended entirely on this step.
He carefully hid the pouch of money inside his inner clothes and tied it tight. This was all he had, and he could not lose it.
Using his hand and the wooden stick to cover his chest, Wei He gripped the weapon hard and walked fast toward the alley entrance.
Passing Stone Bridge Street and crossing the bridge, he saw many farmers carrying baskets of vegetables and fruit to sell.
There were also rich people sitting in fancy carriages, looking dressed up for an outing.
Poor farmers in rags walked the same road as young masters and ladies in silk, creating a stark contrast.
Before he could get far, he saw Chen Biao and his two men standing by the roadside, talking trash.
The three of them chatted in low voices while eyeing every man and woman who passed by. Whenever a pretty woman walked past, they stared intensely.
They looked as if they were memorizing every detail.
Wei He’s chest tightened. He had all his savings on him right now, and nothing could go wrong.
The more nervous he felt, the more he pretended nothing was wrong.
He kept walking forward, quickly reaching the part of the road where the three men were standing.
If Chen Biao had not seen him earlier, taking a detour would have been the best choice.
But since he had already been spotted, making a sudden detour would look way too suspicious.
That would only bring trouble.
Right now, acting natural was the safest path.
Wei He kept his steps steady, preparing to walk past them.
"Hold on."
Chen Biao suddenly reached out an arm, blocking his path directly.
"If it isn't Xiao He. What, heading to the Inner City again? Going there every day instead of finding work in our own district. Did you crawl up to some rich family?" Chen Biao sneered in a weird, mocking tone.
His voice was high and sharp, lacking any power despite his large body. He sounded more like a rooster.
"None of your business. My sister found this work for me," Wei He said coldly, using his usual tone.
"Heh, your big sister, right? Yeah, she’s a real fighter in the Black Water Gang. Fine, fine, I won't stop you," Chen Biao chuckled, pulling back his hand.
Wei He kept moving forward.
"Wait a second." Chen Biao raised another issue.
His small eyes swept over Wei He, locking onto his slightly bulging coat.
"Little brother Xiao He, your coat looks a bit full today... Are you hiding something in there?"
"I work at the construction site every day. I get hungry, so I bring some dry bread. What, is that a problem?" Wei He pulled open his coat, grabbed a piece of hard, dry bread from inside, and shook it in front of him.
"It's fine, of course it's fine," Chen Biao grinned and pulled his hand back, saying no more. "Go on, go on. Remember to say hi to your second sister for me. I've had my eyes on her for a long time."
"..." Wei He said nothing. Keeping a cold face, he quickened his steps and walked away fast.
He walked fast for over a mile before finally letting out a long breath.
