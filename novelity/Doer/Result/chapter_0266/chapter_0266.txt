Liu Zhenyuan kept his head bowed for over ten minutes until the footsteps above completely faded away. Only then did he straighten his back.
He looked up at the milky white mist drifting around the stone pillar and let out a heavy sigh.
Aberrants... Since ancient times, they had been a silent poison in the hearts of all martial artists.
The so-called Aberrants were those born with terrifying talents that normal people could never hope to touch. Calling them "gifted" was a complete understatement.
Born with monstrous strength and iron skin were just their basic traits. Some were born with extraordinary vision, supernatural hearing, or total immunity to fire and water.
Ordinary people knew nothing of them, but as a branch of the Daoist sects, the Innoxious Sect possessed depths far beyond common factions.
"How peaceful the world would be without these freaks," Liu Zhenyuan muttered. He turned and walked down the main peak.
Half a year later.
No matter how chaotic the outside world became, the Thousand Poisons Sect deep inside the Black House Mountains remained thriving.
Wei He focused entirely on his martial path. In his spare time, he stayed with his wife and child, or occasionally went to Xuanjing to drink tea, play chess, and listen to music with old friends. His life was quiet and steady.
The entire Tai Province had settled into a strange calm. Even though neighboring Yi Province had fallen and the armies of the Wu State were gathering at the border, no one seemed worried.
The Wu army had to push through Bian Province first, which meant fighting Li Tong, the general of the Xiangqu Rebel Army.
Li Tong held Bian Province with four hundred thousand men. He was no longer leading a ragtag mob. Over time, he had gathered capable men and locked down the province like an iron fortress. Calling them "rebels" didn't fit anymore.
When the Great Yuan Empire collapsed, its nineteen provinces broke apart. Some leaders restored ancient royal titles and declared themselves kings. Others claimed loyalty to the dead empire. Some raised armies under the excuse of protecting the crown, aiming for the imperial seat.
The land fell into total ruin the day the court fell.
The sound of swinging swords echoed continuously from the training grounds of the Thousand Poisons Sect.
The sect was no longer the crude camp it once was. Funded by the massive profits of the poison trade, it had completely transformed.
The old wooden shacks were replaced by stone towers, linked together and surrounded by twenty-foot stone walls to keep out mutated beasts. The stronghold now covered the size of a small town, standing alone in the dark mountain range.
Vast fields of lethal crops grew outside the walls—poisonous vines, flowers that released deadly pollen, and rare medical herbs. Patrol teams formed by captured criminals constantly walked the perimeter, forced to work off their crimes.
Inside the martial field, Wei He sat on a wooden chair, holding a cup of hot tea as he watched his disciple, Lin Yuan, practice.
Lin Yuan was Lin Xiaoxiao's younger brother. His innate talent was poor, but his willpower was terrifying. Wei He had only taught him basic movements, yet the boy had fully mastered them. He had even modified the forms while keeping the fundamentals rock-solid.
His bones were weak, but his comprehension was top-tier.
Lin Yuan finished the sword routine, sheathed his blade, and bowed with cupped hands.
"Master, this is the result of my recent practice."
Wei He set down his tea, looking satisfied. "You didn't slack off. These basic sword forms come from our sect's Broken Heart Seal. It isn't a high-tier technique, but it builds the body well. For us martial artists, moves are secondary. Internal force is what matters. You've practiced the forms enough. Next, you need to eat the flesh of mutated beasts and temper your blood."
"Master, how do I temper my blood?" Lin Yuan asked seriously.
Wei He stayed silent for a moment.
He knew many advanced techniques. Originally, he intended to hand Lin Yuan a random, low-tier manual to send him on his way. Lin Yuan's poor talent meant his blood conversion speed would be painfully slow, limiting his future. In martial arts, moves without force were useless, like a perfect gun without bullets.
But seeing the boy's drive and sharp mind changed his mind. If Lin Yuan focused on one single technique, he might actually have a small chance of becoming a martial master, fulfilling his sister's wish.
Though in Wei He's eyes, that chance was still almost zero. The boy's talent was even worse than his own used to be.
"Take this then. The Gold Joy Five-Element Technique." Wei He tossed a manual over. "Memorize the blood pathways inside. Once you can recite it backward, come find me to learn blood tempering."
Lin Yuan caught the booklet, bowed deeply, and left.
As the boy grew older, he lost his original wildness and became strict, quiet, and obedient, likely influenced by spending too much time around Wan Ling. Honest, disciplined, and stubborn.
That works too, Wei He thought.
His mind drifted over the past six months. His son, Wei An, was healthy. A short bout of jaundice was easily cleared by Jiu Ying. Wan Ling spent every day using her internal force to clear the child's meridians, laying a foundation for his future training.
Jiu Ying was growing old and weak, but he adored the child, constantly having servants bring Wei An over to build his poison resistance through specialized herbal baths.
Meanwhile, the Thousand Poisons Sect lacked many things, but money was not one of them. The poison business brought in endless wealth.
Because of this, Wei He's connection with the Wanfei Palace grew tighter. The greedy palace sent messengers every month to trade or buy rare poisons. Overnight, the Thousand Poisons Sect had become the center of medicine and poison across Tai Province.
Yet the more famous the sect became, the more uneasy Wei He felt. His whole family was here. If he lacked the power to protect them, one mistake meant total extinction.
He pushed his cultivation harder than ever.
Once Lin Yuan was gone, Wei He stood up and stared at the setting sun in silence.
Still no news on the Moon-Whale Diagram... Even the Wanfei Palace couldn't find it. I can only focus on pushing my Sea-Overturning Force.
Over the past six months, Wei He had converted his strength into numerical levels to gauge his power easily.
Based on the average strength of Tai Province martial masters, his current Sea-Overturning Force was fifteen times stronger than a normal master's internal force.
His original Rain-Covering Force had normal raw power, relying mostly on special status effects. But after stacking dozens of other forces onto it, it had turned into something monstrous.
In his mind, it showed up clearly: Rain-Covering Force +14.
And that was without accounting for the hidden lethal traits.
He renamed this modified force the "Sea-Overturning Force." When he activated all its hidden effects at once—speed, raw power, penetration, shockwaves, and destruction—he called this full-power state the Tower-Climbing State.
In this state, he guessed he could barely stand against a Visceral Sensing expert. But because he lacked exact knowledge about that realm, he didn't dare test it blindly.
"It's time."
Wei He went back to his room, changed into dark martial gear, packed his gear, said goodbye to Wan Qingqing, and vanished into the night.
He needed to know his absolute limit. He needed to find out what the Visceral Sensing realm truly was.
The manuals only described it as extremely dangerous, leaving everything else vague. He guessed the secrets of this realm were only passed down orally from master to disciple.
There were only two Visceral Sensing experts he could reach: You Fu, the leader of the Nest-Wiping Alliance, and the Three-Headed Civet.
The Civet wouldn't talk. You Fu was his only choice.
Wei He burst into full speed. His massive blood reserves gave him endless endurance. Boosted by two different speed traits, he moved like a streak of lightning, covering eighty meters every second. To normal eyes, he was just a gust of wind.
He left the mountain range, bypassed Xuanjing City, and stopped at Mount Laojun along the main road of Tai Province.
He slowed down, standing before a quiet Daoist temple called Laojun Temple.
This was the agreed meeting spot.
Inside the temple, a fire flickered. A group of wealthy, young travelers guarded by strong mercenaries were sitting around the fire to keep warm.
At the center sat two young people.
One was a handsome young man with pale skin, wearing a white turban and a curved dagger encrusted with gold and gems at his waist.
Beside him was a young woman under twenty. She was stunning, with sharp, lively eyes and an unusually full, heavy chest that moved with her every breath. Every gesture she made carried natural elegance—clearly someone born into extreme wealth.
