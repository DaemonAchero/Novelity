魏合 (Wei He) understood. These old guards were the real treasure of the escort agency.
He thought about it and silently memorized the location of the Bridgehead House they had passed.
Cheng Jing spoke up again from the side.
"That night, I was just trying to provoke you out of habit. Don't take it to heart."
"It's fine," Wei He replied. He couldn't tell if this woman was acting back then, or if she was acting right now.
Her words sounded partly true and partly fake.
He glanced over and saw Cheng Kai looking their way from a short distance.
Wei He had a guess. He felt that the head guard Cheng Kai was trying to clear up the grudge between them.
Yet Cheng Jing's tone sounded somewhat sincere.
She said a few more words, then stood up and left.
Wei He picked up his dried bread, stuffed it into his mouth piece by piece with water, and chewed on a bit of snake meat now and then. He stayed silent after that.
They kept moving and soon entered the ten-mile zone of Feiye City.
More bull carts and horses appeared on the road. Everyone felt relieved. The heavy mood from earlier was finally gone.
The team entered the city and returned to the agency. After handing in their logs at the front gate, a staff member came out to count everyone's pay.
By the rules, if someone died on a dangerous trip, everyone got a slight pay raise.
Since carrying too much silver was too heavy, many chose to take dried meat strips instead.
A smoked meat strip weighed over ten pounds and was worth a good amount of silver. Others took cloth. Colorful cloth and expensive silk were the hard currency here.
Wei He hesitated for a moment. He traded most of his money for meat, rice, and cloth, carrying it all home in a large sack.
Silver was losing its value fast. No one knew when the system would break down completely. Storing food was better since it could be used directly to trade.
He set his things down and looked at his small, run-down room. He knew he had to move.
The path from his home to the Shiqiao District where the Huishan Fists school was located crossed several gang territories. That was a constant threat.
Leaving so much food at home with nobody to guard it meant someone might break in and steal it any day.
He sat on the only wooden stool in the room.
Wei He let the sunlight from the window warm his body.
He thought back to everything he had seen on this trip.
He pulled out a stack of yellow paper and a few charcoal pencils from under the table.
He spread the rough paper flat and picked up a pencil.
Using the grip he used for pens in his past life, he drew quickly on the paper.
In a short time, he mapped out the route, terrain, location names, and local customs of the places he had crossed. He marked where to find an inn and where to rest and get supplies.
He drew a circle around the Bridgehead House to mark it as dangerous.
Then came that monster.
Wei He took another sheet of paper to write down its traits. He could not draw, but he could write down its key features, behavior, and attack patterns.
When he finished, he packed the paper away, washed his face, and fell onto the bed to rest.
The mattress smelled like old sweat and weird odors. He could not sleep at all.
His skin started to itch where it touched the blanket.
"This blanket hasn't been washed or dried in months," Wei He remembered.
He had told his second sister not to return to this house because it was unsafe.
Ever since then, the place had grown filthy.
Suddenly, he felt something crawl on his thigh.
Wei He jumped up and pulled back the blanket. A black cockroach was sitting on his leg.
Its feelers twitched. Startled by the sudden move, it darted down his leg and headed for a crack in the wall.
Wei He reacted on instinct and stomped down.
Squish.
He realized he was barefoot.
A gross, slimy texture mixed with hard shell squished under his foot.
He stood frozen for a second, then ran outside to wash his foot with water from the jar.
After washing for a long time, he returned to the room, exhausted. He refused to touch the bed again. A yellow stain now ruined the sheets.
He packed his belongings, picked up his gear, and headed toward his sister's place.
He would drop off his things with Wei Ying, then sleep at the Huishan Fists yard.
He made up his mind. He needed to buy a house right away, move his sister in, and open the steamed bun shop.
Shiqiao District.
Near the Feiye River, a row of houses stood cold and empty.
A yawning old man walked up to a door and used a key to unlock three heavy locks.
He wore a dusty gray robe, carried an unlit lantern on his back, and had a large ring of keys at his waist.
"This place fits your request. It's called Mingshan Residence. A rich merchant used to live here, but he moved his whole family away and left this house for us brokers to sell cheap."
"What's the price?"
Behind the old man stood a tall, strong youth in a gray outfit that showed off his tight muscles.
The youth had sharp eyes and a calm posture. It was Wei He, who had just dropped his things off at his sister's place.
He had lost no time in finding a local house broker.
He demanded to see a house right away.
The broker asked what kind of place he wanted and how much he could pay.
Then he sent this old man to show him around.
The heavy black door opened with a loud creak.
Inside was a small path paved with pebbles.
Past the front gate and a small guardroom lay a courtyard with white walls.
It was not big, but it looked neat.
On the left was a small pond with a bamboo water clock. A few dead leaves floated on the surface, and bugs crawled slowly over them.
On the right stood an old tree of an unknown kind.
Its wide top cast a large shadow. A thick black clothesline was tied between the trunk and the top of the wall.
"The old owner loved art. He often invited friends here to drink tea and look at paintings.
When I came here before, there were many scroll paintings hanging from this rope."
The hunchbacked old man walked slowly to the main house and pushed the door open.
Gray dust fell from the top of the door.
"This is the main hall for guests. There are four side rooms that you can turn into servant quarters, guest rooms, or bedrooms.
There is a toilet and a kitchen in the back, plus a small vegetable patch. The Feiye River is right on the left, so you don't need to worry about water."
The old man took Wei He through every corner of the house.
This was the fourth house they had checked.
It was in Shiqiao District, just one street away from the Huishan Fists yard.
Wei He walked around and reached the left side of the house.
Beside the wall was a small side gate. Outside was a short path leading straight to the riverbank.
He opened the gate. The river was only a few meters away. He could hear the sound of flowing water from where he stood.
"You can fish or draw water right here. Also, you can see inside the Inner City from this spot."
The old man pointed with his finger.
"Look over there. That pavilion belongs to an Inner City mansion. If you live here, you can often hear music playing from across the water. You get to listen for free."
He grinned, showing a mouth of yellow teeth.
Wei He looked where he pointed. Further down to the left, a small pavilion stood by the river, connected to a massive estate.
The mansion had red paint, white walls, and carved beast figures on the roof. It belonged to an important family.
As Wei He looked, two women in white dresses walked toward the pavilion. They wore face veils. One carried a zither, while the other walked empty-handed.
Both came from inside the mansion. Even though posts and fences blocked the river around the pavilion to keep people away, they could not block the view from a distance.
Soon, the two women entered the pavilion and pulled down white curtains on all sides, blocking the view completely.
"See that? Live here and you get nice views all the time," the old broker said with a dirty laugh.
"In the past, artists used to hide near here just to catch a glimpse of the ladies."
He gave a look that men understood all too well.
Wei He knew about those artists. They drew cheap picture books for men.
"Alright. I like this house. How much?" Wei He asked, making up his mind.
This place made getting water, cooking, and washing clothes easy. It had four rooms, which was enough for him and guests. Everything worked out well.
"It's cheap. Just this much," the old man held up a hand.
"Five thousand taels?" Wei He calculated in his mind. "Can I pay in parts?" He didn't have that much cash.
"Five thousand taels of what? I mean rice! Fifty pounds of coarse rice and fifteen strips of smoked pork!" the old man said quickly.
Wei He was stunned.
They didn't want silver?
He looked at the empty houses nearby and remembered how many local shops were slowly closing down.
Then he understood.
"That's the price. This house fits you best. Anything better would be at the Xiaoran Villa, and that costs way more. You won't like anything cheaper," the old man said.
He knew these houses were worth very little now. There were simply too many empty ones.
People were dying out, and empty houses were everywhere. No one knew if they could sell them later.
This young man might not even live here for long before someone else took over. Selling it for food was better, given the plague spreading around.
The old man sighed and went quiet.
Wei He thought it over.
With the supplies he got from the escort trip and his saved income, he had enough to cover the cost.
"Fine. I'll take it."
Once they agreed, he followed the old man to the agency, finished the paperwork, and brought the supplies from his sister's place to pay the fee.
By the time everything was done, the sky was turning dark.
Next, he had to tell his sister to move in, and hire someone to clean the place. Wei He counted his pay. He was a guard at the agency, but he received the pay of a lead guard, the same rank as Cheng Jing.
He realized that was likely why Cheng Jing disliked him in the first place.
