Before she had even come near, a heavy smell of medicine drifted out of the little bundle. Wei Ying hurriedly shut the door behind her and dropped the bar.
"Xiao He, this is for you!" She came over and put the bundle into her brother's hands.
"Second Sister, what is it?" Wei He took it, puzzled, and opened the grey cloth. Inside was another layer of grey cloth.
Three layers of cloth were unwrapped one after another, and innermost was a yellow paper packet. On the outside of the packet, in red characters: Blood-Nourishing Powder. Three large characters, plain to see.
"This is Blood-Nourishing Powder!" Wei He was startled. He went often to the apothecary for golden coin worm meat, so he knew this prescription well. It was a tonic made to warm and nourish qi and blood; very effective, and not cheap.
"Don't worry, it wasn't stolen, and it wasn't bought." Wei Ying smiled, and a touch of shyness came over her ruddy face. "Every day I tidy Old Zheng's medicines and his storeroom for him. There are a great many broken bits and dregs in there, and I gathered them up a little at a time. It took me a long while to make up this one small packet. Don't turn your nose up at it for being so crumbly." At that Wei He opened the paper packet carefully and looked in through a gap.
And so it was: nothing but fine little crumbs inside, broken to pieces. The largest were no bigger than a fingernail, the smallest only a grain of rice. How much time and effort Wei Ying had spent to gather this one packet, he could not guess. He was quiet a moment, then nodded seriously.
"I understand. Don't worry — I'll get through. I will."
"Mm. I believe you!" Wei Ying nodded seriously too. The two of them talked on in low voices a while, and when it was nearly dark Wei He got up to walk Wei Ying back to the rented room over on Mountain Fist's side of the ward.
Back in his own house, Wei He silently steeped the Blood-Nourishing Powder in water and set it to boil at once. As it boiled he pulled his collar open and looked at the Realm Breaking Bead. The pattern there had gone completely black several days ago. An indefinable impulse made him feel as though a balloon about to burst hung on his chest.
One light touch and the balloon would break, and the bead would be used. But he had held himself back all this while. He meant to break through into Mountain Fist's skin-grinding door by his own effort. Yet as the final deadline drew nearer, the hope in him grew smaller and smaller. Often a man had to admit it in the end: between one person and another there truly was a gap.
Wei He watched the fire burning in the stove, set the medicine pot on it, and began to boil. He knew that if he could not get through skin grinding he would have to leave the Mountain Fist yard, and there would be no going further after that.
== Chapter 9 · Breaking Open (Part 1) ==
If a man could truly enter Stone-Hide, Old Man Zheng's fees would drop sharply, and only at Stone-Hide could he truly count as one of Mountain Fist. As things were now, they who had not got through the door — one batch after another, ever changing — were in Old Man Zheng's eyes nothing but tools for making money,
with no weight to them at all. Get over this checkpoint and it would be another country altogether. Wei He watched the broth warming by degrees. He let his mind wander no more, and gave himself wholly to boiling it properly. Then, once it had cooled a little, he began to drink it down, mouthful by mouthful.
Blood-Nourishing Powder was a formula for warming and nourishing qi and blood, and it worked well. Early the next morning, when Wei He began skin grinding again, he felt a clear difference. Striking the sand and grit, the stinging in his fists was less than before, and when he rested, he recovered a little quicker. Faint though it was, to feel these changes plainly was no small thing.
What disappointed him was that the day wore through and still he had not broken through. Of the men who had begun skin grinding along with him, one had finally succeeded. And another chose to give it up, and took his bundles and left with a heavy heart.
