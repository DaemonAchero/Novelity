But needlework went cheap, and earned a woman at most her food money, and such work was not to be had every day. How long it had taken her to save this much, he could not guess.
"Xiao He, still no word of Eldest Sister? And Mother and Father…?" Wei Ying asked quietly, a thread of hope still in her eyes.
"…" Wei He shook his head. He had gone to the Black Water Gang several times, but in the Black Water Gang of today the new men had replaced the old, and you could hardly find anyone who had even heard the name of his eldest sister Wei Chun.
And as for Mingde Temple, he had gone once, and beyond knowing that his parents had gone missing on the road, he had learned nothing. The hope in Wei Ying's eyes faded little by little. She let out a breath and lowered her head. "Whether they are alive or not, there are only the two of us left now. We both have to live well."
"Mm." Wei He nodded. He did not give the money back, because he knew his second sister's temper: if he did not take it, she would not be easy in her mind.
"When you have a moment, give me your old clothes, all the torn ones, and I will patch them for you. And I will make you another pair of shoes. Don't go buying them outside — the shoes I make are better than what's outside. The soles on those outside are not firm, not pressed tight, they come apart in layers…" Wei Ying went on chattering for a long while, all of it the talk of someone looking after you.
Wei He listened, putting in a word now and then. She talked for a good while, until the night-watch drum sounded outside and the hour grew late. Only then did Wei Ying reluctantly go back into the yard and in toward the inner rooms. She still had to boil water to scald the towels for everyone.
At the doorway of the room she turned back to look at Wei He, saw him standing at the yard gate still watching her, and felt her heart settle a great deal. So she smiled and waved to him, then waved him off to his own business. Turning back round, Wei Ying walked as if a good deal lighter.
In these troubled times, when a person worked, if she knew there was still kin outside holding her up, she was not alone and helpless, and her heart felt sure. From today, she thought, she would save money to buy cotton wadding and make her brother a new quilt. The weather was turning colder and colder; he must not fall ill. Wei He stood at the gate watching his second sister until she reached the innermost room, where the sounds of her bustling began, and only then left the yard for his own lodgings.
He still remembered that his second sister's greatest wish used to be to open a steamed bun shop. Perhaps he could earn the money and grant her that wish. To him as he was now, the money to open a bun shop was not much.
After all, in most parts of the city, houses were worth less and less…
He slept about an hour, and in the afternoon Wei He came back to the Mountain Fist yard. There, to his surprise, he found a good many of the senior and junior brothers already there. Everyone, newcomers and old hands, stood in a ring, with Xiao Ran and Old Man Zheng at the centre. Even Old Man Zheng, who on ordinary days was sure to come late, had turned up quite early.
Puzzled, Wei He had barely come in when he saw Third Senior Brother Cheng Shaojiu beckoning to him. He hurried over, stood at Cheng Shaojiu's side, and looked in with the rest. "What is going on?" he asked low. There was an emotion on Cheng Shaojiu's face he could not put a name to: something tangled, and envy in it, and other things besides.
His lips moved as if he would speak, and he made a sound that was hard to catch. "Xiao Ran has broken through."
"?!" Wei He's heart jumped. "Did he not break through only a few months ago?!" He could not keep it to a low voice.
"That he did. Which is why even Old Man Zheng was stirred by it." A senior brother with fairer skin beside him said it softly, his face full of envy. His name was Li Jue, and they said he came of a scholar's family; but later the Robber Gang had come down on his house — no one knew which Robber Gang — and seven or eight parts of the family had died, leaving only him.
