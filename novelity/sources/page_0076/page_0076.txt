Not long after Wei He had gone, two people came slowly up to the front of the bun shop. They were two tall, thin figures in grey-white cloth, wide-brimmed straw hats on their heads. Both carried about them a plain air of being unlike other folk, and if anyone came near, they could smell the faint scent of incense on them.
That was a scent only people who often handled incense sticks could keep on their clothes for long. The two looked up at the bun shop and came one behind the other to its door. The one in front raised her face and showed the kindly countenance of a middle-aged woman. "Sister Yingying, I have come to see you again." she greeted her in a pleasant voice.
"Sister Xu Chun? What brings you here? Come in and sit; I shall be done here in a moment." Wei Ying was just about to work leaven into the dough she had kneaded and set it to rise; seeing who had come, she had to stop for the time being. Xu Chun was a neighbour who lived nearby, and because her name carried the same 'chun' as Wei Ying's eldest sister's, and because she was a kindly sort, a few meetings had been enough to make her and Wei Ying quite familiar.
Xu Chun sat down in the shop, bringing the other one with her, and looking at Wei Ying as she took a seat too, she smiled. "In fact, Sister Yingying, the reason I came today is that yesterday I saw you grieving and missing your family, so I came to have a heart-to-heart with you."
"Sister, you look after me too well…" Wei Ying was a little moved. Yesterday she had only been resting a little while, staring blankly at the carving knife her father and mother had left behind, and she had never thought Xu Chun would see it.
"We are all neighbours here; what talk is this of looking after?" Xu Chun sighed. "Speaking of it, when family go missing it is often simply a thing no one can help. When my eldest son disappeared, then too I was like you — so wretched I wanted to knock my head against the wall. But afterwards, as the time slowly went by, I met a benefactor who found me something to anchor my heart to, and then it was not so hard."
"A benefactor?" Wei Ying asked curiously. "What benefactor?"
"This benefactor is the one who came along with me…" Xu Chun, all warmth, brought the person behind her forward. "Cultivator Mengjin. This is the Wei Ying I spoke to you about before. She is a girl of good family, gentle of temper and kind-hearted, and whenever I see her grieving, my heart here is pricked as if by
a needle, and I think of my eldest son again. So this time I asked you to come, hoping you might help this good-hearted girl just as you helped me then." The second one came forward: a gaunt old woman with a small red mole between her brows. Her face was stern as she put out a thin, claw-like arm and pushed the brim of her straw hat a little higher.
"You poor thing…" Cultivator Mengjin looked at Wei Ying and gave a sigh. "With a face like yours, no wonder… no wonder…" She shook her head as she looked at Wei Ying.
"Might I ask, Cultivator, is there something wrong with my face?" Wei Ying was beginning to be frightened under that stare. She could not read, had never studied, and was only an ordinary girl who knew a little housework and handiwork; stared at like this, she grew
flustered at once. "Do you know that by nature you are…" Cultivator Mengjin began slowly, her face stern. Suddenly a tall shadow fell over her from the doorway. The shadow blocked the door, neither coming in nor going out, only standing fixed there.
== Chapter 26 · Trouble (Part 2) ==
Cultivator Mengjin trembled slightly, sensed something amiss, and cast her eyes toward the door.
Wei He was standing in the doorway, watching her with a calm face. "Go on. Keep talking." He fixed his eyes on Cultivator Mengjin, and in them a murderous light showed, faint and dark. At that, both Cultivator Mengjin and Xu Chun shook from head to foot and scrambled to their feet, heads down, and went. Had Wei He not been there it would have been one thing; with him there, they dared not come so boldly to a door to preach.
They had seen that the Wei family seemed to have a little money, and that they were willing to sell their buns and cakes to everyone at almost no profit. The two of them had reckoned that if they could pull Wei Ying into the sect, they might well reap no small profit. Who would have thought that Wei He had gone out only a moment, and come back so quickly?
