Now the map was in hand and a lead on the strange beasts was in hand; the only thing he still lacked was beast bait. That was so that if he could not beat a strange beast, he could still use the bait to draw it off and make his escape. Cheng Shaojiu had enquired for him before, though: on the market no one at all was willing to put beast bait out for sale. Only Guan's Medicine Shop was willing to sell it to him.
All of this Cheng Shaojiu had arranged in the past, but now Wei He had no choice but to go in person himself.
== Chapter 52 · Stroke of Fortune (Part 2) ==
Towards evening the sky grew darker and darker. Guan's Medicine Shop was not in Stone Bridge Ward but in Red Stone Ward. By the time Wei He arrived the shop was nearly shut. Several shop hands were shifting the door boards into place, sealing the front one by one. There was still a small side door left open, though, for people to come and go.
People were still going in and out even then, as if a last bit of business was being done. Wei He followed them in through the side door. Inside, a shopkeeper in a face mask was saying something to two men; seeing Wei He come in he was slightly taken aback, then quickly bowed to him with folded hands.
Then the shopkeeper called a young hand over and said a few words in his ear. The young man came straight over to Wei He. "May I ask whether this is Master Wei He?" the shop hand said with great respect. "It is." Wei He nodded.
"The young miss knows you were coming. Please come with me," the shop hand said respectfully. Something moved in Wei He's face; he had a guess or two. He followed the young man up a side staircase to the second floor. Up there was a broad, wide-open parlour taking up the whole floor.
In the middle stood a great brass incense burner, and from it pale smoke drifted up, the scent of it pressing in on the nose. A girl in a red skirt sat cross-legged before the burner. Her long hair fell loose over her shoulders, her lips were red as blood, and a pair of almond eyes moved calmly from the burner to this side, to Wei He. "Is this Brother Wei He before me?" The girl stood, showing a bright, open smile.
"That is I. May I ask the young lady…" Wei He bowed with folded hands. "My name is Guan Ye. Did Cheng Shaojiu ever mention me?" It was only when the girl in the red skirt stood that her waist showed — so slender a hand could span it. That waist was the most striking thing about her; the rest of her was well enough proportioned, but the small waist was just so, and was set off tightly by a corset, so that the eye could hardly be drawn away.
"So it is Miss Guan. Brother Cheng did mention you. Only, this…" Wei He did not finish before she cut him off. "I know. The moment I saw you come up the stairs I knew you were not my type, but never mind. To tell you the truth, I never thought much of Cheng Shaojiu's sort of character either. But…" Guan Ye walked a full circle round Wei He and said with a smile, "but ever since you threw away a thousand taels of gold to pull the Cheng family out of the fire, I have known your
character and nature. So…" She spoke as if she liked to say a whole string in one breath and then break off short. "So: you want to buy bait, I will give it to you; you want to buy deadly poisons, I will give you those too. If you could be friends with Cheng Shaojiu, you ought to be able to be friends with me, Guan Ye. How about it?"
The girl was no more than seventeen or eighteen, and there was a quicksilver, mischievous cleverness about her. "I…" Wei He began, at a loss; he wanted to explain that being sworn brothers with Cheng Shaojiu was not a thing made in a moment. But Guan Ye cut him off again at once. "Do you know how much one portion of strange-beast bait costs?"
She held up five white, delicate fingers. "Five hundred taels of gold. A price on it, and nowhere to buy it." "Expensive, isn't it?"
