"Never mind how prosperous the Inner City looks — the truly great families all build forts outside the walls. You will understand when you have seen more of the world. Enough of that for now. Come, let me take you to try the greatest perk I have to offer!" Cheng Shaojiu laughed out loud and pulled Wei He off toward another courtyard. Not long after, the two of them sat facing each other in a small room.
Between them stood a black wood table with two dishes and a soup on it: twice-cooked pork with green bamboo shoots, and cold-dressed celery. The soup was pig's trotters stewed with soybeans. "Little brother, if you come to work for me, the best perk you'll have every day is this." Cheng Shaojiu smiled a little, pointing at the plate of
twice-cooked pork. The blue-green bamboo slices were mixed with the scorched yellow pork, with a few rings of dried chilli and slivers of garlic in among them. It looked to make a man's appetite come up. "Isn't this just stir-fried meat?" Wei He looked up, not quite following.
"This is no ordinary meat. Try it." Cheng Shaojiu said it mysteriously.
"…" Wei He picked up his chopsticks, took up a piece carefully, and put it in his mouth; a few chews, and he swallowed. The taste did not bear praising — too bland, with no seasoning to speak of, dry and stringy, nothing at all like the way it looked. But the meat was truly chewy and fresh-tasting; not like pork at all.
And not long after he swallowed, Wei He felt a thin thread of warm burning come welling up out of his stomach. "How is it? Feel that heat in your belly?" Cheng Shaojiu said, pleased with himself. "This is a speciality my Cheng family alone has." He picked up a slice of the meat and explained: "This thing is called Silver-Kissed Black Snake meat. It is a great tonic. Eat one catty of it and you will not be hungry for two days.
Steeped in wine it works even better. We who train the fists burn far more than ordinary men, of course, so eating it at every meal is no trouble, and it does a great deal of good for building up qi and blood. And the main thing is, you cannot buy it anywhere outside. It belongs to my Cheng family alone and is not sold."
"Silver-Kissed Black Snake…" Wei He picked up another slice, and at once saw what was truly different about it.
The outer skin of this Silver-Kissed Black Snake meat was black, and the inside was remarkably springy, with hair-thin blood vessels clearly visible one by one; looked at closely, it was almost like a work of art. "So, you and I being brothers, I will never do you wrong. Go home and think it over properly, and if you're willing, give me an answer early tomorrow morning. How's that?" Cheng Shaojiu said, sober-faced.
"Good!" Wei He nodded.
== Chapter 11 · Leaving the City (Part 1) ==
On the road home in the evening. As he walked, Wei He kept savouring the whole catty of Silver-Kissed Black Snake meat sitting in his belly. He could feel clearly the same warmth in his body that golden coin worm meat gave, and beside golden coin worm meat this snake meat was plainly a great deal stronger.
Senior Brother Cheng's offer did stir him a little, but Cheng Shaojiu was the only one courting him so far, with nothing else to compare it against, so for the moment he was not decided. Once he got home — and by what Cheng Shaojiu had said — the other people who wanted men would soon come knocking one by one. He could compare them then and decide after; no harm in that.
Wei He hurried home. The Cheng house stood in Stone Bridge Ward, the same ward as Mountain Fist, but where he lived was another ward altogether, and between them lay a stretch of ground that nobody governed. Walking it, Wei He swept his eyes over the rather desolate street. The sky was not yet dark and there was already hardly anyone about, so cold and empty; only from far off, out of the courtyards of the rich households, came barking, burst after burst of it.
Passing a narrow lane, he dimly made out two beggars curled together for warmth, hunched in a corner and not moving; whether they were dead or not, there was no telling. Then two men in rags, pushing two handcarts, came slowly turning out of a lane on the right.
