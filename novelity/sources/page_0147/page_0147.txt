His purpose in coming here was to gather information about the strange beasts. By now, after so long hunting, he had a good deal of experience, and he could try his hand at the strange beasts themselves. That night at the bridgehead on the escort run, the Black Madman he had met there was still sharp in his memory. So to guard against the unforeseen, he meant to make every preparation complete, and gather enough information first, before daring to make his attempt.
After sitting a little while, with still no lead on the strange beasts that day, Wei He finished his tea, rose, pushed aside the door curtain and stepped out of the wine house. All at once he saw, coming the other way, Ouyang Zhuang — the new man he was training — passing along the roadside with a hired hand in tow. "Eh? Senior Brother Wei!" Ouyang Zhuang looked at Wei He in surprise. "You didn't go to the Yonghe Agency today?"
"The agency? Why, what is today?" Wei He was surprised too. "You don't know? Word came out of the Yonghe Agency this morning: they cannot pay the compensation owed to the Zhao family of the Inner City for that escort. They have borrowed money everywhere, but they are still seven hundred taels of gold short. If they cannot pay it now, they can only shut their doors and close down — finished." Ouyang Zhuang answered.
"A debt? Seven hundred taels of gold?" Wei He started. Only seven hundred? Something lurched in his chest. "What is it, Senior Brother?" Seeing that Wei He's face had gone strange, Ouyang Zhuang asked cautiously.
Without another word Wei He turned and ran back towards his own house — he had to fetch his money and get to the agency. This Cheng fellow was too proud for his own good; ask him once, ask him a dozen times, and he would not have said a word earlier. Had he said earlier that he was short only this much, Wei He would long since have lent it to him and carried him over the bad stretch! …
… The Cheng house, the reception hall. At a square table of red hardwood sat Cheng Shaojiu and a middle-aged man in a black mandarin jacket, facing each other.
Behind each of them stood a crowd of men — one side many, the other few, and the few were the ones behind Cheng Shaojiu. His hand lay on the table, his fingers pressed on a sheet of hemp-yellow paper, and his face was very bad to look at.
On the paper, in black and white, were the terms for the bond and the compensation on their last escort run. "Speak up. Today your Yonghe owes us an accounting," the middle-aged man in the mandarin jacket said in a low voice. Slanting sunlight fell into the hall and struck the floor, showing up the motes of dust adrift in it. "Losing the goods made my Zhao family break faith with its clients. How is this to be settled? You should be putting a proposal on the table."
Silence. Cheng Shaojiu's lips were cracked and dry, his face dark. These days past he had strained every nerve and gone everywhere to raise money, and in the end he was still five hundred taels of gold short. This was already the utmost he could do.
"Can there be no few days' grace more?" he asked in a low voice. "Grace, grace — young escort master Cheng, out of consideration for our past dealings we have already granted you this many days. We have done all that kindness requires," the middle-aged man said coldly. "Speak, then: the last gap, these five hundred taels of gold — what of it? If you truly cannot produce it, then let it be my earlier proposal instead —"
take your method of rearing the Silver-Kissed Black Snake, in exchange. How would that be?" Behind Cheng Shaojiu the men all showed looks of suppressed anger. The method of rearing the Silver-Kissed Black Snake was worth far more than a paltry five hundred taels of gold; the man was plainly out to rob a drowning house! But these days past Cheng Shaojiu and the rest had been going everywhere to borrow, and still could not scrape together that five hundred taels of gold.
Those friends who in ordinary days were all generosity on their lips now shut their doors and would not be seen. Let alone five hundred taels — they would not lend a single tael. Everyone knew now that the Yonghe Agency had come to grief, and nobody was willing to lend, lest it be a meat bun thrown to a dog, gone and never coming back.
