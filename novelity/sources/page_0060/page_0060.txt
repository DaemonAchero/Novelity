Wei He took out another sheet of paper and recorded the monster's features. He could not draw, but he could note down its striking marks, its traits, the way it attacked. When that was done he put the yellow papers away, washed up, and threw himself on the bed to rest. Only, the mattress on the bed stank of sweat, with some other unnameable smell besides, and it made sleep hard to come by.
The skin that touched the bedding even began to itch. "This quilt has not been washed or sunned for months, it seems…" Only then did Wei He recall. He remembered warning his second sister a while back not to come back to this house, that it was not safe. And it seemed that from then on this house had grown more and more neglected.
Suddenly he felt a tickling on his thigh. Startled, Wei He flung the quilt back at once, and there on his thigh sat a black cockroach. The cockroach waved its feelers, took fright, shot off his thigh in a flash, and made along the bed for the crack by the wall. By pure reflex Wei He stamped on it.
Splat. Only then did he remember that his feet were bare… A strange sensation, sticky, with bits of hard shell in it, travelled from the sole of his foot through his whole body. He froze for a moment, then got up fast, went out, and scrubbed the sole of his foot hard with water from the vat.
After fussing over it a good while he went wearily back into the room, and this time he had no wish at all to lie on that bed again. There was a yellow stain on the bed now too. He tidied a little, then simply picked up his things and headed for his second sister's. He would leave the goods with Wei Ying first, and then go sleep at the Mountain Fist yard!
He had decided: after this run he would buy a house at once and move himself and his second sister in, and open that bun shop too! …
Stone Bridge Ward. Near the Feiye River, a row of houses stood empty and bleak. An old man, yawning, walked up to the door of one house and used the keys in his hand to open the three big locks hanging on the door. The old man wore a dusty grey long coat, carried an unlit lantern on his back, and had a great bunch of keys of every sort at his waist.
"This house matches your requirements too. It is called Shan Ju. A rich merchant lived here before, but later he went to another place and moved the whole family away, and left the house to be sold cheap to us brokers."
"What is the price?" Behind the old man followed a tall, powerfully built young fellow in a grey-white close-fitting suit that showed off the firm, well-proportioned muscle of his frame.
The young man's eyes were bright and his manner composed. It was Wei He, who had only just left his things at his second sister's. He had not paused for a moment; the first thing he did was find the broker house in the city that dealt in buying and selling houses. Then he asked for a man to take him to see houses at once. The broker asked him roughly what sort of house he wanted, then made clear what price he could bear.
So he found an old man to lead the way and take him to see the houses on the ground. The black-lacquered gate swung open with a creak. Inside lay a pebble path, the walk for entering and leaving. Past the gate and a small gatehouse was a courtyard of white walls.
The courtyard was not large, but it was laid out with quiet taste. On the left was a small pond, with a bamboo-tube water clock set over it. A few fallen leaves floated on the pond, and small insects crawled slowly on the leaves. On the right stood an old tree whose kind he could not name.
The old tree's crown spread wide, casting a broad patch of shade. Between the trunk and the top of the wall a thick black clothesline was tied. "The master of this house used to be very fond of imitating paintings and calligraphy, and often invited like-minded friends here to drink tea and admire paintings. When I came before, there were a good many painted silks hanging on this line."
