"That stands to reason. Masters can teach you all they like and still you will not come out a good hand." Cheng Shaojiu stamped his feet lightly, shifting his body about to loosen up. "These weeks, at the small gathering, I have come to grasp one key thing." He said it with a smile.
"What key thing?" Wei He blurted out, then immediately felt it was ill done — if it came out of the small gathering, passing it to him in private was not proper.
As if he saw what Wei He meant, Cheng Shaojiu shook his head. "Don't worry about that. This one I worked out myself; it needs no one else's consent." He swayed and strolled, turning circles around the place, and for a while said nothing more, slowly, slowly, as if he were loosening his body up. Wei He waited for the answer, but the man circled this way and that with a half-smile on his face and simply would not speak.
So this fellow was a true disciple of Old Man Zheng after all, and had learned the old man's habit of dangling a man's appetite down to the last crooked bit. Right then he stopped being in a hurry himself, and stood silent where he was, refusing to press. After so long learning under Old Man Zheng, he had learned long ago that the old fellow's worst habit was waiting, easy as you please, for others to press him; what he liked best was watching a man half die of impatience while he himself kept his mouth shut.
The more impatient others grew, the happier he was. It was a thoroughly wicked little taste. And sure enough, after a short wait, Cheng Shaojiu looked left and right, circled several more times, and never got Wei He to press him. His discipline was not Old Man Zheng's, after all, and in the end he could not hold out.
"Ah, well. Since you want to hear it so badly, I will go through it carefully." He gave a long sigh and pretended to pick up naturally where he had left off.
"Thank you, Senior Brother, for clearing it up." Wei He followed at once, cupping his fist in salute. Cheng Shaojiu was stopped short and at once felt put out; but he dropped the teasing and said it straight. "In real fighting, the first thing is eyesight."
"Eyesight?"
"Quite so — watching, and looking first." Cheng Shaojiu made it plain. "Look at your opponent, judge your opponent, sort out which kind he is.
"First get a rough look at what kind your opponent is: is he good with his fists, with his legs, or with a weapon? And if a weapon, what sort of weapon?
You have to understand, a man good with a weapon is a great deal more dangerous than the common run. However hard our fists may be, meeting a sharp weapon and taking it head on, we come off the worse. So you must judge ahead.
"But the judging ahead I mean is not only that. That much you will understand on your own once you have fought enough. The key I want to talk about is in overawing."
Here Cheng Shaojiu's face turned grave. "I have heard it from my father and elder brothers: when true experts trade hands, they pay the closest attention to the qi-mechanism."
"The qi-mechanism?" Wei He felt it sounded rather misty.
"Quite so. It sounds very mystic, but put plainly, the qi-mechanism is presence and opportunity." Cheng Shaojiu smiled. "Suppose for a moment
that behind me stand several dozen retainers with crossbows aimed at you at all times, and you come to blows with me — would you dare use your full strength?" Wei He understood at once, a little. "And that is the qi-mechanism?"
"Quite so — that is presence. Presence is a thing I do not fully understand either, but when my father and brothers gave me an example, that was the one
they used. I am repeating it to you word for word." Cheng Shaojiu went on. "The stronger your eyesight, the more detail you can see in your opponent, the better your grip on the fight, and the harder it is for you to make a mistake. And this thing, the qi-mechanism — it means, roughly, thinking up every way to keep your opponent from putting out his full strength, from bringing all his power to bear, and then thinking up every way to bring out the strongest in yourself. Strike the weak with the strong, and you should win!"
"Strike the weak with the strong…" Wei He's eyes lit up, and all at once he seemed to have caught hold of something. Had that not been what he had been doing all along? And so he had won. Killing Chen Biao before had been the same, and so had killing the tracker at Mingde Temple.
The tracker had plainly been stronger than he was; face to face, he would surely have died. Yet in the end the man had died by his hand, and that was because he had cut the man's strength down first by a dirty trick. So that was it: the winner was the one who, by any means and to the last extreme, weakened his opponent and strengthened himself.
