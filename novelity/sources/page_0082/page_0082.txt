"Those are men of the Shanchuan Gang. Their leader at least is a Black Mountain expert." Cheng Shaojiu explained. "Shanchuan Gang men do not as a rule mix in our wards. Inside the gang they are roughly divided into White Mountain, Black Mountain, and at the top Green Mountain. White Mountain is the ordinary membership; Black Mountain you must mind — they are at the least good hands who have broken through once in qi and blood." Wei He felt a chill and nodded again and again.
He noticed that among the leaders of this group, three alone had the Black Mountain mark embroidered on their sleeves. Besides the Shanchuan men, the rest belonged to small gangs; Cheng Shaojiu introduced them briefly and said no more. They had all come to watch the excitement, as he and Wei He had. It was the first time Wei He had come so close to the other gang powers.
"No need to worry. Neither the Three Gangs nor the Two Sects dare run loose in the Inner City; this is the Seven Family League's ground. Apart from Hong Family Fort, no power dares make the least trouble. Do you think the Ou family dare not kill?" Cheng Shaojiu said calmly, as if long used to it. Wei He understood, and at the same time grew curious about the true armies and family powers of this age.
If even they, men of the Outer City, could break through once in qi and blood by taking a little of some qi-and-blood substance, then the sons of the Seven Family League in the Inner City, with conditions far beyond theirs, might rise to what heights? And Hong Family Fort, which even the Seven Family League held in such fear — what level was it at? All at once Wei He was full of expectation and curiosity.
He began to understand Cheng Shaojiu's way of thinking — or rather, hardly any man of the martial arts could bear to miss such a sight, for Hong Family Fort kept itself secluded and seldom came out. "They are coming!" someone near at hand suddenly called in a low voice.
"Junior sister, that is the Tiger of Feiye — the strongest of Feiye's younger generation!" Wei He looked toward the voice and found that at some point two young people in blue robes, swords at their belts, had taken a table in front of them. The man was handsome and bright of face, strongly and slenderly built; the woman wore a white veil, and in her lovely eyes as she glanced about there seemed to be a ripple of water.
The two had been sitting without a word, making no impression at all, and only when they rose and looked down from the window did Wei He notice where they were. But he had no leisure now to study them closely: from below the window came the faint clash of weapons and the beat of warhorse hooves on the ground.
== Chapter 28 · Night Watch (Part 2) ==
On the street that had been so bright with lantern light, along the broad main carriageway, a column of horse-borne cavalry all in black was slowly coming in. There were not many of them — thirty riders at most.
But those thirty riders were every one in black leather armour. The troopers were strong of muscle and tall of frame, and the girth of the bare arms they showed was near twice that of an ordinary grown man. Each wore a black cloth about his head, carried a longbow and quiver at the side of his saddle, and had a huge horse-chopping saber on his back. Every horse under them stood taller than a man; like their riders they were armoured in leather, heavy of snort, tall and sturdy.
As the thirty-odd riders passed, all was dead silent in their wake, as if a mute had been pressed. Everyone was held by the bearing of this black cavalry. Most striking of all was the man at the head of the column. He had a handsome face and a powerful build, and wore the black armour of a military commander, all of it worked in metal; the horse beneath him too was
caparisoned in black metal. His black hair was bound high and he wore no helmet; he was no more than thirty, and at the corner of his mouth there hung a trace of a smile that was not quite a smile, as he rode at the head of them all. The most outrageous thing was the black war spear nearly two metres long on his back.
The spear was as thick through as a child's arm, and at a glance plainly monstrously heavy. He carried it on his back as though it were nothing at all, and even the horse beneath him seemed light and easy in its stride. Had the dull thud of the flagstones under it not been so much heavier than that of the other horses, the people around would surely have taken the beast for an ordinary light-footed animal.
