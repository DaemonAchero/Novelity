What made a man of the second breakthrough formidable was that his body was far stronger than an ordinary man's and his reactions far quicker — but he still could not stand up to poison water, poison powder or a poison dart. So long as he had no immunity to these things, catch him unawares and he was dead.
A second-breakthrough man reacts fast; but Wei He was himself a second-breakthrough man, and struck just as fast. If it were a common man setting some low trick on him, there might be nothing to be done — but between men of the same grade… Wei He turned it over and decided that eating his meat came first. The matter of Xiao Ran was noted down; once he broke through, that would be the first thing he saw to.
With two big sacks of branch-antler deer meat he went home without stopping once, back to the little Wei courtyard. On the courtyard gate hung a pair of couplets. 'Ten thousand blessings open the spring's glory; a fine steed dances in the east wind.' The brushwork was neat and strong, if a little broken and worn away at the corners, and the whole couplet was red, done on red paper — it had the look of
age about it. He had no idea where Wei Ying had found them. Carrying the meat, Wei He went up and knocked at the door. "Who is it?"
"It's me, Sister." The wooden bar was lifted and the gate opened. Wei He walked in and found Wei Ying washing clothes. In the two buckets lay his clothes in one and Wei Ying's own in the other. "Sister, I've something on — don't mind me for the next few days, just leave my food at the door. I'll be at home the whole time."
Wei He mentioned it in passing. "All right. I'll remember." Wei Ying nodded at once. "That reminds me, Sister — where did that couplet on the gate come from?" "Ah, that was written by a schoolmaster I ran into at the market today. The teacher from the Lecture Hall, from before. Back when
you used to sneak off to sit in on his lessons — remember?" Wei Ying said with a smile. "Uh…" Wei He remembered well enough. "Which teacher was it?" "Master Yuan Zhouchi. Thieves got into his house and took every penny he had laid up. There's nothing for it now, so he's out writing couplets and letters for people, to scrape together a bit of food." Wei Ying sighed a little as she said it.
Yuan Zhouchi… Wei He remembered that he had sat in on the old man's lectures too. He lectured in an entertaining way and never stuck to the rulebook, and his learning ran deep. To think that… "Is he at the market every day?" he asked.
"He is. I asked, and he's there every day." Wei Ying nodded. "Oh yes, Xiao He — someone came today from one of the little gangs you hang on with, to say they don't need you any more. The whole gang has broken up…" "Understood." Wei He nodded, picked up his two sacks of meat and went to the kitchen. He meant to use the simplest method — steaming — so that as little of the nourishment as possible was lost.
Wash, salt, slice. Plate after plate of meat was cut into thick chunks and steamed, then taken out when done to cool, and then the next plate. Halfway through, Wei He began eating, dipping the meat in soy sauce and drinking water between mouthfuls: taking in great quantities of protein and fat requires a great deal of water to digest, so the more meat one eats the more water one must drink.
After eating a while Wei He felt his belly swell, but he did not leave it; his hands kept moving, steaming every piece of meat and setting it out to cool. The weather was hot, and fresh meat left unhandled went bad quickly. His house was not like the great families', with a deep well to chill things in. And these days, even among the great families, whether the deep wells still held water
was an open question. "Want a hand?" Wei Ying washed her hands and came into the kitchen to ask. "All right. Sorry to trouble you, Sister." Wei He nodded, handed the work over to Second Sister to deal with, and went off to one side to gnaw on dried radish.
