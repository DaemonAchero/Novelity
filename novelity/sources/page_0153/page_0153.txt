The Cheng family was in Stone Bridge Ward too, no great distance away. Why would they go out of their way to send this thing over? And a letter with it? He went across, picked the stone box up, went into the room and shut the door behind him. Carrying the box, he sat down at the desk and took up the envelope of yellow paper. Tsss.
The wax-sealed envelope was torn open and the pale yellow paper inside drawn out. He unfolded the sheet with a rustle and read closely. From the first it was Cheng Shaojiu's hand. "To Wei He, to be opened by him: the wilds are vast and lone; I must walk them alone. The situation in the city is changing. I hope you will make your plans early."
Wei He's heart lurched; he felt that something was wrong. He read quickly on. Sure enough, the rest was all in Cheng Shaojiu's own hand. "Xiao He, by the time you read this letter I will have left Feiye City with every one of the Cheng family. As the situation in Feiye City stands now, with the Seven Family League and Hong Family Fort at the head of it, the two sides are slowly coming into open opposition, and have begun separately clearing out the middling
and small powers around them. If you do not take a side early, it is best to get clear of the whirlpool as soon as you can. In the outer wards today, if you do not take a side and do not choose, the end is the same as the Cheng family's: forced out. As for this whole business of robbing an escort, laying a snare and scheming to seize what we had — a play scripted and staged by their own hands — I have seen the face and the table manners of the Seven Family League clearly enough now. If a man does not want to be drawn into the whirlpool, the only way is distance.
I sent a man to look for you once before; you were not in, worse luck. Outside the Cheng compound and the agency men have already begun throwing paper money for the dead in at the gate; the agency must go whether it will or no. The one thing in this whole affair I feel I have wronged you over is the money: what you lent me I have carried off with me, and there are still many places at home that need turning over, so I will not be paying you back.
But you told me before that you were gathering deadly poisons and bait for the strange beasts. I have made you a connection with the Guan family; if you have need of anything you may go straight to Guan's Medicine Shop. Last of all, in the stone box I have left you a few things. They may be of use. Should there ever be a day we meet again, the jade pendants matched together will be old friends knowing each other. — left by Cheng Shaojiu."
The letter was not long, but one could see in it the tangle Cheng Shaojiu had been in as he wrote it. Wei He set it down in silence, feeling hollow. Feiye City had been growing more barren by the day, and now the Cheng family had actually gone. He had never had many friends to begin with; with Cheng Shaojiu gone, it suddenly felt as though there were no one in the city he knew at all.
A loneliness rose in him, welling up of itself. He sighed — and found that he had been sighing often of late. He laid the letter down and turned his eyes to the stone box. The box had three tiers; he opened the lid of the first with both hands, and lying quietly inside was a jade pendant.
A fish-shaped pendant, clear green through and through, of excellent material. "Fish, yú — and 'to meet' is yù. Brother Cheng, you really are…" Wei He shook his head. He took out the small, finely wrought pendant and slipped it into the inner pocket close to his body; later he could ask Second Sister to help thread a cord so he might carry it against his skin. The second tier of the box opened quickly after.
Inside lay a stack of drawings. The paper was brand new, and a faint smell of ink still came off it, as though it had been set down not long since. "This is…?!" Wei He picked the drawings up quickly and unfolded them. The whole sheet was a good metre and more across and square in shape, covered thickly with drawings and writing of every kind of terrain, route and place, and
certain small annotations in explanation. "It's a map!" It was the first time in all this age that Wei He had seen a map so detailed. Compared with the maps and drawings he had made for himself, he could not say how much more detailed it was. This single map alone, with the danger points marked on it and the ways to deal with them, was worth more than he could reckon.
A thing money could hardly buy! In this age the reason the common folk had no way out of the city, did not dare leave the city, was precisely that they had no way of knowing which places outside were dangerous and which were safe.
