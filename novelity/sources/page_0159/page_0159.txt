"…" She lifted the wine cup and drained it in one. Life was like that: your not pressing forward did not mean others would halt and wait for you. To halt was to fall behind; even to advance too slowly was to fall behind. A thread of crisis rose in both their hearts at once — to be unable to break through was to mark time, and marking time burned up one's potential. As the days went by,
the closer one came to twenty, the smaller that potential grew; and behind one, newcomers pressed on step by step. Xiao Ran especially knew that his whole present lot came of the potential everyone prized in him. If his potential were no longer one of a kind, if another overtook him, then… everything he had now — his standing, his treatment, his resources — would be cut back by a great
deal. He did not want that! Cup after cup went down. As he drank, brooding, Xiao Ran suddenly stopped with the cup in his hand.
He saw Jiang Su across from him, lovely as a flower, cheeks flushed, the swell of her breast high and every curve of her showing. Before he knew it his throat was dry and hot, and something stirred in him. Both of them were pent up with their own gloom, Jiang Su most of all: since her foundation was hurt, her hope of breaking through had shrunk and shrunk, and now she drank cup after cup, losing count of how much she had had in her haze.
All she knew was that Xiao Ran opposite kept calling for more wine. What she did not notice was that he had drunk heavily only at the start; later, little by little, when he poured for himself the cup held only a splash. The two drank on, and nine parts in ten of what went down was Jiang Su's alone. On she drank, until her head swam worse and worse and her mind began to blur.
"Senior Sister, you're drunk. Let me see you home," Xiao Ran said, standing. "I… am not drunk yet. I can still drink!" Jiang Su shook her head; drunk as she was, the tight knot in her heart had eased a good deal. Xiao Ran's eyes flickered. "All right, then — one last cup each, and we go when it's done. You're drunk, Senior Sister, and too much more could end badly on the way back. Better to be
careful." "Yes… one last… one last cup." Drunk as she was, a martial artist's qi and blood run strong, and she was not wholly gone. For that last cup Xiao Ran poured for them both, and this time he filled both cups to the brim.
Only, as he poured Jiang Su's, a little pale green powder shook down noiselessly from his sleeve. It was a pleasure powder he had mixed in his heedless younger days: let a woman take it, and her wits would drift, her limbs would go weak, and a fire would burn inside her that she could not master. He slipped the powder secretly into Jiang Su's wine, gave the cup a gentle swirl, and the powder melted quickly into the liquor.
"Come!" "Down the hatch!" Their cups touched lightly, and they tilted their heads and drank the last of it. With that last cup inside her Jiang Su stood up, said a few more words, and made to go down the stairs with Xiao Ran.
But the drug was fierce, and warm wine helped it along; she had taken only two steps when she felt a violent heat in her body. At once she knew something was wrong. She drank often and knew well what being drunk felt like; she had had more than usual this time, but even so she should not feel this. The heat grew more and more wrong, turning from warmth into a scalding that ran through her whole body.
Jiang Su felt at once that something was very wrong, and looked back at Xiao Ran. His face was calm, but the corner of his mouth was very slightly curved, as if he were smiling. He reached out a hand, about to come and steady her. "You're drunk, Senior Sister. Let me help you back."
"Xiao Ran… you…!" A dread foreboding had come over her; her body grew hotter and hotter, her legs pressed and rubbed together, and a nameless stirring rose in her. She knew this was bad; she turned and sprang, flying down the stairs from the second floor.
