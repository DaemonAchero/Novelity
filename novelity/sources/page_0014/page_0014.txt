It turned out that the assignment Wei He wrote in his place went over remarkably well, and earned him no small praise from the instructor. The praise was only for neat, forceful handwriting — but even that was a great deal better than what he managed himself. So the two of them fell into the habit of taking what each was short of from the other, making up for their own lacks at the other's hand.
"Now that's what I like to hear! That's the word I was waiting for. No two ways about it — Chen Biao, is it? I'll keep such an eye on him the man won't dare breathe." Cheng Shaojiu thumped his chest and gave his word.
"Thank you, Third Senior Brother." Wei He nodded.
"Wei He! The master wants you." A small-built young man came running out to the doorway of the inner room and called across the yard.
"Coming." Wei He set his stone down and went in.
Inside, Old Man Zheng sat bare-backed while an old apothecary pasted a plaster onto him; the room was thick with the smell of medicine. Zheng did not lift his head when Wei He came in, only flicked his eyes at him.
"Go and buy me five liang of sappan wood, five of angelica root, five of white peony root, and six qian of figwort root. Lay the money out yourself; I'll make it up to you when you're back."
"Yes, master." Because Wei He could read and figure accounts, Old Man Zheng had found him out and now sent him off on errands of this kind. Taking up the cloth bag for the medicines, he left by the small side door and followed the street towards another ward.
The ward where Zheng of Mountain Fist kept his yard stood close to the Inner City — a prosperous stretch of town by then. The apothecary he had to buy from, though, was in a ward nearer the Outer City.
Inside Feiye City there were more than a dozen wards, and each one was the equal of a village that fed itself: houses, a trading street, specialities of every kind, no two of them alike. All the wards ringed the great plaza at the centre, and every one of them had a way joining it to that plaza.
And the Inner City lay at the very heart of it, on the far side of the plaza. It was only just light. Wei He walked quickly down street after street; the shops and the stalls were all still shut, and of the few steamed-bun shops open only the wooden doors stood ajar, with a faint movement of people carrying things in.
Crossing a stone bridge between one ward and the next, he soon came to a medicine shop with an old, worn front: Zhao's Apothecary, the four characters on its hanging board. He was just about to knock when from the street on his right came a rustling, whispering sound.
"Rebirth in paradise — by fragrance ferried across the sea."
"Rebirth in paradise — by fragrance ferried across the sea…" Someone was muttering it low and over and over, in the manner of a leader; and at once a great mass of voices behind him took the chant up as well.
Wei He's heart clenched, and he knocked in haste. Thump, thump, thump. The shop went still; then the wooden door opened half way and a middle-aged man in a round black cap pulled him straight in.
"Get in here, quick! Those people of the Incense Gathering Sect are back!"
The shop was pitch dark, with only the cracks of the door and the windows letting in any light at all. Neither Wei He nor the middle-aged man spoke. They stood by the door and waited, waited on until the chanting outside — like so much scripture — drew slowly away and at last died out. Only then did the two of them breathe a little easier.
"These Incense Gathering people — they come more and more often lately. Who knows when there'll be an end to it." The middle-aged man let out a long breath.
"Manager Qian, do you know — those men just now…" Wei He had seen those chanting, mumbling processions before. Coming on them suddenly was frightening enough, but they had never seemed to make any trouble.
"That is the Incense Gathering Sect. It's only lately the sect has spread about the city. When they find a man who suits them, they make him support an incense head and pay out silver in offering. At the start they pulled in a good many poor folk and got themselves a following; then they took silver by something very like robbery, handed it round among their own people, and grew bigger and bigger from there. Why the authorities don't put a stop to it, I can't think."
