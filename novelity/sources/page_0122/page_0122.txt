White stones were scattered through the wooded hills; the slopes were steep and broken, the growth thick. The mountain was not high, but men came there rarely, and poisonous insects and fierce beasts were many — no ordinary old hunter would truly dare to force a way in at random. At the foot of Shaoyang Mountain just then, a middle-aged man wrapped in grey animal fur, with a tall, powerfully built young man in close-fitting
training clothes following behind him. One after the other the two came to the mouth of the way up and stood still. At the entrance lay a faint, uncertain little path, its surface overgrown with weeds. In all the heat of such a day, a thread of chill still seeped out of the deeps of that path.
"This is Shaoyang Mountain. All the hills and woods for a great stretch south of Feiye City are Shaoyang Mountain's ground. Bears in there, tigers, leopards, wild boar — everything there is. Whatever you want to hunt, you can hunt it." The middle-aged man explained. "Can you still meet the big fellows now?" the young man in training clothes behind him asked in a low voice. He wore dark green garments, several small pouches tied at his waist, a short knife strapped to his thigh, and a long cloth bag slung over his back.
It was none other than Wei He, who had prepared for many days. Ahead of him was the old hunter Ding Hai — the man who, of all those he had met these past days, brought back the most game. "The big fellows? That depends on luck. These days, hunting, you have to steer clear of the houses' earthen forts first. Neither the forts nor the ground around them with markers planted in it may be entered. Do it, and you are in trouble."
Ding Hai walked on ahead along the path and began to draw the chopping knife from his leg and cut down the weeds. "Step careful. Follow me. There are many poisonous insects in this place. The drought has run on lately, so there are a good few fewer than there were; in the old days no one dared come near this spot at all." Wei He nodded and followed, listening to Ding Hai talk up ahead as he went.
"I do not know why you insist on coming out to hunt on your own, but if you mean to get anything, first of all you must have patience. Carry dry rations, a water skin, and a pack that will not let water in, and medicine for wounds, the basic snakebite cure, a spider antidote, and so on. If you can, bring bait too." "Bait?"
"Mm. Something to give off a smell and draw the game in when you truly cannot find any." Ding Hai took a lump of dark brown, meat-like stuff, no bigger than half a palm, out of his bag. "Deodorising powder as well, and fierce-beast dung, and some rope." Ding Hai finished. He led Wei He on along the path, and before long they came to a stretch of withered yellow woodland with something deep and quiet about it.
Fallen yellowed leaves lay everywhere in that wood, spread in a thick carpet over the ground. The trees overhead were bare, with few leaves on them, and the air was filled with a dry smell. "No rain in this place for a long while. Trees and animals both have dried up and died. So only if your luck is good enough will you meet anything at all, and whether it is a big thing still rests on seizing the chance." Ding Hai said quietly.
"All right." Wei He watched him crouch down and search the ground for something, and before long he found, from under a big pine, a track left by some animal he could not name. "In a place like this, besides the fierce beasts you must be careful of, you must watch the men as well." Ding Hai stood up and, following that track, picked a direction,
and walked slowly away. Wei He followed behind, listening closely. "Watch which men?" "The men around you." Ding Hai smiled. "This Shaoyang Mountain is in two parts, an outer ring and an inner ring. In many places of the outer ring you will often meet
those experts who come out to hunt for sport. The moment you meet those men, run." He walked up to the trunk of a tree, fixed his bearings once more, and cut a mark into the trunk with his short knife. "There is something else. There is a forbidden zone here. If you mean to hunt this mountain, you must take good care never to come at dusk." "Why?" Wei He asked.
"Because at dusk, that is when the men of the Shaoyang Sect come out to hunt. Only… heh heh." Ding Hai laughed coldly a few times. "What they hunt is not animals. It is people." "!? People?" Wei He was taken aback. "How do you mean?"
