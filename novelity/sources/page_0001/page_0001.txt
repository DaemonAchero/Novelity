Synopsis: A wasteland at the end of days. In the third year of Shizong, the realm fell into chaos and the people could not live in peace.
The central court collapsed. Warlords carved out their own domains and the great sects stood alone. Demon sects and monstrous cults stirred in the shadows, gangs warred over turf, and the disorder knew no bounds.
Calamity followed calamity — drought, killing cold, torrential rain, plagues of insects — and the people struggled on, desperate for hope and salvation. Out of the chaos, sect after sect came into the open, fighting for resources and territory, each raising a dominion of its own. The ambitious dreamed of swallowing the realm whole and founding a dynasty; the righteous dreamed of saving the common folk and rebuilding their homes. Muscle, the martial way, immortal arts — one warrior after another reached the limits of the martial path, and legend hung on them titles that echoed buddhas and immortals.
Wei He carried the Realm Breaking Bead, a thing that could shatter the walls of cultivation. He would cross into a world in chaos, and step by step carve out a legend of his own.
== Chapter 1 · Chaotic World ==
The sky was faintly bright, and the vast expanse of heaven was cloudless.
Great Yuan, Yun Province, Feiye City.
Chirp!
A bird with white feathers and a black beak beat its wings across the land and swooped down.
It passed over the thick, ancient city walls; over the watchtowers. The bird slowed and settled lightly on the tallest of the dark-red towers in the city, and there it preened its feathers and looked down.
Below it, gray buildings stood in clusters, terraces and towers each of a different shape. Pale yellow houses and pavilions rose one behind another, and courtyards with upturned eaves and sweeping corners could be seen everywhere.
At the very center of the city lay a great square plaza.
The plaza was paved with stone slabs more than a meter wide, and the patterns of huge, strange beasts carved into them were startlingly clear. On ordinary days this was the open ground where the markets and fairs were held.
Today it was a sea of people — a crowd packed tight, all of them come to watch and to listen.
In the middle of the plaza, high up on a crude wooden platform, stood a Daoist priest in white robes, his hair like a crane's. He held a horsetail whisk and preached in a clear, ringing voice.
"A scholar of the highest learning, who recites this ten times over, shall have the Five Emperors attend him as guards, and the Three Realms bow their heads…"
"You scholars — your impure breaths are not yet cleansed, your bodies have not yet attained true insight…"
"Oh! Here it comes, here it comes!" someone whispered in the crowd.
Soon another Daoist came forward holding a wooden basin. He took out handfuls of copper coins, walked a stretch, stopped, then scooped up more and flung them down from the platform.
He threw only a handful, and still the crowd below bent and scrambled for every coin.
Wedged in among them, Wei He looked up from a distance at the old priest preaching scripture on the platform.
He was about one meter seventy, with a faint shadow on his lip and a young, ordinary face. He wore a short gray hemp shirt and a pair of black cloth shoes that were still more or less intact.
Watching the old priest on the wooden platform, he paused only a moment, then turned with the oil cakes he had bought in hand and headed for home.
Even as he pushed his way out through the crowd, he had to endure every foul smell that drifted off the bodies around him.
The stench of sweat, the stink of unwashed feet, the reek of spit — and even, faintly, of shit and piss.
He glanced down and saw a flattened heap of dog droppings underfoot to his right, and quickened his pace. He crossed the plaza, turned into a lane, and then threaded through several alleys heaped with yellow dust and garbage.
Very soon Wei He was back at the only home he had in this city.
It sat in a narrow alley: one room in a row of ramshackle single-story houses, with worn clothes hung out to dry outside the door — the faint difference in their colors the difference between a man's wear and a woman's.
