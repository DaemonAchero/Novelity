The two of them each set out their gear and made ready to begin skin grinding. "I heard about this trip. You have had a hard time of it." Cheng Shaojiu said low.
"It is nothing. Since I agreed to it, it is only what I should do." Wei He shook his head. He had taken the Cheng family's wages for so long; that an escort run should run into danger was something he had long expected in his heart.
"In any case, when you are out and about, be careful in all things." Cheng Shaojiu sighed. "In fact I have long wanted to go out on an escort run myself, but my father will not allow it. Tell me — this bitter training day after day, what use is it really?"
"…" Wei He had no answer. After a silence he thought back to the monster in the dark they had met, and spoke up low.
"Does Senior Brother know what that Black Madman we ran into on the escort was?"
"Black Madman is a general name for the fierce beasts that hunt at night. I asked my father about the two men who died that day; he did not know either. It seems to be a kind of beast that has not appeared before." Cheng Shaojiu shook his head. Wei He fell silent at that. He had thought the country outside the city not so dangerous as people made out, and yet one run, and two men were gone.
Cheng Shaojiu lowered his head and hammered away hard at the sand basin. After a good while he suddenly looked up. "Xiao He, how old are you?"
"Seventeen…" Wei He did not see what he was driving at, but answered.
"Seventeen… how would it be if I found you a good girl?" The words popped out of Cheng Shaojiu all at once.
"…" Wei He could hardly follow the turn his thoughts had taken.
"Just do as I say and leave it to me. I promise I will find you one who is absolutely virtuous and gentle!" Cheng Shaojiu said, patting his chest. These days many a man of seventeen or eighteen was taking a wife and begetting sons; a fellow Wei He's age had come due for it.
Only, he had been busy with his training all along and had no such plan. "…" Wei He had no answer. This Senior Brother Cheng had seemed lately to have a great clutter of thoughts in him, and that was not good; it would not serve his progress in the martial way. After that the two of them spoke no more and gave themselves to their own training. Wei He trained a while, and under cover of wiping his sweat he pulled his collar open and looked at the Realm Breaking Bead pattern on his chest.
It had grown another stretch. Coming back from this half-month-long run, it had gone from the half-progress of before to more than half — roughly a quarter more. He showed nothing and went back to his bitter training. The use of the Realm Breaking Bead, according to the information in his head, required that he himself first come up against a barrier; only then could the Bead break it open in one stroke.
So he too must grind and grow the qi and blood of the Ox-Hide stage to its utmost. When he reached the utmost of Ox-Hide qi and blood, that would be the day he could use the Bead to break through. For now he settled his mind and turned his attention back to skin grinding. To grow qi and blood alone was not enough; at the same time this vessel of his body had to be enlarged as well, before it could hold more qi and blood.
That was what the skin-grinding drill was for. Only, his natural gifts were limited — far below a talent like Xiao Ran's — and he could only accumulate and press forward a little at a time. So time slipped slowly by. Wei He and his second sister moved into the new house, and he invited Third Senior Brother and Li Jue over for a meal, by way of showing them the way to the place.
After that nothing unexpected happened. He rose early and came home late every day, training bitterly at the martial arts and polishing his own body. The days flowed past one after another, like the drops from the bamboo clock in the yard. As for Cheng Shaojiu's talk of introducing him to a girl, Wei He gave it no thought at all. Just now he had no time even to change his own way of living; how could he have a mind to dwell on such things?
And then there was the world as it was. In the streets prices changed from one day to the next and grew more chaotic; it was said that a great flood of privately minted coin had come in, making silver worth less and less. By degrees many shops and stalls stopped taking silver at all, and began to use cloth and meat and rice as the measure of trade. And with prices risen so high, when you took out silver — if the sum was a little larger — you might have to carry a whole sack of silver out of doors. It was
spurned by more and more people. Thinking back to how Wei Ying had worked so hard at the beginning and earned only a few dozen taels, he was sure she had come out the loser; so Wei He told her that in future, when she took in work, she was not to be paid in silver — she was to ask for rice and meat only.
