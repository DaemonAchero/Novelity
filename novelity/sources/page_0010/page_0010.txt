Wei He smiled inwardly. Men who trained their bodies had strong qi and blood and strong male vigor; when they met a pretty woman, this was what always happened.
He paid it no mind, and led his somewhat stiff, frightened sister inside.
"Third Senior Brother, I'll go and greet the master first. We can talk after," he said earnestly.
"Yes, yes!" Cheng Shaojiu answered with a grin.
The moment Wei Ying stepped in, no small number of the men training their strength in the yard turned their eyes her way.
Wei He brought her along to where the old master, Zheng, stood, and bowed low.
"Master Zheng, I've brought my sister. If there's any work to be given out, she can do it," Wei He said.
Old Man Zheng looked at Wei Ying. There was nothing odd in his eyes; she seemed honest and dutiful enough, so he nodded.
"Let's be clear on one thing first — the wages. You two decided to do without them, is that it?"
"Yes, that was us," Wei He put in quickly.
"Then that's settled. From today you train the fist here, and your sister cleans, cooks, and washes. Any problem with that? Your tuition only covers a year, though — after that it has to be paid again, understand?"
Old Man Zheng's small eyes swept Wei He up and down, and his voice was cold.
"Your disciple understands." Wei He nodded.
"Of course, if your aptitude is decent and you've got the hang of everything I teach you, I'll keep teaching you the next stage as well. Anything within the year is fine."
Old Man Zheng waved his hand, and a middle-aged woman he employed came over to show Wei Ying the grounds and the work.
Then he stood and walked a circle around Wei He where he stood.
"Bear it."
Before the words were out of his mouth, his hand reached out and pinched — right on one of Wei He's ribs under the armpit.
Nnh!
Wei He went numb all over; half his body nearly lost all feeling.
"Not bad." Old Man Zheng nodded. "Aptitude neither good nor bad. A bit old for it, but the early stages can still be trained in."
"Yes." Wei He answered at once. "Whatever the master decides."
Old Man Zheng saw that his manner had been good from beginning to end, and that he had brought a free pair of working hands along with him, and his mood improved a little.
"Very well. It's your first day, so I'll tell you what martial training really trains — and why we're stronger than ordinary people, why we can fight."
Wei He at once put on the look of a man listening with both ears.
Perhaps because Wei He could read, Old Man Zheng paced a round, went back to his chair, lifted his teacup and took a sip.
A few of the others, who seemed to be as new as Wei He, drifted closer with interest on their faces.
Old Man Zheng cleared his throat and coughed a couple of times, mouth open — then said: "My mouth's a bit dry. Go get me some tea snacks."
== Chapter 4 · Stability (Part 2) ==
"I'll go!" A bright little man beside him darted into the inner room, and in no time came back with a wooden box divided into many small compartments, each holding a different kind of snack — chicken feet, melon seeds, grapes, chestnuts, malt candy and the like.
In a season and a place like this, such things were still to be had. Old Man Zheng's life was comfortable, to put it mildly. He picked up a chicken foot and gnawed at the pad of the claw.
"A bit salty."
"All right, let me tell you the reason properly." He took his time, set the chicken foot down, licked his lips. "The reason, now, is…"
He looked around at the ring of faces waiting on him, eyes shining.
"What? Nobody's going to train? Think you can grow strength just by listening to me talk? Every one of you's a proper genius now, is that it?"
"Go on — go on, all of you, back to it! Work hard day and night, and when the day comes to use it, you'll have no regrets!"
