His going up Shaoyang Mountain again came of word he had picked up at the wine house: they said strange beasts were now about on Shaoyang Mountain, and that men of the Shaoyang Sect were often to be seen up there, driving them in. Strange beasts. The name stuck fast in Wei He's mind the moment he heard it.
He changed his get-up — no longer the masked, covered-to-the-eyes look of before — got all his gear and kit ready, and went up the mountain again. True, he had killed a man of the Shaoyang Sect; but after all these days, who would know it was he? Who? At the time there had been nothing but weeds and woods about, and no one had seen a thing.
Nor were there any but that band of mountain bandits who had ever come across him — and they were dead. So on paper he was still an ordinary hunter. After half an hour of moving quietly up the mountain, all at once a man's head appeared ahead of him.
A sword-carrying mountain bandit with a grey-white headcloth was squatting beside a corpse on the ground, as if searching it. Wei He checked his step; he knew that headcloth mark. He had killed no few bandits of late and understood well how these men were sorted: all who bound a white headcloth were under the Shaoyang Sect's hand.
So this man before him should be one of the same. He thought it over and decided not to act rashly, and began slowly, silently to back away. If there were watchers about anywhere, it would be trouble. He had backed only two steps, though, when his eye swept past and caught on the bulging money pouch at the bandit's back.
The shape of that pouch stood out, and however he looked at it, it looked like gold ingots… "Take that!" A flung stone cracked hard against the white-headclothed bandit's head. Wei He threw out a fistful of quicklime, and while the lime drifted and spread it screened the whole spot.
He rolled and burst out, and with a hand grown extremely practised ran his fingers over the bandit's body. In an instant the money pouch and a scatter of odds and ends were all in his hands. Before the white powder had come down he darted again, slipped into the grass on the other side and vanished. The whole thing took under two seconds.
When the white powder settled, a bandit on watch leapt out in a fury and saw his companion lying on the ground with blood all over his head. He raised the blade in his hand to shout. Thud!
Another dull thud: a stone struck this bandit on the brow as well and he went down on the spot. Another handful of quicklime went up, and two seconds later the man had been swept clean, money pouch, blade and all. Wei He, content with what he had, made his decision on the instant and went down the mountain. On the way he did nothing but marvel at how rich the bandits on Shaoyang Mountain were — and note that he had come away with two extra bags of quicklime besides.
It seemed that from no long while back the bandits had begun carrying bags of quicklime more and more. Lately he had got to the point of not needing to buy any himself: he simply lifted them all off bandits. Sometimes his spending could not keep pace with what he took in. Of the two money pouches, one held five taels of gold; the other had only some gold leaves and gold beans. A pauper, that one too.
One other thing, though, was a pleasant surprise for Wei He: a small pouch woven of copper wire, extremely fine in its workmanship, holding a heap of black things like stones whose nature he did not know. Wei He recognised them — one of the hidden weapons hardest to come by, called the black caltrop. Once thrown, it burst of itself and gave off a great cloud of poison smoke. A formidable thing.
That poison smoke could hurt a man's eyes and ears and leave him briefly blind, and it screened one's own figure at the same time. Made ready properly beforehand, with protection seen to in advance, a pouch of the things could save a life at the crucial moment. He put them away carefully.
