Wei He stopped and let them pass in front of him. On the carts they pushed lay two bodies askew, their faces blue-green: a mother and daughter, one large and one small. They looked alike, both worn away to skin and bone, past any human likeness; it was plain at a glance that they had starved to death. Wei He waited until the carts were far off before going on.
He was dressed now in the fine uniform of Mountain Fist, his hair cropped to a crew cut, a metre eighty tall and thick with muscle. Especially since breaking through in qi and blood, his eyes were bright and full of spirit; one look and you knew he trained the fists regularly, and he cut a formidable figure. The occasional passer-by kept his head down and did not dare to look at him long. Wei He walked on, his feet on the cracked flagstones, saying nothing.
Now and again, at a street corner, he would see incense heads stuck in the earth. The sticks had long since burnt out, and on the ground before them specks of blood were left behind, sometimes more, sometimes less; whether it was human blood, there was no telling. Wei He quickened his steps without meaning to. In this Feiye City, apart from the hours before noon when some human warmth remained, the rest of the time it was like a land of ghosts.
In daylight the constables still patrolled now and then; come night, they paid no heed to anything. Wei He walked another stretch toward home, and from far off saw Rat Alley before his door. At some point two thuggish men had appeared there, lurking furtively at the mouth of the alley. When they saw him coming near, the two thugs ducked their heads and hurried off to one side.
Wei He had nothing to say. Kill one Chen Biao and there is a second and a third Chen Biao. This world of ours, truly… He sighed inwardly, went through Rat Alley, and came to his own door. He had barely sat down inside when someone knocked.
Wei He got up and opened the door. Outside stood a middle-aged man in a black mandarin jacket with a pockmarked face. "Are you Wei He, Brother Wei? My name is Liu Yu. I am of the Three Tigers Gang." At the sound of it Wei He knew a recruiter had come. He invited the man in to sit and asked his price. If he could earn more easily on the strength of what he could do, joining some power or other was acceptable enough.
Only the Three Tigers Gang was a newly risen gang from nearby, and on inquiry their line was brothels; their price was a shade better than Senior Brother Cheng's and no more, and there was no Silver-Kissed Black Snake meat, which was the point. So Wei He politely declined. Liu Yu had no sooner gone than men from the Changfeng Escort Agency arrived. Changfeng was a big agency whose shopfronts could be seen all about the district,
and their price was not even as good as the Three Tigers Gang's, so they were declined as well. Then the Zhang family, a rich household nearby, invited him to be their chief guard. The price was good, a little above the Cheng family's, but without the Silver-Kissed Black Snake meat; counting it up one way and the other, Wei He still refused. After that came two more gangs, another rich household, and an eating house, all of them asking him to take some small post as a leader of men.
Comparing them one by one, Wei He did feel that Senior Brother Cheng's price was not low; and that Silver-Kissed Black Snake meat, if it could be supplied without limit, might be only a middling attraction to other men, but to him it was a great lure indeed. Since getting the Realm Breaking Bead his digestion was stronger than other men's: no matter how hard a thing was to digest, he could break it down quickly and turn it
into heat and energy for the bead. That snake meat, which filled an ordinary man for two days on one catty, was no loss at all in his case. Wei He was confident that on the meat alone he would eat back everything he cost them. Most likely the reason Senior Brother Cheng's family dared to supply it without limit was that the meat staved off hunger so mightily that an ordinary man, even a man training the fists,
could not eat much of it even with a free belly. But they could never imagine that in this world there was a man like Wei He. His decision made, Wei He rose before dawn the next morning, drew water and washed, and hurried off toward the Mountain Fist yard. When he got there he was not even the earliest; men had already begun skin grinding.
Only, the news that he had broken through at the very last moment and entered the Ox-Hide level seemed to have gone all round. The newcomers who had just got in looked at him mostly with awe; and the senior brothers who had got through before him and had never deigned to look his way plainly set more store by him now.
