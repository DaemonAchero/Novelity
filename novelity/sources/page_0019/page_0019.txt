"I really do have things to do," Wei He said, shaking his head.
"Go on then, what things? If it's a big thing I'm no use, but if it's small, I'll take it on for you!" Cheng Shaojiu waved a great hand and gave his word.
"It's nothing much, really." Wei He smiled. "I thought I'd go and take a turn round Nanshan Ward."
"Uh…" Cheng Shaojiu was struck dumb.
He had only just handed over the news about Nanshan Ward, and now this fellow meant to go there. What his purpose was needed no saying. Had he not asked him to keep watch on Chen Biao's movements? And now, the moment Chen Biao was hurt, off he went to Nanshan Ward…
For the first time, Cheng Shaojiu looked his junior brother Wei up and down, carefully, from head to foot. He had always taken him for his own little hanger-on. Wei He was silent and spoke little, kept a close mouth, never gossiped idly,
and never talked behind anyone's back. Which was why, whenever Cheng Shaojiu had a complaint to make, he went looking for Wei He to talk at. All this time his impression of Wei He had been: silent, stubborn, and careful to keep to his own place. And then, just now, in that one stroke.
Cheng Shaojiu's view of his junior brother Wei had somehow gained a strange new edge. For a while neither of them spoke; they went back to trading moves in silence. There was plainly a wariness in Cheng Shaojiu now, a wariness of Wei He. A junior brother who had always been harmless showing a flash of claw — his mind could not get used to it, could not turn the corner.
And Wei He was silent, as he always was. Silent in his training, silent as he traded moves, silent as he felt his way carefully into how force should be issued and how a move should be answered. Once attack and defence were done with, it would be time to grind the fists in earnest: Ox-Hide, Stone-Hide, Iron-Hide — the three stages of Mountain Fist, and the root of its loud name in the world.
The ground it stood on. The sky was darkening; soon the time came to finish for the day. Cheng Shaojiu wiped his sweat with the towel round his neck and looked at Wei He, who was changing his clothes. He opened his mouth, seemed about to speak, and stopped.
He had trained at Mountain Fist a long while, but a man like Wei He he had never met. That ferocity buried under silence was like a venomous snake in a dark corner, waiting quietly, biding its time — and then the sudden strike. Other men, most of them, were all on the surface; they said a thing and did it.
Cheng Shaojiu pulled himself back to the moment — but he had been away only that little while, and Wei He's figure was already gone from before him.
== Chapter 7 · Checkpoint (Part 1) ==
Nanshan Ward. In this ward, lying south of the city, shops and stalls selling cloth and woven goods stood wherever you looked. But most of them were shut and barred, and the noise and bustle of former days were long gone. These last years the imperial court's taxes had grown heavier and heavier, the levies on trade heavier still; of the old magnificence only a trace or two was left.
Wei He went along the edge of the street, his head down, most of his head and face wrapped in cloth, his steps quick. He had changed into the plainest grey hemp, the dress of any man in the crowd. Nor had he relied on Third Senior Brother Cheng Shaojiu alone for word of Chen Biao and his two; after tailing them carefully a few times he had the addresses of all three.
Ducking under a broken bamboo pole hung with washing, he soon came to a low, broken-down little yard. Its gate stood half open, and just then a woman came out with red eyes, carrying a packed bundle and leading a child only a few years old. The woman had some looks to her, but the tear tracks on her face and the swollen redness of her eyes showed the state she was in.
With the child in hand, the woman glanced at Wei He's get-up, put her head down, left the gate open and went off quickly. Wei He knew her. This was Chen Biao's wife, Shan Ju; before her marriage she had been the best-known widow in that quarter of the ward.
