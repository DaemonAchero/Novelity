Understanding broke over Wei He. Grateful, he bowed low to Cheng Shaojiu with his hands together. "Thank you for the guidance, Senior Brother!"

== Chapter 16 · Real Combat (Part 2) ==
After that Cheng Shaojiu crossed hands with him on the spot, and the two of them began to work out how to raise their odds of winning in every kind of situation. When your own strength and speed stood about equal to your enemy's, the quickest way was to catch him off guard: to strike suddenly at him in ways no one would think of. That was what was called the secret hand.
The two of them studied it out and found that with no outside help of any kind, the best method was to break up the sequence of moves and mix false with true. False and true, true and false; by the way force was applied, a man could leave his opponent unable to tell where the attack was really centred. With this, Wei He felt all at once that a true door into real fighting had opened. In his mind real fighting was no longer only this pattern: throw a move, break a move, and whoever wears out first loses.
It was catching people off guard, sneak attacks, sneak attacks, and more sneak attacks! Both men threw their thinking open and held nothing back, trading the best ways to handle things in a real fight. Cheng Shaojiu's qi and blood were stronger — he had already reached the Stone-Hide level — and the elders of his house were forever drumming things into him, so his breadth of view and his experience were stronger still than his qi and blood.
In these exchanges, a great deal of Wei He's gaps and crudeness in the details were made good. And Wei He's modern way of thinking, his logical cast of mind, gave Cheng Shaojiu no few novel lines of thought and odd notions of his own. The two of them truly helped each other and made each other better. It was nearly dark before Wei He tore himself away and went home.
The next day they carried on the exchanges. The third day, the fourth. As the days went by, both men's experience and thinking in real fighting grew stronger and stronger. In their drills, when they paired up with the other senior and junior brothers, the difference showed more and more plainly.
The plainest sign was that both men's standing in the yard's fighting crept upward. Wei He was all right — there were more than ten disciples at the Ox-Hide level, and on top of that a genius like Xiao Ran, so his showing drew no great notice. But Cheng Shaojiu, at the Stone-Hide level, was not as he had been; he began to stand out, and several times when he crossed hands with Eldest Senior Brother Zhao Hong, both of them at Stone-Hide, he very nearly nicked a few moves off him.
Odd tricks and strange tricks they were, but they won him no small attention. Because of that, Cheng Shaojiu's standing in the yard rose another invisible step, and people thought more of him. And Wei He, who had been close to Cheng Shaojiu all along, rose with the tide, and drew a little envy from no few of the others along with him. Time slipped by, and another month was gone.
Wei He trained bitterly day and night, and thought, and gradually formed a style of striking that was his own. He would not claim that it gave him the upper hand in a real fight, but he would certainly never again be like before — facing someone weaker than himself, in poor condition, and still unable to bring his strength out. And there was another idea, too, that came to Wei He while he talked with Cheng Shaojiu, one he never put into words.

He had not been inside long, and had only just set the basket down off his back, when he heard footsteps coming up behind him. Someone had come into the shop.
