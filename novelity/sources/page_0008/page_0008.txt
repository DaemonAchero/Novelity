In the shade of a tree, a gaunt old man with a goatee sat holding a teacup, sipping slowly, entirely at ease.
"Young Wei? What is it — saved up the money?" The old man glanced at Wei He.
"It's enough, Elder Zheng."
Wei He had come here many times before. His manner was earnest and focused, and he had even run a few errands for the old man without being asked, which had left a good impression.
A good impression, though, was one thing; learning the fist here cost money and would never be free, and so nothing had come of it. Now that the money was in hand, the old man was willing enough.
"Good that it's enough. For the poor, the only way up is fist and leg — and it can only be fist and leg. Why? Because weapons cost too much, and palm and finger arts don't kill the way a fist does." Old Man Zheng stroked his beard, speaking lightly.
"Train the fist, and you change your life. Train it well, and you have a chance to change your fate!"
"The master speaks the truth!" several men at the side hurried to flatter him.
Wei He said nothing. He took the money pouch from his clothes at once, cupped it in both hands, bowed, and presented it.
……………… Dusk.
Wei He wore a brand-new gray cloth training jacket and gray trousers, with a pair of thousand-layer cloth shoes on his feet. On his chest, brightly stitched, was the character Zheng. This was the uniform of Old Man Zheng's disciple students, and nothing else.
The last red sliver of the setting sun faded away at the mouth of the alley.
Wei He walked quickly for home. His face was calm, but his heart was uneasy — Wei Ying was at the house alone, and what he feared was that something had happened to her. He was not the original owner of this body, and his feelings for this family were not deep; but they were family all the same, and they had lived together this long.
He crossed a street, turned through a few alleys, and soon came back to the mouth of his own alley.
Three men were standing there, hunched over their long pipes. The tallest and heaviest of them had a buzz cut and a face full of muscle, and his eyes were fixed straight on the Wei house — who knew how long he had been standing that way. It was Chen Biao. The other two were big and heavy too, plainly well fed; strong men, not worth provoking.
The sound of Wei He's footsteps brought the three of them out of their watching. Chen Biao turned, saw Wei He in the Zheng training uniform, and his face showed surprise. His eyes went deep over the character Zheng on Wei He's chest. He took the pipe from his mouth, glanced at Wei He once, then turned and walked off without a word. His two men hurried after him.
Both of them saw the character on Wei He's chest as they went. Clearly they had recognized what he had become. Old Man Zheng of Mountain Fist was not a nobody in this part of the city; he was a force not to be taken lightly.
Wei He watched them go calmly. Against all three at once he would have had no chance at all.
But it was different now — with this tiger's skin on his back, Chen Biao and his two would probably not dare to make a move for the moment.
Then again, Chen Biao's eye on Second Sister Wei Ying went back a long way, and Wei He had no idea how long this tiger's skin would hold them off.
And as for Old Man Zheng — he took anyone who paid, so asking him for help would be a fool's errand. It was a pure money-for-teaching arrangement; nothing else need be imagined.
And if Big Sister stayed away much longer, once her shadow was gone from the alley, Chen Biao and his two would certainly get ideas again — that much was certain.
"So I still have to train hard, and rely on myself."
He walked quickly to his own door, took out the key, opened it, and went in. Wei Ying was huddled in a corner, not daring to move.
Only when she saw that it was him did she stand up, eyes brimming, and come close.
"Xiao He… I nearly — I nearly thought I'd never see you again!" She burst into sobs.
"It's all right. I'm back. It's all right." Wei He comforted her, low and steady.
