Ten liang… Wei He's heart clenched. That was dear; the money he carried would not buy much of this meat.
"It's fresh snake meat, this. I've wild boar too, and wild ox, and yellow-haired monkey. Golden coin worms, centipedes, scorpions…" The woman rattled off a long list of this meat and that, one after another.
Some were dried, some fresh. Wei He thought it over, then bought a little of every kind, and together they came to a sizeable heap. He spent, in one breath, every coin he had saved all this while.
When he came out he had a bundle the size of a man's head in his hand, packed full of dried meat and chunks of meat of every sort. With the lot of it he went back to the house off Rat Alley, and there, alone, set about working out how each was to be cooked.
Second Sister Wei Ying was living long-term now in the room next to the Mountain Fist yard; here in the old home it was only himself, and quiet enough.
After a good deal of tinkering, Wei He made a paste of every one of them, boiled it, and stored each in a different bamboo tube used for a bowl — twelve kinds in all. Then he began trying them, a little at a time, watching the Realm Breaking Bead on his chest to judge which meat gave the best return for its price, and by that to quicken the filling of the bead.
Two portions a day, and a record kept of each. Before long Wei He had found the one that, weight for weight, moved the bead along fastest: golden coin worm meat. This meat held off hunger exceedingly well and was packed tight with energy, and it went as a rule to patients who needed heavy nourishment. Nor was it dear — a little more than the snake meat, and by the standards of the day still a fair price.
Once the golden coin worm was settled on, he went back to his days of training strength and nothing else. And all at once time had flown: half a year was gone. The bead's store, built in fits and starts over all that while, was a hair's breadth from turning black the whole way through. And Wei He had grown from five foot seven to a shade under five foot eleven, his body drawn many times firmer.
The muscle lay on him in long clean lines, one slab running into the next. He was past the strength-tempering stage at last and had begun to train moves against a partner in earnest — one of the strong men of that yard who did nothing all day but knock each other about. And now that he had come this far, Old Man Zheng began at last to teach him a method for grinding skin and flesh, one that belonged to Mountain Fist and to no other school, that could grind
the skin of a fist into a thick, tough, wholly special state.

"The fighting way, the fighting way — it is the way of putting another man down. The key is to strike the vital points. The movement must be quick, the force full, the aim true." In the yard Old Man Zheng strolled among the pairs of men who trained against one another, hands clasped behind his back, muttering the keys of Mountain Fist from time to time.
It was just past noon. Cicadas shrilled from every tree, and even so could not drown out the shouting of the men in the Mountain Fist yard.
Wei He stood face to face with Third Senior Brother Cheng Shaojiu, the old man's words coming to his ear one after another.
"Mountain Fist has its basic defensive moves, attacking moves and killing moves. You will train the defensive moves first, and they are two parts: blocking, and getting out of the way."
"Blocking is worse than dodging, and dodging is worse than striking first. Remember that saying, always. When you move, think first of how to finish your enemy; finish him, and you have no need of guarding or dodging at all."
"The defensive move is the simplest of them, but its variations are many. Both hands up and clenched; the head lowered, the chin tucked; one fist a little above the other; guarding the cheeks; the elbows set as props…"
The more Wei He listened, the more familiar it seemed. When he had taken up each point of the description in turn and looked across at his third senior brother, he found himself speechless. Was this not the very stance of the fighters he had watched in the free-combat bouts of his last life?
