Even so, that little meat would not keep him going long. Holding the jerky, Wei He bit through it in a few bites, chewed it up and swallowed it down with water. His powerful digestion soon had his belly warm and comfortable. Once he had eaten his fixed portion, Wei He rose and went into his own room; practised at it, he took the bundle he had brought back from the hidden compartment under the clothes chest.
It was a bundle wrapped in a brown outer garment, its surface dusted with a great deal of pale yellow powder. The moment it came out, the pale yellow powder gave off a thick, strange smell. "By my count, two months, sixty days, is one cycle of breakdown powder; the poison on this should be gone by now."
Wei He made a rough reckoning, then carefully put on his gloves, carried the bundle out and set it in a wooden basin he had prepared beforehand. This bundle was the spoil he had fished off Chen Jun the Severing Spear. Chen Jun was all over poison; when Wei He searched the body he had found that before their fight the man had already taken two hidden weapons — a poisoned needle and a poisoned dart — and the wounds stank and festered.
And there was a pungent smell from his back besides, probably another kind of violent poison. Add the mixed poison powder Wei He had scattered on him, and the man was simply made of poison. So he had quickly picked the things off him through his gloves, sealed them into a leather bundle, and on the way back rinsed it once with rainwater.
After carrying it home, he had followed the simple all-purpose detoxifying method Guan Ye had explained: grind the bark of the iron-ash tree to powder and smear it evenly inside and outside the bundle. This powder served as a breakdown powder: over long action it gradually broke down the great majority of toxins, denaturing them and lowering their toxicity.
Wei He carefully shook everything out of the bundle, then, carrying the basin, went back out the side door to the Feiye River and scooped water over them again and again. After all that fuss he brought the cleaned assortment of things back to his room, and only once they were clean began to examine just what Chen Jun the Severing Spear had left behind.
With chopsticks Wei He picked the things out one by one and laid them on the ground to one side: a black money pouch with silver edging, a booklet that looked like an account book, a dark-green jade pendant, a pile of little bottles of wood wrapping porcelain in all sizes, and three discoloured gas masks. Wei He picked up the masks first and examined them closely.
It was clear these were things Chen Jun had prepared in advance to deal with the Guan family, but some poison seemed to have spoiled them. On the mouth-and-nose part of the mask, where it should have been white, there was now pale green at the centre and grey-white around it. Elsewhere there were blue blotches, one patch here and one there, all of different shades. The three masks had their blotches in different places, and one could well imagine what a bitter fight this old senior had been through to press the three Guan family people to the wall.
Wei He sighed and tossed the masks aside. Then came the pile of little bottles. Wood wrapped the outside, porcelain within, and on the outside of each a name was written: Viper Poison Pill, Spider Poison Pill, Grass Poison Pill, Poison-Settling Pill and so on.
There were a full dozen or so little bottles, and most of them were empty. Some had lost even their stoppers, which gave a fair idea of how urgent things had been at the time. Chen Jun must have been throwing the stoppers away and tipping them into his mouth by the handful. Wei He shook his head slightly, threw all the bottles aside, and looked at what was left.
What remained was the jade pendant, the account book and the money pouch. The pendant was cool in the hand and looked to be worth something, but in this age there was not much use for it. Not as good as a chunk of strange-beast meat. He set it to one side and picked up the account book. He opened it slowly: the writing inside had been soaked through with water and nothing could be made out; useless as well.
Wordlessly he threw it straight away. The last was the money pouch. That was the one that mattered.
