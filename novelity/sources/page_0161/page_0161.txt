Wei He looked at him and at Jiang Su. He had run into the two of them on the way home, as it happened, and had seen that something was wrong with Jiang Su. But he had no liking for either of them, and had not meant to meddle. The trouble was that Xiao Ran had not gone far off, nor gone somewhere near — he had to pick this spot, of all spots, to do what he meant to do.
"Junior Brother Xiao… what is it you mean to do, there in the riverside grass at my gate?" Wei He said, word by careful word. Xiao Ran: "…" Wei He: "…" The two faced each other without a word.
== Chapter 54 · Night (Part 2) ==
"I was in fact planning to come and take a… leak…" Xiao Ran could not get the words out himself…
To come to a man's gate to take a leak… Xiao Ran knew now that the sweet thing was not to be, and with Jiang Su on his arm made to shift to another spot. But as he would go, Wei He sighed. It would have been one thing not to step out at all; but he had stepped out, and walked straight into it — and after all they were disciples of the same house.
Others might not see what was going on; but he had robbed so much powder off bandits that the powders of every kind were piling up at home — how could he not know what Jiang Su had been given? He had tried every one of those powders on bandits, kept detailed notes of all their effects, and remembered the particular marks of each: all so that he might match an antidote to every one, and never be caught unable to purge a poison quickly, should he take one by mistake.
So now at a glance he saw it: these were the signs of the pleasure powder. "Junior Brother Xiao, best leave the person here. It's late, and it isn't safe to be walking her about. My elder sister is at home; she can help Senior Sister Jiang sober up." In the end Wei He felt himself too soft-hearted, and spoke up to hold him back.
He felt he was still not hard or ruthless enough — his heart was too soft. It was the same as when he went to the Cheng house: he had thrown out even his gold beans along with the rest, and left himself without money for vegetables. So it was now. This sort of impulse was no good in the end; how much trouble would it drag down on him?
Xiao Ran started at that and stopped in his tracks. "Wei He, what do you mean by that?" A hot wind blew past; by the river at night there was not a breath of chill — it was close and stifling instead. The two stood no more than ten metres apart, yet Xiao Ran felt a sweat creeping out on his back.
"What do I mean? Shall I say the name of that powder, Junior Brother Xiao?" Wei He's face was calm. "If we really tear each other's face off, it does no one any good." He meant to use this to hold over Xiao Ran. It was convenient, too: he had heard that Xiao Ran had no small store of medicines that greatly tonify qi and blood, and with the Cheng family's supply gone, his own qi and blood had slowed in gathering.
These days everything he looked at smacked a little of meat. Wei He's mind turned fast, hunting for the way to get the most out of this. A pity that he was too innocent and kind-hearted, and had never done such a thing before, so that after thinking a while all he had was a thin, simple plan.
Evidence: he had to produce evidence that would make Master Zheng believe there was something wrong with Xiao Ran — that he had drugged a fellow disciple. Wei He turned it over. What evidence, then, once produced, could prove Xiao Ran had drugged Jiang Su? There were no cameras or recording pens in this day and age, no way to leave material proof. And witnesses — he had no idea where to find one.
His mind raced on. Unless he caught the man with the goods in hand and let Master Zheng see it with his own eyes, he truly had no way to nail the matter down. 'But if I nail it down, how do I threaten Xiao Ran then?'
