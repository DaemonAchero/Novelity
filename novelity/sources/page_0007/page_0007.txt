The three of them talked among themselves in low voices, and all the while their eyes kept sliding over the men and women passing by. When they caught a pretty face, or a woman with a good figure, they stared hard — as if memorizing her.
Wei He's heart tightened. He had every coin he owned against his belly now; nothing could be allowed to go wrong.
The more nervous he felt, the harder he pretended there was nothing to be nervous about. He kept walking, and soon came level with the stretch of road where the three of them stood.
If Chen Biao had not already seen him, the smart thing would have been to take another way around. But he had been seen, and to dodge now would look deliberate — suspicious — and that might land him in worse trouble.
Natural was safest. Wei He kept his stride steady and made to walk past them.
"Hold on."
Chen Biao's arm came out and blocked him neatly.
"Well, well, Xiao He. What's this — off to the inner city again? Every day the inner city; never looking for work in our own ward. Found yourself a rich house to hang on to?" He smiled a sly, sneering smile. His voice was shrill, with none of the heft of a big man — more like a rooster's.
"What's it to you? My big sister found me the work." Wei He answered with his usual cold flatness.
"Heh, heh — big sister. Right, right, your big sister's a good hand in the Black Water Gang. Fine, fine, I won't stop you." Chen Biao laughed a couple of times and pulled his arm back. Wei He walked on.
"Hold on."
Chen Biao was at it again. His two small eyes swept over Wei He and caught on the bulge in his clothes.
"Little brother Xiao He… your clothes look a bit thick today. Hiding something in there?"
"I work at the worksite every day. Work makes a man hungry. I bring a couple of dry cakes with me. What, is that not allowed?" Wei He pulled his coat open, dug out a dry cake and gave it a shake.
"Allowed. Of course it's allowed." Chen Biao pulled his hand back, all smiles, and said no more. "Go on, go on. And remember me to your second sister — I've been fond of her for a long while now."
"…"
Wei He said nothing, kept his face cold, quickened his step and moved off fast.
He walked at a hard pace for more than a li before he let out a heavy breath.

"Good… He didn't find it."
Chen Biao was a scoundrel and a thug by nature; if he had spotted the money pouch at Wei He's belly — that much money — greed would have done the rest.
Wei He wove his way across the city, through one street after another, and came to a stretch of single-story houses near the inner city. From one of the courtyards came a constant din of men shouting as they trained.
A plaque hung over the courtyard gate. On it: Mountain Fist — Zheng.
Thump, thump, thump. Wei He stepped up and knocked.
After a wait the wooden door was pulled open from inside, and a face came into view, heavy with muscle.
"Who is it? What do you want?"
"I've come to learn the fist." Wei He's voice was steady.
"Got money?" The face looked him up and down.
"I do."
"Come in, then."
The door swung wide and Wei He stepped in. In the courtyard beyond, a dozen men with bare torsos were locked around chunks of rock, working at their strength without pause.
