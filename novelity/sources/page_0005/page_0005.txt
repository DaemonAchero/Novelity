Wei He pulled on an outer coat. It was patched all over, but it would keep the cold off well enough.
He pushed the wooden door open, and at once saw the tall, heavy-built men standing at the mouth of the alley to the right. Three of them, in short gray work clothes with their arms bare, dark skin over hard slabs of muscle.
He knew these three. Chen Biao — the best-known thug in this neighborhood — and his two hangers-on.
Chen Biao did no work at all. He drifted around all day with nothing to do, and no one knew where his money came from or how he stayed so solid. But Wei He had heard the guess people made: that Chen Biao was tied to the Robber Gang, and that he helped them snatch people — quietly, on the sly — to be sold off. Children, single women, even good-looking men.
None of that mattered on its own. What mattered was that Chen Biao had once laid eyes on Wei He's second sister, Wei Ying, and after that he had begun hovering around the neighborhood. Then he had a run-in with Big Sister Wei Chun and drew in his horns a little. Wei Chun belonged to the Black Water Gang — she was no leader there, but she was far beyond the reach of a street thug. Ever since, Chen Biao came prowling near the house only when Wei Chun was away.
Looking over from a distance, Wei He happened to meet Chen Biao's eye. Something strange glinted in the big man's small eyes; he smiled at Wei He and turned back to talk with his two followers.
Wei He's heart tightened.
He knew what the man was waiting for: Big Sister to come home. And part of the reason Wei Chun came home on time every day was probably him. Gang life meant rough weather and rough trouble; anyone could meet with an accident on any given day. Whatever Chen Biao was after, it was nothing good.
Trouble, Wei He thought. He quickened his pace, left the alley, and headed by a familiar route for the Black Water Gang's branch hall.
His eldest sister Wei Chun did not look like her siblings at all. She had been born big-boned and sturdy, hot-tempered, and stronger than most men. Which was why she had a small name for herself at the Black Water branch hall.
The branch hall was a square little courtyard. Inside, several men were lifting stone locks, and a man with a little queue of hair stood to one side, yawning.
"Wei Chun? She took a job this morning. She said one run and she'd have enough to move the family into the inner city, and enough saved up for her little brother's fist lessons. Sounded like a serious job. What, she didn't tell you?"
"No." Wei He shook his head, and his heart sank.
"That job had half the brothers fighting for it. The hall leader picked out a dozen good hands, and your sister was one of them. No rush, no rush." The man answered with a smile.
"Do you know when they'll be back?" Wei He asked.
"No idea. A job that size usually isn't short. Don't worry about it. All right, I'm still training here — go home and wait. The moment they're back, your sister will be the first to hear it from me." The man waved his hand, meaning: out.
Wei He knew that was all he would get; press further and the man would tell him nothing. He turned and walked slowly out of the courtyard gate.
Standing outside, he looked back at the plaque hanging over it. Four characters: Black Water Branch Hall.
The Black Water Gang was nothing but a third-rate gang. There were dozens like it in the outer city, if not a hundred. Where in a place like that would a job come from that paid that kind of money?
Wei He's heart was heavy. He had a bad feeling about this.
He was not one of those commoners with no learning and no eye for anything; he knew there had to be something wrong with it.
But she was already gone, and he was a sixteen-year-old boy who could do nothing. He could read a little. What was he supposed to do?
Back at his own alley, there was no sign of Chen Biao and his two men.
Only when he opened the door did he see Second Sister Wei Ying, her face white, her whole expression tight with panic and something held in.
The moment she saw him come in, it was as if a weight had lifted off her; she let out a long, heavy breath.
"Xiao He… Big Sister, is she — how is she?"
