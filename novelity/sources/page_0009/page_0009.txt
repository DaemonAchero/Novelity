"Chen Biao was right at the door — kicking it, slamming his boot into it. He waited a long time, and I didn't dare make a sound!" Wei Ying told him through her tears.
Wei He said nothing. His eyes only went colder and calmer.
"I know. Don't worry, I'll deal with it. Starting tomorrow, you come with me to the Mountain Fist place. I've spoken to the master — you'll go along and help with chores around the house. The pay, though…"
"I'll go! I'll go even without pay! I really can't stay here any longer! When you're not around, that Chen Biao comes by several times a day, and I don't dare say anything… Before, I was afraid of making trouble for you, so I didn't dare say…" Wei Ying explained, crying, her feelings running high.
She knew the place where Old Man Zheng taught Mountain Fist; there were very few people nearby who did not.
If her little brother could truly take a stand there, lean on it and hold his ground, that would be security enough.
"Then it's settled. We go tomorrow." Wei He said seriously.
He had already burned Chen Biao and his two men deep into his memory. Clearly his second sister had taken no small amount of fright and bullying while he was out.
After comforting her, Wei He fetched several buckets of water from the stream nearby and filled the water jar.
Then he and Wei Ying began to pack what they would take to the Mountain Fist place the next day.
The sky darkened little by little; the moon rose, and its light came in through the window. Wei He lit the oil lamp and had his sister finish mending the clothes that were left. Then he went carefully back over everything he had done, made sure nothing was wrong with it, blew out the lamp, and dropped off to sleep.
Some time past midnight he was woken by a rustling sound.
He opened his eyes, got up quietly, and glanced at his second sister on the other bed; she was sleeping soundly. He crept to the window, eased it open a crack, and looked out.
In the lane, a column of ordinary townsfolk — dressed every which way, their eyes shining in a way that was frightening to look at — was filing past, following a pair of thin, tall men and women in white.
"Rebirth in paradise, rebirth in paradise, rebirth in paradise…" the whole column mumbled, over and over, never stopping.
Accents from different places, the same words — together they made a strange, grinding unease in the chest.
Wei He eased the window shut; he did not dare look longer.
He recognized some of the people going by. Not a few of them were neighbors who lived right around him. He had never imagined they had this hidden side to them. Uncle Zhao who worked iron nearby, Sister Feng who sold steamed buns, Boss Xue of the pawnshop — all of them in the column, faces devout, minds dosed with who knew what poison.
Farther from the window, Wei He went back to his bed without a sound. The column slowly drew away, and the sound of it slowly faded out; after that he slept in a haze.
Just before dawn he rose on time, woke his second sister, and the two of them took the luggage and the little money left over and set out for Mountain Fist — Zheng.
He could do nothing at all right now; there was no beating Chen Biao's three, and the world threw out one trouble after another besides. A good stretch of real training was the only way to protect himself.
Wei He took this chance to learn martial arts very seriously. He had the Realm Breaking Bead in him besides, and he had landed in an age of chaos; he knew perfectly well that at a time like this, martial arts were the greatest guarantee a man could have.
When the two of them went out, the air was still cold and overcast, and the sun had not fully risen. Wei He led his sister the long way around, by a slightly farther route, back to the Mountain Fist courtyard.
Early as it was, the shouts of men training their strength were already coming out from inside.
Wei He went up and knocked, and gave his name.
"Coming."
The heavy wooden door was drawn slowly open. A big man with a pockmarked face looked at Wei He, then turned and flicked a glance at Wei Ying — and his lazy look turned warm in an instant.
"Come in, come in, the master is already drinking his tea. You're Wei He, our little junior brother? Good, good. My name is Cheng Shaojiu, and I rank third here! Just call me Third Senior Brother."
The pockmarked man introduced himself with perfect amiability, while his eyes drifted toward Wei Ying, again and again, without his noticing.
