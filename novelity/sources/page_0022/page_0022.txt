He had found out beforehand that Zhao Deli had a child, a young one. What he had not expected was that the man had been sitting there holding his own child, dead in his own home for no one knew how long. This was what a world in chaos came to. This was what a human life was worth. Wei He sat alone on his bed, the thin moonlight from the window falling on the side of his face and printing it a faint, pale white.
He slept badly all night, tossing from side to side, the look of that dried child's corpse rising before him again and again. Only at daybreak did he get up and eat the golden coin worm meat he had bought at the apothecary. It held off hunger longer than ordinary food and filled the Realm Breaking Bead on his chest faster. Wei He strove to shake the image out of his head, and the thought that he still carried this one gift across from his last life steadied his heart a little.
Only this golden coin worm meat, though the essence it fed the bead was quick, did not bear praising for its taste. Wei He put a small piece in his mouth, and chewing it was like chewing dry wood; a big mouthful of water was needed to get down one little piece.
Elsewhere.
Nanshan Ward, at Zhao Deli's earthen hut. The sun was not yet over the hill when several strong men in grey short tunics came to the door of the hut.
"This is the place?"
"It is."
The men spoke a few words, low. Then one of them kicked the door in, and one went inside quickly for a short while and came out again just as quickly.
"All dead."
"The last place that woman stopped was here, and those black worms are the trail. Have a look round about — the black-letter worms are still lively, so she can't have gone far," the man leading them said, low. "By the trail of it, she stopped here and borrowed this hut a while to hide in, and then left."
"She has to be found. And besides that — leave one man behind to silence anyone who might be involved, anyone who has seen the black-letter worms."
"Right!"
Not long after, the whole hut went up in roaring flame, and once the men had made sure everything inside was burned away they turned and slipped off.
… Time flies. In the blink of an eye another month had gone.
Wei He trained his moves hard every day, and began skin grinding for the first time as well. Shh, shh, shh. In the great yard of Mountain Fist, before a large wooden tub, Wei He bared both arms and struck his fists again and again into the sand inside the tub. Dry, hard sand is the first step of skin grinding.
His movement was neither slow nor fast; each time he drove his fists deep into the sand so that every part of them was thoroughly rubbed. After grinding like that for half an hour he drew his fists out and put them into the medicinal broth beside him to soak for fifty breaths. Beneath the broth charcoal burned to hold the heat. Round him were several other men at the same work of skin grinding.
Further out others stood with arms crossed, waiting their turn. Every one of these skin-grinding stations was precious.
