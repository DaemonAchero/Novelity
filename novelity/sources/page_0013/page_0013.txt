Huhh… the sky was not yet light. Wei He had both arms wrapped round a great black stone, and he drove the whole strength of his body into moving it — forward and back, up and down, without pause. Around him the other men of Mountain Fist were at the same work, tempering their strength together.
Once Eldest Senior Brother had spent a while correcting his posture and his key points, he stopped hovering over him and went off to teach another newcomer. Wei He trained strength every day. It was a dull, tasteless business, but to feel himself grow stronger day after day put his heart very much at ease.
"Junior Brother Xiao He, how is it today? Can you get onto the second row of black stones?" Off to the right, in the clear space, Third Senior Brother Cheng Shaojiu had stripped off his outer robe and stood there in a short training tunic.
Then he picked a stone up out of the corner and began to warm up on it. These past months, oddly enough, it was he who had come to stand closest to Wei He. At the start he had drawn near the younger man only because of Wei He's second sister. But as the two of them spent time together he found that Wei He, silent and close-mouthed on the surface, was in truth stubborn, true to his word, and full of views that were not at all ordinary.
As the days went by, Cheng Shaojiu took a real interest in the friendship. And Wei He, for his part, felt no dislike for this third senior brother of his, scattered as the man's nature was. The two of them grew close.
"The second row is still beyond me. I've been training strength too short a time." Wei He shook his head, and set the stone he was holding — a size smaller than the rest — lightly down, working his joints loose.
"No hurry, no hurry. There's a music party this afternoon, over in one of the wards south of the city. They say a pipa master is coming — he'll be out of the ordinary, that much is certain. I mean to go and hear him. Coming along?"
Cheng Shaojiu did not treat Wei He the way he treated other men. This fellow simply loved to dress himself up in culture, and for a junior brother who could read and whose views were out of the common, he had soon made up his mind to count him one of his own.
So whenever some such outing came up, he told Wei He of it at once. Wei He had never gone to one of them; yet the reason Cheng Shaojiu kept asking was, more than likely, a faint and harmless showing off. In years like these, after all, a man who could still afford to sit and listen to music was no poor commoner.
"Not going, Third Senior Brother. That thing I asked you to look into — any word?"
Wei He rested a moment, then took up another stone and went on with his practice.

"Your Black Water Gang? Some days back, the Black Water Gang was swallowed up by one called the Horned Snake Gang. Now—" Cheng Shaojiu shook his head, meaning there was nothing to be done. "As for the other — that thug, Chen Biao — I did hear a thing or two from my elder cousin."
The Cheng family was large and thriving, and news moved through it easily; in the several wards about Feiye City they were counted as somebody. And Cheng Shaojiu himself liked to spend his silver on his friends and stand by them, so his friends were many. Every trade and every walk of life had its own doorways, and through them a little of every rumour reached his ear.
"What is it?" Wei He asked, low.
"It's some of the thugs round Rat Alley. They've all joined that Horned Snake Gang that's just climbed up. Now they're out every day pushing their turf wider, fighting and killing — lively enough, I can tell you. Only, why are you asking after a thug? What — has the man a grudge against you?" Cheng Shaojiu shot him a glance.
"Nothing. Thank you all the same. If you can keep an eye on Chen Biao for me, I'll finish that exercise from last time for you."
Wei He was driving a bargain now. There was one more reason the two of them had come to stand close: this fellow was forever in misery over his lessons at home. His father had hired an instructor to teach him one to one, and every few days another assignment was laid on. It gave Cheng Shaojiu no end of grief.
He was a boy who loved play, loved a crowd and loved making friends; how was he to sit still and dabble in ink? So when he discovered that Wei He could write an assignment in his place, he made his request without a moment's hesitation and set Wei He to try it.
