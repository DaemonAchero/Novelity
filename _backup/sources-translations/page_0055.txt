No wonder Li Er had won two bouts so easily before. But it was a pity… Wei He's free left fist shot forward, a straight punch of the same kind, driving at the man's chest. With a whoosh his fist became a white blur; a straight punch like the other, and this one was more than a notch faster than Li Er's.
Just as Li Er meant to twist away, the fist turned a corner on him and struck him square on the shoulder. Thud. Li Er staggered back, one move lost. Unconvinced, he came on again.
The two traded on, and a few moves back and forth were all it took before Li Er took another punch to the belly. This time he saw it clearly: their strength was much the same, but the gap in the speed of their hands was far too wide. Wei He had all but stood in one spot, never moving, and settled the fight with a few punches. "Impressive! I bow to you!" Li Er, red-faced, cupped his hands and slunk off.
The cheers and shouts around them slowly died down. Not many of them knew Wei He well, so naturally no one cheered for him.
"Whoever wants to go up, go up. But before you do, think it over: Li Er's Nine Mountain Fist has fierce force and he still lost, so if you are weak, do not go looking to be thrashed." Cheng Kai said it loudly from the side.
"I will." A man stepped up, a short sabre in his hand. "Sorry — weapons are what I am good at."
"No matter." Wei He answered. Out in the world, sooner or later you meet a man with a weapon. This fellow came at Wei He with two swift slashes — the flat of the blade, to avoid real injury — but the blade light still flashed and the look of it was fearsome. Yet Wei He's qi and blood outstripped these men, and his reactions had long since left the ordinary behind; he dodged a few times, and,
tripping the man's foot, took his balance away, then threw a hook. The short sabre was knocked from his hand and clattered to the ground. This bout was won too.
Wei He did not leave the ground when the bout ended, but went on looking around, meaning to add to his real-fighting experience.
Although he said nothing, everyone understood him: he meant to go on. Soon two more men came up, and Wei He settled them easily. With that gap in qi and blood, handling these ordinary escort masters was very light work. Only now did he feel deeply what a great increase in strength the qi-and-blood stage gave. Everyone else looked to him like men moving in slow motion.
"Then let me try." At last a tall woman with long legs stepped out. Her name was Cheng Jing; she too was of the Cheng family, though of a distant branch. But she was no escort master — she was one of the only two escort captains in the whole party.
"If it is girl Jing, then we have something worth watching." Cheng Kai stroked his beard and smiled. He had just been wondering whether he ought to go in himself; otherwise, if Wei He swept aside the face of every old hand, it would be hard on them. As one of the rare women among the escort masters, and an escort captain at that — the one who managed the escort men — Cheng Jing was no ordinary hand, and in Stone Bridge Ward, where the Cheng family lived, she had a name as a tough fighter.
"I have crossed hands with a good many of the formidable people in these wards. Stone Bridge Ward, Nanshan Ward — five small gangs in all, and one by one I went to the door of each gang's best man and fought him. I lost only one bout. Inside the agency I am second only to the great escort masters and the young master. If you beat me, then in the whole of Stone Bridge Ward, leaving aside the martial masters, you can rank in the top ten." Cheng Jing spoke it word by word, clear and round, her eyes bright, not blinking, looking straight at Wei He.
"You have been provoking the old hands one after another, meaning to walk over everyone's names and make a name for yourself in one go. If I were not here today, never mind — but since I am here, I will not stand by and let it happen!" Her voice rang clear and firm. Wei He was silent a moment. "I have no such…"
"You need not quibble. I have seen plenty of your kind — silent on the outside, but inside more full of ambition than anyone." Cheng Jing said it flatly.
"You misunderstand…"
"I do not misunderstand. Wanting to rise is no fault; the fault is that you chose the wrong way."
