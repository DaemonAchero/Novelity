"Here's today's." The braided boy fished a rolled-up sheet of paper out of his clothes and tossed it into Wei He's arms.
"Same as always — half a tael of silver for a hundred characters," Wei He said flatly. These days silver was worth so little that if he did not charge more he would never save anything.
"Fine, fine, fine! Just hurry it up. The instructor wants it by morning." The boy lowered his voice, dug a chip of broken silver out of his clothes, tossed it to Wei He, and sauntered off.
This was homework for hire — a little business Wei He had picked up in the course of stealing lessons outside the wall.
The boy with the braids was called Li Bian, the only son of a wealthy merchant house in Feiye City, and free with his money.
Wei He tucked the silver away and did the sums.
"Like this, I'm getting close to the tuition for Old Man Zheng's Mountain Fist."
Since he had a cheat of this kind inside him, he was not about to give up on a way to switch it on.
The Realm Breaking Bead's information said it was meant for breaking through martial realms. Wei He had tried training under his eldest sister — ordinary free-hand fighting technique, simple drills — and none of it had worked.
Later, from his sister, he learned that a small number of people in this world held a method called the Internal Cultivation Method.
Those people were the true experts of the world. The Black Water Gang, where his sister spent her days, had such fighters in it.
But the Internal Cultivation Method was never passed to outsiders lightly. Only through his sister's connections, after more than a month of searching, did Wei He find somewhere near at hand that would teach it — Old Man Zheng's place.
The tuition there, though, was far from cheap.
== Chapter 2 · Hope ==
At the Lecture Hall Wei He did not take only Li Bian's work; three other children slipped over to him for ghostwriting as well. All because of one trick he had brought from his previous life: he could imitate different styles of handwriting, and a child's scrawl was the easiest of all.
Once he had a stack of assignments in hand, Wei He rolled them up, tucked them inside his clothes, stood, and walked quickly for home.
Passing Stone Bridge Street by the river, he heard a thin noise of quarrelling come out of the weeds on the bank. A woman's crying and a man's roaring were mixed into it, along with the repeated crack of a club against a human body.
Wei He paused a moment, then quickened his step and passed by on the far side.
The Lecture Hall stood in the inner city, where things were safe — constables patrolled at all hours. But his home was in the slum quarter, and that was a different matter. Stone Bridge Street was already inside the slum quarter's bounds.
Back home, Wei He took out the charcoal sticks he kept ready and worked through the assignments one by one. Then he patted off the charcoal dust, rolled the papers up again, and put them away.
Second Sister Wei Ying cooked one dish and one soup from what was in the house, and the two of them sat at the table waiting for the rest of the family.
The sky darkened by degrees. Big Sister Wei Chun still did not come back. From their parents there was no word either.
Wei He's parents were both craftsmen who carved stone images for a living, and lately they had been at Mingde Temple outside the city, carving Buddha statues.
"Mother and Father are probably staying over at the temple again." Second Sister Wei Ying's voice was low. "But why isn't Big Sister back?" Big Sister Wei Chun came home every single day without fail.
"…"
Wei He said nothing. He looked up at the window; the sun had nearly sunk out of sight. The wind blowing in had turned cool, and there was a faint smell of other households cooking.
"Big Sister always comes back at this hour." Wei Ying glanced at her brother and murmured — as if to him, as if to herself.
"I'll go and look. Stay here, don't go anywhere." Wei He stood up. Something felt wrong; he could not settle.
"Go and come back quickly," Wei Ying said.
"Mm."
