Even so, Wei He took only the money pouch; he flicked the rest open with his knife tip and scattered them on the ground. The sachet was full of all sorts of crushed spices and petals. In the bamboo tube was a white flying insect, soaked half dead, an index finger long, with four wings on its back — a rather frightening thing to look at. And the envelope was mostly soaked open, with only the wax seal still holding the letter together.
Wei He carefully cut the seal open with his knife tip, spread out the pale yellow paper on a larger cobble, and read it closely by the last of the daylight. It was a set of instructions on the use of the scent-seeking worm — an instruction sheet, in plain terms. The letter said the scent-seeking worm was used specifically to track black-letter worms.
And the black-letter worm was a most peculiar insect: when stirred up, it left a trail of half-transparent slime where it crawled. Once the slime dried it slowly gave off a breath that only its own kind could smell, and that breath lasted extraordinarily long, months without fading. The letter noted that this trait could be used to track the demon woman who carried black-letter worms.
"A demon woman?" Wei He narrowed his eyes. He had his guesses. He picked up the bamboo tube, brought it closer to his own body, and, following the instructions, put his nose softly to the hole at the top of the tube and sniffed. If there was a trace of black-letter worm on him, the scent-seeking worm would give off its scent.
If there was none, naturally there would be no scent. But to his surprise, the tube really did give off a faint scent. He had meant only to try it and see how it was used; he had not thought that at some time or other he had picked up a trace of the black-letter worm himself.
His face darkened. Very likely, without knowing when, he had been done in by this demon woman; most likely she had used him as a decoy to split the attention of her pursuers. Whoever you are, the score is opened, and I will remember it. He filed it away in his heart. Anyone suddenly attacked and nearly beaten to death would set down a heavy mark in his mind.
"Time to go." Wei He rose, kicked the things on the ground into the little stream, and, with a handful of broken silver he had taken from the pouch, hurried down the hill. His heart was dark. A trace of the black-letter worm had been put on him at some unknown time, and he had known nothing about it.
Who was the demon woman the letter spoke of? What was her standing, why was she hunted, and when had the trap been set on him? Wei He's heart was full of questions. Halfway down the hill, he suddenly recalled what the letter had mentioned about the black-letter worm living in colonies, and that a single worm would not usually go far from its mother
colony. "In other words, the demon woman with the worms is very likely still nearby?" Wei He's heart jolted. Judging by the strength of the leg-work man just now, the people hunting her were no ordinary lot. If he had not blinded the man's eyes first, he would certainly have lost this fight.
"From the tone of the letter, the black-letter worms are kept by that demon woman, and these people have some purpose in hunting her. And silencing even the bystanders who touched the worms — plainly they do not want anyone knowing that woman's whereabouts, and want to stretch out the time before word leaks." The more Wei He reasoned, the more alarmed he grew.
"In other words, there must be two groups of them: one the main force hunting the demon woman, one scattered about doing the silencing. And the hunting group must be stronger than the silencing lot." He was sure of the guess now. The letter had not said much, but it was enough for him to judge the key points.
"And now I have killed one of their number — whatever the reason, they will send someone to look into it." Wei He went back over how he had handled things, and abruptly feared that the corpse might show how the man had died. He stopped dead and turned to go back and deal with the body.
