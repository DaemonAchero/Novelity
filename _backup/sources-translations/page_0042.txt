He knew well that his only advantage was having struck first, with surprise on his side — the man had had no guard up against him, and that was why it had gone so well. But the same trick would not work a second time.
So… a ruthless light flashed in his eyes, and he led the man off deeper and deeper into the woods. Without either of them noticing, the two came to a slope, and by now the man was panting like an ox, drenched with sweat. He knew this was bad; if it went on, the end would be his death. And so the thought of withdrawing came to him too.
After all, nine parts of his strength were gone, his heart was like a drum, close to bursting; and on top of it his eyes were blinded and burning with sharp pain — he even wondered whether they were ruined for good. Under all these worries the man quietly eased off, meaning to pull back. It was just then that Wei He lunged forward and drove a Mountain Fist straight punch into his chest.
Thud. The man heard it and kicked his right leg up, meeting the fist square. In his usual state that kick would have been enough to break the arm of this lad who had only just got his foot in the door of qi and blood. But as things stood, with his strength worn to the very dregs, he could only barely knock the fist aside.
"Who are you, really? Why did you ambush me?" he demanded loudly. Wei He did not make a sound, only hammered at him with Mountain Fist. This was the very time for him to sharpen his real fighting; worn down as the man was, his experience in real combat was truly rich. Though he was in an extreme disadvantage, over and over he still found a sliver of life in the openings and gaps of Wei He's moves.
Sometimes Wei He struck with too much force and left an opening; sometimes he struck too late and failed to link up the combination before; sometimes his aim was not fine enough and he hit empty air. Off this blinded man Wei He studied the attack-and-defence system of Mountain Fist in careful detail — a chance to train that came once in a thousand years.
But this time could not be drawn out. Some tens of breaths later the man could hold up no longer; a punch took him in the chest, he staggered back several paces, dropped down sitting, and could not get his breath. Stars burst before his eyes, his chest went numb, he could hear nothing in his ears, and he felt as if every vessel in his body were scalding, about to burst.
"Aah!" he roared, choked with outrage. All his leg skill, years of bitter training, and in the end he had fallen to a green boy who had barely entered the gate of qi and blood! Thud. A great stone came down on his head from behind, and the sound cut off.
All was quiet. Wei He, his face blank, struck a dozen or more times in a row, and only when he was sure there was no breath left in the man did he throw the stone aside. "Too dangerous… Come to a temple for a stroll and run into a highway robber. And a highway robber who is as strong as that… The country beyond the walls lives up to its name."
He meant it from the heart. Then he looked at the bloody, battered corpse on the ground and touched the quicklime bag, now flat and empty; the cards he had been saving up so long were all spent in one breath. He would have to buy more.
As for corpses, Wei He had been through it once before and was a good deal more used to it now. So for this fellow with the remarkable leg work before him, he decided on a body search. His mind made up, he moved fast: crouching by the corpse, he used the knife the agency had issued to flick the clothing open, and turned out a money pouch, a sachet, a bamboo tube, and a letter.
He took off his outer coat, wrapped these things in it, picked the bundle up by the cloth so as not to touch them, and quickly left the scene. Finding a stream in the hill woods, he dropped the bundle straight into the water, soaked and shook it there a short while, and only then took it out again. That way, if there were drugged incense or poison in any of them, the stream would wash it through and thin it away.
