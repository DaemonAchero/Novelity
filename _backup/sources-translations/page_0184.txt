Soon the group cut past not far from Wei He and went off towards the other side. Wei He stepped lightly out from behind the big tree and studied the little earthen fort. It was about twenty metres long and no more than ten wide; it certainly could not hold many men, and yet guards still patrolled here so late. Wei He recognised what these men wore: the clothes of ordinary Shaoyang Sect disciples.
Unlike the Shaoyang Sect disciple he had beaten to death, these men were plainly a grade lower. One and all, for reasons he could not see, they said not a word, and even their breathing was slow and steady, without a ripple. He crept close to the fort and quickly pulled several rats out of the cloth bag at his back, tossing them out ahead. He had caught the rats beforehand for just this; the moment they landed they ran off in every direction and were gone into the dark.
Not one rat, though, went towards the fort. Wei He pulled out a few more and threw them closer in. This time one of the four made for the fort, and ran a circuit along its wall. Wei He moved, following the rat fast and light on his toes.
He was very quick; his feet pattered on, and in a moment he was making no more noise than the rat itself, slipping into a patch of shadow inside the fort. Before long a patrol was drawn by the sound of the rat and came that way, and shortly after came the squealing shrieks of the rat being killed. Wei He did not move rashly: he had come to catch a man of some standing and examine him, and outer patrol men like these would yield no clues even if caught.
They might even put the fort on its guard and spoil his work. He meant to succeed in one try, taking them unawares and finding out the truth. The patrol men still said nothing; having killed the rat, they paced in neat step slowly away along the edge of the fort. Wei He pressed to the wall, using a long wooden stick he had just picked up to test the ground inch by inch, moving along the wall.
Before long he had made a circuit and could find no gap or entrance at all. With no other way, he felt his way to the fort's entrance. The entrance had a round wooden door, extremely thick, and two white-clothed fat men stood to left and right of it like wax figures, not moving at all. Wei He came softly to a stop, then stamped down hard, his figure shooting out like a bat, flashing past from behind one fat man. Then his feet pattered, his shape swerved, and he skimmed lightly past the other fat man's back.
Thud. Neither fat man made a sound; both dropped dead. Wei He picked up one of the bodies and rolled forward along the front gate with it. Pff, pff, pff!
A run of muffled sounds, and two rows of round holes the size of fingernails appeared in the fat man's corpse. Wei He narrowed his eyes and recalled the speed at which the hidden weapons had just fired, and made up his mind. If that was the speed of them, then no matter how many such weapons there were, it did not signify. He went in first, and laid a light palm on the wooden door.
The Five Ridges Palm pressed softly on the gate and sank deep into it without a sound. Wei He set his hands and tore the lock apart, pulling the gate open. Inside, two white-clothed fat men of exaggerated build were playing dice; sensing something wrong, they roared and both came lunging at Wei He.
Both arms struck out at once, two kinds of Shaoyang Palm, one behind the other, at Wei He. "Too slow." Wei He met them with both hands and let out the force of his Five Ridges Palm. Two muffled thuds, and the two fat men were driven staggering back by the huge recoil, their faces flushing rapidly to the colour of pig's liver.
Clutching their arms, they fell back at speed, meaning to shout for help. But it was too late; Wei He was very fast, and in the instant they stepped back he pressed in, chasing them down with a palm each, laid on their chests. The Flying Dragon Art was far too fast for them.
Soon another patrol came up at a quick pace, and at the gate, just as they were about to come in, Wei He threw a handful of quicklime in their faces and blinded them. Palm shadows flickered, and several men went down on the spot.
