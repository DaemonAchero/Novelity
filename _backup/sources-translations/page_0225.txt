Now that she had come to know Wei He a little, she saw more and more clearly that a man of his temper could never follow another man, nor be taken in hand by anyone. He looked placid and said little, but at bottom his nature was proud and he held himself very high. The one way to be his friend was to treat him as an equal, honestly.
"Master Zheng pulls out of Feiye City the day after tomorrow. Have you decided?" Wei He asked it casually, reaching out with his chopsticks for a share of the celery stir-fried with dried tofu. The salty savour of the tofu and the crisp, faintly bitter celery went well together; it tasted very good. "I have decided." Jiang Su nodded. "I go. The Jiang family stays."
"Mm? You alone?" Wei He looked up, startled. "No. My father has already thrown in with the Wang family of the Seven Family League, so he cannot leave now. I will take some of our relatives out with me as a branch of the family and go with Master Zheng. Call it keeping a spark alive for the clan," Jiang Su said calmly. "That makes sense." Wei He nodded.
"Are you not worried at all?" Jiang Su gave him a look. "Your celebration is this afternoon. Every martial academy in the wards round about will send disciples to congratulate you, and some of them will surely want a bout." "Not many academies are on good terms with our teacher. I have noted them all down. The rest I can brush off as they come. I will play it by ear," Wei He said evenly.
He had seen very little of the other academies' people since he joined Mountain Fist. This would be a chance.
== Chapter 75 · Gathering (Part 1) ==
After the meal Wei He walked Jiang Su home, and on his way back to his own house he saw Master Yuan Zhouchi again. In the fierce noon sun the old man sat in an ornamental pavilion at the corner of the street, a big winnowing basket on his knees full of waterweed fished out of the river. He kept turning the weed over with his fingers so that more of it would catch the light, and now and then he raised the waterskin at his side and drank a mouthful.
This old gentleman had once taught books in the Lecture Hall, honoured and looked up to, and had read the classics and their meaning to the children of many a great house in the city. Now he wore clothes thick with patches, and the beard that had been going grey was white through. His eyes seemed to be failing him; hunched on the cracked stone bench of the pavilion, he picked over the waterweed, hunting out the bad, rotted pieces.
Wei He checked his steps and stood where he was, watching Yuan Zhouchi pick over his weed. Perhaps he stood there too long: slowly the old man noticed him and looked him over. Wei He answered with a smile, and did not disturb his way of living, but passed by on one side.
He had meant to go over and leave something for the old teacher. But he remembered how much face had always mattered to Yuan Zhouchi, and that stiff old face of his, and now it looked exactly as stern as ever. The teacher had come down in the world, but there was nothing wrong with his breathing or his colour, so Wei He let it be. He quickened his pace, crossed a great stretch of the Outer City, and made for Stone Bridge Ward.
At home, Wei Ying was drying the family's salted vegetables, and the yard smelled strongly of pickle. Wei He had already told her that they would soon be leaving the city, so she had begun packing the house up into bags and bundles. "Sister, before I came back, did anyone come looking for me?" Wei He asked suddenly.
"No? There has been nobody all morning. And we are so close to the yard, if anyone wanted you they would go to the yard for you." Wei Ying set down her winnowing basket, puzzled. Wei He stood before the door of his own room, looking at a hair he had wedged in the crack of the door. It was broken. He was quiet a moment. One hand slid slowly into the pocket at the small of his back, the other gathered qi and blood, and with one finger he gently pushed the door open.
Creak. The wooden door swung slowly open. Inside the room a figure stood with its back to him, dressed all in plain white, still and silent.
