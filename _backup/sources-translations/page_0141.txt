With that thought he got up and went quickly back to his room, where he put on his full kit with care. Then he asked Second Sister Wei Ying closely about the route she took on ordinary days, and only after that opened the door and strode off. Out of the house he did not head for the Mountain Fist yard, but turned the other way, towards the ward's market.
The Stone Bridge Ward market opened for only one double-hour in the morning; go late and the goods would soon be snatched up. Wei He followed the street towards the market. All along the way, and the nearer he came to the market the more so, he saw men and women in every kind of dress, carrying every sort of container, hurrying to the market.
Some of these people were in rags, some passably clean and neat, but the common note was that none were dressed richly. The best off there were neat with a patch or two. Wei He's own clean, neat, close-fitting clothes drew a little notice in that crowd. He did not mind. The whole way he had never discovered that out of those shabby, quiet houses along the road
so many living people could come. He had almost come to think the Outer City had no people left in it. But now, one by one, gaunt, ill-fed people came out of them, and that gave him a sense of the bleakness special to this age. Soon, past a small spread of grey single-storey houses, a row of stone tablets stood ahead, with two large characters painted on them in black: Vegetable Market.
Tablet after tablet joined into a screen that hid the broad open ground behind. Only one small entry was left open for people to pass in and out. At that entry a group of sturdy men in black stood guard with short knives in their hands, and they looked like trouble. "Line up, in one at a time, no shoving!" The shouts kept coming.
Wei He came closer and stood outside the ring of stone tablets, watching the crowd file in by degrees and begin trading at the vegetable stalls and meat stalls out on the open ground. "Who are all these men in black?" He caught hold of a thin young man to ask. The man glanced at Wei He and was minded to snap at him, but one look at his build and his air and he had to swallow his temper and answer low.
"They are good fellows of the Tongcheng Gang. Going in costs nothing, but coming out, whatever you bought or traded for, you hand a tenth of it over to them. That is the rule of this vegetable market." "A tenth?" Wei He understood. He let the man go and did not go in; instead he turned and made for the next place.
Every day, Wei Ying came here first to buy vegetables, then went to the oil shop for oil, sometimes took a turn through the cloth shop, and only then went home to begin straining water, cooking, washing clothes and cleaning up. Wei He followed the street away from the vegetable market and soon came to the oil shop. There were only two rooms to it, with a long queue at the door, and the shop opened only one small door less than a metre wide, enough for one person to pass in
and out. The people buying oil here were much better off in dress and in spirit than those at the vegetable market, and there were far fewer patches on their clothes. Wei He took a turn around, and wherever he went the people there dropped their heads and did not dare look at him.
It was simply that he stood a metre eighty-five, was muscle all over, and had killed no small number of men and game; at a glance he was no one to cross. So the others did not even dare glance at him twice. Wei He took his turn and then walked towards where the cloth shop stood. Leaving the short street the oil shop stood on, the houses on both sides of the road grew cleaner by degrees.
Plainly someone swept them often. Here and there by the roadside one could see burnt-out stubs of incense stuck in the dirt. On some doors new prints of door gods and fortune generals had been pasted, and some had even put up couplets written in their own hand. The stretch of street between oil shop and cloth shop was not long, but there were a good few shops along it.
This was the hour when the market opened wide and every shop put up its shutters in turn. It was the first time Wei He had seen such bustle. At this hour he was usually in the Mountain Fist yard grinding away at his skin, so how would he ever have seen a sight so lively.
