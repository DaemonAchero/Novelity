"Even so, there is no need for you to go with them now, is there?" Wei He knew that in this yard, if a man could not break through for a long stretch, he was forced to think of another way to live, for the tuition had to be paid every month. For the likes of them now the sum was not large, but the trouble was that most of his time had to go to training and drilling, and little was left for earning.
As a man grew older, living on and eating up what he had was no solution either, so many of them took work outside — as Wei He himself did, serving part-time at the Cheng family's Yonghe Agency. "I hear it is Hong Family Fort outside the city, taking in soldiers… the pay and treatment are very good, and a man who distinguishes himself can be made a squad leader or a ten-man leader straight away." Ouyang Zhuang clicked his tongue in admiration.
"They say that from squad-leader rank up, a special medicated dish that greatly supplements qi and blood is provided, and a stronger expert instructs you on breaking through to your face." Wei He understood. For the old hands who had been unable to break through for so long, leaving like this was excusable. After all, twenty was the peak; once past it the body began to slip slowly away, and by then everything was too late.
And speaking of it, he was himself about to turn eighteen… "How good can a medicated dish be? It still means staking your life." Wei He said, reproving him, and seeing the envy in Ouyang Zhuang's eyes he lost no time in urging him to get on with his training. All that day Ouyang Zhuang was unsettled and could not train at all, while Wei He, steady as ever, went through his moves, his skin grinding and his rests in due
order. Nothing about it disturbed his rhythm. Only, having heard Ouyang Zhuang out, he too was a little curious: just how formidable were the experts in the army? Toward evening, when the day was nearly done, Cheng Shaojiu came late, having spent the whole day seeing to the family agency's affairs.
Wei He had a mind to ask about it, but since the other had not spoken of it there must be a reason for keeping it from him, and he was in no hurry. His qi and blood were almost complete now, and his greatest task was to gather them all the way up and break through. Cheng Shaojiu came into the yard, was taken aback to see how many men were missing, and was quickly told the reason. He mused a while, then went into the inner room, where he seemed to say something to Old Man Zheng.
Not long after he came out and beckoned to Wei He. "Xiao He, are you finished?"
"Just about." Wei He nodded, hung the sweat towel from his neck on the rack behind him, and went over. "Brother Cheng, what is it?"
As their friendship grew closer, he and Cheng Shaojiu had taken to calling each other by warmer names. "Nothing much. Come along with me to the Inner City in a while and take the air." Cheng Shaojiu patted Wei He's shoulder, then at once drew his hand back in distaste; his palm was all over Wei He's sweat.
"The Inner City?" Wei He paused. He had never been to the Inner City.
"Never been, have you? Never mind — your brother here will show you the world!" Cheng Shaojiu smiled a little.
"All right." Wei He was brief. In fact he had never gone to the Inner City: partly because he had always heard the spending there was enormous, and partly because he had neither the time nor the spare spirit. He tidied himself, gave Ouyang Zhuang a few words about his training, then washed, changed his clothes, and set out with Cheng Shaojiu,
climbing into his family's carriage. The carriage drove all the way toward the Inner City entrance. The Inner City and the Outer City were in essence two separate regions: the Inner City was ringed about by a high wall and was in truth a city within the city, wholly cut off from the outside.
The two went in at the entrance — a small city gate in its own right. Once inside, Cheng Shaojiu did not wander about but found a three-storey restaurant and went up. The restaurant was called Hundred Flowers Tower. The name could not have been more ordinary, but its style was something else. Coming in, Wei He saw that at every table stood a girl of sixteen in a white skirt that bared her shoulders and legs, a feast for the eye,
and their one business was to pour wine and fill the bowls of the guests. "Well? Heh heh heh, today your brother has brought you here to open your eyes!" Cheng Shaojiu patted Wei He's shoulder, pleased with himself, and led him to a seat by the window.
