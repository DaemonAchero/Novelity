The fists came at him like bolts from a crossbow. Bang, bang! Both were blocked. Wei He was forever ambushing other men, and the art of the ambush, and the preludes to one, he knew extremely well.
The moment he saw Cheng Shaojiu take that stance, with a little of the flavour of a feint about it, his guard went up; looking closely, he found the punch aimed at his belly. Having turned both of Cheng Shaojiu's hands aside, he forced down the urge to fling a handful of quicklime in his face. He brought his knee up — a knee strike. It was chiefly that Cheng Shaojiu, thrown open like that, made too good a target: his guard wide, the opening perfect.
But he did remember, at least, that this was only a sparring match; he held the impulse down and settled into the exchange in earnest. He raised his qi and blood as well, and a faint grey-white came over both his fists. The two of them fought hard by the light of the moon, Stone-Hide meeting Stone-Hide — a level of collision no ordinary man could even come near. The mere sound of fist on fist was like a war drum beaten.
Grey grit flew up about them again and again; in the rising dust their two figures grew hard to make out. The longer Cheng Shaojiu fought, the more alarmed he became. Not long ago he had crossed hands with Wei He, and then he could still press him down with ease. But now he could not hold him down in qi and blood at all, and even in the experience of technique he could hardly keep up with Wei He's counters.
And he had begun training years before Wei He; now the gap between them had come down to this. Dull impacts rang out over the training ground without pause. Cheng Shaojiu gave an occasional shout; Wei He said not a word, silent throughout. The two fought in altogether different styles.
Before long came a heavy thud. Wei He staggered out of the cloud of dust, his feet going back a dozen paces over the ground before he caught himself. Panting hard, he looked at the spot on his chest where a grey fist mark had been printed. "No wonder they call you Brother Cheng. I still lost."
The dust settled slowly and showed Cheng Shaojiu standing where he was, a smile on his face. "You are already very strong. To have this much right after breaking through — your foundation must be very solid. No wonder Master Zheng thinks so much of you."
"You are joking, Brother Cheng. That last chain of strikes just now — I never would have thought it could be used that way. I have learned something from you here
today." Wei He was still turning over the place where he had lost. He had gone all out, and still Cheng Shaojiu had broken him down head-on. Plainly, even with his breakthrough in qi and blood, he was short of Brother Cheng by a good deal of seasoning. And though both were Stone-Hide, there was still a gap in the strength of their qi and blood: a man at Cheng Shaojiu's level of perfection and one who had only
just broken through were far apart. Trading blows with everything he had, Wei He's fists ached dully, and his strength was spent at a fearful rate; in the end, after a dozen-odd exchanges one after another, it was because his strength failed him that he lost. "Enough. It is late. I will have Old Huang take you back first." Cheng Shaojiu said.
"No hurry. Let me mull it over a while; I will go back on my own presently. If it gets too late, Old Huang would be unsafe coming back alone." Wei He waved a hand.
"As you like. Take care of yourself." Cheng Shaojiu nodded. He turned and walked off the training ground.
Only when he had reached a corner the light did not reach did he raise his hand and look at his slightly reddened fist, a flicker of something complicated passing through his eyes. Just now, had he not brought out in time a killing move his uncle had helped design, he could not have been sure of beating Wei He at the end. The gap between them was very small now. Wei He struck carefully and cautiously, never wasting strength.
Altogether unlike himself. Without that killing move, with the two of them grinding on to the end, it was impossible to say who would have won. Without his noticing it, the junior brother he had once been able to put down with a casual hand had slowly turned into a real hand, hard to handle.
