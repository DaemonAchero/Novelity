Before long the pot had boiled down and was set down in front of Wei He, and others came in one after another, led into the pavilion. Guan Ye rose each time to introduce them to Wei He, saying they were all brothers and sisters she had come to know of late. They were men and women both, dressed every which way, some fawning, some indifferent, some cold. Some, like Wei He, were martial artists; some knew nothing of the martial way and were only ordinary folk. Some were
rank with bandit airs, their eyes not daring to settle on Guan Ye but rolling about the serving girls at her side. A crowd of ox-demons and snake-spirits gathered together; one sweep of Wei He's eye and there were few he could think anything of. Soon the servants laid the banquet, dish after dish and bowls of white rice coming up. The company ate mightily, pleased beyond measure, juice running everywhere.
When the meal was done, Guan Ye had everyone seen off one by one. Wei He had also eaten a pot of that great tonic, all meat and medicinal herbs; the taste was decent enough, and of qi and blood he felt nothing yet. Seeing tiredness creeping over Guan Ye's face, he too rose of his own accord to take his leave.
Guan Ye had someone take Wei He back, and set about directing the servants to clear away, then rose to go. She yawned and stretched, with nothing of a lady's grace about it, about to go back and wash and rest. "Little sister! What do you mean by stopping me at the gate?" Suddenly a young man of fine brows and starry eyes, tall and straight in white, strode into the pavilion and demanded coldly.
The young man wore a silver belt, held a black folding fan, had a black jade at his waist, and walked with composure. "This is my place. I let in whoever I please; what are you coming to me for?" Guan Ye said, waving him off impatiently. "Mother has learned you have been gathering riff-raff and is afraid you will be cheated; she sent me to watch you." the young man said with a frown.
"Riff-raff, is it? The friends I have made — Brother Chen, Brother Wei, Brother Zhang and the rest — are all men of uncommon worth. What do you know?" Guan Ye retorted. "That pack of goods just now? Men of uncommon worth?" The young man's voice rose a note. "Never mind, do as you please. I came for another thing too: to remind you that the withdrawal is about to begin. The situation is growing clear,
and you must make ready soon. The family will send people to help…" "I don't need it." Guan Ye said coolly. "I have my own poison-water squad for guards; the family need not trouble over me." "Why must you be so stubborn? One poison-water squad may not be enough. The family means to send a poison-net squad as well. Poison water and poison net together will make it certain beyond doubt." the young man said gravely.
The Guan family had once used a poison-water squad and a poison-net squad together to strike down a great master of the third breakthrough outright, in one stroke. That was how they had settled into their place as Feiye City's great merchant house, and how the Guan family had become famous — and in the house, craftsmen and poison studies had come to be held more and more important. No martial strength, however great, stands up to corrosive poison water, poison smoke and poison powder.
Hearing what her elder brother said, Guan Ye fell silent for a while, then asked: "At the latest, when?" "A month from now." …………
…… The moment Wei He went out he felt something was off. His belly was warm and glowing, and the medicine was beginning to work. At first he thought the tonic was nothing much, that its effect would pass in a while. But one hour went by, then two…
until the middle of the night, and he still felt warm all through. The potency of this strange-beast meat far surpassed any meat he had eaten before. Branch-antler deer, red-earth fish, whatever else — none of them came close.
With one pot of it inside him he slept badly all night, his body burning and restless, his spirit strong, his vigour brimming with nowhere to go. In the middle of the night Wei He suddenly opened his eyes and got out of bed. Outside all was silent — no insects, no birds, only the faint sound of his Second Sister Wei Ying's breathing from the room opposite. Wei He pushed the door open quietly, stepped around the clothes and boots hanging on the line, and walked to the middle of the yard.
