Hearing Wei He say that, he heaved a long sigh and looked at him, not knowing whether to laugh or cry. "You rascal. You would throw away a bargain as good as the Jiang family's, only to come and plunder a few plates of my meat. Come on, come on — we will talk in the carriage." It hurt his purse, and yet somehow it also left him with a strange sort of refreshment.
He called the waiter over and paid. He swore it was the most satisfying payment of his life. The gold notes in his wallet were all but emptied at a stroke, yet his mood was like drinking iced plum soup in summer — sour and bracing to the last drop. Going downstairs, he saw Jiang Yan looking down at him from the window of the second-floor private room.
Cheng Shaojiu squared his chest, and with an expressionless face went out of the door with Wei He. Without a backward look, the two got into the Cheng family carriage waiting at the entrance. Jiang Yan was a little startled; he seemed to read something in it. "What is it, Brother Yan?" asked the young gentleman across the wine table, puzzled.
"Nothing." Jiang Yan withdrew his gaze. This manner of theirs — was it a declaration of where they stood? Interesting. His heart did not stir; he lifted a helping of pale-green vegetable hearts with his chopsticks and put the whole matter behind him. If the man was unwilling, then let it be; his Jiang family was great in estate and plentiful in enterprise, and could spare a Wei He.
A good hand like him was uncommon, but not unheard of. In a Feiye City this size — a dozen or so wards, the Inner City, and a dozen-plus earthen forts beyond — could it really lack a second-breakthrough hand? Only, that Wei He, in refusing him now, would have cause to regret it later. After all, he was a fellow whose potential was used up — nothing more than a lucky breakthrough; Feiye City had seen his like before.
And even if the other powers made an offer, none would be as generous as his. "What is it? Has something been troubling you, Brother Yan?" asked the blue-robed young gentleman opposite, smiling. "Nothing. Only that I tried to recruit a junior brother, and was refused." Jiang Yan smiled and told him what had just passed. "A second-breakthrough hand, bought for five years at that price, is no loss. If he refuses, it is only that he is waiting for a better price." The blue-
robed gentleman laughed. "When he finds later that no one offers a higher price than you did, he will surely regret it. After all, he is no genius. A man of his sort will at best be a middle ranker in days to come; if he is given weight and seated in an important post he may burn a stick of high incense in thanks. He will never amount to much.
"Do not give it a thought."
"That is true as well." Jiang Yan considered it, then let it go. "But there is that side — the Flying Bear Saber, and that young genius who is a cut above even Xiao Ran. Have you any thoughts on him, Brother Yan?"
"The Flying Bear Saber… you mean Zhou Jihan, who came out of yesterday's fist-off?" Jiang Yan smiled. "My eldest brother has already taken men over
there, though I cannot promise he will be won. After all, the man's talent is even stronger than Xiao Ran's. We shall see whose means are best." That Zhou Jihan was in truth the key every house would strain for. The Flying Bear Saber was itself the greatest of the Outer City wards and its strongest martial academy. And Zhou Jihan was only sixteen, yet had reached the perfection of the second breakthrough in qi and blood, with monstrous potential ahead of him.
Most crucial of all, this man was not like Xiao Ran, proud of his talent and ready to offend a man with a few words. He was also much regarded by Old Man Yun of the Flying Bear Saber. Old Man Yun's influence was far greater than that of Old Man Zheng of Mountain Fist in Stone Bridge Ward. That Old Man Yun had even taught a good number of third-breakthrough masters, men who held important posts in every power. To be regarded by Old Man Yun… where Zhou Jihan went was no longer merely the weight one genius carried.
