People like that were to him no more than slightly stronger ants, easily harvested. In truth, in all of Feiye City the only ones he had to be wary of were the older generation of martial masters, or the experts of the Inner City's great families; the rest, even the little gangs sprouting like weeds, were not worth a mention. Wei He was still holding the old monk, asking this and that, pressing him about the stonemasons lost half a year ago.
"At that time the masons never even reached the temple; they disappeared halfway along the road. As to the benefactor's question, this old monk truly does not know how to answer it." The old monk said it with difficulty.
"Then there was not one survivor afterwards?" Wei He frowned.
"None was found. All the masons vanished on the way, and because of that, in the end this temple hired costly masons from the Outer City to finish
the Buddha images for us." The old monk shook his head.
Wei He questioned him over and over and got no lead at all, only that the masons had all gone missing on the road before ever reaching Mingde Temple. Yet from Mingde Temple to Feiye City was less than ten li, with passers-by on the road at all hours. So many masons could not simply vanish with no sign at all, not even the least trace to be found.
The likeliest explanation was that there were traces, but whoever found them at the time had no wish to make them public. Wei He felt helpless about it, and let the old monk go. He walked the temple over; two great halls were barred and closed to visitors, the rest he could pass through freely. The light was fading, and the worshippers grew fewer, most of them gone down the hill.
Wei He asked a few more monks, and still had no news, so there was nothing for it but to leave the temple and go down the hill. The sun had sunk entirely below the horizon. On the stairs down from the temple the cold pressed in from both sides, and the woods that had been clear and open had turned into a dark, deep curtain. Wei He did not notice in the least that behind him a shadowy figure was quietly, slowly falling in after him.
He felt a little chilled, and quickened his pace down the hill. Then all at once a small black worm crept out quietly from a gap in the hem of his clothes and flew off on its wings. The man behind paused slightly, and his brows drew together. "The scent's fainter…" He picked up the bamboo tube and shook it gently, and found that the scent had indeed grown faint.
"So it is not the man in front." The judgement flashed through his mind. He took his eyes off Wei He's back. The man turned and looked in the direction the black-letter worm had flown. "Hm?"
"No — there is only me and the man in front on this road. If it is not him, then who? Could this be a feint?"
The man's guard went up, and his gaze came back to rest on Wei He's back. Doubt had entered him. So he quickened his step and came up near Wei He. "Brother, hold on a moment," he called out loudly, as if he were a worshipper.
Puff! All at once a handful of white lime came flying at his face. The man's heart tightened; caught off guard, he shut his eyes tight and very nearly had them blinded. But being a man who trained the fists, he was quick as anything: before the lime could reach his eyes he swept both arms in succession, beating up a fast wind that scattered the lime away.
He knew in his heart by now that the other man had very likely seen through him. Fury rose in him, and he whipped his right leg up and lashed it at Wei He like a whip. But his right leg met a blade first; the man's face changed, he switched his move fast, pressed the leg down to try to stamp on Wei He's instep, and at the same time threw his other leg up and spun it in a side kick at Wei He's chest.
This was the Life-Taking Chain Kick he had made his speciality. The technique could switch from one move into the next in a chain, shifting and unpredictable, now false, now true, impossible for a man to hold off. The man kicked out in a chain, fast, kicking and lashing a dozen times over. When he opened his eyes and looked, there was no one before him; the man of a moment ago had been gone no one knew how long.
"Curse him! So treacherous…" He had had his eyes shut no more than an instant and the other man was gone out of sight. Was the fellow a rabbit spirit in human shape? Cursing, the man hurried down and gave chase along the stairs with all his strength.
