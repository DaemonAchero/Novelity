Only two men, and yet they gave him the feeling of coming upon a great beast. Wei He was no new hand at hunting now; up the mountain he had come across several large beasts — grey bears, black tigers. The beasts of this world were a full circle bigger than those he had known before: grey bears of three metres and more, giant tigers whose bodies measured three metres without the tail.
Every one of them looked as if it were pumped full of hormones, monstrously strong and fast besides. Wei He had not dared to meet one head-on; if he caught sight of one from far off he would give it a wide berth in good time. He had confidence in his own strength, but not the confidence to walk up and face that kind of beast on impulse. Especially after he watched a grey bear shatter with one paw a great slab of bluestone that he used for his own practice — after that he grew more careful still.
At his full strength he could at most leave a fist print on a slab of the same size, and could not split it. Poison could kill such a thing, true, but then the meat would be spoiled; so it was better to steer clear for now and come back for the hunt after his breakthrough, when he was sure of himself — think of them as stores of food laid by. Only once the two had left did Wei He walk slowly into the inner yard.
"Master Zheng." He said it softly. Zheng Fugui's heart was knotted with gloom, but at the sound of the voice he still did not want to pass his feelings on to a disciple of the inner yard. He settled himself, turned, and worked up a smile for Wei He. "What is it, Xiao He? Something not going right in your training?"
"Nothing much. Only, Master Zheng, it's time I drew this month's red-earth fish…" Wei He said, with a helpless air. He did not want to be asking for meat month after month either, but there was no help for it: he had no way to hunt a strange beast. And having seen the great beasts up the mountain, he now doubted whether he could kill even a poisonless strange beast. So it was better to keep his head down, break through first and act after.
"Go and draw it from Auntie Hong." Zheng Fugui looked at Wei He; his mouth opened to ask how the boy's progress stood — but remembering the state of things the last time he had looked, the urge to ask suddenly left him. 'If all the red-earth fish went to Xiao Ran, could he go one step further?' His own share, and Wei He's, all of it together, given to Xiao Ran — perhaps…
The thought came up in Zheng Fugui all at once; but after a moment's hesitation he put it aside. Xiao Ran was hanging on in so many places that this little bit of red-earth fish meant nothing to him… whereas for Wei He, with the Cheng family's strange-beast meat gone, this portion was not something to be shorted on his qi and blood. Zheng Fugui waved a hand, wearily, and let Wei He go.
All his hopes now rested on Xiao Ran: if Xiao Ran broke through, then the Mountain Fist school would after all have someone to carry it on. And with an Iron-Hide master of the third qi-and-blood breakthrough sitting in the Mountain Fist yard, he himself could spare the time to go about other business, instead of being nailed to one spot every day and unable to stir. The two men of the Zhao family, one from the second branch and one from the third, were both Zhao masters of the third qi-and-blood breakthrough, skilled in the art of striking in concert, and their fierceness was known through the whole Seven
Family League. With the two of them joining hands against him — a jin-refining martial master with half a body already in the ground — the outcome was not so sure a thing after all. Wei He left the inner yard with a sack of things in hand, went home first, and then went on to the Inner City. His Realm Breaking Bead now wanted only a little to be complete, and to complete it as soon as he could he had decided to spend money on branch-antler deer meat and take it in at the same time as the
breakthrough. He had been saving a long while, and scraped a little money together at last, just enough to buy up a whole month's portion, carry it home and eat it slowly, aiming to break through in one go. He went along streets growing emptier by the day, in from the main thoroughfare that ran the whole length of the city.
Soon Wei He was inside the Inner City. The Inner City, once so flourishing, seemed to have taken some of the strain as well. Everything there was said to be self-sufficient; Feiye City had two city gates of its own, given over entirely to the Inner City's use, so that its supply of goods and services would be secure. Inner City it was called, but the place kept growing, and had by now come to half the size of the whole of Feiye City.
The outer wards grew more and more desolate, and a great mass of the population were slowly reduced to service hands serving the Inner City. Those who could find no way to live either went far away or joined the small forts and the various powers outside; the rest scraped a living out of little trades.
