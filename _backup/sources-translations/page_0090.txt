"Xiao He, where did you go just now? I heard noises outside and took a fright; I wanted to find you, but there was no one to be seen." Wei Ying relaxed and hurried over. All at once she saw specks of blood on the clothes at Wei He's chest. "Oh! Why is there blood on you?!"
She was greatly alarmed. Wei He's face did not change; he pinched his own nose hard. "It is a nosebleed I had just now… mm…" He tilted his head back at once and let a drop or two of blood from his nostril run down. "Oh dear, you really are bleeding from the nose. How…!" Wei Ying was worried at once.
"Wait, I will find something to plug it with." She turned and hurried off to look for something to stop the nose. In a flurry of hands she soon had Wei He's nostril stuffed, and only then let out a breath. "How did it come to bleeding from the nose? Can the weather be so dry?" She thought she would stew something for her brother tomorrow to clear the heat from him — the weather was so dry, perhaps his inner fire had risen.
Wei He sat down and felt what it was to have broken through to Stone-Hide, the second breakthrough in qi and blood. Blood ran murmuring through his whole body, and it seemed that every moment his body was being remade under the working of the greater mass of qi and blood — remade without pause: a larger vessel of a body, tougher skin, more explosive power, keener five senses, lighter limbs.
'So this is what breaking through feels like…?' He bent his head and pulled his collar open to look at the pattern of the Realm Breaking Bead, emptied once more. The second emptying of the Bead left him with a hollow feeling at heart. 'I had meant to try breaking through on my own, slowly… but it was not to be… this world does not allow it…' he sighed inwardly.
Early the next morning. He rose very early and set off for the Mountain Fist yard. He had no intention of hiding his strength for use as a trump card: the more a time like this, the more a man needed to show his potential and so draw a greater share of the resources.
To hide one's strength always was rather to be denied the fuller nourishment that let the body grow. The door creaked as he pushed it open. Wei He saw that Li Jue and two other disciples had come early and were moving the training gear that belonged to them. He went over and lent a hand, and only then looked about for Cheng Shaojiu and Ouyang Zhuang.
Alas, he had come early, it seemed: Cheng Shaojiu had not arrived at all and Ouyang Zhuang was not there either, likely off on leave or at his outside work. Old Man Zheng had taken his men to the fist-off some days ago, and now things would no doubt go back to the old way, with him dawdling in only when the sun was three poles high. Wei He rested a while; since no one was there he did not waste the time, and set to training on his own.
It was not that he wished to show off his strength; it was that after breaking through to Stone-Hide the later training drills, the ways of raising qi and blood, the skin-grinding methods, were all different, and he would have to tell Old Man Zheng before he could be given the methods of the next stage. So unless he meant to stand still where he was, he must tell Old Man Zheng how far he had come.
Cheng Shaojiu, for his part, had no time now for any other business: he was at odds with his uncle over family matters. Cheng Zhengxing, chief escort master of the Yonghe Agency and Cheng Shaojiu's uncle, had no son of his own and treated Cheng Shaojiu alone as if he were his own child.
In ordinary days he did not mind what the young master of the agency wanted to do, or if he kept a few idlers. When business was good and money plentiful, keeping a few idlers was neither here nor there. But now that the agency's business was worse than before, and the boy still dragged his good-for-nothing cronies home to be kept, calling it loyalty and honour, that went too far.
Over this matter Cheng Zhengxing had already argued with Cheng Shaojiu many times, but the fellow simply would not hear such reasoning. In the great hall of the Yonghe Agency. Cheng Zhengxing tried to make his look severe, but face to face with his only nephew, the anger in him would not weigh heavy however he tried.
