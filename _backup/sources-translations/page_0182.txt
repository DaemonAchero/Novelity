Wei He said nothing, and set about the skin grinding and the condensing of red-cloud flowers with care. The quality of this third breakthrough's qi and blood was plainly far above the second's; his fists beat again and again into the iron sand, and the hot sand ground at his skin, and each time he finished a round and put his hands into the medicine basin he could feel qi and blood gathering in a steady stream into his forearms. Every man's red-cloud flower sits somewhere different — Cheng Shaojiu's is on his back; he had never seen Jiang Su's; the eldest senior brother Zhao Hong's
is on his chest. Wei He's own sat on the upper side of his arms, on his forearms. Qi and blood of that quality, digestion and blood-making of that speed, and then the third breakthrough, which seemed to have opened every part of his body up, qi and blood flowing far faster with much that had blocked and slowed it gone from the middle —
Wei He could at times feel plainly the progress of a red-cloud flower condensing, bit by bit. Slow, steady, unstoppable. He trained straight on until the afternoon was done. His upper right arm felt scalding, as if something in it were condensing — but when he lifted his sleeve to look, there was nothing to be seen.
In the afternoon Master Zheng had gone out, and near the end of it he came back with a dark look on his face, as if something had displeased him. When Wei He had finished his training and was about to tidy up and go, he was called into the inner room by a junior brother on his way out: Master Zheng had sent for him. Zheng Fugui went through a warning with the inner-yard disciples one by one — for the time being they were to keep together as much as they could, not to go about alone, not to go out at night, and to mind their safety.
There was nothing beyond that, but Wei He had the sense that something was coming. After he went home, day after day the Mountain Fist yard seemed as though nothing had happened at all. Apart from Master Zheng, who seemed a little older, everything else was as it had been.
Fortunately there was Zhang Lu, newly promoted and as much a prodigy as Xiao Ran, which seemed to have given Master Zheng a fresh hope. Wei He paid these things no mind now; he had broken through the third qi-and-blood breakthrough, and had gone back to training Mountain Fist, where his qi and blood condensed far faster than before. In only four days he had condensed his second red-cloud flower. And the red-cloud flowers grow harder one after another — each
takes more qi and blood to condense than the one before. Yet he had reached the second so easily and so quickly, which proved his thinking entirely workable: the crooked first, the straight after, the two joined into one. Nor was that all. In his spare time Wei He had begun training the Flying Dragon Art as well, and that leg art too, fed by his powerful qi-and-blood supply,
broke through again and again in quick succession — ten days or so and he was at the second layer. The Flying Dragon Art calls for blood-red dragon lines to be condensed on the legs; the principle is the same as with the red-cloud flowers, and the dragon lines are the mark of the art's layers. The art is extremely hard, making the greatest demands on talent and on temperament; and if one breakthrough fails, the qi and blood turn back on the man and lance into his heart —
it is very dangerous. The art has six layers in all; each new dragon line is another storey up. Step, Walk, Run, Cross, Skim, Fly. Six layers, each answering to a speed in a realm of its own. The art goes at most as far as the third qi-and-blood breakthrough, but leg arts are an exceedingly rare category.
This leg art, as Old Man Zheng had said, all leg arts that can enter the jin are precious things. For a truly formidable leg art can attack when it advances and hold when it retreats, putting a man in a position that cannot be lost; and it comes and goes like the wind, so that support and withdrawal from the field are both very fast. Wei He had therefore set his heart on the Flying Dragon Art: there was no art for it beyond entering the jin, but if the third breakthrough could be forced through with the Realm Breaking Bead,
that would be good as well. Training it back up from the third breakthrough, he broke through two layers of the Flying Dragon Art with great ease, and his speed quickened a good deal too; after that it slowed somewhat. Still steady progress with nothing in the way of an obstacle, but the pace fell off noticeably.
And against this he grew all the more wary of other masters of the third breakthrough. Seen that way, the older a third-breakthrough master was, the more likely he had spent great time, and qi and blood, training a second, even a third martial art.
