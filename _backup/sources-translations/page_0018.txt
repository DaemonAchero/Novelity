"And then there's Second Senior Sister Zheng Hongxiu, the master's granddaughter. Her fists are at Stone-Hide too; only her build is small from birth, so she has less strength and is a shade weaker — weaker than me, still, by a long way."
"Second Senior Sister seems to come here very seldom?" Wei He asked.
"Mm, seldom. She keeps to another yard; the women disciples are all over there." Cheng Shaojiu nodded. He knocked Wei He's right hand aside as it came in, then remembered something and made a stop sign.
"That business you asked me to look into — there's word of it. The carvers who went out beyond the city last year: a group of them did go missing. Whether they're dead or alive nobody knows, and no bodies were found round about either."
"…Missing… Is that a common thing?" Wei He's face did not change, but his eyes sank a little.
"Now and then. It's wild out there. Bandits, mountain brigands, highway thieves, savage beasts, poisonous things, and the gangs — lose your grip on yourself for one moment and the man is gone." Cheng Shaojiu sighed as he said it. Beyond the city wall was a subject people half avoided: the tall wall of Feiye City shut out a world of war and confusion, of dangers lying in wait at every step.
Inside the walls things were disorderly too, but at least a man could find some way to live. Outside was another matter; the danger there was many, many times higher.
"And there's this too. That Chen Biao you've had me watching — he joined the Horned Snake Gang lately, didn't he? My cousin tells me the Horned Snake Gang was fighting the Green Bamboo Gang over turf in Nanshan Ward yesterday, and a good many of them got broken. That Chen Biao was in it. You might want to keep an eye on that."
Cheng Shaojiu's cousin belonged to Green Bamboo, and was even a small boss among them. He liked to brag, but his word was reliable enough.
"Nanshan Ward? Thank you!" Wei He nodded gravely. Truth to tell, Cheng Shaojiu had helped him a great deal over these months. He had paid it back by doing the man's coursework, but set the two side by side and what he gave back was nothing much at all.
"What are you thanking me for." Cheng Shaojiu waved it off. "Honestly, I know my own case well enough. A taste of this, a taste of that; I want to learn everything and I am good at none of it. But there is one virtue in me: I like making friends. A friend in trouble — if there's anything I can do, I do it, no two words about it!" He patted his chest. "Of course, and in truth, I do judge who I help. Men who are no use to anyone, I can't be bothered with." He smiled, and with meaning in it,
he glanced towards a corner. There sat a thin, dark youth — a newcomer Old Man Zheng had brought in himself last month. His name was Xiao Ran. The name itself was of an entirely different order from the rest of them; hear it and you heard a scholar's air.
And that was not the chief of it. The chief of it was that Xiao Ran's gift and understanding far outran the whole pack of them. Anything taught him he had at the first pass, and in training he was quick to the hand and quick to master it. Old Man Zheng prized him like a treasure found, gave him private lessons again and again, and treated him wholly as his last disciple. Beside the way he was with ordinary disciples like themselves, the two were not the same at all.
"Junior Brother Xiao Ran is not like us." Wei He shook his head. When he first came in he too had dreamed he might be a genius; the truth, alas, had dealt him a heavy blow. His own gift was no different from Cheng Shaojiu's — lost in the crowd.
"That Xiao Ran! I asked him out for a drink once, and the boy lectured me in front of everyone for never training properly, for wasting money and wasting effort. In front of the whole crowd I very nearly slapped him across the face!" Even now Cheng Shaojiu burned a little at the memory.
"He's only fifteen, isn't he? Probably too young," Wei He said.
"Fifteen is not so young. At fifteen I was already betrothed!" Cheng Shaojiu snapped. "Never mind him. Later, when we've finished, let's go and have a cup together?"
"No. I've things to do." Wei He shook his head.
"Things? Don't I know you. It's not a day for collecting assignments, and you've nothing on, so what things? Honestly, what I cannot stand is those sanctimonious types who go on about how busy they are, how they must train their fists, and then slip out behind everyone's back to drink flower wine. There's a rare few of that sort in this yard of ours, let me tell you." Cheng Shaojiu was off on his grumbling again.
