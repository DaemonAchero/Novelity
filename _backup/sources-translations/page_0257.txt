Thirty li north-west of Feiye City, in the barren hills. Krak. Lightning cut across the night sky. Rain came down in swarms, striking leaves and grass with a clatter.
Inside a cave in the hills. Wei He carefully set the black bear hide he had just skinned over the fire to dry. Two bear cubs huddled together in terror, pressed into the deepest part of the cave. "This weather turns the moment it likes. A while ago it was drought, and now, after so little time, it rains every day."
He frowned and looked out of the cave mouth. The night was deep, and the steam of the downpour blurred the woods, so nothing could be seen clearly. "Thank goodness you are here, Senior Brother Wei, or we truly would not know what to do." Ouyang Lin looked relieved beyond words. If Wei He had not said on the road, out of nowhere, that it might rain, they might still be trudging on like fools, and never have looked for a place to shelter.
"Senior Brother, what about the two little bears here?" Ouyang Lin looked at the two bear cubs deep in the cave; their pitiful look made her hold back. "They die in a while. Young meat tastes good." Wei He answered. "Why kill them… can we not leave them?" Wei Ying said, also unwilling.
"That works too, but bear cubs this small will not live long out there." Wei He replied. Jiang Su and Zhang Qi slept soundly against the cave wall not far from the fire. The two of them had to rest while they could; later they had to get up and keep watch, taking turns so Wei He could rest. Out in wild country like this, someone had to stay awake at all times, or there could be great danger.
A fire is not completely safe. Some fierce beasts, and strange beasts, hardly fear it. Wei He, Ouyang Lin and Wei Ying sat around the fire, listening to the rain and thunder outside and the odd howl of some nameless beast. In a place so dangerous, with Wei He at hand, one could still feel a deep sense of safety.
He arranged everything neatly: what to do when, what had to be done when. He seemed to know it all. "Little He, how long will we walk like this, always on — how long?" Wei Ying asked softly. "I do not know… half a year at the least." Wei He said, low. In fact that was the low count. Once on the road he found he had overrated himself. This way he had chosen had the escort agency's markings, but few people walked it, and the whole road was wild: weeds and brush packed thick, hiding the path. He had to find the way, correct his direction and also watch for fierce beasts and poisonous insects, all at once. The five of them moved so slowly it was more than twice as slow as he had expected. "Half a year…" Wei Ying lowered her face, arms around her knees, and stared at the fire. "So long…"
"Let us hope all goes well." Ouyang Lin reached over and took Wei Ying's hand, squeezing hard to comfort her. Over these days the two of them, both gentle girls, had drawn closest, being so alike. Jiang Su was a girl too, but she was far tougher and harder than either of them, and there was little for her to talk about with the two. Zhang Qi had grown quieter and quieter since his brother died. He could have gone back to Feiye City to find his parents.
But he had chosen not to go back, and instead decided to leave with Wei He. There was always some hunger in his eyes — hunger for the Seven Family League, for revenge. Yet he knew very well that staying in Feiye City, relying on himself alone, he could never take revenge, no matter how long he waited. So when he learned Wei He was going to the prefectural city, he did not know what moved him, but he decided to follow along.
The fire burned stronger and stronger. Outside, the rain slowly eased. Wei He rose and walked to the deepest part of the cave, lifted a bear cub in each hand and carried them out. "Go on." He patted the two little bears on the head and set them loose. "It is still raining outside…" Wei Ying began, and stopped.
"A bit of rain does no harm. I do not want these two, while I am out, kicking up a fuss in the den and drawing trouble down on us." Wei He replied. The meat from one black bear was enough to feed five people a long while, but eating only meat brings many illnesses, so he still had to go out for wild vegetables and mushrooms and the like.
