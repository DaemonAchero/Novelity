Only their weapons were more complete, their leather cuirasses a little newer, and a pair of black cloth boots on their feet — that and nothing more. With soldiers like these, Wei He simply could not believe that the safety of Feiye City rested on them. The train moved on, and the carts being escorted shuddered and rattled as they rolled over stones in the road. Gradually the sky moved toward afternoon, and at last the escort train reached its destination.
By the rules they were to rest a short while at the place, take on supplies, and then go back. Wei He used this spare time to tell Chen Shiniu he was going, and set off alone for Mingde Temple. Since everything within ten li of the walls counted as safe, how was it that his parents had gone missing at Mingde Temple? Now that he was here, he meant to look into it quickly.
== Chapter 13 · Mingde (Part 1) ==
Mingde Temple stands to the south of Feiye City, set among hill woods, with a boundless sea of trees and mountains at its back.
Round about it there was only one mountain road, winding out to its gate. The sun was going down now, and red light lay over everything. Before the temple gate a scattered few worshippers were leaving. It was already afternoon; few came to burn incense at this hour.
Because out in the wild, night was far more dangerous than day. Just then, at the mouth of Mingde Temple's hill path, stood a tall, powerfully built youth, half grown. He wore dark green hemp clothes and a broad conical bamboo hat, carried a rough short knife at his waist, and walked light-footed up to the temple gate. His face was ordinary, but his eyes were bright; one look and you knew he had not come out of some ordinary, half-starved household.
But who would have guessed that a few months ago this same young man had been thin and small, weak as a bean sprout, with no strength to speak of? This youth was Wei He, who had slipped away from the train. He had run the whole way, spending no little effort, to reach Mingde Temple in so short a time. Step by step up the grey stone stairs, Wei He soon came before a broad temple with yellow tiles and red walls.
On the plaque over the main gate, in red characters on a black ground, were three large words: Mingde Temple. Two young novice monks were sweeping fallen leaves outside the gate with brooms, piling them into a heap. Wei He stood outside and looked in. Past the temple gate was a great courtyard, and in it a black incense burner as wide as two men's arms could reach around, in which a great bundle of incense heads was burning slowly.
The last thin smoke of the incense heads drifted up; it seemed nearly burnt out. Just then two more worshippers arrived together, stepped over the temple gate into the courtyard, bowed to the incense burner, made their reverence, and went into a side hall. Wei He did not follow them in. He turned left and began to wander, walking round the temple wall into the hill woods.
The two young novices swept and talked in low voices as they went, paying his movements no mind at all. Wei He made a full circuit of Mingde Temple and found the place recently repaired; in many places the work looked barely finished. The monks' robes, too, were of no poor make. Plainly, money was not a problem here.
Coming back round to the main gate, Wei He was about to go in when he saw a small squad of soldiers come out of it. These soldiers were thick-waisted and broad-shouldered, big of frame, in black regulation leather cuirasses with sheathed broad-backed sabres at their belts, and they moved quickly and with training.
Six soldiers in all came out one after another, guarding a pair of women between them. Of the two women, one wore a jade-green skirt and red shoes and had her hair bound in a maiden's bun, following the other woman step for step; plainly a maid or the like. The other woman wore a white veil, her black hair over her shoulders, with silver hair ornaments faintly showing among the strands.
Even veiled, the mild and courteous air in this woman's eyes made Wei He look at her a few extra times without meaning to. Seen from a distance, the woman at the centre of the soldiers wore a white dress that was spotless; it was plain that someone at home was kept to wash her clothes for her.
