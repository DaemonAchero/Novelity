"I once swore I would make something of myself in this life! And now — I have been churning all my life, and what is left of it?" "That is right, I do not think I have said where that place is, have I? Look at my memory…" Zheng Fugui laughed a few times and patted his own head.
"That place is…" He put out his hand and suddenly pulled a stack of things out of his own clothes. "Right here!" Zheng Fugui looked at the blank faces of Wei He and Jiang Su and burst out laughing.
He waved what he held. "You two — you… have only this much spirit in you!" Laughing on, blood ran from his eyes, his nose and his ears. Wei Ying clapped a hand over her mouth and turned her head away.
The laughter slowly died. Zheng Fugui's eyes stayed open, his hand still gripped hard on the stack wrapped in fur. He sat there stiff and still, without breath. Wei He and Jiang Su stepped back, knelt, and knocked their heads to the ground before him. "Teacher, walk well!"
Wei He said it calmly. "Teacher… walk well." Jiang Su could no longer speak for weeping. The firelight wavered, and on Zheng Fugui's pale face there was a faint trace of relief, and a trace of not wanting to let go.

An arrow arched up from far away, drawing a fine curve over the Feiye River and over the wide empty ground before the city gate, and struck hard into the wooden gate tower on the wall of Feiye City.
With a dull thock, a guard on the wall opened his sleepy eyes without much strength, stuffed the grass cake in his hand into his mouth in a few bites and looked outside. From the tower, on a wall ten-odd metres high, he looked off into the distance.
In the distance all was waste; the wide stretch of good fields lay cracked and barren, and on the great road down the middle something seemed to be rising into the air. It was a solid black mass — like locusts, and yet like… "Enemy attack!!" The guard gave a fierce shout and pulled at the alarm bell with all his might, striking it again and again. In the Inner City, where the fire had only just been put out, the soldiers surged again.
The blue smoke had not yet cleared when squad after squad of soldiers, led by their squad leaders, rushed out and climbed to their places along the wall. Props were slowly set against the gate, and rolling stones were hauled up onto the thousand-metre wall piece by piece. Logs, boiling metal, fire oil — a stream of defence goods gathered without pause. Just then a great black rain of arrows arched down out of the sky like raindrops, thick as could be, and cut down great numbers of the men on the wall.
War drums sounded. Outside the city three officers of powerful build, backs like tigers and waists like bears, galloped up on horseback and stopped before the wall. One young officer of a lithe, quick build spurred his horse a step forward and looked at the heads packed on Feiye City's wall above, thick as bees. He reached out a hand.
An officer beside him handed him a long spear. "The people of Feiye are hard pressed; I cannot bear to harm the innocent." He took the spear, walked his horse in a few circles on the spot, and suddenly shouted aloud.
