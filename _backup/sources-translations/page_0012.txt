"Yes — but there's a technique to the lifting too. When you lift, go slow. Take it easy. And there's a bit of the Mountain Fist method in what I'm about to say. Remember it."
Zhao Hong led Wei He, seriously, to a corner where stones of every size were piled up.
"The method goes: body like a drifting willow, light as the wind; both feet rooted, and the strength must move." He recited it like a rhyme.
"…" Wei He had nothing to say. The verse was not quite what he had imagined.
"Try it first."
Zhao Hong took up a stone about as wide as his own body and hugged it against the middle of his forearms, then raised and lowered it. A boulder as thick as a man's embrace, and in his hands it went up and down with ease, soft and floating — and though his upper body moved, his feet were planted as if rooted, fixed to the same spot.
Wei He thought he was beginning to see.
"This is strength training?" he asked.
"This is it. Just find a stone that suits you." Zhao Hong explained. "After strength comes stances, then skin grinding, then footwork, and only at the end real fighting. All the way through you have to train until your qi and blood run thick, until you can break a wooden post with your bare fists and feel no pain. That is about when you're ready for the next step."
"Eldest Senior Brother, is there any marker for this thick qi and blood? How do I know how far mine has got?" Wei He asked carefully.
"Mountain Fist has a few grades: Ox-Hide, Stone-Hide, Iron-Hide. They refer to how hard your fists can become. You can test yourself against them later on. Once you reach Ox-Hide, your qi and blood are enough."
"Understood." Wei He nodded. "So when can I begin?"
"Now." Zhao Hong wasted no time at all.
Wei He did not say another word. He stepped up, and with Zhao Hong's help, picked a stone.
He trained the whole afternoon. Before long his whole body ached and his limbs had no strength left. And then the strange part came.
Besides teaching Mountain Fist, Old Man Zheng's place also supplied a special medicinal soup called Lesser Myriad Decoction. Anyone who had paid could get it — one bowl a day. Zhao Hong brought him a bowl, and after Wei He drank it down, the very next morning he had no soreness left at all.
At once he understood: this world might look much like the one he had left, but in the details it had to have its own peculiar strengths.
Old Man Zheng of Mountain Fist could make a living teaching people, keep a big name, and charge those prices — there had to be something extraordinary in him. A single year's tuition, after all, was equal to everything a family of three spent in a year.
The times were bad; silver had lost much of its worth, and market prices had slid into chaos. Yet the old man still lived very comfortably. His ability to make money spoke for itself.
From that day on, Wei He trained hard with Zhao Hong every day, resting only one day in three. On those holidays he went on doing assignments for the young masters and young ladies, and stealing lessons in letters while he was at it. He earned on one side and spent on the other, and somehow still came out a little ahead.
As for Second Sister Wei Ying, she simply moved into a room near Old Man Zheng's place. The room belonged to him; seeing that she asked no wages, he let her live there free. It was a bit run-down, but better than being harassed by Chen Biao and his pair.
With everything settled for the time being, Wei He threw himself into training with a single mind.
As for Chen Biao's three, in the short term he had no strength to deal with them. It did not matter. There would be a long time ahead.
The days went by, one after another. Wei He put his head down and trained, day after day.
Before dawn every morning he was up, tempering bone and muscle.
Old Man Zheng provided meals, but he still prepared meat cakes for himself besides, eating a little after training — to keep up with what his growing body demanded, and to feed the Realm Breaking Bead as well.
A great deal of silver went out, and as the time passed Wei He's frame grew sturdier and sturdier. The Realm Breaking Bead on his chest slowly darkened too. Only it still had not reached the pure black that meant it could be used.
And seeing that he had taken root at the Zheng courtyard, Chen Biao and his two drew in their horns a little. Still, every time he and his sister went home, he could feel Chen Biao's furtive, ill-meaning eyes on them.
In the blink of an eye, more than three months had gone by.
