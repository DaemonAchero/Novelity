The girl explained it all smoothly, in the practised way of someone who had said it many times over. A relief token? Would the Tongcheng Gang, one of the Three Gangs and Two Sects, be so kind-hearted? Wei He did not believe it. In this world there was no goodwill without a cause, and there had to be a scheme behind it.
"If the customer has no relief token, you can also pay in Inner City gold beans," the girl reminded him. Gold beans Wei He did have. He had killed a good many mountain bandits of late, and the commonest thing in the money pouches he took off them was this: things called gold beans that were in fact only copper bits alloyed with gold.
Ten of them would buy one gold leaf, and ten gold leaves one tael of gold; it was the simplest currency in circulation in the Inner City. That the Inner City still kept its prices steady at all rested on the Seven Family League holding firm. How much longer the Seven Family League could hold, though, was another matter. Wei He sighed inwardly and ordered a pot of buckwheat tea, set it on the table and drank quietly. It cost one gold bean, and there was change besides.
The owner threw in a dish of peanuts for nothing. The peanuts had a musty smell about them; the pot of buckwheat tea, on the other hand, was packed full of buckwheat and coarse grain, so that once the liquid was gone it could serve as a meal. Wei He settled down comfortably, meaning to hear out properly how the city stood these days. Places like wine houses and tea houses were just the spot for idle talk
and the bragging that went with it, and were of course the gathering places where news circulated. The sort who came to such places were men with no worry about food or drink and nothing to do with themselves — idlers. Men like that had time and to spare, and since their families went wanting neither for food nor clothes, they were no common households, but had means and standing behind them. Being what they were, they were naturally well informed.
Once such men met with anything amusing or strange, they would come here to spread it about and share it with friends, and by that become the centre of everyone's attention and get their fill of vanity and of the satisfaction of standing high. And their gathering drew in, in turn, the men who made a trade of them: storytellers, wandering singers, sellers of songs and the like. More ways to be entertained drew in yet more custom.
And these storytellers and their kind, who tramped north and south, could bring in yet more news and information, of uncertain truth. That was the channel Wei He wanted. Now that he was at Stone-Hide anyway, with no Master Zheng at his back to drive him on, his free time was plentiful; a day spent looking into the situation was a necessary thing. …
… The Yonghe Escort Agency. On a rather bleak flagstoned square.
Several of the great escort masters were there, and the main-line men of the Cheng family. No few escort men had come as well, from the agency's branches in the various wards. Scores of them, close on a hundred, stood gathered in a black press of heads, all come to wait for one piece of news. Cheng Shaojiu stood in the front rank of the Cheng family's men, waiting quietly for word — for word of the chief escort master, Cheng Zhengxing.
Before long Cheng Zhengxing came in from outside the square with several escort men. His face was stiff, and he said not a word as he walked the whole way towards the wooden speaking platform at the highest point. The escort men about him cleared a path for him one after another without being asked. When Cheng Zhengxing came level with Cheng Shaojiu he paused, glanced at his nephew, and looked as if he would say something.
But in the end he did not speak, and walked on towards the high platform. Up on that platform, Cheng Zhengxing climbed step by step and stood at the very top, looking down at them all. The crowd's eyes gathered on him in turn.
The hush that had already fallen grew deeper still, until it was almost frightening. Everyone held his breath a little, waiting for the final word. Cheng Zhengxing looked out over them: this great escort force had been built up by his own two hands, over decades of striving.
