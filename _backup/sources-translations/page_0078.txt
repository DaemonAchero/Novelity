Wei He was in his own courtyard, stripped to the waist, working away at the sandbag punch by punch. Soon there came knocking at his own front gate as well. He stirred at the sound, but his face was calm as he went over and opened the door. "Who is it?"
Outside stood a knot of men and women with sticks and kitchen knives in their hands, fierce-faced and all set to demand of whoever was inside whether they had heard or seen what was going on next door. But when the door opened and they saw the solid, powerfully built man standing there, his bulk beside them nearly the equal of two of their scrawny selves,
the woman at their head found her manner softening without her meaning it to. "Little brother, might I ask whether you heard anyone being beaten next door just now? Did you see anyone run out of the house next door?"
"No. Anything else?" Wei He was all impatience, wearing the look of a man about to hit someone if his time were wasted any longer. The several of them outside were frightened by it, and for a moment hardly dared speak.
Bang! The door was slammed shut with force. Wei He closed it without another word. Outside the gate the group flinched in fright, yet not one of them dared say a hard word.
In fact the one who led them had seen, the very moment the door opened, the black-belt training suit of the Mountain Fist yard hanging to dry in the courtyard. That was the crux of her change in manner. These black-belt entered disciples of the fist yard were every one of them formidable hands; pull any one of them out at random and he could make easy work of five or six of their sort.
What was more, those fist-yard disciples had ties with rich houses and men of power, and were often hired as heads of house guards or as temporary bodyguards, so they were not lightly to be offended. Before long the group were looking at one another, unable to vent their feelings, and with their temper pressed down inside them they went off to knock at other doors.
Soon the neighbourhood was again a din of chickens flying and dogs leaping. Wei He paid it no more mind, settled his heart, and went back to his punches. After this round of beatings, more than ten days went by and Wei He did not once set eyes on Xu Chun again.
He was a good deal easier about Wei Ying's safety as well, and she went on opening the bun shop day by day as before. The days ran on like a stream, slowly flowing past. By degrees the prices in the Outer City seemed to steady, and some people began to use a mixed flour ground from every sort of coarse grain and bean as the basic medium of exchange; barter of goods for goods circulated in great quantity.
Mixed-grain flour was traded for white rice, for meat, for everything else, and a market of it slowly took shape. Wei He noticed that however things were outside, the food at the Mountain Fist yard stayed fixed and unchanging: every day the same measure of rice and meat, no more and no less. Only there was one new rule — nothing was to be taken away.
Old Man Zheng still wore his careful, close-reckoning look, lounging about all day in his easy chair gnawing chicken feet. Where he got so many chicken feet, no one knew. Winter quickly ran through the greater part of itself. Wei He's qi and blood advanced another step, moving slowly toward completion.
Slow, but steady. After all, the Realm Breaking Bead was complete; now he had only to gather his qi and blood to the top, and he could break through Ox-Hide and go a step further. Hope was before his eyes, and with the small advances he could feel each day besides, it gave Wei He a pleasure like playing a game in his former life. In a blink winter was gone, and the weather showed not the least sign of winter's cold — it was still all heat.
The uncanny weather had brought the Feiye River down by more than half its level. The water grew muddier and muddier. Grain in the markets grew scarcer and scarcer. Even the money and meat Wei He drew from the Cheng family each month began to shrink. Plainly the Yonghe Agency was finding things hard too.
