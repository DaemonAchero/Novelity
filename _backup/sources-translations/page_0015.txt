Manager Qian spoke with helplessness in his voice. "If they come on you face to face and you don't put out a little silver, the lot of them will block your door every day and never leave. You may as well forget about doing any business."
"…" Wei He had nothing to answer. He bought the medicines he had come for from the manager, counted out the silver, packed it all up, and went back out of the shop.
On his way home he saw the procession of the Incense Gathering Sect again, far off. A dense, packed line at a glance — two hundred of them at the least, drawn out in a long file like a snake. Just then they had come up against several constables who had stepped forward shouting at them. The leading few sect members were being shoved this way and that, and looked about to be seized and hauled away.
Through the rest of the procession, a wrongness seemed to stir, faint and unaccountable. Wei He took one look from far off and did not dare look longer; he hurried his steps towards the yard of Zheng of Mountain Fist. He had not gone far when a shriek rose up behind him.
"They're killing people!!"
Then came cursing, shouting, screaming. Wei He quickened his pace and broke into a faster trot, hurrying back. He had trained Mountain Fist three months and no more; he had only just worked through the basic forms and had never once fought for real. If he were swept up in that, who knew what would come of him.
He ran the whole way back to the yard without stopping and delivered the medicines to the inner room, and settled the accounts. Only when he came out did he find a fine sweat standing on his back. Whether it was from fright or from the running, he could not say. Having lived one life already beyond this one, he knew better than most what a sect like that could do once it rose — in an age like this.
"What's this?" Third Senior Brother Cheng Shaojiu looked him over, puzzled that a trip to buy medicine should leave a man so heated.
"Ran too hard, most likely," Wei He said, low.
"Carry on, then." He went to his own place, and took up a stone again and trained along with the rest. Evening came.
When his practice was done, Wei He drank his bowl of decoction, put on his clothes and made ready to go and collect the assignments. Going out at intervals to gather assignments and earn his money had become his long-term trade. Only today he was uncommonly restless. In his head the thing he had seen played over and over — that long, long file of the Incense Gathering Sect.
"I have to grow strong, and soon." His hand went unconsciously to the Realm Breaking Bead mark on his chest. After all this time of storing up, the bead was only half turned to black; what was left was still its original colour.
Coming out from the Lecture Hall, he passed a medicine shop on his way home. Thinking back to the errand he had run for his master, Wei He felt a stir in his heart, and turned in at the door.
The shop was full of the reek of medicine, sharp enough to make a man cough. A woman of forty-odd in a long black-and-grey gown was pressing down on a pestle and grinding with all her strength; beside her a shop hand wiped lazily at the counter with a rag.
"Manager, have you any ready-made prescription for everyday nourishment?" Wei He asked.
"A ready-made prescription for building yourself up?" The woman smoothed her hair and set down what she was holding. "One hearing of you and I know you for a layman. Prescriptions are written out by a physician, or made up by an apothecary — who ever asks for one straight across a counter like ours?" Her voice was a little hoarse; it did not sound like a woman's voice so much as an old man's.
The voice startled Wei He for an instant, and he recovered himself quickly. "Lately what I eat doesn't hold me long, so I thought I'd come and see whether there's anything a man can eat once and stay full a long while after."
"Meat. Nothing holds a man like meat. I do have some fresh game here, only the price is on the dear side. Take a look, see whether you want it." The woman turned and sent the shop hand into the back room, and he came out with a dark, lean strip of meat like a cudgel.
Slap. She laid the strip of meat down on the table. "White-patched black snake meat. Ten liang the catty, and the price is fixed."
