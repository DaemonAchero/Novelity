"Do you get the feeling that the dangers we ran into were not so many?" Cheng Jing plopped herself down beside him and said it low. Only now that they had come this far was everyone's mood a little better, with leisure to talk.
"…" Wei He glanced at her, not knowing how to answer.
"Every red dot on that map stands for a place where men have died." Cheng Jing said low. "The reason we can get through most of an escort run safely is the great escort master's leadership.
"Which red dots to steer around, what hour is best for travelling, what has to be prepared — the great escort masters have all of that in mind. And maps like that one, I have seen them before; the agency has this many." Cheng Jing held her hands apart to show a thickness, about the length of a palm.

"…" Wei He understood. These veteran great escort masters were plainly the agency's true and greatest wealth.
He thought it over, and silently noted for himself the position of the bridge-head house they had passed on this run. But Cheng Jing had more to say. "That night I only prodded you with words out of habit. Do not take it to heart."
"It is nothing." Wei He could not quite tell whether this woman had been play-acting then, or was play-acting now.
Her words sounded now somewhat true, now somewhat false, or perhaps half and half. But a moment later he saw Cheng Kai not far off, looking this way. He made a guess in his heart: it was Great Escort Master Cheng Kai smoothing the quarrel over for him. Yet by Cheng Jing's tone there seemed a note of sincerity in it too.
Cheng Jing said a few more words and then rose and left. Wei He picked up a dry cake and put it to his mouth in small pieces, washing it down with water and now and then a piece of snake meat, and said no more. So the road went, and before long they were back within ten li of Feiye City. Ox carts and horses on the road gradually grew more numerous. Everyone's mood eased a good deal as well; at least they were no longer as pent up as before.
Soon the party entered the city and came back to the agency. After the records had been handed over before the gate of the Yonghe Agency, a man from the agency came out to verify each man's wages for the trip. By the rule, when danger on the road has killed men, everyone is to be paid a little more. But paying it all out in silver would be too heavy to carry, so many of the men chose to take it in strips of cured meat.
Smoke-dried strips of cured pork, one strip of over ten catties, would cover a good deal of silver. Some men took cloth instead; cloth of every colour, and the dearer silk cloth, were the hard currency of the day. Wei He hesitated, then also turned the greater part of his silver into meat and rice, and cloth, and carried it home on his shoulder in one big sack. Silver was worth less and less these days; there was no telling when it might collapse altogether, and it was better to lay in more food, which could also serve
as hard currency for trade. Home again, he put the things away and looked over his rather bare room, and felt more and more that he ought to live somewhere else. From this home to Stone Bridge Ward, where Mountain Fist stood, he had to cross a good deal of grey territory, and that in itself was a nuisance. And now with so much food stored here and no one to watch it, one day it might just be stolen.
He sat down on the only wooden stool in the room and let the sunlight coming in at the window fall on him, to be a little warmer. He went back over all he had seen and heard on this run. Then he simply took out from under the table, and from behind it, a stack of yellow paper, with several charcoal sticks tucked in with it.
He spread the rough yellow paper on the table and took up a charcoal stick. Holding it the way he had held a pen in his former life, he quickly began writing and drawing on the paper. Before long he had set down the places they had passed along the way, the route, the rough lay of the land about it, the place names, the ways of the people there. Where a man could stay at an inn, where he could take on supplies and rest.
He recorded it all roughly. Then at the bridge-head house he drew a circle to mark the danger. And that monster, too…
