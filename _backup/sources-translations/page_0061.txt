The old man, stooped, ambled slowly to the door of the main house and pushed it open. A fine white dust sifted down inside. "This is the main house, for receiving guests. The side rooms on either side — there are four of them — can be made a servants' room, a guest room, a bedroom, whichever way you want to change them.
"There is also an outhouse at the back and a back kitchen. It comes with a small vegetable plot as well. And the Feiye River is right on the left, so no need to worry about water." The old man took Wei He bit by bit over the whole house. This was the fourth house they had seen. It too was in Stone Bridge Ward, only one street from the Mountain Fist yard.
Wei He made a circuit and came to the left side of the house. Besides the wall, a small side door had been set on the left, and beyond that door lay a path leading straight to the bank of the Feiye River. He pushed the door open: the river was very close, only a few metres off. Standing here you could hear the rushing water going on and on. "Out this way is the riverbank — you can fish, draw water, all very convenient. And another thing: from Shan Ju you can even see some of the sights of the Inner City."
The old man pointed in one direction. "There — see over there? That pavilion is part of a great mansion in the Inner City. Living here you can often hear the sound of a qin drifting over. Listening to music without spending a coin — is that not a bargain?"
He grinned, showing a mouthful of big yellow teeth. Wei He looked where he pointed, and there, far off to the left, a small pavilion stood by the river, part of a large mansion. The mansion had red lacquer and white walls, and on the eaves you could faintly make out ridge beasts; plainly not an ordinary household.
Just as Wei He looked, he saw far off two women in white skirts, veiled, one carrying a qin and the other empty-handed, walking toward the little pavilion. Both had come out from the mansion. Though the river about the pavilion was fenced with stakes and railings and no one was allowed to come near, a railing alone cannot block a distant line of sight.
But soon after the two women entered the pavilion they hung white cloth curtains on all four sides and shut out the view. "You see? Living here there is often a fine sight or two." The old broker smiled a leering smile. "In the past there were even men who painted little pictures who kept watch nearby, just for a glimpse of a beauty's figure." The expression on his face was one every man understood.
Little pictures… Wei He had heard of such things too. Put another way, they were this age's dirty books — the illustrated kind.
"All right. This house satisfies me. How is it sold?" Wei He decided on the spot.
Water was easy to draw here, washing clothes and cooking were both light work, and there were four side rooms, enough for receiving guests and for living. Every way you looked at it, it was good.
"The price is cheap. Only this much." The old man held out one hand and showed a number with his fingers.
"Five thousand taels?"
Wei He did a reckoning in his head. "Can it be paid in instalments?" He did not have so much money on hand just now.
"What five thousand taels? Rice! Fifty catties of coarse rice, fifteen smoked pork strips!" the old man said quickly. Wei He was rather taken aback.
They did not want money at all?? …But then he thought of the great swathes of empty houses around with no one in them, and how lately many shops he knew were slowly vanishing and closing their doors. All at once he understood something.
"That is the price. I tell you, this house is the best fit for you. Anything dearer and you would be going to the Xiao Ran Waterside over that way, and that
would jump you to another price bracket altogether for a big house. And anything cheaper you would not look at." The old man pressed him. In truth he knew very well that most of these houses were worth nothing, because there were too many of them. And as things stood now, the people were thinning out, empty houses were everywhere, and whether they could still fetch a price at all in future was anyone's guess.
