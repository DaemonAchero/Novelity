Wei He shifted a rotten wooden stool that was partly blocking the door and stepped into the dim room.
"Second Sister, I'm home," he called.
In a corner of the room a thin, frail young woman stood up.
Her sleeves were rolled up — she had been washing clothes and her hands were still wet. She pushed her hair back with her forearm and smiled at Wei He.
"Why are you back so early today, Xiao He?"
"The work ran out early, so I came back first," Wei He said.
"And the job repairing the Mu family's main house — is that finished too?" She walked to the window, and the weak light fell across her, showing a face that was a little delicate. Her skin was rough and her complexion none too good, but her figure was full and shapely, quite lovely.
Her name was Wei Ying, Wei He's second sister.
Wei He had an eldest sister too — Wei Chun — and she was the pillar of the household these days, scraping a living with the Black Water Gang in the city. That was also what kept the Wei family more or less safe in this neighborhood.
"That was finished long ago," Wei He answered. "Afterward I helped repair a few of the guest rooms beside it."
He had been in this world for several months now. From the bewilderment of the first days, to a calm acceptance, to this — running all day just to stay alive. The whole turn had taken him three months. Three months, and it had gone by so fast.
Wei He's parents were both alive, and he had two older sisters.
His eldest sister Wei Chun treated him very well; so did his second sister Wei Ying; and his parents kept every good thing for him. The belief that sons mattered more than daughters, together with poverty and no way to plan a family, had given the Wei household two daughters in a row before Wei He, a boy, finally arrived. Which was why the whole family prized him so.
"Big Sister still isn't back. Little Brother, be careful when you go out. When I went out to look for mending work, I heard another family had been robbed in the middle of the night." Wei Ying lowered her voice, her eyes nervous.
"She's still not back? How much did they get from that family?" Something moved in Wei He's face.
"They'd just been paid their wages. Half of it, they say. What's left won't be enough for that family to live on… And over in Rat Alley across the way, someone was robbed yesterday too. Whoever it was tried to fight back a little, and lost a finger for it."
"Robbed at night again? Didn't the Robber Gang just leave?" Wei He asked.
"I don't know… Maybe it's a new bunch come to take the turf." Wei Ying sighed, picked up a piece of clothing, and began to mend it with needle and thread.
"Whatever happens, you're out on the streets on your own — give way, and be careful. If somebody comes for what you have, hand it over. We're still here at home; we won't let you go hungry." Second Sister Wei Ying warned him earnestly.
"I know." Wei He nodded. His face was a little wooden.
He had come to this world, crossed over and been reborn as Wei He — an utterly ordinary, unremarkable young lad.
It was not that he had nothing to lean on. It was that the one thing he had could not be switched on at all.
It was a bead. Or rather, the shape of a bead, printed on the exact center of his chest, over the hollow of his heart.
From the fragments left in his mind, the bead was called the Realm Breaking Bead.
Its power was simple. Realm Breaking.
Once it had stored up enough power — and he himself had reached the critical point — it could carry him through one breakthrough in a martial art's realm.
He knew all this because, at the moment of his crossing, the bead had left a scrap of information in his mind, like a memory someone had planted there.
Whether it was true or not remained to be seen.
When the Realm Breaking Bead finished storing power, it would change from a translucent shade close to skin color into pitch black.
And now, though more than three months had passed, Wei He had never once seen the bead's pattern darken. At most the color had deepened a little.
Then again, every meal he ate barely kept him fed. There was no spare energy at all to lay aside for the bead. The bead fed on his daily eating: surplus food refined into essence energy, taken in and stored away, bit by bit.
