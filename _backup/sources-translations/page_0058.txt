After the window-hole had been blocked a while, Cheng Kai signalled for the planks to be left a small gap so that air could get in. With so many men in one closed space, and two fires burning, it was only a very short while before people felt the air
close and their breathing laboured. The moment the gap opened, the thin moaning of the wind came in from outside, and the cold mountain air, carrying a thread of blood-smell, drifted into the bridge-head house. It made the weight on everyone's heart heavier still.
No one spoke; whoever meant to speak was pressed down by an old hand beside him and warned to make no sound. Everyone held it in. Those holding the planks were relieved by fresh men every little while. Wei He went up to take his turn twice as well. And so it went, over and over.
At last a sliver of fish-belly white crept up the sky outside. Thin birdsong came in through the gap, and everyone's spirits lifted at once. Some calls were sharp, some low, some went took-took like a bird pecking wood. Sometimes near, sometimes far, sometimes joined into one sheet of sound. "It is light. Open the door; I will go and look." Cheng Kai gave the order in a low voice.
At once an old escort master, without a word, opened the wooden door a crack. Cheng Kai moved, quick as a startled hare, and shot outside. The door closed fast behind him. The men waited, slowly; before long Cheng Kai's somewhat weary voice came from outside.
"It is over. Come out, all of you." After a silence, the escort men moved the planks and the door and came out of the bridge-head house one after another. Outside now, two pools of blood were plainly visible on the ground, drying on the grass. The blood seemed to have been licked by some animal; the middle of it was a little thin.
But taken with the marks around them, you could still see how desperately the two men dragged off the night before had struggled before they died. Wei He walked over to the blood, crouched, and examined the marks closely. He soon felt out four sets of claw marks far deeper than a man's finger scratches, each with five fingers driven deep into the grass, reaching far down.
And pressed between the claw marks were fine lines, clear as the lines of a palm. Not far off, Cheng Kai and a few escort men had used the baggage and clothes of the two who were taken to make a simple grave, and set some things that would not easily rot as markers. This was all the trace those two had left behind.
The men quickly made ready the escort carts and wasted no time; they gathered their things and crossed the bridge and left that place. Before they set out, some poured cups of water in place of wine at the two cenotaphs; some silently laid a few things on the graves. Everyone fell silent, saying nothing more, quietly packing, quietly driving away. Only as they left did Wei He notice that a horse was missing too — no doubt dragged off the night before by the monster, the Black Madman.
They crossed the long, ancient stone bridge and went on until they reached the town that was their destination. Only after the escort goods had been handed over did the weight lift from their hearts. On the road back no one spoke; with no goods on them they travelled light and moved a great deal faster. The way out had taken eight days; the way back took only six. Two days saved, going and coming.
But no one had any taste left for chatting or for bouts, and they exchanged only the words the rules required. On the way back, when they came to the stone bridge again, they crossed it in one quick dash and stopped not at all. When they were near Feiye City they halted a short while to rest. mark at the stone bridge. Glancing at it, he saw that along the route on that map were at least a dozen dense red dots. Yet on this escort run they had run into nothing like so many dangers; and the bridge-head house at the end, that must also have been something they had not met with before
