Hunting "This map..." Wei He stood speechless.
There were no markers for the types of beasts. No notes on where powerful creatures laid claim to the land. Nothing useful. But he had no choice. He needed to find his guide and ask for help.
Packing the map away, Wei He left the cave and moved fast back to the Jade Wall. Following Yao Wan’s method, he passed through the barrier again. Once more, he entered the pitch-black inner mountain—the Inner Sanctuary.
It was time to head down the mountain, hunt a beast, and prepare for his first Sensory Anchor.
A few days later.
The Haizhou wilderness was a wasteland of dry, frozen yellow grass.
Two black horses with sharp horns tore across the plain like lightning. These were Black Horn Horses. Bred with the blood of thorny wild oxen, they had insane endurance, fast feet, and could easily withstand the biting cold. Zhou Muqing had bought them to give Wei He a fast ride.
Wei He rode side-by-side with a round-headed, slightly chubby man. The man had a broad, honest face and tiny eyes that always seemed to be smiling. He was fair-skinned, and his soft belly bounced with every step and every word.
This was his guide and guardian, Senior Brother Chang Xuezhong.
An icy wind mixed with freezing rain slammed into their faces.
Wei He glanced at his surroundings. "Senior Brother Chang, are we near Shuangshi County?"
"Yeah," Chang Xuezhong nodded. "The winds around here have softened up lately. It’s the perfect time for you to hunt a beast."
He reached into his robe, pulled out an old object that looked like a pocket watch, and checked the needles.
"We're close. A strange event happened in Shuangshi County recently. Probably a beast making trouble. We'll check it out first. If the beast is too weak, we’ll move on to the next spot."
"Understood!" Wei He nodded.
As his guide, Chang Xuezhong would lead him a few times to show him how to hunt these creatures. Once Wei He got the hang of it, he would be on his own.
The two pushed their horses forward.
They rode hard for several minutes, but the land ahead remained dead and empty.
"That's weird. I checked the map. Shuangshi County should be right here... Why isn't it?" Chang Xuezhong frowned. He pulled the map out of his saddlebag, unrolled it, and stared at it closely.
"Senior Brother... the map is upside down..." Wei He couldn't help but point out.
"Huh?" Chang Xuezhong blinked. He looked closer at the paper. The words really were upside down.
His old face flushed red, and he quickly flipped it around. "Sorry, sorry! My eyes have been bad since I was born. I can't see the path too clearly."
"It’s fine," Wei He said seriously. "Honestly, the fact that you reached the Real Master Realm with bad eyesight makes me respect you even more."
"You really are a good guy, Junior Brother," Chang Xuezhong sighed. "Truth is, my eyes were poisoned during a life-or-death fight with a beast. That’s why I can’t see..."
Didn't you just say you were born with it?
Wei He was speechless, but he kept his mouth shut.
They turned their horses around, locked in the right direction, and took off.
This time, they got it right.
Soon, paved roads appeared ahead. Wrecked houses and abandoned fields lined the dirt path. As they rode deeper, they finally saw people. Farmers were back in the fields, working the dirt.
The two slowed their horses to a calm pace.
Finally, a gray walled town appeared on the horizon, growing larger as they approached.
"This is Shuangshi County. Do you want to rest first?" Chang Xuezhong asked.
"No need. Time is short. Let's go straight to that stone bridge," Wei He said flatly.
He wasn't here to waste time. He needed to find the source, kill the beast, and see if it was what he needed.
"Fair enough." Chang Xuezhong looked up at the sky. Dark clouds heavy with freezing rain had been dripping for hours.
"By the way, your target sense is touch, right? You can't see beasts from a distance. So how are you planning to fight? Walk up and feel around first?" Chang Xuezhong looked at him with curiosity.
Wei He let out a dry laugh. "I didn't think it through at first. Someone told me that anchoring vision or smell would cause too much stress in a short time, which speeds up the sensory cycle. To stay safe and drag out the process, I chose my left hand. I didn't expect it to be this annoying."
Only now did he realize how brutal life was for rogue fighters. No senior brothers to protect them, no free medicine, and they had to hunt down their own technique manuals. To stay safe, they had no choice but to stretch their preparation time as long as possible.
Sect members were different. They picked whatever was fastest and easiest. That was why anchoring sight, smell, or hearing was standard practice for them.
"Just be careful," Chang Xuezhong warned. "Getting close with touch means you're putting yourself in danger. Once this is over, you should anchor your sight as soon as you can to avoid future trouble."
"Thanks for the warning, Senior Brother." Wei He nodded.
They slowed their horses down to a slow walk as they passed through the town gates.
People on the street scrambled out of the way. One look at their high-grade horses told anyone that these two were trouble. Even the guard at the gate kept his head down, not daring to say a single word as he let them pass.
Shuangshi County was small. It only had five crisscrossing streets and a population under a thousand. It was dead silent. A fortified dirt fort in the center was the only safe place around.
They found a small inn, boarded their horses, and went straight for the weird stone bridge.
Out in the suburbs, right next to a ruined temple, lay their target—the Green Ivy Stone Bridge.
The bridge had been abandoned. Wooden signs blocked both ends, painted with big warnings: DANGER. DO NOT CROSS.
The bridge was built entirely from gray stone, covered in dirty moss and dark stains. Beneath it, thick, yellow, filthy water flowed slowly, filling the air with a terrible stench.
The surrounding trees stretched out like bare, clawed bones, looking twisted and evil. A few crows sat on distant branches, staring at the bridge in dead silence, cawing once in a while.
"This is it," Chang Xuezhong said, matching the scene with his map. He walked up to the sign at the edge of the bridge.
"Reports say a beast is likely here. But they could only confirm something was wrong, not what kind of beast it is." He turned to Wei He. "How about it? Confident?"
"I'll give it a shot." Wei He didn't brag. This was his first time hunting a beast, and his heart was beating fast.
Staring at the stain-covered bridge, he took a deep breath to calm his nerves. "What do I do first, Senior Brother?"
"Take out the Mist Incense you were given and light it. That shows other Masters that a sect member is handling this place, so nobody interrupts," Chang Xuezhong said.
Wei He obeyed. He stuck a bundle of incense sticks into the dirt, pinched a piece of wood dust between his fingers, and rubbed them together. Friction sparked a flame, lighting the dust and then the incense.
The incense ignored the freezing rain. It burned steadily, releasing a faint, subtle scent.
"The incense is lit. Easy, right? Now go find the beast, pin its spot down, and kill it," Chang Xuezhong said.
Having guided someone before, he secretly activated his visual Super-Sense to watch the area, making sure nothing else would jump Wei He.
"Got it."
Wei He steadied his mind, looked at the empty bridge, and took a deep breath. He snapped open the Super-Sense in his left hand.
After taking the Mind Pill, he had already unlocked True Force.
The moment his Super-Sense opened, an icy cold rushed into his left hand. The freezing wind tried to snap his fingers with frost. But a thin layer of True Lock Force quickly flooded his hand, blocking the chill.
The cold drained his energy, but Wei He checked his reserves and felt nothing to worry about. The force was refilling on its own just as fast as it was burning away.
"Go on. You haven't anchored your sense yet, so you can't hold this mode for long. Find the beast and kill it fast," Chang Xuezhong urged. "Killing it isn't the hard part. The hard part is finding the damn thing."
Wei He nodded. He stepped onto the empty stone bridge.
His left hand reached out, waving blindly in the air. Because his range was so short, he had no idea what was waiting ahead. Now he truly understood how terrible touch Super-Sense was.
He was like a blind man, waving his left hand around as he walked forward.
Thanks to the Mind Pill and his True Force, the ghostly wind barely harmed him.
He moved step by step until he reached the middle of the bridge.
Chang Xuezhong’s voice transmitted directly into his ear from a distance.
"The spot mentioned in the report is slightly to your left. See that clean spot on the ground? Over the past year, more than ten people have jumped off the bridge from right there."
"Got it." Wei He nodded.
He looked left and spotted a section of the bridge floor that looked slightly cleaner than the rest.
Squinting his eyes, he let his energy circulate through his body. Slowly, he reached out his left hand toward that empty space.
The wind blew. Suddenly, Wei He’s fingers froze. His pupils shrank.
He felt it.
His fingers had touched something.
Fine strands floated through the wind, brushing against his skin.
He had touched hair.
A massive pile of hair. Cold, smooth, and fine.
He pushed his hand further forward.
Behind the hair was a human face.
A nose, a mouth, ears, cheeks...
Wei He tracked the outlines, feeling every single feature.
Right here... standing on the bridge... was a person.
An invisible person.
"Senior Brother?" Wei He called out.
They had agreed earlier that if danger appeared, Chang Xuezhong would call out immediately and order him to stop. But there was complete silence behind him.
Wei He whipped his head around. The edge of the bridge was empty.
Chang Xuezhong was gone.
A chill ran down Wei He's spine. He tried to pull his hand back.
Snap!
A pair of ice-cold, slender arms suddenly reached out and clamped around his wrist like iron cuffs.
Wei He’s skin crawled. Where their skin touched, an intense, crawling itch began to spread at terrifying speed.
Far off in the shadows, Chang Xuezhong watched the scene unfold with a grin.
This was a classic tradition for the Mountain Lock branch: scare the living hell out of the new kid.
Before the fight, you tell the rookie, Don't worry, I've got your back. Then, right when the beast shows up, you vanish and leave them to face it alone.
Most rookies were cocky when they first came up from the lower realms. They were used to being unstoppable gods in the mortal world. This trick was designed to smash their ego to pieces and teach them a harsh lesson: the real world was deadly, and a single beast could ruin them.
Chang Xuezhong was having a great time watching the bridge.
With his visual Super-Sense active, he could clearly see a blood-red female spirit covered in long black hair wrapping her arms around Wei He’s hand. Dozens of pale, sickly arms squirmed out from her back like a spider's legs.
This beast was a ghost-type. It wasn't super strong, but it was hard to kill. Unless a fighter dumped a massive amount of True Force into it, it wouldn't die. It was the perfect tool to terrify a beginner.
Beginners rarely had enough True Force to spare anyway.
Smiling, Chang Xuezhong watched the ghost squeeze Wei He in a tight hug. Seeing that the timing was right, he leaped out of hiding and yelled, "Junior Brother, don't panic! I’m here to save—!!"
BANG!!
The ghost's head exploded into red mist.
An insane wave of invisible energy tore her body into shredded pieces. It was at least double the force of a normal Master on their first Sensory Anchor, unleashed like it cost nothing at all.
Blood rained down across the stone bridge.
The moment the ghost grabbed Wei He's wrist, his raw strength combined with his True Force had smashed her to pieces.
Her broken torso flew into the air, and her severed arms rained down like falling leaves, slamming onto the stone floor.
The smile froze on Chang Xuezhong’s face.
Wei He calmly pulled his hand back and turned around, looking confused. "Senior Brother? Is something wrong?"
Inside, Wei He was sweating nervously.
He had held back and only used ten percent of his strength in that punch. That was... still within normal range, right?
He assumed beasts were supposed to be much stronger than this. He hadn't even used ten percent of his full power, let alone his high-level battle forms.
He thought he had been extremely subtle.
