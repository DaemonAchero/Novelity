When news broke that Wei He had broken through to Stone Skin, it did not cause much of a stir.
What no one knew—except for Old Zheng—was that Wei He had actually achieved this breakthrough two months earlier. Because the truth was hidden, the news caused only a slight ripple in the courtyard before quickly dying down.
These days, people cared far more about when the Seven-Family Alliance and Hong Fortress would go to war. Panic gripped everyone inside and outside the city.
Many wanted to run, but outside the city walls was even more chaotic and lethal. Bandit gangs and wild beasts roamed everywhere. In times like these, humans were just another resource to be harvested.
Captured men were driven into hard labor until they broke. Women with any looks were dragged off to brothels, while the rest were forced into heavy manual work. The most brutal bands did not even bother with labor—they slaughtered captives and dried their meat for food.
In the outer city, anyone with the money to flee had already left. Those who stayed were either too poor to move or had nowhere left to run.
Early that morning, Wei He finished his usual routine, wiped away his sweat, and sat down with his sister. They ate a simple meal of coarse grain porridge with minced pork and scrambled eggs. Once done, he set off for Old Zheng’s place.
Old Zheng had ordered him to head straight to the inner small yard today instead of training out front.
Dawn was just breaking as Wei He stepped outside. From an alley on the right came the muffled sounds of a street fight.
He glanced over as he passed. A lone youth was fighting two others at once.
With a loud yell, the solo fighter threw a handful of lime powder straight into his opponents' faces. The two fell back, howling in pain and clutching their eyes.
Before the victory was sealed, one of the blinded boys grabbed a brick off the ground and hurled it back with all his force.
The brick struck the lime-thrower, sending him stumbling back. Snarling with rage, he threw even more lime into the air and rushed forward, swinging his fists in a wild, blind frenzy.
Wei He shook his head slightly.
Too sloppy. That is not how you use lime powder... and a brick should always be aimed at the head.
His evaluation of this new generation of street thugs sank even lower.
Leaving the alley behind, he reached the main street, where a few shops were just beginning to open. They did not open their main doors. Instead, thick wooden boards blocked the entrances, leaving only narrow gaps to trade through.
Customers slipped items through these cracks, bartering directly with the shopkeepers.
Wei He glanced at one of the trades. Copper coins were long gone, and no one accepted silver anymore. They were trading small, unmarked sacks of random goods.
Even in the inner city, gold and official gold notes were now the only real currency accepted.
Looking down the empty street, only three shops were open. Counting all the pedestrians on both sides, there were fewer than ten people in sight.
Trash piled up on the ground with no one to clean it.
This place is falling apart...
Wei He sighed, quickened his pace, and turned through several backstreets until he stood before the gates of the Mountain-Returning Fist Academy.
He unlocked the gate and walked inside.
A few disciples were already training in the outer yard. As he stepped through, their eyes instantly shifted toward him.
Wei He kept his face calm and walked across the yard at a steady pace.
He could feel the weight behind those stares. Back when he was only at the Leathery Hide stage, people barely glanced at him before looking away.
Now, those looks held real weight.
Some were cold. Some were bitter or envious. Others were warm and friendly.
This was what strength brought you.
Wei He swept his gaze across the yard. The disciples who met his eyes quickly looked down, unable to hold his gaze.
As he walked, he noticed that disciples nearby unconsciously tracked his movements, adjusting their distance from him.
Everything had changed.
This was the respect that came with a breakthrough. Whatever he did now, people could not help but notice.
Stone Skin was the true core of the Mountain-Returning Fist Academy. It was the top level an outer disciple could reach—the level that earned Old Zheng’s direct favor and set a person completely apart from the rest.
Wei He suddenly understood what Old Zheng had told him before: an elite fighter with Second Blood status could easily become a mid-level leader in the Seven-Family Alliance.
And a mid-level leader in the Alliance was strong enough to rule a small local gang on the outside.
"This is bad! Yang Zhou, your brother is trapped again! It’s the Theft Gang!"
A boy in rags sprinted to the academy gates, shouting at the top of his lungs.
A short-haired youth who was mid-punch stopped instantly, face pale.
"Take me there!"
The voice was clear and sharp—distinctly female.
Her face was smeared with dirt, her short hair was a tangled mess, and her body was as flat as a bean sprout. Looking at her, no one would ever guess she was a girl.
"Where are they?"
Her name was Yang Zhou, a newcomer who had joined the academy just a few days ago.
Wei He had a faint memory of her. Not because she was a girl, but because she came from the same place he did—the old Rat Alley.
She lived in those same run-down shacks near the alley edge.
Hearing the words "Theft Gang," Wei He paused. For a second, he thought of his eldest sister, Wei Chun—how she used to carry all the weight on her own shoulders, protecting the family and her younger siblings.
A heavy feeling settled in his chest. He stopped, tempted to take a look.
Yet Yang Zhou was just a newcomer who had only learned basic moves. They had no connection.
He had no reason to meddle in other people’s business.
He lifted his foot to head toward the inner yard again.
Before he took three steps, angry shouts echoed from outside the gate. It was Yang Zhou and several others, roaring curse words at the top of their lungs.
"They're fighting right outside the academy?"
Wei He stopped, turned around, and walked back out.
Several other disciples stopped training and followed him to see what was going on.
The Mountain-Returning Fist Academy was one of the most famous places in the Stone Bridge District. Who dared cause trouble right on its doorstep?
Out on the street, Yang Zhou and another youngster were fighting like wild dogs against two burly men.
On the ground nearby lay a thin, battered boy, bruised and barely breathing.
The two men should have had the upper hand, but Yang Zhou and her partner fought dirty, throwing cheap shots that caught the men completely off guard.
For a moment, the two sides were locked in a desperate stalemate.
Just then, another group of men rushed down the street toward them.
They all wore the black marks of the Theft Gang.
The leader’s face turned cold as he stepped up, rolling up his sleeves to jump into the fight. Then his eyes caught Wei He and the other disciples standing outside the gate.
His face froze.
A man beside him leaned in and whispered something quickly, recognizing Wei He.
The leader's expression shifted instantly. He cupped his hands in respect toward the academy gate. "Since Brother Wei is here, we apologize for the trouble. We'll leave right now!"
Without another word, he grabbed his two men and pulled them back.
The gang turned and vanished into the deep alley as fast as they had come.
Wei He stood there, stunned. He had not said a single word, yet they had scattered.
He had not realized how much weight his name carried now.
These Theft Gang thugs were no different from Chen Biao’s crew back then—street scum who roamed the area, robbing people or dealing in human trafficking whenever there was profit to be made.
And now, they ran at the mere sight of him.
Wei He felt a strange sense of irony. Before he joined the academy, holding money in his pocket while facing Chen Biao’s men would have left him terrified, trembling in fear.
Now, facing the same kind of thugs, he did not even need to speak to make them run for their lives.
Without noticing, he was no longer the weak, helpless boy he used to be.
He sighed softly.
Yang Zhou ran over, bowing deeply to him. "Thank you, Senior Brother Wei."
Wei He shook his head. "I didn't do anything. No need to thank me."
He turned to walk back toward the gate. After a few steps, he paused.
"Train hard. That is the only way to stop people from stepping on you."
Yang Zhou’s body trembled. She gave a heavy nod, bowed again, and hurried back to her brother. Together with the other youngster, she lifted the injured boy and rushed toward the medical shop.
Far down the road, the gang members stopped beside a pile of garbage, gasping for breath.
"Boss... who was that guy back there?" one of them asked, chest heaving.
"The Mountain-Returning Fist Academy announced it just two days ago," the leader said, shaking his head. "Wei He is their new core disciple. He reached Second Blood..."
He sighed, his face dark. "Don't even think about it. All you need to know is that he is a monster. Even if we all ambushed him together, we would just be giving him free kills."
The leader had enough experience to know better. He smacked the henchman across the back of his head.
"Keep your eyes open from now on! Learn this lesson well: keep track of every little change inside those martial arts yards. If a new big shot pops up and you accidentally cross him, you won't even know how you died."
"In times like these, a man dies like a dog in the street, and no one bothers to bury him."
He wiped his face and stood up straight against the wall.
"Let's go. Leave those two alone for now. We'll collect from other families instead."
The men exchanged glances, keeping their mouths shut as they followed him into the shadows.
BAM!
Wei He hit Old Zheng’s forearm with a heavy punch. Before his power faded, he spun on his heel and drove a sharp elbow straight toward the old man's temple.
"Too slow between moves. Openings are everywhere."
Old Zheng shoved gently, deflecting the strike into thin air.
His open palm pulled back into a fist and snapped straight forward.
"Got you!"
BAM!
Wei He stumbled back, a fresh red fist mark showing on his shoulder.
He pulled back, coughing twice, and raised his head with genuine respect.
"Master is truly amazing!"
"What? Did you think an old man like me couldn't handle you?" Zheng Fugui chuckled, shaking his head. "I'm telling you, you kids are still way too green!"
"You have some skill now," Old Zheng smiled. "So do you know why you still can't beat a bad old man like me?"
"This disciple does not know."
Wei He found it strange too. Master Zheng was neither tall nor heavily built, yet his strikes carried terrifying power.
Wei He could tell Old Zheng was holding back. A casual blow from him held far more force than Wei He could output.
"Xiao He, compared to the others, you keep to yourself too much," Zheng Fugui said. "Go out more. Talk to people. Build connections and gather information."
"What do you mean, Master?"
"Position," Old Zheng said calmly. "You have strength now. But do you know where you stand in this Feiye City?"
Wei He fell silent. He had never thought about that before.
"This disciple does not know," he answered honestly.
