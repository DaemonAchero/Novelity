Coocoo.
A weird sound echoed through the woods.
Wei He stopped and looked up at the dark, creepy forest.
In the distance, a pair of pale green eyes flashed and vanished into the shadows.
Wei He swallowed hard, pulled his gaze back, and focused on the ground right in front of him.
He was standing on a bed of dry grass, fallen leaves, twigs, and tangled vines.
He used his curved knife to gently press down the thick brush. He had to be careful. A single prick from a hidden thorn or razor grass could slice his skin open.
In a dense forest like this, bleeding meant trouble. The smell of blood would draw in deadly predators.
He cleared a path step by step, staying on high alert.
His main goal today was simply to get used to hunting. As for investigating if the Shaoyang Sect was linked to his family's disappearance, he wasn't in a rush.
Even if he found the truth right now, he wasn't strong enough to take revenge. It was better to focus on hunting, kill some rare beasts, build up his strength, and deal with revenge later.
Of course, if he stumbled upon any clues along the way, that would be a nice bonus.
Pfft!
A soft rustle came from the bushes ahead.
Wei He focused. He slowed his steps and listened closely.
He stopped, then used his blade to part the bushes on his right.
His movements were dead silent.
Compared to the wind blowing through the trees, his actions made no sound at all.
On the other side of the brush, a dark green python as thick as a man's arm was swallowing a black rat. Its body twitched as it forced the rat down its throat.
Wei He's face remained blank. He raised his heavy knife.
Whoosh!
His speed and power as a Second Blood Refinement martial artist far surpassed any normal human.
Even if the Reversion Mountain Fist only boosted explosive power and didn't focus on raw agility, it was more than enough for a wild beast.
To the naked eye, his slash was completely invisible.
A gray shadow flashed.
The knife flew through the air, and the python tried to dodge like lightning.
Both happened in a fraction of a second, but the snake was still too slow.
Pfft!
The heavy blade hit the python right below its head.
It didn't hit the "seven-inch" mark.
Pinching a snake's neck at the seven-inch mark was a trick only professional snake handlers used. That was where the vital spine sat, and grabbing it made the snake go limp.
Wei He didn't have that kind of delicate skill.
Besides, according to the old hunter Ding Hai, the whole "hit a snake at seven inches" thing was just a myth.
A snake's spine ran through its whole body. Breaking it anywhere would kill it. The seven-inch spot was just for controlling it alive.
If you wanted to kill a snake fast, you smashed its head.
Ding Hai had also taught him another rule: if you meet a snake you can't beat, run uphill. Snakes are slow going up, but insanely fast coming down. So...
Wei He looked down at the python. His blade had nearly chopped it in two.
The snake writhed on the ground, bleeding heavily, struggling to stay alive.
Wei He stepped forward and slammed his boot onto its head.
His move was fast, cold, and precise.
Squish.
The snake's head caved in. It stopped moving.
"A python. That counts as a good catch," Wei He muttered. He picked up the python by its tail and gave it a violent shake.
He was testing out another trick from Ding Hai.
No matter what snake it is, if you grab its tail and shake it hard, it can't bend up to bite you.
Of course, different snakes required different amounts of force.
A normal small snake only needed a gentle shake.
But this python was as thick as his forearm.
Wei He shook it wildly.
Thump.
The half-swallowed black rat popped out of the snake.
It didn't come out of its mouth, though. It burst out of the crushed, ruptured belly where Wei He had stomped it.
Wei He looked at the rat, felt a wave of disgust, and decided to leave it behind.
He coiled the python up, sprinkled scent-masking powder over it, and sealed it inside an insulated leather sack.
This single snake had enough meat to feed him for several days. This trip was already a success.
After wandering around for days, this was the first time he had caught something this big.
"Ding Hai definitely has specialized hunting and tracking skills that he kept to himself. But what he taught me is enough for now. I can figure out the rest on my own. After all, those tricks are how he makes a living."
Wei He reasoned that if an inexperienced person tried to survive solely on hunting, they would starve to death before finding this much game.
He shoved the python into his backpack. The flat, empty bag instantly puffed up, looking stuffed and heavy.
Wei He decided to head back for the day. He couldn't push his luck; he needed to take things step by step.
He looked left and right, turned around, and slowly retraced his steps.
Swish!
Suddenly, a rock whizzed in from his left.
"Hold it right there!"
A rough shout echoed from the trees.
The rock smashed into the dirt right in front of Wei He's feet, leaving a small crater.
The throw carried serious force.
Wei He glanced at the hole in the ground and stood still, not moving an inch.
Soon, several men in gray clothes emerged from the thick brush. Some were built like tanks, while others were lean and wire-thin.
Leading them was a bald, muscular man holding a oversized slingshot mechanism.
"I smelled blood from a mile away! I knew we'd find something good!" the bald man grinned.
He stared at Wei He, who was wearing a heavy hunting sack, and froze for a second.
Wei He was dressed in thick, dark green clothes. He wore a round hat and a face mask that covered everything below his eyes.
In this kind of heat, wrapping himself up like a mummy felt bizarre.
Is this guy insane? the bald bandit thought to himself.
On top of that, the stranger looked bulky, and his eyes were disturbingly calm. He didn't show a hint of fear facing a group of armed bandits.
The bald man quickly made a mental calculation.
"Drop the bag and walk away. Don't make us make you," he threatened in a low voice.
Wei He didn't make a sudden move. In deep mountain forests like this, groups like these were usually local bandits. If they could survive out here for a long time, they were definitely not easy targets.
Right now, he was pretending to be a regular hunter, so...
After a brief thought, he slipped the python bag off his shoulders and set it on the dirt.
"I'll leave right now," Wei He said in a raspy, low voice.
He began backing away, step by step.
"Wait!" The bald leader narrowed his eyes when he saw how smoothly Wei He surrendered. Is this bastard just acting calm?
He had seen plenty of people try this trick before. Real martial art experts were supported by big factions and rich clans. Who would come out to hunt alone like this?
Even if there were any, they were rare.
A lone traveler like this, even if he possessed real skill, could easily be tricked and killed by a group using dirty tactics.
The bald leader had killed a real expert once before. That man was strong and killed one of his brothers, but in the end, he still died to poison and hidden weapons.
Ever since that day, he understood one truth: even experts were made of flesh and blood.
As long as you bled, you could die. If a cheap trick worked, it didn't matter who the target was!
Thanks to that kill, he had scored a martial arts manual, trained like crazy, and finally broken through his First Blood Refinement.
With his new strength and ruthless methods, he had become a feared boss among the mountain bandits.
"You look way too bulky under those clothes! What are you hiding? Take it out!" The bald leader glared at Wei He.
There was definitely a lot of gear hidden under that oversized coat.
He had judged thousands of men, and his gut was rarely wrong.
Besides, a real master would never give up a fresh kill without a fight.
The bald leader's eyes gleamed with greed. He felt sure that Wei He's calm attitude was just a front.
Giving up the bag so easily proved he was hiding something far more valuable under his clothes!
The more he thought about it, the more convinced he became.
"Take off your coat and pull out whatever you're hiding!" he roared, a bloodthirsty look on his face.
His men took the signal and fanned out in a arc, blocking Wei He's escape routes.
They sneered, swinging their shiny cleavers through the air, making terrifying whistling sounds.
"I don't have anything under my clothes," Wei He said flatly.
Aside from poison powder, quicklime, toxic darts, full leather armor, and a pair of poisoned punch daggers, he really wasn't carrying anything else.
The bandits didn't buy it. They stepped closer.
"Nothing? Your coat is bulging like a sack! Do you think I'm blind?!"
One bandit stepped right up to Wei He and reached out to grab his collar.
Wei He paused. He didn't want to cause extra trouble. He didn't know how strong these guys were, and this was their home turf. He had no idea if they had back-up nearby.
At the same time, he couldn't let them touch the deadly traps hidden on his body.
He took a step back, dodging the bandit's hand.
"Brothers, I really have nothing left. I waited in the woods for days just to catch that single snake. That's all I have."
"Liar! You're definitely hiding something under there!" The bald leader grew more convinced. The guy was wrapped up like a package and gave up a massive python without blinking.
Something was definitely wrong!
He had to be carrying rare treasures!
"Grab him! Search him and strip his clothes off!"
Wei He scanned his surroundings, planning his escape. This was unfamiliar territory. If he wanted to play the role of a helpless hunter, he had to act like one.
The woods were thick. He couldn't tell if there were archers or snipers hiding in the trees.
Getting hit by a stray arrow or a poisoned blowdart would be bad news.
Wei He backed up another step, pulled out a small food pouch from his shirt, and placed it on the ground.
"Brothers... this is the last of my dry rations..." he mumbled, keeping his voice weak.
"Dry food? Ha! You still have plenty of stuff hidden under that coat..."
A bandit suddenly lashed out with his blade, slicing at Wei He's waist.
Wei He dodged backward instantly.
Rrrrrip.
He avoided the blade itself, but his baggy outer coat wasn't fast enough. The tip of the blade caught the fabric and sliced it wide open.
The cloth tore apart.
The bandit snickered. "Heh, let's see where you hide it now..."
His voice trailed off.
His eyes locked onto the revealed lining of the coat.
Underneath the ripped fabric sat a neat row of gleaming poison darts and a dark purple punch dagger that practically reeked of deadly venom.
Why would a simple hunter carry weapons like this?
The smile vanished from the bandit's face.
He slowly raised his head and looked into Wei He's eyes.
Wei He stared right back at him.
