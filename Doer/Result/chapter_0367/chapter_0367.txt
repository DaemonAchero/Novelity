Wei He could tell from Huan Songzi’s expression that the man was still holding back many secrets.
He remembered what Senior Sister Yao Wan had once told him: Xuanmiao Sect possessed a dark, hidden place known as the Abyss Hell.
"So that underground space is part of the Abyss Hell? The place where ancient true beasts are sealed?" Wei He asked.
"Yes," Huan Songzi nodded. "To be exact, it is just one sector. Ever since the Black Seal Kunpeng vanished more than two hundred years ago, that area was abandoned and turned into a safe zone."
Wei He understood now.
This battle had finally given him a clear picture of his true limits.
He was undeniably strong, but he was far from invincible. Against ordinary Quanzhen practitioners, he could handle those in the lower ranks with ease. But against top-tier experts like the Buddha Masters, his current power fell short.
The earlier fight against the Black Cross was a brutal wake-up call.
Although he possessed a massive amount of Huazhen Qi, raw force alone was not always enough to crush everything. He had almost drowned in that fight. If it were not for the physical strength granted by his Whale Flood Technique, he would have suffered his very first total defeat.
"Speaking of which," Wei He asked, "Grand Senior Sister always gives off a mysterious vibe. Was she always like this?"
Huan Songzi chuckled softly. "Grand Senior Sister looks gentle on the outside, but she is far more dangerous than she appears. Spend enough time around her, and you will see."
Wei He nodded silently. He stood up and walked toward the massive corpse of the Black Line Whale anchored beside the ship.
It was time to cultivate according to the Whale Flood Technique.
Whale oil usually required complex processing to be stored safely. But would consuming it fresh yield a far better result?
Wei He decided to find out himself.
Grey clouds rolled heavy across the sky.
At the edge of Tianhai Island, the massive fleet of the Wu State surrounded the land from all sides.
On the flagship, Commander Xie An stroked the coarse mane of his pet, a three-horned lion. He stared at the silent island, a cold knot of dread tightening in his chest.
Earlier, he had ordered his navy to cover the charge of Buddha Dingyan and the other masters. Their plan was simple: the army would breach the outer array, while the Buddha Masters infiltrated the inner core from the shadows to destroy the formation from within.
Yet, hours had passed, and the array remained active.
Xie An scowled.
In the distance, the glowing white light of the Star Array rippled like water over the island, unbroken and firm.
This array offered no physical shield. It could not stop arrows or deflect blades. Its sole purpose was to inflict terrible curses and debuffs on anyone who entered it.
The higher a warrior's cultivation, the harsher the suppression. For high-ranking generals with True Blood, the array cut down their power mercilessly.
The Wu State was at the peak of its power. Centralized control meant every resource was fed to those with talent. Power dictated status; the strong ruled, and the weak fell. A century of this relentless doctrine had built an army of absolute monsters.
Still, powerful warriors were not disposable assets.
The Xuanmiao Sect’s Star Array was strong enough to crush a True Blood General’s power down to the level of a basic martial artist. It stripped away an entire tier of strength.
Without destroying the array first, Xie An refused to throw his best men into a meat grinder.
"General! Look!" the vice-commander suddenly shouted, pointing toward the peak.
Xie An raised his eyes.
Heavy shapes were clashing near the top of the volcano. The Buddha Masters were clearly engaging the high-ranking elders of the sect.
Oddly, the sect members who had been fighting off the naval landing suddenly broke off combat. They retreated deep into the thick, dark forests of the island, vanishing into thin air.
The vanguard of the Wu State quickly landed on the shore. Squads of armored soldiers rushed into the trees.
Xie An was cautious. He only sent one thousand men from the vanguard forward. Even if it was a trap, losing a thousand soldiers was a cost he could easily bear.
However, once those men entered the dense woods, they went dead silent.
No battle cries. No distress calls. Not even a single signal flare hit the sky.
Xie An’s face turned dark. "How long has it been since Buddha Dingyan went up?"
"Twelve minutes, General," the vice-commander replied, checking a pocket watch. "At their speed, five minutes should have been enough to reach the peak. But there is still no signal."
"Could they be locked in a stalemate?" the vice-commander guessed nervously.
Suddenly, the grand white light covering Tianhai Island dissolved like dying ripples. The Star Array vanished completely.
The island fell deathly quiet. Not a single soul from the Xuanmiao Sect could be seen. The vanguard troops who had entered the forest were completely gone.
"General, the array is down! The Buddha Masters must have succeeded!" the vice-commander said eagerly. "Should we launch a full assault?"
Xie An narrowed his eyes, staring at the shrouded peak. White mist covered the inner sanctuary, hiding whatever horrors had taken place up there.
Something was deeply wrong. If Buddha Dingyan had won, why was there no word from him?
A cold smirk crossed Xie An's lips.
He raised his hand. "Pull everyone off the island. Bring them back now."
"General?"
"Bring out the heavy cannons," Xie An commanded coldly. "Blast the coastline first."
No matter how strange the sect was, their numbers were small. Cannon fire was something only true masters could block, and even then, only a few shots at a time. He wanted to see how they would handle a salvo of hundreds of cannons at once.
Before his order could be carried out, movement appeared on the shore.
The vanguard troops who had vanished earlier were coming back down the mountain, carrying urgent messages.
"What?! Buddha Dingyan and the others are all dead?!" Xie An’s eyes widened in sheer shock.
Then, a sudden, terrifying realization hit him.
"Dammit! The Xuanmiao Sect controls the Abyss Hell! If they were pushed to the edge, could they have..."
If the sect was desperate enough to break the seals and release all the ancient monsters trapped in the deep hell for a thousand years, the entire fleet would be wiped out.
Just as he raised his hand to order an immediate retreat, a spine-chilling coldness gripped the back of his neck.
"General Xie An of the Wu State, I presume?" a calm, soft female voice echoed right behind him.
Xie An did not turn around. His massive body burst forward with unnatural speed, leaping like an ape to the far edge of the deck before spinning around.
What he saw froze the blood in his veins.
The vice-commander and over a hundred elite guards standing near his former spot were locked in place. None of them moved.
Between every man’s eyebrows, a small, clean hole appeared. Blood and brain matter seeped out slowly. Every single one of them had been killed instantly before they could even react.
Standing where Xie An had just been was a beautiful woman in a black taoist robe. She was blindfolded, her long hair hanging loose over her shoulders.
Blood dripped steadily from the tips of her white fingers.
"You?!" Xie An’s heart hammered in panic. "Yuan Duzi?! How can this be..."
It made no sense. Intelligence reports claimed Yuan Duzi was merely at the limit of the Quanzhen level, a step away from Grandmaster, right on par with him.
How could she slip onto his flagship without triggering a single alarm and slaughter his elite guards, including several high-ranking officers, right in front of his eyes?
"You run fast," Yuan Duzi said quietly.
She raised her arm. Her black sleeve slid down, exposing skin as smooth as white jade.
Yet, red, chain-like marks were spreading across her arms.
It was the military array deployed by the Wu fleet, designed to heavily suppress any master or beast stepping onboard. If fully powered, it could even weaken a Grandmaster significantly.
Yuan Duzi felt the heavy weight pressing down on her power.
"Impressive," she murmured, testing her own strength. "This suppression is much stronger than our sect's array."
"So, you really broke through to the Grandmaster realm..." Xie An gritted his teeth, his voice low and raspy. "What did you do to Buddha Dingyan and the others?"
"Buddha Dingyan?" Yuan Duzi paused, a strange, beautiful smile curving her lips. "Their flesh and blood have already merged into mine."
She looked straight at him. "Now, it is your turn."
"Arrogant fool!" Xie An roared. "You dare step off your island and walk into my army's formation? Even if you are a Grandmaster, I will make sure you die here today!"
Xie An spread his arms wide. His muscles swelled grotesquely as thick black scales burst from under his skin.
"Die!"
He leaped high into the air, spinning down like a descending hurricane, bringing both arms crashing down in a lethal cross-strike.
The sheer force of his movement shook the air for dozens of meters around him. The collective aura of his entire fleet gathered around his body, feeding into his strike. He was leveraging the strength of his entire army to crush the woman below.
"What a pity," Yuan Duzi sighed softly.
"Your array is strong... but your army is far too small."
The morning sun cast their long shadows across the wooden deck.
In an instant, Yuan Duzi’s shadow distorted wildly. It swelled into a colossal, grotesque shape and lunged forward like a striking serpent.
Pfft!
A massive, shadowy maw snapped shut in mid-air, swallowing Xie An whole.
