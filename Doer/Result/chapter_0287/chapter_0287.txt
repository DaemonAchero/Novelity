Weifang City was much smaller than Xuanjing. In fact, every city in Jin Prefecture was smaller than those in Tai Prefecture.
However, Jin Prefecture was crowded. Cities were packed tightly together.
Each city specialized in different goods. This made it easy for traveling merchants to pick where to buy their stock.
Wei He left his house. He now went by the fake name Chen Wei.
In the Great Yuan empire, Chen was a common family name. Many government officials shared it. It was the easiest disguise for a retired martial artist trying to hide.
He stepped outside and walked down the street, watching the strange, busy crowd.
Looking around, you could not tell that war was raging elsewhere or that foreign enemies were invading.
The war felt far away from the ordinary people here.
Vendors shouted loudly to sell their goods. Children ran in circles playing games and pretending to be different characters. Women gathered in shops, browsing for scented powder, silk, and shiny jewelry.
Compared to Tai Prefecture, there were far fewer fighters on the streets here. Instead, the roads were full of merchants and commoners.
Fewer fighters meant the local martial arts scene was weak.
As Wei He walked, he stopped in front of a small martial arts hall with a storefront.
A wooden sign stood at the door. It listed the entry rules and the strength of the head instructor.
The place was called the Pine Boxing Hall. The master, Xu Chang, was a mid-level Martial Master. The sign bragged about his great power. It claimed he killed a gang of bandits in the wild back in the second year of the Qing Reign and beat a man-eating black bear to death with his bare hands.
"Looking to learn boxing? The fee is cheap, and we have a discount right now!"
Someone inside saw Wei He standing by the sign and rushed out with a fake, eager smile.
"No thanks. Just looking," Wei He said, shaking his head.
He felt strange. In Jin Prefecture, even boxing halls acted like cheap merchants hustling for customers.
He left the hall and walked toward the place where Jiu Ying lived.
The house Wei He bought for Jiu Ying was not far away.
During the last plague, Jiu Ying had worked himself to exhaustion to keep the sickness contained to a small area. But he was powerless to stop it from spreading to distant towns, and the effort had drained his remaining strength.
Wei He turned into a quiet, cold alley and cleared his mind.
He waited for a few kids playing with a bamboo ball to run past. Then he walked up to Jiu Ying's front door and knocked softly.
The copper ring on the door was old, but it was smooth and shiny, worn down by countless hands over the years.
Wei He remembered that a well-connected small trader used to live here. Looking at the smooth ring, that trader really did have a lot of visitors.
Knock, knock, knock.
He knocked again.
Ruff! Ruff!
A loud dog bark came from inside the yard.
"Coming!" yelled Yuan Bao, Jiu Ying's young student.
The wooden door opened, showing Yuan Bao's closely shaved head.
The boy was over a meter tall now, but his old chubby fat was completely gone.
He had almost died from the plague, but Jiu Ying had managed to drag him back from the edge of death.
Back then, Jiu Ying had even grabbed Wei He's hand to beg him to take care of the boy after his death. Who would have thought the old man himself would stubbornly survive?
"Uncle Wei! Please come in!" Yuan Bao had grown thin. Seeing Wei He, he quickly stepped aside to let him enter.
"Where is your master?" Wei He asked.
He walked into the courtyard and saw a pretty young girl with long, loose hair. She held a scroll made of bamboo slips and stood stiffly by a stone bench.
On the stone table lay stacks of yellow paper and black charcoal pencils. Two pencils were laid out, and half-written words covered the yellow paper.
"This is Miss Lin from next door. She came over to learn how to write with me," Yuan Bao quickly explained.
"Lin'er, this is my Uncle Wei. He is Master's best friend. You can call him Uncle Wei."
"Hello, Uncle Wei," the girl said obediently, giving a small bow.
She had bright eyes, white teeth, and fair skin. Though she was young, she was as thin and graceful as a willow branch.
She was a bit too skinny, but anyone could tell she would grow up to be a total beauty.
Wei He felt a bit confused. Why would her family let such a young girl stay alone in a house with two males to learn writing?
Looking at the girl's shy, lowered head, he guessed there was a secret reason behind it.
He did not dig into it and walked into the inner room.
An oil lamp burned inside, even though it was daytime.
Jiu Ying squatted on a long wooden bench, holding a pipe and taking slow puffs of smoke.
Seeing Wei He come in, he set the pipe down.
"You are a busy man. What brings you here to see a useless old codger like me?"
"To play chess," Wei He smiled.
"Ha! Playing chess with you is boring. Winning or losing, it feels like nothing," Jiu Ying picked up a teacup and took a sip to wet his dry throat.
"Fine. I heard your health is failing again, so I came to check on you," Wei He said truthfully.
"I might really be done for this time," Jiu Ying said, not caring much about death. "Thanks to you supporting us these past few years, my life became stable. Otherwise, I would have died during that plague."
"Since you don't have much time left, tell me. Is there any wish you want fulfilled? If it is within my power, I will help you." Wei He's voice was serious.
"No wishes. With Yuan Bao around, I have let go of everything else."
Jiu Ying clapped his hands. A fat yellow dog ran out from the corner of the room, trotting over to rub its head against his leg.
"What about you?" Jiu Ying looked up. "An old man like me is going to die soon anyway, so nothing matters. But you, don't think you are safe just because you are hiding here. In a world at war, no one can stay out of the blood fire."
"Did you hear something?" Wei He paused.
How could a lonely old man like Jiu Ying get fresh news?
"Kong'an City in Yi Prefecture was slaughtered. Everyone inside was killed," Jiu Ying said calmly.
"A whole city slaughtered?" Wei He froze, falling silent.
Ever since the war started, he knew such cruel massacres might happen. But hearing it actually take place gave him a suffocating feeling of doom, pressing down like a mountain.
"Is the news real?" Wei He asked.
"The news should reach this city by tomorrow. I have my own secret ways of getting information, so I found out early," Jiu Ying replied flatly.
Wei He had no words. He knew Jiu Ying had no reason to make up a sick joke like this.
"The man who wiped out the city is General Tang Yan from the State of Wu," Jiu Ying continued. "Think about it. If your city is slaughtered one day, how will you protect yourself?"
"What are you trying to say?" Wei He asked back.
"I just want to tell you this: if you have the chance, never relax. Do not wait until you need power to feel regret."
"I know."
Wei He had always suspected that Jiu Ying was secretly supplying goods and medicine to some underground faction. Otherwise, he could never have such fast news.
Besides, the amount of poison and herbs Jiu Ying used up during his old experiments was far too large for one man.
However, since Jiu Ying had helped him find clues for the Silk Force, the Moon Whale Art, and had helped him finish his core cultivation method, Wei He chose to turn a blind eye to it.
"Also, there is one more thing... this item..." Jiu Ying reached into his robe and slowly pulled out a wooden token painted with blood-red words. A single word was carved on it: 'Help'.
"For my sake, if someone comes to you with this token, please help them once. Only if it is within your power," Jiu Ying said in a low voice.
"...Fine," Wei He nodded and took the wooden token.
"Good. Now I can rest easy." Jiu Ying let out a long breath. It was clear he had built up a lot of courage just to ask for this favor.
"What about the girl outside?" Wei He asked casually.
"Her mother was a prostitute next door. Nobody knows who her father is. Her mother died of sickness a while ago, leaving the girl alone. I took her in to keep Yuan Bao company. She is a poor, unlucky child," Jiu Ying sighed.
"Then I will be going," Wei He nodded. As long as her background was clean, it did not matter.
"Are you interested in taking Yuan Bao and that girl as your students?" Jiu Ying suddenly asked. "That girl has incredible talent and comprehension."
"Oh? Incredible talent?" Wei He raised an eyebrow. "How strong is she?"
"Her body's bones and blood are average. But her comprehension is terrifyingly high. I am teaching her to read, write, and train in martial arts right now. If you are interested, you can watch her." Jiu Ying smiled.
"Forget it. I have seen too many people with great talent but poor bodies. Aren't you and I the same type?" Wei He smiled back.
"What if her talent is even higher than ours?" Jiu Ying chuckled. "For any martial art below the Master level, she learns it the moment she sees it once. She understands it the moment it is explained."
Wei He's smile froze on his face.
He had seen that girl just now. She was eleven or twelve years old at most. At her age, if she could really do what Jiu Ying claimed...
That would be terrifying.
He stared deeply into Jiu Ying's eyes. Though he could not read the old man's face, he felt that shoving the girl onto him had a dark hidden motive.
"Jiu Ying, do not play tricks with me," Wei He said softly.
"Are you scared?" Jiu Ying laughed.
"Of course I am," Wei He said naturally. "I am scared that one day I will have to kill my own student to clean up a mess."
"Where is your pride as a grand master? Saying you are scared without any shame... I have lived for over half a century, but this is the first time I've met a master who cares so little about his reputation," Jiu Ying said, completely speechlessly.
"So, tell me your real motive," Wei He pressed.
"What motive could I have? Little Lin is still young. Whatever happens in the future can wait. Since you don't want to, then forget it," Jiu Ying shook his head.
"Fine." Wei He did not want to get dragged into trouble.
The girl's mother was a prostitute, and her father was a mystery. It meant her real background was a ticking bomb.
She was likely tied to the dark group hidden behind Jiu Ying.
Wei He put the thought away and turned to leave the room.
Walking through the yard, he saw Yuan Bao and the girl sitting close together, practicing their writing with great focus. They did not even notice him leaving.
He ignored them, stepped out of the yard, gently closed the door, and walked away.
Leaving Jiu Ying's house, Wei He headed straight for the Baisong Merchant Guild.
This guild was a mid-sized business in Weifang City. The owner had a fierce, heroic name, Xiao Jingyun, but he was actually a very polite and gentle man. His personality was the total opposite of his name.
When Wei He arrived, Xiao Jingyun was already waiting outside the main door.
Wei He was his biggest buyer, so he treated him with the highest level of respect.
Xiao Jingyun was a fat man wearing a pair of round glasses. The glasses made him look slippery and sharp, but his loose silk robes made him seem soft and friendly. Anyone meeting him for the first time would instinctively drop their guard.
"Long time no see, Master Chen! I hope you have been well," Xiao Jingyun cupped his hands in greeting.
"I'm fine. Bring out the good stuff quickly. I won't owe you a single coin," Wei He said without fake politeness, following Xiao Jingyun inside.
Guided by a maid, they walked into a private room.
Xiao Jingyun carefully walked to the corner of the room. He used a key from his belt to open a heavy metal chest, pulling out several rolled-up scroll paintings.
"Here, here! Last time, Master Chen asked me to keep an eye out for ancient landscape paintings, so I searched everywhere. Take a look. These are the ancient paintings we can get our hands on this time."
He unrolled a master scroll. Inside were drawn samples and sketches of various ancient paintings, displayed side by side.
