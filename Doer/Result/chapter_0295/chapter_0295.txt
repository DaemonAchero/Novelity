Nightingale's Laugh is one of nine ghost winds.
That alone shouldn't keep the grandmasters of the No-Beginning Sect trapped on their mountain.
Wei He was no fool. From how the sect acted and the secret files he gathered, he knew the three main peaks hid deep secrets.
The powerful masters up there rarely came down. They couldn't. Something held them back. Was it a strict rule? Or was there a prize on the mountain keeping them bound to the high peaks?
Wei He didn't have the full answer yet, but he had a guess.
"The records on the Nightingale's Laugh are too vague," he muttered. "Every book says something different. I have to test it myself."
He took a slow breath and focused his inner force, bringing up the Cloud-Rain Skill.
This was his core technique. It had nine levels. He was already at the eighth.
The ninth level was called the True Realm—the ultimate limit of this art.
He wondered: if he reached the peak of the eighth level and used his secret Break-Through Bead, could he jump straight to the ninth? Would that trigger his first sensory awakening in one move?
It was worth a try.
For now, he needed time. He had to max out his Whale-Flood Technique first.
"Now, let's see what happens when I open my senses completely..."
Wei He lowered his left hand. Slowly, he stripped away the protective energy he always kept around his skin. He poured a flood of raw blood-energy into his left hand, swelling the flesh, making every inch raw and sensitive.
Then, using the special method of the Cloud-Rain Skill, he vibrated his hand bones at a rapid rhythm. The vibrations forced his nerves to go wild.
This was the secret technique of the eighth level—the key to entering the True Realm. Every martial art had its own secret trick, some better than others.
Bzzzz...
A faint vibration echoed in the dark room.
Wei He controlled his energy with extreme care, spreading it evenly across his palm and fingers.
Slowly, his skin turned hypersensitive. His supernatural sense was opening up.
Hiss...
A wave of bitter cold crept over his skin. It felt like invisible streams of icy air were wrapping around his fingers.
Other senses blurred his focus. He closed his eyes, shutting out light and sound to focus entirely on the cold air touching his skin.
Crack... crack...
Frost bled out from his skin. His hand turned pale green, drained of life and blood.
In just a few seconds, the pain became unbearable.
Wei He sighed and cut off the energy. The forced sensation collapsed.
He looked down. A thick layer of white frost covered his left hand. A chill ran down his spine.
"This is terrifying. With my thick blood and iron body, I barely lasted a few seconds—and I didn't even open the sense fully. If I reached the ninth level and kept this sense open all the time, my hand would rot away. My strength would crash."
A realization hit him.
This was why the grandmasters of the No-Beginning Sect never stayed down the mountain for long.
"If the mountain has something that blocks these ghost winds, then of course they all stay up there."
He tried again. And again. The moment his left hand recovered, he pushed the limit once more.
Out of eight tries, he heard a eerie, laughing voice three times. The other five times, his hand froze solid within fifteen minutes—even with his protective energy turned on.
It was as if his defense meant nothing against the cold wind.
He logged every feeling, every change, and every detail in his mind. By the time he finished, he was exhausted.
He ate a simple meal, rested his mind, and went right back to practicing his Whale-Flood Technique.
Day in, day out. This was his routine.
It went on until dusk.
Wei He slowly opened his eyes from his meditation. He looked at the sky and stood up.
"Come here."
A young maid stepped inside instantly. "Master, what do you need?"
These maids were bought from slave markets. In this war-torn world, desperate people sold themselves just to eat. Wei He picked girls under fifteen—they were easy to train and loyal.
"Get me a change of clothes. I'm going out for fresh air."
His wife, Wan Qingqing, and his son were away. Staying in the big house alone was boring.
Work without rest burned a man out. Even with his deep blood reserves and boundless energy, resting his mind was necessary. Of course, even while walking, he kept a fraction of his mind running the Whale-Flood Technique. It was slow, but every minute counted.
Wei He changed into simple, cheap clothes and tied his long hair back.
He didn't bring any bodyguards. With a light step, he cleared the outer wall of the Chen estate and slipped away into the dusk.
The guards and servants didn't mind. They were used to it. Wei He liked walking alone, and with his terrifying strength, no assassin on earth could hurt him anyway.
He walked aimlessly along the thick stone wall of Weifang City.
Using his bone-shrinking skill, he brought his height down to five feet eight inches. He looked like an ordinary passerby in a plain, faded robe. Unless his closest friends looked right into his eyes, no one would ever guess this average man was the feared Wei He.
To his left stood the massive wall. To his right was a noisy line of street stalls.
Fried tofu, grilled meat skewers, crispy pancakes, sweet ginger cakes—the mixed smells floated through the cool air, thick and mouth-watering.
Wei He walked straight to a fried tofu stall like a regular customer and joined the line.
A big cloth sign hung beside the stall: Zhang's Fried Tofu.
The words were written in bold, sharp brush strokes, like wild animal claws striking paper.
Wei He had been drawn in by the handwriting a year ago. After tasting the fried tofu once, he became a regular.
The line moved fast. People grabbed their small bowls of hot tofu, threw on chili powder and green onions, paid their copper coins, and left.
"Boss, the usual. Six stuffed pieces," Wei He said, tossing six copper coins into the wooden money bowl.
"Coming right up!"
The owner was a strong young man dressed like a scholar. He smiled and picked up six soft tofu blocks with wooden tongs, sliding them into the boiling oil.
Wei He had eaten here for over a year. As a loyal buyer, the owner always stuffed his tofu blocks with extra minced meat and spices until they almost burst.
Soon, a hot plate of crispy tofu was handed over.
Wei He sat at a tiny wooden table in the alley, picked up his chopsticks, and took small, slow bites, enjoying the rare peace.
He had only eaten two bites when wild shouts shattered the air.
"Catch them!!"
"Don't let the little brats run!!"
"Stop right there if you got the guts!!"
A gang of big men waving heavy blades and thick wooden clubs came charging down the street, hunting two young boys.
The two boys dodged through the crowd, pulling down vegetable carts and wooden crates to block the narrow street, barely keeping ahead of the angry men.
"You bastards! Catch them! Don't let them vanish!" The black-faced leader roared, trapped behind a tipped cart.
Scared citizens dove into doorways. But a few rogue fighters stayed on the side, watching with smirks.
Suddenly, a white figure dropped down from the second floor of a nearby tea house, landing right in front of the two running boys.
"Brother Song? What's going on here?"
The man wore a fine white robe with a jade belt. He was young, handsome, and carried an easy, calm air.
"Brother Wang! Save us!!"
The leading boy yelled in joy and threw himself forward, grabbing the handsome young man's leg like a drowning man grabbing a rope.
"We just ate a quick meal in that small shop, and those thugs demanded fifty big copper coins! That's daylight robbery!"
Wang Zui sighed, gently shaking his leg to free himself from the boy's grip.
"Brother Song, fifty coppers is a bit high for a street meal, but it's not crazy expensive..."
"But we only ate two small dishes and some rice!" Song Shixiong cried out, looking wronged.
"It wasn't even fine meat! Just sliced pork with cauliflower and a bowl of sheep gut soup!" he added quickly.
"Bullshit!" The black-faced leader shouted, stomping over with his blade raised. "You two little pigs ate three whole wooden tubs of rice! You ate my shop dry and tried to slip away without paying a single coin! I'm breaking your legs today!"
Wang Zui was a rich young master born in Weifang City. He had never seen anything this ridiculous.
He had befriended the two Song brothers because they seemed smart and skilled. But now... he questioned his own judgment.
He liked making friends and spending money, but he wasn't a fool.
"How about this? I will pay for their meal," Wang Zui said, stepping forward to settle the mess.
"Are you serious?" The black-faced thug hesitated. He had seen Wang Zui jump down from the second floor with light steps. He knew this rich kid was a trained fighter, so he didn't want to push too hard.
"Of course." Wang Zui pulled a few gold leaves from his pouch and flicked them through the air with two fingers.
The gold landed right into the thug's palm.
"Keep the extra as an apology. If you ever have a problem, come to the Wang Mansion in Weifang. My name is Wang Zui."
The two Song brothers cheered, bowing and thanking him again and again.
The thugs took the gold and left without another word. The crowd, seeing the fight was over, slowly moved away.
Sitting at his small table, Wei He chewed his tofu, feeling amused and slightly annoyed.
He had sent people to escort these two Song brothers far away to a safe city. Yet somehow, the two boys had snuck into Weifang City and run right into him again.
He watched the whole comedy unfold.
Wei He had taught them the Lock-Heart Mark himself. From their footwork and dodges just now, he could tell they had practiced hard since he left.
Those thugs had raw strength, but if the two boys actually fought back, they could have beaten the gang easily. Yet they didn't throw a single punch. They just ran and yelled.
And the second Wang Zui stepped down from the building, Song Shixiong had thrown himself at his feet without a hint of surprise.
It was a setup.
Wei He knew Song Shixiong well. The boy was sly, smart, and didn't care about rules. He would use any trick to get what he wanted, though he still held a basic line of morality.
The two little rats had an agenda, and it involved this rich boy, Wang Zui.
Wei He sat quietly, watching Wang Zui lead the two boys up to the second floor of the tea house.
"Interesting," Wei He muttered.
He glanced sideways at a man standing in the crowd.
The man had a stiff, dull face—a clear sign of wearing a fake leather mask. His eyes were glued to Wang Zui's back like a snake watching its prey.
And he wasn't the only one.
At least three different groups—over a dozen hidden fighters—were lurking in the street, all watching Wang Zui through different angles.
This rich young master of Weifang City was like a piece of fat meat, surrounded by starving wolves without even knowing it.
