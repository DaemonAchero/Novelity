Torchlight flickered across the clearing.
Cheng Jing sank her weight into a low stance, her leg sweeping through the air with a heavy thud.
As a fellow martial artist who had broken through her vitality once, her footwork was clean and composed. Even though it was a surprise attack, her form, power, and precision were top notch.
Caught off guard, Wei He knew he had lost the upper hand after being provoked by her words. He had no choice but to cross his arms before his chest to shield his head and body.
Bang!
Her long leg smashed hard into Wei He's arms, bringing with it the foul smell of muddy soles.
Legs were naturally stronger than arms. Even if Cheng Jing was a woman and slightly weaker, her kicks still packed more power than Wei He's arms.
The direct hit forced Wei He back several steps.
"Is that all you've got?"
A hint of disdain flashed in Cheng Jing's eyes. She was about to press the attack when a sudden gust of wind howled nearby.
Ah!
A guard standing on the outer edge let out a bloodcurdling scream. He collapsed to the ground, his hands clawing wildly at the dirt and grass.
It was useless. An immense force in the dark dragged him rapidly into the shadows. Within seconds, his screams faded into the distance and abruptly stopped.
Silence returned.
The guards forgot all about the duel. They quickly raised their torches to light up the area.
Unfortunately, all they saw were deep fingernail scratches in the dirt and a fresh pool of blood.
"What was that?!" "Old Sun got dragged away!"
"How is that possible? Sun Zhu is one of the strongest among us!"
"I saw a dark shadow grab his legs and run! I wanted to throw something at it, but it was way too fast. I couldn't even react before..."
One guard trembled all over, whether from pure terror or regret for failing to save his companion.
Wei He and Cheng Jing stopped their fight and rushed over.
By then, Cheng Kai was already crouching at the scene.
His face was grim and his eyes were cold. He touched the dirt behind the claw marks and bloodstains, then brought his fingers to his nose and sniffed.
"It's a Black Craze! Everyone get inside now!" His face suddenly changed as he shouted.
Argh!!
Before he could even finish his sentence, another scream rang out from the other side.
Another guard was grabbed by the legs and pulled into the dark in the blink of an eye.
"Die!!" A new guard threw a torch at the shadow.
He was still a step too late.
me The torch only illuminated a small part of the creature's body.
In that brief moment, everyone caught a glimpse of a strange monster with a black and red head.
It had the head of a black and red bird with milky white eyes. The creature was over two meters long, half buried in the shadows and half exposed under the torchlight.
Its two front claws were like human hands with five fingers, tipped with razor-sharp nails that gleamed like silver metal.
The monster grabbed the guard's legs and pulled. Its massive force instantly slammed the man to the ground, dragging him into the darkness before anyone could react.
"Get inside! Move!!" Cheng Kai roared, snapping the stunned guards back to reality.
The crowd backed up quickly into the bridge house. They slammed the door shut and blocked the two window openings with thick wooden boards from their supply carts.
Most of them squatted down right away, holding their breath in deep silence.
A newcomer was so terrified that he could not control his heavy panting. In the quiet room, every breath sounded like a roar.
An veteran guard quickly reached out and covered the rookie's mouth with a cloth.
Wei He's heart pounded too. That monster moved like the wind. Even he had only caught a blurry shadow.
He thought to himself that if he were the one attacked, he would only have a fifty-fifty chance of breaking free.
Everyone stayed quiet. The only sound in the room was the crackle of two campfires. The wood snapped in the quiet night, blending with the howling wind outside, making the atmosphere even more tense.
Wei He looked around and suddenly noticed that the man who had been carving wood beside him was gone. Only a small bag of luggage remained on the ground.
A chill ran down his spine. He quickly scanned the room again.
He remembered that man's face. After traveling together for days in a team of twenty, he recognized most of them.
Yet no matter how hard he looked, he could not find the wood carver.
The only sign that the man had ever existed was the bag left beside Wei He.
An old guard nearby noticed his gaze, looked down, and sighed softly.
"Life and death are unpredictable..."
Bang!!
Suddenly, the wooden board over the window took a heavy blow.
The massive impact almost knocked down the two guards holding the board. Two more men rushed forward and pressed their weight against it to hold the line.
The old guard turned pale, swallowing the rest of his words in fear.
Wei He's heart skipped a beat, but having killed before, his nerves were much stronger now. He forced himself to stay calm.
"Fire!"
Cheng Kai stood up and yelled.
Someone grabbed a torch, lit it at the campfire, and passed it over.
Cheng Kai reached out to take it.
Bang!!
The wooden board shattered, and a dark mass burst inside. It knocked the torch straight out of Cheng Kai's reach.
"Get out!"
Two guards and Cheng Jing slashed their blades at the shadow simultaneously from three sides.
Three heavy sabers slammed into the creature's body, forcing it backward out the window.
Someone quickly picked up a torch and threw it at the beast.
The creature seemed afraid of fire. It took the blow and scrambled back, leaping out the window and vanishing into the night.
Puff... puff...
Everyone breathed heavily, their faces pale under the firelight.
Cheng Jing and the two guards dropped their weapons, only to realize that the webs of their thumbs were torn and bloody from the force of the impact.
"It's a Black Craze... Block the windows and hold the line until dawn!" Cheng Kai roared.
He was the one who had picked up the torch just now.
His face was slightly pale, but there was no panic in his eyes, only calm resolve.
The veteran guards obeyed at once, lifting the heavy wooden boards to seal the window again.
These were no ordinary boards. They were treated with special methods and bound with tough vines and leather ropes, making them extremely sturdy.
Even after several heavy hits from the monster, they were only slightly dented in the middle and remained usable.
After sealing the windows for a while, Cheng Kai signaled them to leave a tiny crack for airflow.
With so many people squeezed into a closed space with two campfires, it took only a short moment for some to feel suffocated.
As the crack opened, a faint whistling wind blew in from outside. The cold mountain breeze carried a whiff of blood into the bridge house.
It made everyone's mood even heavier.
Nobody spoke. Anyone who tried to open their mouth was quickly stopped by the veterans.
Everyone held their breath.
The guards took turns holding the wooden boards every short while. Wei He took two shifts as well.
The night dragged on until a pale white morning light finally appeared in the sky.
Faint bird chirps drifted in through the crack, lifting everyone's spirits.
Some bird calls were sharp, some deep, and others sounded like wood peckers tapping away. They sounded near and far, weaving together in the morning air.
"It's daylight. Open the door, I'll go check," Cheng Kai said in a deep voice.
An old guard opened the wooden door slightly without a word.
Cheng Kai moved like a darting rabbit, bursting out in an instant.
The door quickly shut behind him.
Everyone waited in silence. Before long, Cheng Kai's tired voice sounded from outside.
"It's safe. Come out."
After a short pause, the guards removed the boards, opened the door, and walked out of the bridge house one after another.
On the grass outside, two fresh pools of blood were clearly visible.
Animals seemed to have licked the blood, making the center faint.
Yet judging by the surrounding marks, one could still tell how fiercely the two missing guards had struggled before dying.
Wei He walked to the edge of the bloodstain, crouched down, and examined the marks carefully.
He soon found four claw marks that were much deeper than human handprints. Each mark had five fingers dug deep into the soil.
In the middle of the claw prints, there were clear, fine lines resembling palm prints.
Not far away, Cheng Kai and a few guards used the clothes and luggage of the two men to build a simple grave, marking it with durable items.
This was all that remained of the two guards.
The crowd packed up the transport carts without delay, crossed the bridge, and left the area immediately.
Before leaving, some poured water on the ground as wine for the tomb, while others left small personal items on the grave.
Everyone fell silent. No one talked. They packed quietly and drove the carts away without a word.
Only when they departed did Wei He realize that one of their horses was missing too, likely dragged away by the Black Craze during the night.
They crossed the long, ancient stone bridge and finally reached their destination town.
After handing over the cargo, everyone felt a weight lifted off their shoulders.
The journey back was quiet. Without the cargo, they traveled light and moved much faster.
It took eight days to come, but only six to return, saving two full days.
Yet no one had the mood to chat or spar anymore. They only spoke when necessary according to protocol.
When they crossed the stone bridge again on their way back, they passed through as fast as they could, without stopping for a second.
As they approached Feiye City, they took a short break.
Wei He saw Cheng Kai pull out a simple hand drawn map and use a red crayon-like stick to draw a thick mark at the stone bridge location.
Glancing at it, Wei He noticed the map was packed with at least a dozen red dots along the route.
Yet during their trip, they had barely encountered so many dangers. The bridge house was likely a new threat location they had never faced before.
"Do you think we didn't run into as many dangers as marked?" Cheng Jing sat down next to him and whispered.
Only now did everyone's mood lighten up enough to talk.
"..." Wei He looked at her, not knowing how to reply.
"Every red dot on that map marks a spot where people have died," Cheng Jing whispered. "The only reason we survive most of our runs is because of the Chief Guard."
"The Chief Guard knows how to avoid every single red dot, what time is best to travel, and what supplies to prepare.
