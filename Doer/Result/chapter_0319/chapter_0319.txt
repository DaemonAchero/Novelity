In Lu City, Yizhou, the streets crisscrossed like a net between rows of packed buildings. Patrols of Wu soldiers rode through the town on horseback.
These soldiers were much taller and bigger than the local Yuan civilians. They wore heavy armor, and their bodies overflowed with raw, boiling vitality. The heat coming off them was so intense that it raised the temperature of the whole city just by them walking past.
The streets still bore the fresh scars of recent battle and slaughter.
Down the main road, a lavish white carriage pulled by four huge oxen rolled forward slowly, as if inspecting the town. The carriage was much larger than normal ones.
Inside the cabin, two giant men over three meters tall sat across from each other. They silently watched the ruined scenery pass outside the window.
The man on the left had broad shoulders, a thick chest, and a square face with sharp eyes and a full beard. He looked fierce and powerful. This was General Xue Wenyuan, a True Blood commander stationed here by the Wu State.
The man on the right looked noble, though he was thin, with arms that hung all the way down to his knees. His name was Li Rong. He was one of the most trusted advisors to the National Master, famous for his cold, flawless calculations.
"Lord Li," General Xue spoke up slowly, breaking the silence. "You came all this way to inspect the town, but you must have another reason too, right?"
Lu City had just been looted and massacred. It was poor, broken, and lacked everything. Why would someone like Li Rong leave the side of the National Master to come to a useless, ruined place like this? Xue Wenyuan was confused.
Li Rong smiled softly. "Do not worry, General Xue. This Yuan Empire focuses on the path of Martial Force. Alongside our True Blood path, it was once a grand road to power. But... do you know the real reason why the National Master is banning martial arts among the common people?"
"To spread the luck of our Wu Kingdom and swallow up the Yuan people?" Xue Wenyuan guessed. He was just a warlord and did not care for deep politics.
Li Rong laughed.
"The Yuan Empire has immense resources, yet its army cannot match even one-tenth of our Wu forces. The reason is simple: waste and internal chaos."
He paused, then continued. "In Yuan, everyone practices martial arts. There was no ban. Anyone could easily learn how to fight. But when people gain power, their blood boils, and they make stupid, emotional mistakes.
If a weak man makes a mistake, the harm is tiny. You can ignore it.
But when a powerful master makes a mistake out of anger, the destruction is massive. You cannot cure this human flaw. As long as people have emotions, they will lose control and make mistakes."
Xue Wenyuan nodded. He knew Li Rong was originally a native of the Yuan Empire from the Chaos God Sect. But after seeing how dark and hopeless Yuan was, Li Rong betrayed them and joined the Wu Kingdom.
The old doctrine of the Chaos God Sect was survival of the fittest. But Li Rong built upon that idea. He believed that a state must rule with an absolute iron fist, control all weapons, weaken the common people, and ban martial arts. Only then could there be real peace.
"You speak the truth, Lord Li," Xue Wenyuan said, starting to guess his goal. "But you still haven't said why you came to Lu City. Is it an order from the National Master?"
"Be patient, General," Li Rong smiled. "I came here on the order of the National Master to run a test."
"A test?"
"Yes." Li Rong tapped his fingers rhythmically against the wooden window frame. "I did not come to enforce the martial ban. I came to test a new system on our Wu immigrants here: the Public Law System."
"The Public Law System?!" Xue Wenyuan's face darkened. His mind shook as he stared at Li Rong.
"Is the National Master serious? Do you know how much pushback this system will face? And the pushback won't come from the Yuan people. It will come from inside our own Wu Kingdom!"
"I know," Li Rong said calmly with a slight smile. "But history moves forward. If you do not push ahead, your nation dies. Look at Yuan. They fell into ruin because they locked their doors and only fought among themselves."
Both men went quiet.
The Public Law System was another experimental rule created by the National Master. It combined the True Blood path of Wu with the Martial Force path of Yuan.
Under this law, the state distributed all resources fairly, based purely on talent and strength.
The government collected and controlled all martial arts manuals and resources. Anyone who wanted to learn could sign a contract with the state to get free techniques. They could earn better resources through military service and merit.
This completely cut off the old habit of sects hoarding secrets. It put the right manual into the right hands for maximum power. As long as you had the talent, the state would give you the ultimate combination of skills.
No more secret master secrets. No more sect rivalries.
Martial artists under this system would become far stronger than anything seen before.
"But doesn't this go against the martial ban?" Xue Wenyuan asked in a heavy voice.
"That is why we only give this right to our Wu citizens," Li Rong chuckled. "It will drive the Yuan people crazy with envy, making them beg to join our empire. It will make controlling them much easier."
"And where will we get the resources to feed all these fighters?" Xue Wenyuan pressed.
"Doesn't the Yuan Empire have plenty?" Li Rong replied. "We use this system to turn everyone into hunters. They will work tirelessly for the state to earn the power they want."
Xue Wenyuan took a cold breath.
He suddenly realized the terrifying truth behind this plan.
Ban martial arts for the conquered Yuan people to keep them weak. Grant the Public Law System to the Wu people to make them hungry for power. The massive urge to learn martial arts, suppressed for so long, would explode. Millions of Wu citizens would rush out like wild beasts to raid and plunder resources for merit.
This was turning the entire nation into a giant army!
It made Xue Wenyuan's spine run cold.
The Yuan Empire might have lasted another ten years, but this strategy would bleed it dry in a fraction of that time. This was a dark plan to swallow an entire empire whole.
He looked at Li Rong again with newfound fear.
If this test in Lu City succeeded, the world was about to enter an era of blood and chaos. The power gap between the two nations would grow so wide that Yuan would never recover.

CRACK!
A flash of lightning turned the inside of the Mo Ming Temple blindingly white.
Wei He held onto the hand in front of him, but the hand had changed completely.
Yuan Duzi's cold, beautiful body was gone.
In her place stood a five-meter-tall humanoid monster, covered in rusty, filthy sludge.
The monster had no eyes, nose, or ears on its head. It only had a massive, saw-toothed mouth that took up its entire face.
Wei He stared at her face. He could still feel a gaze staring back at him from the dark.
That was Yuan Duzi.
"Look around you," Yuan Duzi's voice rang out again.
But unlike before, her voice sounded artificial now, as if she was vibrating the air using raw force instead of real vocal cords.
Wei He forced down the urge to pull back and strike. He turned his head to look around the temple.
The quiet, ordinary shrine was now decaying rapidly. Even the walls had changed. The gray stone had turned into a pale, bone-like shell material, covered in countless scratches and deep pits.
CRACK!
Another bolt of lightning flashed.
Out of the corner of his eye, Wei He spotted something outside the window. He whipped his head around to look.
A giant, blood-red eye, as big as the window itself, was pressed against the glass, staring directly at him.
"Do not be afraid," Yuan Duzi said quietly.
"You are sharing my hyper-perception right now. This is what the world looks like through my eyes. Of course, what each person sees is a little different."
She held his hand and pulled him slowly toward the exit of the temple.
They reached the front gate.
The wooden door was coated in a thick, dark layer of foul sludge, looking like sewer filth. It was wet, stank terribly, and dripped like slime.
"This is the Forbidden Gate," Yuan Duzi said. She did not open it.
She raised a finger that was sharp like a steel blade and lightly tapped the door with her nail.
RIPPLE.
A soft sound echoed, like a stone dropping into a pool of water.
The gray slime on the door began to flow and twist. It slowly gathered together, forming swarms of pale, ghostly insects. They stacked over each other in thick layers and quickly morphed into two gray door rings.
"When you can freely create these door rings here on your own, you will be able to leave and enter this place whenever you want," Yuan Duzi explained.
"Mo Ming Temple is one of the few safe points in our Profound Mystery Sect's domain. And this place is the main core," she said, pulling her hand back and looking down at him.
"Remember this well: our domain is not completely safe like the Wu Shi Sect's. The only thing this place guarantees is protection against the Ghost Wind. But the real dangers out here are not just the wind. There are True Beasts too."
"This disciple understands," Wei He nodded.
He felt the heavy monster hand loosen and let go.
SWOOSH...
The terrifying vision snapped away instantly. The lightning vanished.
The temple returned to its quiet, normal state. The giant blood-red eye outside the window was gone.
Wei He opened his eyes wide.
There was no one in front of him. Yuan Duzi and the other two leaders had already left.
Only a cold-looking woman with a ponytail remained. She wore a simple white robe with a long sword strapped to her back, leaning quietly against the right wall.
"You're awake?" Seeing him open his eyes, the woman stood up straight. "I am Yao Wan. Senior Sister Yuan Duzi asked me to teach you the Thousand Mountains and Ten Thousand Locks Technique. I am here to help you unlock your hyper-perception. By the way, which part of your body are you trying to sense?"
Yao Wan? Sounds like 'going to die' in slang, Wei He thought to himself. But he quickly shook the bad thought away. It was actually a poetic name.
"Greeting, Senior Sister. I am focusing on my left hand," Wei He answered respectfully.
She was here to teach him, so he had to show proper respect.
"Your left hand?" Yao Wan frowned. "Then how do you plan to sense danger? Do you plan to walk up and touch a True Beast to know it's there?"
"I was just trying to be safe and take it step by step... I didn't think that far," Wei He replied truthfully.
"That is why almost no one starts with touch," Yao Wan explained. "Most people choose vision, hearing, or smell first. That way, you can spot danger from a distance. If you choose touch, you need to awaken hyper-perception over your entire body just to feel vibrations around you."
She glanced outside.
"Enough talk. Follow me."
She turned around and walked toward the back of the main hall.
Wei He fell in step behind her, following her deep into the temple.
