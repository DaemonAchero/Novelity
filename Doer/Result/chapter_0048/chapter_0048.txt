Holding his books, Wei He walked toward the cloth shop. Before long, he spotted a tavern by the side of the road.
Yet, there was no smell of booze at the door. Instead, the heavy stench of hot tea filled the air.
Even in this harsh season, loud boasts and chatter drifted out from inside.
Wei He paused, pulled back the door curtain, and stepped in.
About ten people sat scattered inside. Most wore decent, clean clothes.
In times like these, only people with money could afford to look clean and sit around drinking tea.
In a quiet corner of the room, three thin, dirty figures crouched together. Wooden signs stood in front of them with crooked writing: Selling myself to bury my father. Selling myself to bury my mother. Selling myself to bury my brother.
The three sat in a line, looking miserable. Wei He felt speechless for reasons he could not name.
He carried his books to an empty table and sat down.
A young girl hurried over to pour him warm water. She whispered the prices with care.
Different teas cost different amounts. They did not take grain flour for payment here. Instead, they took relief tokens from the Tongcheng Gang.
"Tongcheng Gang relief tokens?" Wei He frowned. "What are those?"
"You might not know, sir," the girl explained quickly, having said this a thousand times before. "The Chief pities the poor souls struggling to live, so he hands out relief tokens. People use them to get food. One token gets you a set amount of grain flour."
Relief tokens?
Would a gang like that really be so kind?
Wei He did not buy it. No one gave out free favors in this cold world. Something dark had to be hidden behind this generous act.
"If you don't have tokens, you can pay with inner-city gold beans," the girl added.
Wei He had those.
He had killed plenty of bandits lately. The money pouches he stole were packed with these so-called gold beans—bits of cheap copper mixed with a tiny bit of gold.
Ten beans made a gold leaf. Ten leaves made a full ounce of real gold. It was the simplest coin used in the inner city.
The inner city stayed stable only because the Seven Alliance kept prices under control.
He wondered how long that alliance would last.
Wei He sighed to himself and ordered a pot of buckwheat tea. He placed a single gold bean on the table—it was more than enough.
The shop owner even gave him a small dish of peanuts.
rights reserved.
The peanuts smelled stale and moldy, but the tea was packed with buckwheat and grains. Once he finished the liquid, he could eat the leftovers like a meal.
Wei He settled in to listen. Taverns and teahouses were full of gossip and big talk, making them the best places to gather news.
People who came here were usually rich enough not to starve. They had time to waste. Since their families had wealth, their roots ran deep.
Naturally, they knew things.
When weird or interesting things happened, these men came here to brag and share stories with friends. It made them feel important.
Their gatherings brought in street performers, singers, and storytellers looking to make a coin.
More entertainment brought bigger crowds.
And because these storytellers traveled everywhere, they brought news from all over—some true, some fake.
This was the exact stream of information Wei He needed. Now that he had reached the Stone Skin stage, Master Zheng was no longer watching his every move. He had plenty of free time, and spending a day checking the pulse of the city was a smart move.
Yonghe Escort Agency.
Inside the dreary stone courtyard, senior chiefs and main men of the Cheng family stood gathered. Escorts from every branch in the city had rushed over too.
Nearly a hundred men packed the yard, waiting in tense silence for news.
Cheng Shaojiu stood near the front of the family line, waiting silently.
He was waiting for news from the Chief, Cheng Zhengxing.
Moments later, Cheng Zhengxing walked in from outside, flanked by a few escorts.
His face was stiff. He did not say a single word as he walked toward the high wooden stage.
The crowd parted silently to make way for him.
As he passed Cheng Shaojiu, Cheng Zhengxing stopped for a second. He looked at his nephew as if he wanted to say something.
In the end, he kept his mouth shut and walked up to the platform.
He climbed the steps one by one, turned, and looked down at his men.
Every eye fixed on him.
The quiet yard turned dead silent. The air felt heavy, almost terrifying.
Everyone held their breath, waiting for the final word.
Cheng Zhengxing looked across the crowd. He had built this massive team with his own sweat and blood over decades.
And now...
He opened his mouth to speak.
The sun beat down hard, making him break into a thick sweat.
His chest felt tight. He took a few deep breaths, but the pressure only grew heavier.
He had stayed up for nights on end, running everywhere just to keep the agency alive.
This last shipment was his only chance to bounce back.
He had gambled everything on it.
And yet...
Cheng Zhengxing felt a wave of icy cold run through his blood, despite the scorching heat.
He squeezed his fists tight, but his palms were completely cold.
"I..."
He finally spoke.
Everyone stared at him, waiting.
He opened his mouth again.
"I... have failed you all..."
Suddenly, his vision went black. A loud ringing filled his ears, blocking out all sound.
His body tilted backward.
Thud.
He crashed to the ground, lost to the world.
The Cheng family was falling.
The news spread like a wildfire as escorts took their final pay and left.
They had been waiting to see if this last job could save the agency.
It was a total disaster. The cargo was stolen.
And the Chief, who carried all their hopes, had collapsed and fallen gravely ill.
Because they lost the goods, Yonghe Escort Agency owed a massive fine they could never afford.
With Cheng Zhengxing bedridden, no one was left to lead. The agency was already running out of money to pay wages. Now that the leader was down, the remaining escorts lost all patience.
A few old-timers stayed behind out of loyalty, but most of the young escorts packed their bags and left to find new work.
In a single day, the busy courtyard turned empty. Cheng Shaojiu, with the help of two chiefs, counted up all the family grain and assets.
He was horrified by the results. They were still short.
"By the rules, we have to pay double..." His wife, Cheng Zhang, whispered with a heavy frown. "We are short by at least seven hundred ounces of gold..."
"Seven hundred ounces..." Cheng Shaojiu ground his teeth, staring at the few remaining crates of food and supplies.
In normal times, seven hundred ounces of gold was a lot, but a large family business like theirs could pull it together.
But now?
His uncle was unconscious. Every family member had cleared out their personal savings, and the remaining chiefs had given everything they had.
They were still missing seven hundred ounces...
Where could he borrow that kind of money?
Who around them even had that much gold?
Cheng Shaojiu felt lost, as if a mountain were crushing his chest.
"Young Chief..." Chief Cheng Kai started to speak, but he swallowed his words. He had no way to raise that much gold either.
"They want an answer in two days," his wife's voice drifted into his ears. "Or they will take our secret technique for raising the Silver-Kiss Black Snake as payment."
Cheng Shaojiu bit his lip so hard it almost bled. He did not even remember how he walked back to his room.
The next day, he wandered out like a ghost, telling his family he was going to look for a solution. He dressed and walked toward the Return Mountain Fist Hall.
Once inside, he stood frozen in his usual corner, staring blankly at the iron sand tub and the burning coals.
The fellow martial brothers who used to call him friend stood far away. Not one came over to greet him.
Xiao Ran, Jiang Yan, and Jiang Su had long distanced themselves from him, giving him the cold shoulder.
He looked around, knowing they had all heard about the disaster at his agency.
"Brother Cheng."
Wei He walked over to him.
"I heard about the agency yesterday. If you need anything, just say the word."
Cheng Shaojiu was a close friend, and Wei He was listed as an escort under his family's agency. Seeing the trouble, Wei He wanted to check on him.
Cheng Shaojiu looked up at Wei He.
He wanted to ask about money, but a bitter smile crossed his mind.
It was seven hundred ounces of gold. He knew Wei He's income well—at most, he earned forty ounces a month from the agency.
How could Wei He possibly have that kind of cash?
The words died in his throat. Cheng Shaojiu stayed silent for a second, then forced a bright smile onto his face.
"It's fine. I can handle it. Don't worry, Xiao He."
Rather than dragging Wei He down with his troubles, he chose to keep it to himself.
This was the Cheng family's mess. It had nothing to do with Wei He.
Telling him would only make him worry for nothing.
"Don't worry," Cheng Shaojiu said, patting Wei He's shoulder with a laugh.
"If you need help, speak up," Wei He said in a heavy voice. "We are brothers, and I am Cheng He's godfather. Don't bottle it all up inside."
Wei He had robbed plenty of bad guys lately and saved up his money. He had over fifteen hundred gold notes sitting in his pouch, and he was ready to step up if needed.
"I know. Don't worry," Cheng Shaojiu replied. Hearing Wei He's warm words made him even more determined not to ruin his friend.
He had to fix his own family's mess. He couldn't drag his brother down with him.
"Though... I might have to hold off on your pay this month," Cheng Shaojiu added with a bitter laugh.
"That's fine. Take care of your business first," Wei He nodded.
Seeing that Cheng Shaojiu seemed composed, Wei He slowly let down his guard.
"Are you really okay?"
"Really, no problem at all," Cheng Shaojiu smiled brightly.
He knew everyone in the yard was watching him. The more dangerous things got, the less he could show weakness or panic.
His uncle had already fallen. If he showed any fear now, the Cheng family's old enemies would not just stand by.
They would act like hungry wolves, rushing in to tear the family apart the moment they smelled blood.
