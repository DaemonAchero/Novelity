Wei He shook his head and turned to leave the courtyard.
He saw the sister and brother again. They were asking around, trying to find out what happened to their father. But with so many people rushing in, no one recognized him. They just shook their heads.
After asking for a while with no answers, the little boy was on the verge of tears. His sister kept trying to comfort him. She stooped down to hug him tight, then softly sang him a song.
"Sect Master..." A female disciple of Wanqing Sect could hardly watch anymore. She stepped closer, wanting to speak, but didn't know what to say.
"I know," Wei He said softly. "Kids like them have no one left. Wanqing Sect can take them in and raise them as our own. But only if they want to."
"Thank you, Sect Master!" She wiped her tears, overjoyed, and quickly walked toward the children.
Wei He left the courtyard and leaped up, landing quietly on the roof of an empty two-story house.
It used to belong to a middle-class family in Qingqi County. The head of the house was known for his kindness, with no real faults other than a small family. But the plague had wiped out all five of them in just two weeks.
Stories like this were everywhere in Qingqi County. White banners hung from every doorway, and every survivor wore black bands of mourning. Normally, an empty house like this would have been looted by bandits. But now, no one dared to come. Those who tried were already dead.
Wei He stood on the roof tiles, looking out over the county.
It was a small place, just a dozen streets surrounded by mountains, with a river running through the middle. Far down the river, a few white paper lanterns floated along the water—a funeral custom of the rich.
Wei He didn't linger. He set off toward Xuanjing.
He passed through Qingqi County, then Heshan County. Heshan was slightly better, but white banners and funeral paper still covered the ground. Beyond Heshan lay a few small villages. They were completely empty, completely dead.
It was darker and colder than Feiye City ever was. Back then, Feiye City had the Xiangqu Cult. They were evil, but at least people were still alive. Here, there was no life left at all.
With a plague this bad, the provincial government should react soon, Wei He thought. Let's see what they do. If they fail... I need to be ready to run.
The land around Xuanjing was becoming unlivable.
He sighed and picked up his speed, heading for Xuanjing City. He needed to talk to Wang Shaojun and see how bad things really were.

A few days passed.
Thanks to Jiu Ying's medicine and his own strong body, the hunter slowly began to recover. Such recoveries were rare, but they happened.
To repay Jiu Ying and Wei He, the hunter finally led Wei He into Black House Mountain.
Black House Mountain was massive, stretching across a thousand miles. It was a endless sea of trees, packed with deadly insects and sharp peaks. The Wanqing Sect only held a tiny fraction of its outer edge.
The hunter led Wei He deep into the mountains, past the border, toward the main ridge. He had a strange knack for dodging the territories of dangerous beasts.
Soon, they reached a massive white pine tree deep in the woods. White pines were rare, and this one was as thick as a man's embrace. The trees around them stood tall, and sunlight cut through the thin mist.
A tiny red squirrel sat on the hunter's shoulder. It stared into the distance toward a cliff, making sharp squeaks.
"Over there," the hunter said. "The cave is less than a mile ahead. Straight line. A big rock blocks half the entrance, and I carved a mark on it."
"Good. Wait here," Wei He replied. He had already poisoned the hunter to make sure he didn't try anything slick.
Wei He cut a few deep marks into the pine tree as a sign. Then he sprang forward, rushing into the deep forest.
...
Deep inside Black House Mountain.
The pine trees parted to reveal a green slope. On the slope sat a cave that looked man-made. It was five meters tall, blocked by a large rock covered in moss and vines.
A group of people had already gathered inside the cave. Most of them were not dressed like locals. They carried curved daggers and small shields, wearing white or grey turbans. Their clothes were embroidered with dark red trim. The only way to tell them apart was their clothing styles—some wore simple clothes, while others were decked out in pearls and jade.
Over a dozen people stood inside, all staring at the entrance.
A few martial artists dressed in local attire were blocked at the mouth of the cave, unable to step inside. Martial artists in turbans stood guard with blades and shields, refusing to let them pass.
"We found this wall technique first! Ge Muyi, what right do you have to keep us out?" one of the local martial artists snapped.
"This is the border, and this cave sits on our side," a young man in a silver turban sneered. He took a drag from a pipe and blew out a ring of smoke. "That makes it ours. What right do you have to look at it?"
He shook his head and laughed. "Besides, techniques belong to the strong. If you don't like it, force your way in. Too bad you can't."
"You..."
Before the local martial artist could finish, the crowd gasped.
"Sha Sikui is here! Flat-Top Sand Blade Sha Sikui!"
"Romu Mayi with the Golden Bull Staff is here too!"
"Even the experts from the Red Flower Sect arrived!"
"It's been four days. Everyone who matters is here."
The crowd whispered and backed away, afraid of getting caught in a fight.
The turbaned experts from Jin Province arrived one after another, eyeing each other with cold glare. Meanwhile, only a handful of martial artists represented Tai Province. This place was too far from the local capital, and few dared to cross the poisonous mist to get here. Jin Province martial artists, on the other hand, dealt with poison and insects every day.
The technique on the cave wall was a rare art discovered by accident. It detailed every step of cultivation, from First Blood all the way to Organ Refinement. As soon as word got out, factions rushed over.
One by one, the Jin Province martial artists took turns entering the cave to read the wall. As time passed, almost every major group from Jin Province got a turn.
Yet the local Tai Province martial artists were still blocked outside.
Xue Dan, the man leading them, trembled with rage. He and his men lived in the safe zones of Black House Mountain near Jin Province—areas free of toxic mist and beasts. Since it was near the hunting grounds, people often came here to gather herbs, and there was rarely any trouble. But when real profits were on the line, the Jin Province fighters always banded together against outsiders.
Just like now.
They had all taken turns copying the technique, yet they still refused to let Xue Dan's group step foot inside.
"I heard there's something hidden underneath the wall technique—a subterranean palace."
"Makes sense. Otherwise, copying a wall technique wouldn't take this long."
Quiet whispers drifted into Xue Dan's ears, fueling his anger. Technically, this land belonged to both provinces. Yet these bullies wouldn't even let them make a copy, even though Xue Dan's group had found the cave first.
"Hero Lei Wu is here!" someone shouted from behind.
Xue Dan's face lit up. Lei Wu was a famous local fighter known for his strength and his habit of helping the weak.
A muscular man in black clothes and a white turban dashed through the trees, landing lightly near the cave.
"What's going on?" Lei Wu asked, his voice deep as he saw Xue Dan and the others blocked outside.
"Hero Lei, you came at the right time!" Xue Dan rushed forward and explained everything.
Fights like this along the border were nothing new. Jin Province martial artists were far more aggressive than local ones. Even though there were more local fighters in total, the Jin Province side was united and hated outsiders.
The bad blood went back to trade.
Tai Province focused heavily on martial arts and needed massive amounts of food to sustain its fighters. Jin Province was a major food producer, so food flowed in from there. But greedy merchants slowly jacked up the prices as Tai Province became more dependent on them. Eventually, locals began to see Jin Province as a land of greedy scammers.
The boiling point came during a major crisis in Tai Province. The merchant guilds from Jin Province banded together to hoard food, driving prices through the roof and making a fortune off the suffering. From that moment on, the two sides became bitter enemies.
