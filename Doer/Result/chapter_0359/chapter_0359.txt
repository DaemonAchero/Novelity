“If every trace of True Power carries a pull, then as it stacks up... with how much force I have, maybe quantity will turn into quality.”
Wei He pondered this in his heart.
The God of Gravity was his first visualization. In this world, he was likely the first person to turn this concept into a deity.
Because of that, he had to figure out its effects step by step.
“So, if I keep strengthening this pull, what happens down the line?”
Wei He ran a brief mental check. Immediately, a flood of ideas popped up—mostly drawn from the martial arts novels of his past life about stealing other people's inner force.
He kept testing the ideas in his head, and the inspiration kept coming.
It got to the point where his conversion rate slowed down.
Only then did he snap out of it.
“Doesn't matter. I just need to take it step by step until I hit perfection.”
Right away, Wei He locked his focus back in. He kept pouring his thoughts into the image of the god. At the same time, his True Power flowed like a stream through his thirteen acupoints, nourishing the vision without end.
In the days that followed, he buried himself in training. Time lost all meaning in the mountain.
Now and then, he took a trip back home to check on his parents, his wife, and his kid. It helped cut through the sheer boredom of non-stop cultivation.
Whenever he hit a wall, he looked for Senior Sister Yuan Duzi. But he could rarely find her. Most of the time, he turned to Chang Xuezhong and Yao Wan instead.
Those two were ahead of him in their training. Their raw combat strength was lower than his, but they knew the cultivation paths much better. They could always point him in the right direction when he got stuck.
On top of that, his main art moved along fast. Maybe it was because gravity actually existed all around him—it was real, everywhere, and impossible to miss.
The conversion rate was terrifyingly high. His progress moved faster by the day.
Once all the True Power in his body finished converting, it would turn into the force of the second layer of the Black Lock Art. When that happened, he just needed to sink the God of Gravity into one of his acupoints to reach the second realm.
After his power was full, he could trigger the Over-Perception state and push through his second Perception.
After all, even with enough power, he still needed a real touch of the world outside to trigger a complete shift in his force. It was a spark, a catalyst.
Wei He knew the road ahead clearly.
It was just that, outside of training, he still had one issue to clean up.
Sizzle...
Clear green tea flowed slowly into a cup.
Bright sunlight hit the window, falling right beside the two people sitting inside.
“What brings you out to look for me today? Stuck on your training again?”
Yao Wan’s body had fully recovered.
A near-fatal wound like that had only taken her a few months to fix completely. The natural healing power of a Grand Perception expert was plain terrifying.
“I have a few things to ask you, Senior Sister.”
Wei He had just stepped out of deep training. Three straight months of isolation had left him looking a bit stripped of humanity.
When you go too long without talking to anyone, your mind gets stiff and slow. It was only natural.
“Spit it out. Knowing you, it’s about martial arts again.” Yao Wan sighed softly, feeling helpless. “Good thing you caught me early. I have business of my own. In a few days, I’m taking a long trip. You won’t be able to find me then.”
“What kind of business?” Wei He asked.
“Nothing major. Just tagging along with a friend to pick some herbs and guard their back.” Yao Wan shook her head.
She glanced at Wei He’s expression and smiled. “Don’t worry. It’s no big deal. The place isn’t even in Dayuan. I’m just going to back them up, so it won’t take long. I’ll be back soon.”
“All right... take care out there.” Wei He nodded.
He truly saw Yao Wan as one of his own now. All those months she had spent watching over him counted for something. Even if he hadn't strictly needed it, it was still genuine care and effort. Plus, she had answered so many of his questions that he owed her a massive debt.
“Alright, ask away. Is it about the visualization again?” Yao Wan laughed.
“Yes. There are still a few small details I can’t wrap my head around...” Wei He laid out his doubts one by one.
Yao Wan caught on instantly and cleared up his questions with ease.
Once that was done, she brought up the topic of the retainers.
“Speaking of which, both of your retainer spots are open now. What are your plans?”
Wei He shook his head. “No plans yet. What are you getting at, Senior Sister?”
“People have been coming through me to get to you. They want those two spots.”
Yao Wan listed off the names of the people who had sent gifts and favors to ask for a chance.
Wei He agreed to think it over.
With his questions answered, he headed back to his cave to dive right back into training. At his current speed, it wouldn’t take long to max out the Black Lock Art.
The first level of the Black Lock Art mostly came down to two things.
First, having enough raw force.
Second, finishing the visualization completely.
The first point wasn't even a question for Wei He—he had cleared that bar a long time ago.
The second point was the real key. Once the vision was complete, he had to convert his force, then sink the image into his acupoint to build up momentum.
Wei He ran the numbers. At his current speed, half a year would be enough to convert all his True Power into the second-layer force.
Time kept moving, and soon, a letter from afar arrived at the Xuanmiao Sect.
Without realizing it, two more months had passed.
Inside the cave dwelling.
Wei He slowly opened his eyes.
He looked at the thick layer of dust coating the ground around him, then turned his head toward the timekeeper in the corner.
It was a crude sun-dial tool that tracked light. It didn't just track hours; it marked days too. They called it a Sun Clock.
“So much time has passed already... Living without retainers really is inconvenient.”
Wei He let out a quiet sigh and stood up from the cold floor.
Having spent so long without a break, he felt like he had drifted away from the world, like a ghost hiding out in the dark.
He knew this state was bad for his mind and body.
He walked to the stone entrance and pushed the door open.
“Qingyuan greets Senior Brother.”
Someone was outside?
And not just one person—four people were waiting right outside his door.
Looking at their tired faces, they had clearly been standing there for a long time.
Wei He was taken aback.
When he trained, he locked his mind in so hard that he never paid attention to what happened outside. He only watched his immediate surroundings, often shutting off his senses entirely.
He hadn't expected a whole group to be waiting on him.
“What are you all doing here...?” His eyes swept across them.
Two men and two women. All of them looked respectful, ranging in age.
“Senior Brother, we are all from the outer mountain. Senior Sister Yao ordered us to guard this place and wait for you to come out. She said it was about the retainer spots you agreed on.”
An older man with graying hair stepped forward and spoke in a deep voice. He moved with steady ground, showing no fear or timidness.
“You don't beat around the bush,” Wei He nodded. “For Senior Sister Yao’s sake, I can offer one spot to your group. As for the other...”
Right, he suddenly remembered the letter he had sent to Taizhou. The reply should have come back by now.
Wei He stepped back into the cave and pulled out the letter.
Yao’s retainers had delivered it yesterday. Since he had no staff of his own, he had to rely on others to run small errands for him.
Yesterday he had been too busy converting his force to open it. Now that he was resting, it was time to read it.
Wei He tore the envelope open and pulled out the paper.
It was a long letter.
Wang Shaojun gave a short breakdown of everything that had happened since Wei He left.
In the end, he turned the offer down.
He wrote that if he left alone, his wife and children would have no protection. His family couldn't escape the worldly mess, which meant he couldn't drop everything to pursue cultivation.
He had no choice but to turn down Wei He’s kindness.
Near the bottom, the words carried a bitter, lonely weight.
Wei He could almost see the look on Wang Shaojun’s face as he wrote it.
He folded the paper back up, his eyes dark with mixed feelings.
Outside, the four people were still standing in silence, waiting for his word.
The fact that these four could freely enter the inner mountain showed they had powerful backings. They clearly belonged to the families of Inner Mountain Experts.
The reason four people like this wanted to become his retainers was simple.
They wanted to pull him to their side, or bet on his future.
Someone in the sect had recognized his talent and wanted to place their chips on him.
Which meant...
These four weren't just acting on their own.
Wei He understood the game.
He stepped back out of the cave and looked at the four waiting figures.
“I’ll say what I said before: there is only one retainer spot. You’ve seen my demands, haven't you?”
The fourth layer of the Whale Flood Art needed a special state called "Sea Line." To push into that state, he needed the oil of a Black-Line Whale as a burnable supply.
“We have seen them, of course. As for the Black-Line Whale, I already have a lead. It’s just that getting it... might be a bit tricky.”
A smaller girl stepped forward and spoke up in a clear voice.
She threw a smug look toward the other three.
They were all from Expert families inside the sect. Being the first to find a lead gave her the upper hand in this fight.
“Oh?” Wei He’s interest flared.
The Whale Flood Art was tied directly to his total energy reserves and his raw physical strength.
That whale oil was crucial.
He locked his gaze onto her. “What is your name?”
“My name is Lou Shengyue. Regarding the Black-Line Whale you need, my family has already found a trail. It’s just extremely hard to get, so... we haven't managed to secure it yet...”
The girl was clearly young, her eyes still holding a sharp, lively bounce.
The Lou family was a name Wei He had heard from Chang Xuezhong. They were a massive faction inside the Xuanmiao Sect.
Their lineage ran deep, with at least five Inner Mountain Experts in their bloodline. They held real weight.
Of course, a family like the Lous couldn't match the top three giants—the families standing behind Senior Sister Yuan Duzi, Baihezi, and Qingmeizi.
Still, the Lou family was formidable. They had numbers and stuck together.
Their only flaw was that none of their Experts were extraordinarily strong. Their best fighter had only managed to break into the Transformation Realm a few years back.
