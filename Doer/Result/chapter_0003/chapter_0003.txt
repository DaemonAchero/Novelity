“Good… they didn’t find it.”
Chen Biao was nothing but a lowlife thug. If he had spotted the money bag hidden on Wei He’s belly, he would have taken it by force without a second thought.
Wei He twisted through the narrow city streets until he reached a rundown residential neighborhood near the inner wall.
Shouts from heavy physical training echoed from inside one of the large courtyards. A wooden board hung above the main entrance, carved with four bold words: Mountain Returning Fist, Zheng.
Knock, knock, knock.
Wei He knocked on the door. After a short pause, the heavy wood creaked open, revealing a broad, scarred face.
“Who is it? What do you want?”
“I’m here to learn martial arts,” Wei He said, his voice deep and steady.
The man scanned him from head to toe. “Got the cash?”
“I have it.”
“Get inside.”
The heavy door opened wide, and Wei He stepped through the entry.
In the courtyard, about a dozen shirtless men were sweating and lifting heavy stone blocks to build up their strength. Under the shade of a large tree sat a thin old man with a goat beard, sipping tea with a cool, relaxed expression.
“Young Wei? What’s this? Saved up enough money?” The thin old man looked at him and spoke up.
“Old Zheng, I have enough.”
Wei He had visited this place many times before. He was always focused and hard-working, and he had even run a few free errands for the old man. Because of that, Old Zheng had a decent impression of him. But good impressions didn’t buy training. Business was business, and learning martial arts required real coin.
Now that the money was here, the old man was willing to talk.
“Good. For poor people like us, the only way out is to master fists and legs. Why? Because weapons are way too expensive, and flashy palm or finger strikes don’t hit as hard as a solid fist.” Old Zheng stroked his beard and spoke in a flat, dry voice. “Only fists can change your life! Master them, and you might actually change your fate!”
“The master speaks the truth!” A few men nearby quickly called out, trying to kiss up to the old man.
Wei He didn’t say a word. He pulled the money bag from his clothes, held it up with both hands, and bowed low to offer it.
By late afternoon, Wei He wore a crisp, gray cloth training vest, matching pants, and sturdy cloth shoes. A bright red character, Zheng, was stitched right onto his chest. This was the uniform worn only by Old Zheng’s official student disciples.
The last red rays of sunset faded from the edge of the alley. Wei He walked fast toward home. His face was dead calm, but his heart was full of anxiety.
His second sister, Wei Ying, was all alone at home, and he was terrified something might happen to her. Even though he wasn’t the original owner of this body and didn't have deep emotional roots, they were still family now, and they had lived together for a long time.
He cut through a main street, turned through a few dark side alleys, and soon reached the entrance of his home lane.
Three men were hunched over right at the entrance, smoking heavy tobacco. The tallest, roughest guy had a short crew cut and a cruel, fleshy face. His cold eyes were locked onto Wei He’s house, and he had clearly been watching it for a long time.
It was Chen Biao.
The other two guys were just as big and heavy. They looked well-fed, tough, and dangerous.
The sound of Wei He’s fast footsteps alerted the three thugs. Chen Biao turned around and froze when he saw Wei He wearing the Zheng family martial arts uniform. His eyes locked onto the bold Zheng character stitched across the chest.
Chen Biao took the pipe out of his mouth, gave Wei He one long, silent look, then turned around and walked away without saying a single word. The two thugs behind him quickly scrambled to follow. Both of them had caught a clear view of the word on Wei He's chest.
They knew who that character belonged to. Old Zheng of the Mountain Returning Fist was a famous name around these parts, and his school was a force nobody wanted to mess with.
Wei He watched the three men leave with a cold face. If he had to fight all three at once, he would definitely lose. But things were different now. With this official uniform on his back, Chen Biao wouldn’t dare act recklessly for the moment.
Still, Chen Biao had been preying on his sister for a long time. Wei He didn’t know how long this fake protection would keep him scared. As for Old Zheng, he took money from anyone who paid, so asking him for personal help was a foolish dream. It was a pure money-for-skills business. Nothing more.
Once his eldest sister stayed gone for too long and the threat of her presence faded, Chen Biao and his thugs would move again.
So, I still have to train hard and rely on myself.
He walked up to his door, pulled out his key, unlocked it, and stepped inside.
Wei Ying was huddled up tight in the dark corner of the room, too terrified to move. When she saw it was him, her eyes filled with tears, and she hurried toward him.
"Xiao He... I thought... I thought I'd never see you again!" She burst into loud, heavy sobs.
"It's okay. I'm back. You're safe now," Wei He said softly, trying to calm her down.
"Chen Biao was right outside the door! He was kicking it, pounding on it for so long! I was too scared to make a single sound!" Wei Ying sobbed out, her words trembling.
Wei He didn't speak. His eyes just grew colder and sharper.
"I understand. Don't worry, I will handle this. Starting tomorrow, you are coming with me to the martial arts yard. I spoke to Master Zheng, and he's letting you help out with chores and daily work. But as for pay..."
"I'll go! I'll go even if there's no pay! I really can't stay here anymore! When you were gone, Chen Biao came by several times a day. I was too scared to tell you before... I didn't want to burden you..."
Wei Ying cried as she explained, her voice rising with emotion. She knew about Old Zheng’s training yard. Almost everyone in the area did. If her brother could build a life there, it would be the safest place they could hope for.
"Then it's settled. We go tomorrow morning," Wei He said seriously.
He carved the names of Chen Biao and his two thugs deep into his memory. His sister had clearly lived through endless horror while he was away.
After comforting her, Wei He fetched several heavy pails of water from a nearby stream and filled the water storage tank. Then, he helped Wei Ying pack up their few belongings for the move tomorrow.
The sky turned dark, and the bright light of the moon spilled through the window. Wei He lit an oil lamp and watched as his sister finished mending their remaining clothes. He carefully reviewed everything he had planned today, made sure there were no mistakes, blew out the flame, and went to bed.
Late into the night, a quiet rustling sound snapped him awake.
Wei He opened his eyes, quietly slipped out of bed, and checked on his sister in the opposite corner. She was fast asleep. He crept over to the window and cracked it open just a tiny sliver to look out.
Down in the dark alley, a long line of ordinary townspeople moved in silent formation. Their clothes were mismatched, but their eyes glowed with a terrifying, unnatural light. Leading them were a man and a woman, both tall and painfully thin, dressed in pale white robes.
"Enter eternal bliss... enter eternal bliss... enter eternal bliss..."
The crowd repeated the same chant over and over in endless, dull murmurs. Different accents blared together into one creepy, unsettling noise that made his skin crawl.
Wei He silently closed the window frame and pulled back, not daring to watch any longer.
He had recognized several faces in that line. They were his everyday neighbors, but he had no idea they carried such a dark secret.
Uncle Zhao the blacksmith, Sister Feng who sold steamed buns, and Manager Xue from the pawnshop were all marching in that line, their faces blank and full of strange devotion, as if under a dark spell.
Wei He backed far away from the window, climbed back into bed, and stayed completely quiet.
The sounds of the marching cult slowly faded into the night. Wei He drifted in and out of a light, restless sleep until dawn.
As soon as the sky turned pale, he got up, woke his sister, and the two grabbed their bags and remaining cash before hurrying toward the training grounds.
He didn't know how to fight yet, so he stood no chance against Chen Biao's crew. In a broken, chaotic world like this, learning real power was his only path to survival. He took this opportunity with absolute seriousness.
With his secret Break-Through Gem skill hidden inside him, combined with the rising chaos of the world, he knew martial arts was his absolute best weapon.
The morning air was cold and damp, and the sun hadn't fully broken over the horizon yet. Wei He took his sister through a longer, safer detour before reaching the main gates of the Mountain Returning Fist compound.
Even at this early hour, deep shouts from heavy strength training echoed from inside the heavy walls.
Wei He stepped forward, knocked hard on the wood, and called out his name.
"Coming!"
The heavy door creaked open. A broad man with a pockmarked face looked at Wei He, then glanced at Wei Ying. His tired, lazy look suddenly vanished, replaced by an eager, overly warm smile.
"Come in, come in! Master is already inside drinking his tea. You must be Junior Brother Wei He, right? Very good, very good! I am Cheng Shaojiu, the third disciple here! You can just call me Third Senior Brother."
The scarred man gave a friendly, welcoming greeting, but his eyes kept darting toward Wei Ying.
Wei He smiled inwardly. Young men who practiced heavy martial arts were full of raw blood and high hormones, so reacting like this around a pretty woman was entirely normal. He didn't mind it, leading his nervous, frightened sister through the gates.
"Third Senior Brother, I'll go pay my respects to Master first, then we can talk," Wei He said politely.
"Go ahead, go ahead!" Cheng Shaojiu laughed.
The moment Wei Ying walked into the courtyard, many of the heavy men lifting stone blocks turned their heads to stare.
Wei He brought his sister directly before Old Zheng, bowing deep before the old master.
"Master Zheng, I brought my sister. You can assign her whatever work you need," Wei He said.
Old Zheng looked at Wei Ying. His eyes were cold and showed no strange desires. Seeing that she looked honest and quiet, he gave a slow nod.
"Let's get one thing straight first. You two were the ones who said she would work without pay, right?"
"Yes, that was our own choice," Wei He replied quickly.
"Good. Starting today, you practice your fists here, and your sister cleans the grounds, cooks meals, and washes clothes. Any problem with that? Also, the fee you paid only covers one year of basic training. After that, you pay again if you want to stay. Got it?"
Old Zheng's tiny eyes swept up and down over Wei He as he spoke in a cool tone.
"This disciple understands," Wei He bowed.
"Of course, if you have decent talent and master everything I teach you, I will keep teaching you the next steps within this one year."
Old Zheng waved his hand, calling over a hired middle-aged woman to show Wei Ying around and teach her the daily chores. Then he stood up and walked a slow circle around Wei He.
"Hold still."
Without warning, his fingers shot out and pinched hard right against a rib under Wei He's armpit.
Ugh!
Wei He's whole body went numb, and he almost lost feeling in half of his frame.
"Not bad," Old Zheng nodded. "Talent is neither great nor terrible. You're a bit old to start, but you can still build a decent foundation."
"Thank you, Master. I follow your guidance," Wei He said right away.
Seeing how respectful the young man was from start to finish, plus gaining a free worker for his yard, put Old Zheng in a slightly better mood.
"Fine. Since today is your first day, I'll tell you what martial arts really is, and why guys like us are stronger than regular people in a fight."
Wei He immediately locked his focus, ready to listen.
Knowing that Wei He could read, Old Zheng paced around for a moment before sinking back into his heavy wooden chair and taking another slow sip of tea.
A few other new disciples nearby saw this and quickly crowded closer, eager to hear the master speak.
Old Zheng cleared his throat, let out a few dry coughs, and opened his mouth.
"My throat is dry. Go fetch me some tea snacks first."
