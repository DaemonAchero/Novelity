Wang Shaojun looked up at the moon and sighed.
He thought of his close friend, who had already stepped into that mysterious realm.
The letter had given him hope, but it also left him torn.
Stepping forward meant giving up everything he had right now. All the years he had bled and strived for—his entire foundation—would go up in smoke.
The Wang family was not like the Thousand Poisons Sect. They would not pack up and move just because he felt like it.
Suddenly, a warm cloak was gently draped over his shoulders from behind.
Wang Shaojun flinched. He was so lost in his head that he had not even heard someone walking up.
He turned around. His wife was standing there, her eyes soft and full of tenderness.
"Husband. Whatever you decide, I am with you."
"You... you're not asleep yet?" Touched, Wang Shaojun reached out and took her hand.
"I was waiting for you," she said softly.
Looking at her face, Wang Shaojun realized he already had his answer.
Tianhai Island.
On the back side of the sea, a medium ship slowly raised its sails. Catching the wind, it drifted out into the deep blue.
Chang Xuezhong and Wei He stood by the railing, watching Tianhai Island shrink into the distance.
Chang Xuezhong stole a glance at Wei He, his mind a mess of thoughts.
He knew people were not created equal.
This might be one of his last chances to stand next to a guy like Wei He.
An ordinary practitioner like him, who took decades just to make a breakthrough, was not even in the same world as a genius like Wei He.
People like Chang Xuezhong were limited. If their power did not grow fast enough, the next attempt at breakthrough could mutate them or kill them outright. They might get an extra hundred years of life, but with no real hope for the future, most ended up rotting away in cheap pleasures.
They hit a wall. That was the truth.
But Wei He was different.
In a few short years, Wei He would leave him in the dust, step onto the high Cloud Platform, and become one of the grand geniuses of the sect.
He would stand shoulder-to-shoulder with the Dao Seeds.
The Dao Seeds were the absolute elite of the sect, the golden children bred to inherit the future.
Wei He was not there yet, but the potential was screamingly obvious.
Just look at the other newcomers of his generation. Who else got personal instruction from First Senior Sister Yuan Duzi?
A top expert from the Dao Records, one of the three grand masters of the Zhen realm, acted as his personal bodyguard.
On top of that, Yuan Duzi ordered the sect to give Wei He double the normal resources.
"Did First Senior Sister tell you to hang out with the other disciples of our generation?" Chang Xuezhong asked out of nowhere.
"No, she didn't. Why?" Wei He asked, surprised.
"Then you better brace yourself," Chang Xuezhong shook his head. "Our Xuanmiao Sect has three main Dao Seeds. They are built to be the pillars of the three branches. If First Senior Sister wanted you to handle management, she would have forced you to make contacts. Since she didn't, it means something else. Either way, she will definitely throw you and those Dao Seeds into the same room eventually."
Wei He got it instantly.
If he was not being raised to be a leader, then the future leaders would be told to suck up to him instead.
That made sense.
"Don't worry," Chang Xuezhong added. "The three Dao Seeds have very different personalities, but they all try to recruit guys with your level of talent."
He looked out over the water and suddenly stopped. "Alright, we're almost there."
"Where?" Wei He looked in the same direction, but saw nothing.
"Look down," Chang Xuezhong grinned, pointing a finger toward the water.
Wei He narrowed his eyes and looked down over the edge.
Outside the ship, the dark gray sea had changed. The thick, nasty floating sludge that usually covered the water was gone.
In its place was jet-black, crystal-clear water.
Through the deep ocean, Wei He caught a faint shape hiding underneath.
"Want to go take a look?" Chang Xuezhong chuckled.
"We can just go down?" Wei He asked.
In the hyper-sense state, you never knew what horrifying monsters were waiting in the deep.
"Of course. This place is different," Chang Xuezhong nodded. "It's one of the rare safe spots near our sect."
Without another word, the two jumped overboard.
With their protective energy shields, they did not have to worry about getting wet. They just needed to hold their breath. As experts, they could easily stay underwater for over ten hours.
Splash.
They cut through the water like two fish, Chang Xuezhong leading and Wei He right behind him.
Before long, a pale blue glow grew brighter and brighter, lighting up the pitch-black ocean floor.
They swam straight into the blue light.
As they got closer, Wei He finally saw what was glowing.
It was a massive stone statue of a coiled dragon!
The statue was over a hundred meters wide. The dragon head looked terrifying, every scale carved with insane detail. It looked like a real, living dragon fast asleep.
The whole beast radiated a soft blue light, purifying any nasty microorganisms or sea trash that tried to come near it.
Even though Wei He had prepared himself, the sight took his breath away.
They landed on one of the dragon's giant scales. Compared to the massive stone monster, the two men looked like small black specks.
"Is this really just a statue?" Wei He sent his voice through the water.
"It's a statue. An elder once tried to smash it. After breaking off a small piece, he found nothing but solid stone inside," Chang Xuezhong explained. "Besides, the stone is ridiculously hard. That elder went all out and could barely chip off a tiny piece."
Wei He nodded. He swam around the entire dragon, running his fingers over the scales.
It was cold and hard, but felt smooth like polished jade.
Finding nothing else weird about it, Wei He gave up and followed Chang Xuezhong back up to the surface.
"Monsters usually hate this spot," Chang Xuezhong said as they climbed back onto the deck. "If a beast you can't fight comes after you, you can hide here. Though, if you are in the normal world, you won't be able to see the dragon at all."
Wei He nodded. "Are there many places like this?"
"Not really. There are only a few across the whole Great Yuan realm," Chang Xuezhong replied. "It's called the Coiled Dragon Spot."
He smiled and added, "Fun fact: almost every major sect is built right next to one of these spots. A lot of people think our sect's sacred ground gets its power directly from this stone dragon. But that's just a rumor."
Wei He nodded quietly. He had a feeling these strange spots were tied to the deepest secrets of the major sects.
"Alright, we can't stay long. That blue light isn't just bad for monsters; it messes with us too if we stay near it too long. Let's go."
Chang Xuezhong turned to order the crew to turn the ship around.
Wei He frowned slightly. That blue light felt a lot like radiation.
The ship slowly turned and headed back toward the mainland.
The wind picked up, driving rough waves against the hull and making the boat sway violently.
The sky turned dark and heavy with rain clouds, lit up by quick flashes of lightning.
As the light faded, heavy rain began to fall.
Wei He looked up at the drops.
In this hyper-sense world, the raindrops acted like strong acid. Every time a drop hit the sea or the deck, thick white smoke hissed up into the air.
Inside the smoke, twisted human faces seemed to scream and evaporate.
"Cough... cough!"
Suddenly, a crew member doubled over, coughing violently.
Wei He turned and saw dark red, grape-like tumors bursting out across the man's face and skin.
A long, pale gray worm with a tiny human face wriggled its way out of the man's ear.
The worm hit the deck, but before it could crawl away, the acid rain melted it down to a tiny skeleton, dissolving it into thin air.
"That's a plague worm," Chang Xuezhong said, stepping up to send his voice over. "There are too many types to count. But when they crawl out like that, it means he's actually getting better."
Wei He was speechless.
Today, everything he thought he knew about the world had been crushed and rebuilt.
"Shocking, isn't it?" Chang Xuezhong asked quietly.
"Yeah. It really is," Wei He agreed.
"If you keep your hyper-sense active like this, your second breakthrough will come fast. But if your internal energy isn't strong enough by then, you'll be in deep trouble."
"How deep?" Wei He asked. "Mutation?"
"Mutation is just the outside," Chang Xuezhong sighed. "The real danger is losing your mind. The deep world will chew up most of your consciousness. If you're lucky, you might keep a tiny scrap of who you were."
Chang Xuezhong let out a long breath. "Then again, the world outside is in total chaos. People are dying everywhere. Sometimes, losing your mind isn't even the worst way to go."
Wei He said nothing. He just stared out into the dark, twisted ocean, wrapped in complete silence.

Outside the window, a one-armed little girl held the hand of a filthy little boy. They stood by the dirt road, staring blankly as the carriage rolled past.
An old man with white hair carried his blood-soaked son on his back, dragging his feet step by painful step.
A few skin-and-bones women sat in a circle, desperately chewing on dry tree bark. They forced the rough bark down their throats, not caring if it shredded their throats on the way down.
Slam.
Wan Xuetian pulled the window curtain shut, blocking Wan Ling's view.
"Stop looking," Wan Xuetian said, his voice heavy.
The heavy wooden wheels kept turning, and horses' hooves beat against the dirt. Inside the carriage, the only sound left was their soft breathing.
"How did it end up like this?" Wan Ling looked up at him. "The Central State had so many forces. Grand Marshal Li Zhouzhao had five hundred thousand troops! How could they lose so fast?"
Wan Xuetian could not answer.
Li Zhouzhao had the backing of the terrifying Chaos God Cult, and he was even one of its deputy leaders. His personal power was terrifyingly high.
He was backed by ancient families that had lasted a thousand years, each boasting countless top-tier experts.
The Central State was packed with masters, loaded with armies, and protected by a massive array that could block a grandmaster.
Yet, that unbreakable fortress fell in just a few short months.
Millions of people in the capital were slaughtered or burned alive.
This fire was so much worse than the last one.
Even the ancient families who joined forces could not stop the tide. The survivors gathered what was left of their clans and ran, fleeing toward the few states Great Yuan had not lost yet.
The Wan family was no exception.
Wan Ling and Wan Xuetian had just made it back to the Central State when the nightmare hit. The family elders were already gone or dead, leaving behind only the weak, the old, and the sick.
Wan Ling's parents were long gone.
The core leaders of the Wan family had cut their losses, abandoning these relatives like trash so they could escape first.
Left with nothing, the two had no choice but to gather the remaining survivors and run for their lives toward the outer lands.
