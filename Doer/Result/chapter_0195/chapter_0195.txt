“Where’s the person with the clue?” Wei He felt a flicker of hope.
“On the other side of Qingqi County, less than a mile away. I’ll take you,” Jiuying said. He stood up, but stumbled and almost fell.
Wei He caught him.
“You need rest.” Wei He frowned. Jiuying’s balance was terrible, completely unlike a martial master. He clearly hadn’t slept in a long time and was still carrying injuries.
“There’s no time,” Jiuying shook his head. “More people are getting sick every hour. It’s a full plague now. If we don’t find a cure soon...”
“Let’s walk together. I might have some advice,” Wei He said. Modern science understood plagues well, especially how infections spread. Jiuying clearly knew nothing about transmission.
“Alright.” Jiuying agreed and looked at Sun Yan’er. She sat by the coffin, quietly burning joss paper, oblivious to their talking.
“Yan’er, I’ll be back soon. Stay inside,” Jiuying muttered. He looked at the coffin one last time, sighed silently, and walked out with Wei He.
Outside, white banners hung from almost every doorstep. Mourning music and faint crying drifted down the empty streets.
Before they had gone far, they saw a five-year-old boy in dirty gray clothes sitting in front of a small dirt hut. He stared blankly at the road.
Jiuying recognized him. A few days ago, the boy’s parents had given their last bowl of medicine soup to him.
“What is it?” Wei He asked gently.
“Nothing.”
As they passed the hut, both men glanced through the open door. Two straw mats lay in the yard. On them were two pale corpses—a man and a woman. The boy’s parents.
Jiuying looked down. He had poisoned plenty of enemies and innocent bystanders in his life without hesitation. But today, his heart felt heavy.
Wei He noticed his mood and stayed quiet.
They soon arrived at a square stone yard on the other side of town. The front door was half-open, and the place was dead silent.
Jiuying knocked.
“Anyone home?”
A weak, raspy voice came from inside. “Come in.”
They stepped into the small yard. A small garden of vegetables in the corner was already dying. Clothes hung on a line in the middle.
“Hold your breath,” Wei He warned. “Plagues can spread through the air.”
Jiuying nodded, and they held their breath as they walked into the main room.
It was dark inside. Bows, hunting knives, and several intact animal skins hung on the walls.
A pale, thick-built man lay on the bed. He held a bloody cloth over his mouth.
“Doctor Jiu! Please, sit. My cough is getting worse. The soup only helped for two days, and now it’s back. Can you write me another prescription?”
The man looked at Jiuying with desperate eyes. His calloused knuckles showed he was a martial artist, though not yet a master.
“Let me check your pulse first,” Jiuying said.
Wei He stood by and picked up Jiuying’s medical notes.
Variant 5: Coughing blood, fever, eye inflammation, thick mucus, red spots and cracks on tongue. Cold limbs, hot torso. Prescription 4 failed. Symptoms only suppressed temporarily, then rebound worse...
After a quick check, Jiuying wrote a prescription and offered a few words of comfort. Then he asked about the cave with the Silken Force record.
“I don’t remember the exact path,” the man gasped. “I was lost at night. But my partner in the forest is a squirrel. It guided me to the cave.”
“A squirrel?” Wei He and Jiuying were surprised.
“Yeah. I call her Little菱 because her tail is sharp like a water chestnut,” the man smiled weakly.
“Can she lead us there?” Wei He asked. “We’ll pay you.”
“She’s shy. She won’t show herself to strangers. I have to be there.”
“Alright,” Jiuying agreed. Whether the man was telling the truth or just holding out for a cure, they needed to heal him first.
The man described a few details about Silken Force. Wei He compared them with what Wang Shaojun had shown him before, and it matched. A low-level fighter couldn’t fake those specifics.
Jiuying gave the man a few more instructions before they stepped out.
“How is he?” Wei He asked.
“Bad,” Jiuying said grimly. “I was wrong before. The root isn’t in the lungs—it’s in the kidneys.”
“Kidney deficiency? Pathogens causing a total organ collapse?” Wei He frowned.
“It’s not that simple. My previous formulas didn’t work. We must clear the pathogen first, but I haven't identified it yet. I need more patients to compare.”
“Tell me what you need,” Wei He said. The plague was spreading fast. From what he heard at the tavern, half of Taizhou was already infected. Even without the Silken Force, he needed to stop this before it reached his own people.
“I need manpower to sort and screen patients,” Jiuying said quickly. “I also need people to process herbs, boil medicine, and handle isolation.”
“Done.”
The Wanqing Sect was small, but they could spare the hands. Herbs were easy to buy.
Together, they set up a makeshift clinic in Qingqi County. The local government sent men to help as well.
Jiuying and a few doctors placed dozens of iron beds in a large courtyard. Iron was hard to sleep on, but boiling water could instantly sanitize it—a tip from Wei He.
Wearing simple masks, the team began taking in patients. Jiuying’s old friends, the Suns, were already dead, and he had promised to protect their two surviving children. He had to figure this out.
Sect members washed their hands with running water and got to work. Pale, coughing patients filled the beds. Screams and bloody coughs echoed through the yard while crates of herbs were hauled in to be boiled.
Wei He walked out to see a long line forming down the street. Few doctors dared to open their doors during a plague, so people rushed to Jiuying’s clinic.
Just then, two sect members carried in a man with blood on his chest. His face was ghostly pale, but his ears were deep red.
Two children followed behind—a seven-year-old girl holding her three-year-old brother’s hand. She gently patted her brother’s head, telling him daddy would be fine.
“Sect Leader, Doctor Jiu needs you inside,” a disciple said softly.
Wei He nodded and walked into the inner room. The heavy smell of herbal medicine filled the air.
Jiuying was using a thin glass tube to collect a saliva sample from the man on the bed.
The man gasped for air as blood seeped from his nose. “Doctor... am I going to die?”
Jiuying stayed silent. The man’s internal organs were completely fried. His blood was as thick as syrup. Surviving this long was already a miracle.
“I don’t want to die...” the man sobbed, clutching Jiuying’s arm. “Please... save me! If I die, what happens to my girls? They’re so small... Who will feed them? I don't want to die...”
A massive gush of thick blood poured from his mouth, choking him. He thrashed violently on the bed like a fish out of water, tears streaming down his face, but no more words came out.
A moment later, his body went limp. His eyes slowly turned lifeless.
Jiuying sighed and waved his hand. “Take him out.”
In just half a day, more than six patients had died right in front of him.
“Use the side door,” a disciple suggested quietly. “His two little girls are still waiting at the front.”
Jiuying paused. “Where’s the rest of his family?”
“He’s a widower. It’s just him and the kids.”
Jiuying closed his eyes for a second. “Side door it is.”
