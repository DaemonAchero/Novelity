Nanshan District.
Located in the south of the city, this district was packed with shops and stalls selling cloth and textiles. But most of the storefronts were locked tight today. The noise and energy they once had were gone. Taxes had grown heavier and heavier over recent years, and trade fees were far too high to bear. Only faint traces of past glory remained.
Wei He walked along the side of the street. He kept his head low, wrapping most of his face with cloth, and moved with quick steps. He had swapped his clothes for common grey hemp garments.
These past few days, he had not relied only on his Senior Brother Cheng Shaojiu for information. After following Chen Biao and his two men carefully a few times, he had tracked down where all three of them lived.
Ducking beneath a broken bamboo pole used for drying clothes, Wei He quickly arrived before a low, rundown courtyard. The gate sat half-open. Just then, a woman with red eyes walked out, holding packed bags and leading a child who was only a few years old.
The woman was somewhat attractive, but the tear tracks and swollen eyes on her face showed she was in a terrible mood. Carrying her child, she cast a glance at Wei He’s outfit, kept her head down, and left without even closing the gate.
Wei He knew who she was. This was Chen Biao’s wife, Shanju. Before marrying him, she was a famous widow in the area.
"Who’s outside?!" Chen Biao’s voice came from the courtyard. He sounded confused.
Wei He pushed the gate and stepped inside. In the middle of the yard sat Chen Biao on the steps before the inner room. One of his arms was wrapped in grey bandages, with spots of blood seeping through.
"Chen Biao, I came as soon as I heard you were hurt," Wei He said with a smile.
"You? Who are you...?" Chen Biao stood up, puzzled.
He wanted to say more, but his vision suddenly went blurry. A handful of lime powder was tossed hard into his face.
"Ah!!"
Chen Biao swung his right arm wildly, trying to block any attack. Wei He kicked him hard in the belly, grabbed a hoe from the corner of the wall, and smashed it down on Chen Biao’s head again and again.
Thump! Thump! Thump! Thump!!
Tossing the hoe aside, Wei He did not even look at Chen Biao, who lay lifeless on the ground. He turned, left the yard, and pulled the gate shut behind him. Then, without a word, he covered his head and walked away at a quick pace.
He did not stop until he left Nanshan District and reached a small river between the districts. Sitting down among the wild grass, he panted heavily.
Everything had gone just as he planned, even smoother than expected. The injured Chen Biao could not fight back at all, especially after being blinded by the lime powder. He had run this through his mind so many times, yet when it came to real action, it was far easier than he had imagined.
The big, strong, and intimidating Chen Biao he had pictured was finished off in one quick sequence without even making a sound.
'I... no one saw me enter or leave. My head was covered the whole time, and I never said my name. Only the widow Shanju saw me, but she shouldn't have seen my face.'
Wei He’s hands trembled as he untied the cloth from his head, took off his outer coat, and flipped it inside out to hide the blood spots.
'I wonder how Chen Biao is? Hitting him that hard... he should be...' Wei He stopped thinking about it.
It was common for people to die in these dark times. Bodies were pulled from the city gutters all the time. Outside the walls, wild dogs often gnawed on human bones, and those dogs were later trapped and eaten by people. But the difference this time was that Chen Biao might really be dead, and he died by Wei He's own hands.
Wei He took deep breaths, trying to calm his body down.
'Chen Biao kept his eyes on my second sister, Wei Ying. He often does human trafficking, and he almost stole my money for learning martial arts. If he doesn't die, my sister will never be safe. So, I am removing a evil for the people. I am right. I am right.'
Wei He kept making excuses for himself, but deep down, he knew the truth. Originally, he only planned to cripple Chen Biao, not kill him. But when the fight started, his mind went blank. All his strength burst out at once because he was terrified Chen Biao would scream his name. To stop him from speaking, he swung at his head instinctively. It took five or six blows before he realized he had gone too far.
Thinking back, his head was wrapped, so Chen Biao probably had no idea who he was anyway.
'Besides, a hoe made of solid iron, swung with my eighty-pound lifting power... and all hits to the head...'
Wei He knew in his heart that Chen Biao was done for. He was not worried about the court or guards. The officials did not care at all. People died and crimes happened every day inside and outside the city, but the guards acted like they were blind and deaf. They ignored it completely.
'So... this is what it feels like to kill someone...' His hands were still shaking, but he knew he had to get used to it. In an era like this, if you are not ruthless, you cannot survive. He did not want to die, so others had to.
After a long time, he stood up and walked back into the district.
A short while later, at a small flat house where one of Chen Biao’s followers lived, Wei He walked in with no expression on his face. Soon after, he walked out with a bit of blood on his hands. It was scary the first time, but after getting used to it for a bit, he was no longer that nervous.
Next was the third location: the home of Chen Biao’s second follower. It was a tiny dirt hut situated between two districts. By the time Wei He arrived, dusk was falling.
The dirt hut stood isolated in an empty, desolate lot, surrounded by stacks of dried corn stalks. Wei He walked to the door and knocked gently.
Knock, knock, knock.
Silence. No sound came from inside.
Knock, knock, knock.
He knocked again and waited. After a moment, there was still no reply. He thought for a second, then walked over to the window and peeked through the gap.
Houses like this were roughly built, with wide cracks in the doors and windows. You could see what was inside if you got close. Using the faint evening light, Wei He caught sight of someone lying sideways on the bed. It was a man wearing a black short vest.
'There's someone inside.'
He walked back to the door, looked left and right, and saw no one around. He stepped back a few strides, charged forward, and threw a heavy kick.
Bang!
The kick smashed the broken wooden door open with ease. After training for half a year, he was no longer that weak, skinny kid. His build was now similar to Chen Biao's, and his strength was likely the same. One or two average men might not even be able to hold him down.
The door was kicked open, but there was still no sound from inside. Puzzled and alert, Wei He stood at the doorway and looked in.
"Zhao Deli, get up," he called out.
The person on the bed did not move at all, as if he heard nothing. A foul odor filled the room, smelling like rotting meat.
Wei He's expression changed slightly as a guess formed in his mind. He stepped closer, reached out, and pulled the person on the bed around.
A pale, skin-and-bones face appeared before his eyes. The two eyeballs in the face were dried up and drained of moisture. Small black insects crawled in and out of the nostrils.
This Zhao Deli, Chen Biao's follower, had been dead for who knows how long. No one had even noticed he was dead.
Worst of all, a small child was curled up in the man's arms. The child's body was shriveled into a tight ball, its tiny face equally reduced to skin and bones. Black insects were crawling in and out of its ears and open mouth as well.
Wei He felt his scalp go numb. He quickly backed away, ran out of the house, and gasped for air. Part of it was fear from seeing the corpses, but most of it was the stench.
Three men. Two died by his hands, and one had been dead for a long time.
The grudge was settled, but Wei He felt no joy at all. He washed his hands in a nearby river and returned to his old house in a daze.
The image of that dried corpse on the bed kept flashing through his mind. He had asked around before and knew Zhao Deli had a young child. He just never expected the man to die at home holding his own kid for so long.
'This is a world of chaos. This is what human life is worth here.'
Wei He sat alone on his bed. Faint moonlight hit the side of his face through the window, making him look pale. He could not sleep well all night, tossing and turning as the image of that dead child kept appearing in his head.
When daylight came, he got up and ate the Golden Money Bug meat he had bought from the pharmacy. This stuff kept hunger away better than normal food, making the Breakbound Pearl on his chest gather energy faster.
Wei He tried to shake the images from his mind. Thinking about the special ability on his chest since he crossed over brought him a bit of peace.
However, while the insect meat helped gather energy quickly, the taste was awful. Wei He put a small piece in his mouth. Chewing it felt like chewing dry wood, and he had to wash it down with a big gulp of water.
On the other side.
Nanshan District, outside Zhao Deli's dirt hut.
Before the sun rose, several burly men in grey short shirts arrived at the door.
"Is it here?"
"Yes."
They spoke in low voices. Then, one of them kicked the door open and quickly went inside. He came out shortly after.
"They're all dead."
"That woman stayed here last. Those black bugs are the proof. Search the area. The black word bugs are still active, so she couldn't have gone far," the leader said in a deep voice.
"Judging by the tracks, she must have stayed here to hide in this hut for a bit before leaving."
"We must find her. Also, leave one person behind to silence anyone who might have seen the black word bugs."
"Got it!"
A short while later, a raging fire consumed the dirt hut. After making sure everything inside was burned to ashes, the men turned and slipped away quietly.

Time flew by. In the blink of an eye, another month passed.
Wei He practiced his moves hard every day and began his first skin-grinding training.
Whoosh, whoosh, whoosh.
In the main yard of the Mountain-Returning Fist school, Wei He stood bare-armed before a large wooden tub, punching the sand inside again and again.
Hard, dry sand was the first step to grinding the skin. Wei He moved at a steady pace, thrusting his fists deep into the sand each time so every part of his knuckles rubbed against it completely.
After grinding like this for half an hour, he pulled his fists out and dipped them into a tub of medicinal liquid nearby, soaking them for fifty breaths. Charcoal burned beneath the tub to keep the liquid warm.
Several other men were practicing skin-grinding around him. Outside the circle, more people waited with arms crossed for their turn. Each spot for skin-grinding training was hard to come by.
