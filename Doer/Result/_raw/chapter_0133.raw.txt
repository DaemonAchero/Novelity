His knowledge of medicine had hit a bottleneck and needed constant experimentation. Practicing his secret martial arts also required a larger, more private environment. Staying at the branch all the time was dangerous; it was too easy for someone to catch him in the act.

Once the thought crossed his mind, he could not shake it off.
He read for a while longer, adjusting his notes on current martial arts systems. Then he recorded new, useful intelligence before starting his daily training routine.
The Rain-Gathering Cloud Skill was his top priority.
Next was the Whale Flood Art, followed by the Iron Ridge Armor used to strengthen his Rain-Gathering Force.
The Rain-Gathering Cloud Skill progressed as slowly as ever. The speed at which it converted Blood Mark into inner force was average. He had only finished the third layer, and he was still far from the fourth. He had not even reached halfway.
As for the Whale Flood Art, Wei He frowned.
He was working on the third layer, but progress was painfully slow. It seemed like there was trouble with his Blood Mark.
The Whale Flood Art worked by weaving Blood Marks into separate blood sacs to store more blood and raise the body's total blood capacity. However, after the second layer, one had to enter a state called Whale Breath.
Only in this state could a practitioner weave the next layer of blood sacs.
'The third layer of the Whale Flood Art should be a massive jump in power. But this Whale Breath state... I have no clue how to reach it. The manual explains it in a total blur. And since it is not a bottleneck barrier, I cannot use the Breakthrough Bead to force through it.'
Wei He had a headache thinking about it.
Still, his current strength was enough for now. There was no rush; he could take his time to figure it out.
Standing up, Wei He stretched out his hands and stared at his fists.
His fists used to be slightly dark and hard, but now they were bruised and swollen. This was the payback for beating two martial masters to death earlier.
'Brute force is high, but equal forces react. Without enough defense, killing others damages my own body. Things will only improve once my Rain-Gathering Force upgrades my defense.'
With his increased blood and energy, Wei He no longer felt held back by his natural bone structure.
The boost from the Whale Flood Art finally allowed his body to match his fellow disciples. He was no longer below average.
In actual combat, his explosive strength could take down an Inner Force master in one punch. This sheer power even outmatched his Rain-Gathering Force.
Yet Wei He knew very well that the original goal of the Whale Flood Art was longevity, not combat. It did not involve inner force at all; it only boosted physical blood and energy.
More blood meant more strength, but raw strength alone did not make one invincible. If his body could not take the recoil, killing the enemy at his own expense was pointless.
He practiced the Whale Flood Art for a bit longer without making any progress.
Wei He shifted his focus to the Iron Ridge Armor.
He had reached the Second Blood stage in this art. As soon as the Breakthrough Bead filled up again, he could break through to Third Blood. The progress was smooth.
Having trained through many Third Blood martial arts before, he knew the key lay in sensing and understanding inner force. This presented no real bottleneck for him.

There were three types of mixed poisons from the trade. All of them could attach to inner force, circulate with it, and coat the body's surface.
These poisons had different toxic levels and speeds of effect. To bypass the sharp senses of martial artists, most were colorless, clear, and almost odorless. They were extremely hard to spot.
Suddenly, a thought struck Wei He.
If Wang Shaojun could get these drugs, Yan Jiuhai on Wan Qingqing's side might also get them.
Yan Jiuhai had a bad reputation. If he drugged Wan Qingqing to force a marriage, both the Tianyin Sect and the Lishan Faction would likely accept the result.

Average martial masters rarely saw drugs that could affect Inner Force practitioners, making them drop their guard easily.

His motive was clearly not simple.

There was likely a deep secret hidden behind this.
Wei He wrote the letter quickly. Aside from basic greetings and martial questions, he warned her about the odorless, colorless poison.
As long as Wan Qingqing stayed alert, the chance of her getting caught would drop.
Then again, Wei He thought he might be overthinking it. She might have seen similar drugs before.
He sealed the letter and ordered Ding Yang to send it to the Tianyin Sect.
Now, all he had to do was wait until October, return to the sect, and see what changed.
If it was within his power, he would lend a hand.
The long period of peace was coming to an end.
Wei He had a feeling that quiet days like these would be rare in the future.

Late September.
Rain.
Outside Xuanjing City, Taizhou Prefecture lay straight to the north. The other three sides were large areas of wild mountains and forests.
The Jing River flowed through them like a ribbon.
The people in the city split these wild mountains into two areas. One was called Yanshan, shaped like an inkstone. The other was called Yangshan, shaped like a goat.
Deep inside Yangshan Mountain.
A large, tall figure moved fast through the woods. He left almost no footprints on the ground, leaping mostly from tree trunks and branches.
The figure had long hair over his shoulders and wore a black face mask. Standing two meters tall, he was a rare sight even in Xuanjing City.
Normal martial artists were usually around 1.75 meters. Those who were taller usually practiced special body skills, making their strange bodies easy to spot.
Yet this figure had a well-shaped body with no odd spots, despite his height.
Soon, the figure slowed down and jumped softly onto a patch of open ground covered in fallen leaves.
On the open ground stood a stone table covered in dry leaves.
The table had chessboard lines carved into it. On both sides stood wooden stool stumps of just the right height.
Beside the stone table stood a grass hut. It looked long abandoned, surrounded by thick weeds that almost blocked the door.
The figure let out a soft sound of surprise and walked up to the stone table.
Light filtered down through the trees, shining on his face. It was Wei He, who was supposed to be staying at the Tianyin Hall.
Ever since his breakthrough, his training had caused too much noise. He felt staying at the Tianyin Hall was no longer safe or easy, so he decided to build a hidden base outside.

‘I did not expect to find a place where someone used to live. Looking at this chessboard, people must have played chess here before.’
Wei He wiped away the fallen leaves, showing the deep lines carved into the stone below.
The lines were rough, looking as if they were drawn with human fingers.
Wei He did not mind it. Any third-blood martial artist who trained in palm or finger techniques could easily write on stone.
He turned his gaze toward the grass hut nearby.
It was dead quiet. There were no sounds of bugs or birds around, which felt strange.
He walked closer, his internal force flowing through his body as he stayed alert for any sudden danger.
He had only come to find a spot for a temp base, but stumbled upon this place by chance.
