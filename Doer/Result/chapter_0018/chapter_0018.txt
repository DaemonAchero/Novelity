Ten miles out, the view was the same.
Wei He looked out. It was noon, and the bright sun lit up the distant woods on both sides. There were no farms along the main road, only wild grass. Clumps of tall grass grew everywhere, but they were scattered.
Despite Cheng Kai’s warnings about the dangers outside, the trip was quiet.
On the first day, they reached a village named Fan Family Village and stayed at a roadside inn on its edge. Cheng Kai knew the innkeeper well, so the group spent a peaceful night there. Early the next morning, before sunrise, they moved out again.
This became their routine: ride all day, reach a village, sleep at an inn, and repeat.
Wei He shared a room with Cheng Kai. The room had two beds, and they slept in their clothes near the window to watch for danger. But nothing happened. The route, the stops, and the lodging were all planned in advance. It took away all the mystery of escorting a convoy. Wei He even began to doubt if the world outside the city was truly as dangerous as people said.
On the seventh night, they were close to their destination. They stopped near a large stone bridge between barren hills. The men lit a bonfire and parked the carts and horses near the bridgehouse to form a half-circle guard wall.
The wind howled through the hills, making the shadows dance in the firelight. The old stone bridge stretched for hundreds of meters, with a guardhouse at each end. These old buildings were abandoned and falling apart, just like the bridge itself.
The guardhouse was round, seven or eight meters high, and about seventeen meters wide. Inside, it was empty. Over twenty men crowded around two bonfires, talking and laughing. The cold wind blew through two open window holes, but the heat from the crowd kept the place warm.
Wei He sat in a dark corner, poking the fire with a thick stick to make it burn brighter. To his left, a few men were bragging in a dialect he could not understand. To his right, one man carved wood with a small knife while another slept against the wall.
Cheng Kai, the head escort, walked back inside after setting up the night watch. He spoke to the men at the other fire, and a few of them got up and left.
Soon, the faint sound of fists and feet hitting each other came from outside.
"It's starting again! Let's go watch!"
A few escorts got excited and ran out the door. The three men near Wei He also jumped up and followed them. One of the men talking in dialect noticed Wei He sitting still and laughed.
"Come on, Brother Wei! You train in real Return-Mountain Fists. Come outside and show the boys what you can do!" He pulled Wei He’s sleeve.
"What?" Wei He asked, confused, as he was dragged out into the cold air.
Behind the guardhouse was a flat piece of ground used for cooking and chopping firewood. Two escorts stood facing each other, circling slowly. One man held a torch for light, while others cheered. This spot sat against a cliff, blocked by the building, so the wind was weak here.
Cheng Kai saw Wei He walk out and smiled. "This is our fun event for every job."
"Fun event?"
"That's right," Cheng Kai touched his mustache. "On the road, we can't drink or gamble. All we can do is talk, and sometimes we can't even talk loudly. After a while, men get frustrated. So right before we reach the end, we hold a fight. It wakes everyone up, gets the blood moving, and lets us see who is strong and who is weak. That way, if real trouble comes, we know who can handle what."
Wei He understood. Wherever men gathered, they always wanted to see who was superior.
A loud shout cut through the air. The two fighters smashed together.
The tall one swung an iron staff with good skill, moving back and forth in a steady rhythm. The short, thick one fought empty-handed. His punches were fast, hard, and loud.
After a short fight, the staff user got too close to use his weapon well. He was not fast enough to pull back, so he took a hard hit to the shoulder and lost.
"Bai Shengfeng's staff style is solid," Cheng Kai noted. "He once beat five thugs alone in Nanshan Town with that stick. But he still lost to Li Er."
Wei He watched quietly. To him, the fight was mediocre. Their speed and reaction time were average. They were only slightly stronger than normal people, just with proper martial arts training.
Li Er won another round right after. His strength was his deep stamina; even after two fights, he was not out of breath.
"How about it? Want to give it a try?" Cheng Kai turned to Wei He with a smile. "Li Er's Nine-Mountain Fist is tough. Even I have to focus to beat him."
"Okay," Wei He said. He took off his cloak and stepped into the open area.
"Wei He," he held up his hands in respect.
"Li Er," the short, muscular man replied. He looked mean, with a scar running through his left eyebrow. His thick muscles bulged in the firelight.
They stood still. Wei He watched closely. Even though he had seen Li Er fight twice, he did not rush.
Under the torchlight, Li Er grew impatient. After two wins, his confidence was high. He threw a quick straight punch at Wei He's face. It was a test punch, fast and easy to change into another move. If Wei He was slow, Li Er would rush him down. If Wei He blocked it, Li Er could feel his power.
Wei He raised his hands to protect his head and lightly slapped the punch away.
Pah.
Their hands clashed. Wei He felt the hit. The force was heavy; even with his upgraded blood power, the impact made his hand feel numb. No wonder Li Er had won so easily before.
But that was all.
Wei He shot his left fist straight at Li Er's chest. His hand turned into a pale shadow. It was the same straight punch, but much faster than Li Er's.
Li Er tried to dodge, but the fist shifted mid-air and struck his shoulder.
Bam!
Li Er stumbled back, losing the opening hit. Unhappy with the result, he charged in again. They exchanged a few more blows, but after three moves, Wei He hit him hard in the belly.
Li Er finally saw the truth. Their raw strength was close, but their speed was on different levels. Wei He barely moved from his spot. A few quick strikes were all it took to end the fight.
"Impressive! I lose!" Li Er’s face turned red. He bowed and backed away into the crowd.
The cheers around them quieted down. Few people knew Wei He well, so nobody cheered for him.
"Anyone else?" Cheng Kai shouted to the group. "Think carefully first. Li Er is strong and he still lost. If you're weak, don't ask for a beating."
"I'll go," a man stepped out holding a short knife. "Sorry, brother, but I'm best with weapons."
"Fine," Wei He nodded. In this world, he would face weapons sooner or later.
The man slashed twice at Wei He with fast, flash moves. He used the back of the blade to avoid killing him, but the attacks still looked fierce.
However, Wei He's reaction speed was far beyond an ordinary man now. He dodged the blades, tripped the man to ruin his balance, and threw a heavy side punch.
The punch knocked the knife straight out of the man's hand. It clattered to the dirt.
Wei He had won again. He stayed in the center, looking around to get more real combat experience. Though he said nothing, everyone knew he wanted the next fighter.
Two more men stepped up, but Wei He beat them both with ease. The gap in their blood strength was too big. Facing normal fighters was simple for him now; their movements looked like slow motion to his eyes.
"Let me do it," a tall woman with long legs walked out.
Her name was Cheng Jing, a distant relative of the Cheng family. She was not a basic escort, but one of the two leaders on this trip.
"Ah, Jing is going in. Now this will be fun," Cheng Kai chuckled. He was just thinking about stepping in himself so Wei He wouldn't embarrass all the veterans.
As a female leader, Cheng Jing was strong. She had a good name in Stone Bridge Town.
"I've fought many strong people in the five local gangs," Cheng Jing said, staring straight at Wei He with bright eyes. "I only lost once. In our escort house, I am second only to the Head Escort and the Young Master. If you beat me, you'll be in the top ten fighters in the whole town, excluding the grand masters."
Wei He stayed quiet.
"You keep fighting the old members to make a name for yourself," she continued in a sharp, clear voice. "If I wasn't here tonight, fine. But since I am, I won't let you!"
Wei He paused. "I didn't mean to—"
"Don't lie," Cheng Jing cut him off. "I've seen many people like you. You act quiet on the outside, but you are wild and greedy on the inside!"
"You misunderstand—"
"I don't! Wanting to climb higher isn't wrong, but you chose the wrong way to do it!"
"Will you just listen—" Wei He raised his voice.
"Listen to what? Nothing you say can hide what you are. Save your breath!"
Cheng Jing raised her long leg high and brought it down like an axe.
Whoosh!
The kick was fast—just as fast as Wei He. She was someone who had broken through her blood limits too!
